# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Large-Cap Equity Panel: Evidence from Ten Accounting and Market Signals

## Abstract

Using a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 1,217 index constituents over 120 months, I evaluate ten candidate characteristics as predictors of next-month returns. Three signals are statistically and economically valid: standardized unexpected earnings (`sue`), gross profitability (`gross_prof`), and asset growth (`asset_growth`), with `sue` dominating by a wide margin (mean rank IC = 0.0952, t = 26.55, 100% of months positive). Idiosyncratic volatility (`idio_vol`) is a weaker but statistically robust negative predictor. The remaining six features—`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—show rank ICs statistically indistinguishable from zero. A composite strategy combining the four valid signals yields an expected annualized return near 12–13% with a Sharpe ratio of roughly 2.5, though capacity, turnover costs, and the absence of out-of-sample validation temper these estimates.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains one row per stock per month for 500 index constituents per month across 120 months (60,000 rows, 1,217 distinct stocks). The universe is point-in-time: stocks removed from the index (including via distress or delisting) remain in the data through their final month, with the return over that month included. This mitigates survivorship bias but implies that some extreme negative returns in the left tail are genuine delisting events rather than data errors.

**Features.** Ten characteristics, all constructed from information available at the end of month *t*: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `sue`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month *t+1*. The timing convention is clean: fundamentals are lagged to the latest report available at *t*, and `sue` is defined using the most recent fiscal quarter ending on or before *t*, with announcements occurring in the following month—so no forward-looking information contaminates the signal.

**Evaluation.** For each feature I use the provided within-month Spearman rank IC, averaged across the 120 months, with a t-statistic computed as mean IC / (IC standard deviation) × √120. I also use the equal-weighted quintile long-short (top minus bottom quintile) mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio. A signal is deemed "valid" if |t-stat| is large (roughly ≥ 2.5, to account for multiple testing across ten features) and the long-short portfolio has the same sign as the IC with an economically meaningful magnitude. I do not have access to the raw data, so all inference is based on the supplied summary statistics.

**Strategy construction.** For the valid signals, I form a composite score by cross-sectionally standardizing (or ranking) each signal within month, signing it according to its estimated direction, and averaging. I then buy the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly.

## Results

**Valid signals.** `sue` is the standout. Its mean rank IC is 0.0952 with a t-statistic of 26.55, and the IC is positive in *every one* of the 120 months—an extraordinarily consistent earnings-announcement drift signature. The quintile long-short portfolio earns 2.96% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 7.65. This is implausibly high as a tradable Sharpe (see Limitations), but the direction and dominance of the signal are unambiguous.

`gross_prof` is the second-strongest signal: mean IC = 0.0378 (t = 6.07), positive in 70% of months, long-short return 1.16% per month, Sharpe 1.94. This is consistent with the well-documented profitability premium.

`asset_growth` is a valid *negative* predictor: mean IC = −0.0303 (t = −4.29), positive in only 39% of months, long-short return −0.99% per month, Sharpe −1.48. Firms growing their asset base aggressively subsequently underperform, consistent with the investment/asset-growth anomaly.

`idio_vol` is a valid negative predictor: mean IC = −0.0261 (t = −4.19), positive in 35% of months, long-short return −0.74% per month, Sharpe −1.20. High-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks underperform, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.

**Rejected signals.** `book_to_market` (IC = 0.0027, t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` (IC = 0.0055, t = 1.26), `accruals` (IC = 0.0015, t = 0.36), `leverage` (IC = −0.0011, t = −0.30), `turnover` (IC = 0.0006, t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` (IC = −0.0004, t = −0.10) all have t-statistics well below conventional thresholds, and their long-short portfolios have Sharpe ratios between −0.36 and +0.55. Notably, `accruals` and `book_to_market`—classic value and accrual anomalies—show essentially no cross-sectional predictive power in this large-cap, point-in-time universe, suggesting the anomalies are either arbitraged away, concentrated in small caps excluded here, or measured with noise at annual frequency.

**Composite strategy.** Combining the four valid signals (long `sue`, long `gross_prof`, short `asset_growth`, short `idio_vol`) into a single composite score should diversify idiosyncratic signal noise. Because `sue` and `gross_prof` are only weakly correlated in most equity panels (earnings surprise vs. profitability level), and `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` capture distinct risk/behavioral channels, the composite long-short return is likely between the strongest single signal and the simple average of the four. A reasonable estimate is 1.8–2.2% per month gross, translating to roughly 12–13% annualized, with a Sharpe in the 2.0–2.8 range—substantially below the `sue`-only Sharpe due to diversification of both signal and noise, but far more robust to the possibility that the `sue` Sharpe is inflated.

## Discussion

The results are broadly consistent with the academic literature, with two notable features. First, `sue` is by far the dominant signal, with a 100% monthly hit rate that is almost certainly too good to be literally true for a tradable strategy. Post-earnings-announcement drift is real but typically produces rank ICs in the 0.02–0.05 range and long-short returns of 0.5–1.5% per month. The reported 2.96% monthly return and Sharpe of 7.65 suggest either (a) the panel's `sue` is constructed with unusually clean analyst data, (b) the quintile spread captures a small, illiquid, high-spread segment, or (c) some look-ahead or survivorship-related bias remains despite the point-in-time universe. The fact that the universe is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents argues against pure micro-cap illiquidity, but index membership itself changes over time and reconstitution effects can create transient return predictability.

Second, the failure of `book_to_market`, `accruals`, and `leverage` is informative. These are precisely the signals most likely to have been arbitraged in large-cap US equities, and their near-zero ICs here are consistent with that. The survival of `gross_prof` and `asset_growth` suggests quality and investment-based signals remain effective, while pure value and balance-sheet signals do not.

The negative `idio_vol` result is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly and with the interpretation that high-volatility stocks are overpriced (lottery preference, limits to arbitrage). The negative `asset_growth` result is consistent with the q-theory of investment: high investment predicts low returns.

## Limitations

Several caveats apply. (1) I cannot see the raw data and rely entirely on the supplied summary statistics; I cannot verify data quality, outlier treatment, or the exact construction of `sue`. (2) The `sue` Sharpe of 7.65 is implausibly high for a tradable strategy and likely reflects some combination of data construction, universe selection, and the absence of transaction costs. (3) The long-short returns are gross and equal-weighted; no bid-ask spread, borrow cost, market impact, or turnover cost is modeled, and these can easily consume 1–3% per month for a high-turnover strategy. (4) No out-of-sample or subperiod analysis is possible from the summary table; the 120-month IC series could be driven by a few regimes. (5) Multiple testing across ten features inflates the apparent significance of the marginal signals; a Bonferroni-style correction would raise the bar for `idio_vol` and possibly `asset_growth`. (6) The universe is fixed at 500 large-cap names per month, so results may not generalize to small caps or to markets outside the sample. (7) Correlations among the valid signals are unknown, so the composite strategy's diversification benefit is an assumption rather than a measurement.
