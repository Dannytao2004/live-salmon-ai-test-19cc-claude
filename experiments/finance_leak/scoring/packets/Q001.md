# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a 500-Stock Index Panel: Evidence from Nine Accounting and Market Characteristics

## Abstract

We evaluate nine firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents (34,708 stock-month observations). Only one feature, `leverage`, exhibits a statistically reliable rank information coefficient (mean IC = 0.0164, t = 2.74, 59% of months positive), and its long-short quintile spread earns 0.52% per month (Sharpe ≈ 0.92 annualized). All other eight features have IC t-statistics below 1.2 and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.8, consistent with noise given the sample length. We propose a simple monthly long-short strategy on leverage, and caution that its profitability may reflect a distressed/risk-premium exposure rather than a durable anomaly.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 34,708 monthly observations on 500 stocks over 120 months. Coverage is unbalanced: the number of stocks grows from 143 in month 1 to 500 in month 120, reflecting survivorship-driven index membership (all names are constituents *at the end* of the sample). This is the single most important methodological caveat: the universe is conditioned on survival to month 120.

**Signals.** Nine characteristics are provided: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`. All are point-in-time at end of month *t*, lagging annual-report data where applicable, so they are usable in real time.

**Evaluation.** We use the supplied signal statistics: (i) average monthly Spearman rank IC between each feature and `ret_next`, with t-stat = mean(IC)/std(IC) × √120; (ii) equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile long-short mean monthly returns; and (iii) annualized Sharpe ratios of those long-short portfolios. We treat |t| ≳ 2 as the threshold for a credible signal, recognizing that with nine features tested, one marginal hit (t = 2.74) is only modestly above what multiple-testing noise would produce.

**Strategy construction.** For the one surviving signal, we form a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio buying the top quintile of `leverage` and shorting the bottom quintile, rebalanced at each month-end with no transaction-cost model applied.

## Results

**Signal screening.** The table below summarizes the evidence:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | t-stat | Months IC>0 | LS monthly return | LS Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| leverage | 0.0164 | 2.74 | 59% | 0.52% | 0.92 |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | 54% | 0.24% | 0.41 |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | 50% | 0.13% | 0.19 |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | 52% | 0.37% | 0.74 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | 56% | −0.07% | −0.14 |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | 50% | 0.12% | 0.22 |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | 52% | −0.06% | −0.11 |
| turnover | −0.0005 | −0.10 | 52% | 0.13% | 0.23 |
| book_to_market | −0.0017 | −0.28 | 51% | −0.05% | −0.08 |

**Leverage.** The only feature with a t-statistic above 2. Its positive sign means high-debt firms subsequently outperform low-debt firms. The quintile spread of 0.52%/month is economically meaningful, and the 0.92 annualized Sharpe is respectable for a single-factor, equal-weighted, cost-free portfolio. The IC is positive in 59% of months, indicating some consistency rather than a few outlier months.

**Second tier.** `asset_growth` (t = 1.16, Sharpe 0.74) and `accruals` (t = 1.03, Sharpe 0.41) are directionally interesting but statistically indistinguishable from zero at conventional thresholds. Notably, the signs are the *opposite* of well-documented anomalies: the accruals anomaly predicts *negative* accruals-return relation, and the asset-growth anomaly predicts *negative* growth-return relation, yet here both are mildly positive. This suggests either sample-specific noise or a regime/selection effect.

**Null tier.** `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, `rd_intensity`, and `idio_vol` all have |t| < 1 and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.25. Their ICs are indistinguishable from zero.

**Strategy performance.** A leverage-based long-short portfolio would have earned roughly 0.52% per month, or approximately 6.4% per year before costs, with annualized volatility near 7% (implied by Sharpe ≈ 0.92). This is gross of transaction costs, borrow costs on the short leg, and slippage.

## Discussion

The headline result is that eight of nine standard characteristics fail to predict returns in this panel, and the one that "works" — leverage — does so with a t-statistic that would not survive a Bonferroni correction for nine tests (required |t| ≈ 2.77 at 5%). The honest interpretation is that **this panel contains at most weak evidence of any cross-sectional predictability**, and the leverage result is best treated as a candidate hypothesis rather than a validated anomaly.

Two features of the data plausibly explain the weak results. First, **survivorship conditioning**: because the universe is defined as index members at month 120, the early sample (months 1–60, when only 143–270 stocks are present) is dominated by firms that survived and grew into the index. This induces look-ahead bias that compresses cross-sectional return dispersion and attenuates anomaly signals. Second, the **positive leverage-return relation** is the opposite of the standard "low-leverage premium" often found in the literature and is more consistent with a distressed-firm risk premium: highly levered survivors may be compensated for bearing financial distress risk, or may simply be firms that recovered. That is a risk story, not a mispricing story, which matters for capacity and drawdown expectations.

The mild positive coefficients on `accruals` and `asset_growth` also run against established findings. Rather than overturning those literatures, we read these as noise: their t-statistics are ~1, and with nine correlated tests, sign flips of this magnitude are expected.

## Limitations

- **Survivorship and look-ahead bias** in universe construction likely attenuate all signals and may manufacture the leverage result.
- **No transaction costs, borrow costs, or capacity analysis**; the leverage long-short is gross and untradeable as stated.
- **Multiple testing**: nine features were screened; a single t ≈ 2.74 is not robust to family-wise correction.
- **Unbalanced panel**: early months have few stocks (143), so early ICs are noisier and the time series of ICs may be heteroskedastic.
- **No factor neutralization**: leverage correlates with size, value, and distress; the apparent alpha may be compensation for known risk exposures.
- **Single sample, single market**: no out-of-sample or cross-market validation; results may not generalize.
