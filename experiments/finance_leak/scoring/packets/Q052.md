# Point-in-Time Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Large-Cap Monthly Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 large-cap index constituents per month. After enforcing point-in-time availability, we reject `analyst_rev` as a look-ahead-biased signal and find robust evidence for `gross_prof` (positive), `asset_growth` (negative), and `idio_vol` (negative). A composite long-short strategy using these three signals has an estimated gross annualized return of about 12% with a Sharpe ratio near 2.0 before costs. The remaining characteristics—`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—have rank ICs indistinguishable from zero in this sample. Our results highlight the importance of timing checks and suggest that profitability, investment, and low-volatility effects are the main actionable signals in this large-cap universe.

## Methods
We use a monthly stock panel with 60,000 rows, 1,217 distinct stocks, and 120 months. Each month contains the 500 stocks that were index constituents in that month. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month \(t+1\). We evaluate signals using monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank ICs, their t-statistics, and equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature.

Crucially, we check when each variable becomes available. `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, and `rd_intensity` are computed from the latest annual report available at the end of month \(t\), so they are available for month \(t+1\) prediction. `log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, and `turnover` use market data through the end of month \(t\), so they are also available. `analyst_rev`, however, is the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month \(t+1\), scaled by price and recorded at the end of month \(t+1\). It is therefore not available at the end of month \(t\) and uses information from the prediction month itself; we treat it as invalid for real-time prediction.

For the proposed strategy, we form a composite score at the end of each month: \(\text{score} = z(\text{gross\_prof}) - z(\text{asset\_growth}) - z(\text{idio\_vol})\), where \(z(\cdot)\) denotes cross-sectional ranks standardized to zero mean and unit variance. We long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, and rebalance monthly.

## Results
The table below summarizes the valid signals. `gross_prof` has a mean rank IC of 0.0378 (t = 6.07), a long-short monthly return of 1.16%, and an annualized Sharpe of 1.94. `asset_growth` has a mean rank IC of -0.0303 (t = -4.29); after sign flip, the long-low/short-high portfolio earns 0.99% monthly with a Sharpe of 1.48. `idio_vol` has a mean rank IC of -0.0261 (t = -4.19); the long-low-vol/short-high-vol portfolio earns 0.74% monthly with a Sharpe of 1.20. Each signal is positive in the economically expected direction and is statistically significant.

| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | Positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | Negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | Negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

`analyst_rev` shows an extreme apparent signal: mean rank IC of 0.2963, t-stat of 80.29, 100% of months positive, long-short monthly return of 8.73%, and Sharpe of 24.23. This is a textbook look-ahead artifact: the feature is recorded at the end of month \(t+1\) and captures analyst revisions that occur contemporaneously with `ret_next`. It cannot be used to predict month \(t+1\) returns at the end of month \(t\).

The remaining features are weak and statistically insignificant. `book_to_market` has an IC of 0.0027 (t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` 0.0055 (t = 1.26), `accruals` 0.0015 (t = 0.36), `leverage` -0.0011 (t = -0.30), `turnover` 0.0006 (t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` -0.0004 (t = -0.10). None reaches conventional significance, and their long-short returns are small.

For the composite strategy, the average of the three sign-adjusted long-short monthly returns is approximately 0.96%. Annualized simple return is about 11.6%, and compounded return is about 12.2%. Assuming modest positive correlations among the three signals, the composite Sharpe ratio is estimated near 2.0. We therefore use 12% expected annual return and a Sharpe of 2.0 in the summary below.

## Discussion
The three valid signals align with well-known asset-pricing effects. Gross profitability is a quality/profitability signal: profitable firms tend to outperform. Asset growth is an investment signal: firms that expand their asset base aggressively tend to underperform, consistent with the investment anomaly. Idiosyncratic volatility is a low-volatility signal: high-idio-vol stocks underperform, so a long-low-vol/short-high-vol portfolio earns positive returns. These signals are conceptually distinct and likely provide some diversification benefit when combined.

The `analyst_rev` result is a warning rather than an opportunity. Its enormous IC and Sharpe are impossible to trade in real time because the variable is only observed after the prediction month ends. Any strategy using it would be trading on future information. This underscores the importance of point-in-time data checks before accepting a signal.

The composite strategy is simple and transparent. It avoids the weak or insignificant characteristics and focuses on the three signals with both statistical and economic support. However, the estimates are gross and should be interpreted with caution given the limitations below.

## Limitations
- The sample is limited to 120 months and 500 large-cap index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to other universes or periods.
- The strategy is evaluated gross of transaction costs, shorting costs, and market impact, which could materially reduce net returns.
- The composite weights are equal and ad hoc; no formal optimization or risk-model neutralization is performed.
- The t-statistics assume independent monthly observations, but signal returns may be autocorrelated and cross-sectionally correlated.
- Multiple testing across ten characteristics increases the chance of false discoveries, although we focus on the strongest and most economically motivated signals.
- The `analyst_rev` feature is rejected for look-ahead bias, but its presence in the panel underscores the importance of carefully checking data timing.
