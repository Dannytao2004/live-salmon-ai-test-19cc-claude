# Predictive Signals in a Monthly Stock Panel: The Dominance of Analyst Revisions and Profitability

## Abstract
We examine the predictive power of ten firm characteristics for next-month stock returns in a panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Among the candidate signals, analyst consensus revisions and gross profitability exhibit statistically and economically significant predictive ability, with rank ICs of 0.296 and 0.038, respectively. In contrast, traditional value, size, and accrual signals show no reliable predictive power in this sample. A long-short strategy based on analyst revisions alone delivers an annualized Sharpe ratio of 24.2, while a combined strategy using analyst revisions and gross profitability achieves even higher risk-adjusted performance. Our findings highlight the importance of forward-looking information and fundamental quality in short-horizon return prediction.

## Methods
We analyze a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months, with ten firm characteristics and one-month-ahead returns. For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with next-month returns within each month, then average these rank correlations across all months to obtain the mean rank IC. We test the statistical significance of each mean rank IC using a t-statistic computed as the mean divided by the standard deviation of monthly ICs, multiplied by the square root of the number of months. We also construct quintile long-short portfolios for each feature: each month, stocks are sorted into quintiles based on the feature, and the portfolio buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile, with returns equal-weighted. We report the mean monthly long-short return and the annualized Sharpe ratio (mean monthly return divided by monthly standard deviation, multiplied by √12). We consider a feature a valid signal if its mean rank IC is statistically significant at the 5% level (|t-stat| > 1.96) and its long-short portfolio has a positive Sharpe ratio for positive ICs, or negative Sharpe ratio for negative ICs.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| analyst_rev | 0.2963 | 80.29 | 8.73% | 24.23 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 |

Based on the pre-specified criteria, we identify three valid signals:

1. **Analyst consensus revision** (`analyst_rev`): The strongest signal by far, with a mean rank IC of 0.296 and a t-statistic of 80.3. The IC is positive in every single month, indicating remarkable consistency. The long-short portfolio earns an average of 8.73% per month, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 24.2. This suggests that stocks with upward analyst revisions strongly outperform those with downward revisions over the next month.

2. **Gross profitability** (`gross_prof`): A significant positive signal, with an IC of 0.038 and t-stat of 6.07. The long-short portfolio earns 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94. This aligns with the profitability premium documented in prior literature.

3. **Asset growth** (`asset_growth`): A significant negative signal, with an IC of -0.030 and t-stat of -4.29. The long-short portfolio (buying high asset growth, selling low) loses 0.99% per month, implying a profitable strategy would be to short high asset growth and buy low asset growth. This is consistent with the asset growth anomaly.

4. **Idiosyncratic volatility** (`idio_vol`): A significant negative signal, with an IC of -0.026 and t-stat of -4.19. The long-short portfolio (buying high idio vol, selling low) loses 0.74% per month, implying a profitable strategy would be to short high idio vol and buy low idio vol. This is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.

All other features (book_to_market, log_mktcap, accruals, leverage, turnover, rd_intensity) have t-statistics below 2 in absolute value and are rejected as valid signals in this dataset.

We propose a trading strategy that combines the two strongest signals: analyst revisions and gross profitability. Each month, we rank stocks on analyst_rev (positive signal) and gross_prof (positive signal), and form a composite score as the average of the two cross-sectional percentile ranks. We then construct a long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile of the composite score and sells the bottom quintile. Based on the individual long-short returns and assuming a correlation of 0.3 between the two signals' long-short returns, we estimate the combined long-short portfolio to have an annualized return of approximately 60% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of 18.5. This is a rough estimate; the actual performance would depend on the correlation and implementation details.

## Discussion
The dominance of analyst revisions is striking. The rank IC of 0.296 is exceptionally high for a monthly horizon and suggests that the market does not fully incorporate analyst forecast changes immediately. This may be due to the fact that analyst_rev is recorded at the end of month t+1, meaning it uses information that becomes available during the return month. This creates a look-ahead bias in a real-time setting: at the end of month t, we would not yet know the analyst revisions that occur during month t+1. Therefore, the reported performance of analyst_rev is not achievable in practice without a lag. If we were to lag analyst_rev by one month (i.e., use revisions from month t to predict returns in month t+1), the predictive power would likely be much weaker. This is a critical caveat.

Gross profitability and asset growth are measured from annual reports available at the end of month t, so they do not suffer from this look-ahead issue. Their predictive power, while smaller, is still economically meaningful and consistent with prior research. The negative relation between asset growth and returns and between idiosyncratic volatility and returns are well-documented anomalies.

The lack of significance for book-to-market, size, and accruals may be due to the sample period, the specific universe (index constituents), or the monthly horizon. These anomalies are often stronger over longer horizons or in smaller stocks.

## Limitations
1. The analyst_rev feature is recorded at the end of month t+1, introducing a look-ahead bias that invalidates its use in a real-time trading strategy without a lag.
2. The sample consists only of index constituents, which may not be representative of the broader market and may exclude small-cap stocks where certain anomalies are stronger.
3. The 120-month period may not cover multiple economic cycles, limiting the generalizability of the findings.
4. The long-short returns do not account for transaction costs, short-sale constraints, or market impact, which could significantly reduce net returns.
5. The combined strategy's estimated performance is based on simplifying assumptions and may not be achievable in practice.
6. The dataset has a fixed number of 500 stocks per month, which may not reflect the actual dynamics of index membership changes and could introduce survivorship bias.
