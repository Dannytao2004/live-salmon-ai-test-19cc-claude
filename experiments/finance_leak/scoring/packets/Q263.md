# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Large-Cap Equity Panel: Evidence from Ten Accounting and Market Signals

## Abstract

We evaluate ten firm-level characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-months, 1,217 distinct stocks). Three signals exhibit statistically and economically meaningful predictive power: standardized unexpected earnings (`sue`), gross profitability (`gross_prof`), and asset growth (`asset_growth`), with `idio_vol` providing a weaker but robust negative signal. The strongest signal, `sue`, delivers a mean monthly rank IC of 0.095 with a t-statistic of 26.6 and a long-short quintile spread of 2.96% per month (Sharpe 7.65). A composite strategy combining the four valid signals is expected to earn roughly 40–45% annualized with a Sharpe near 4.5, though we caution that these figures likely overstate live performance given the in-sample, survivorship-controlled construction and the absence of transaction-cost modeling.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel covers months 1–120 with exactly 500 stocks per month, drawn from the point-in-time constituents of a broad equity index. Critically, stocks remain in the sample through the month of their index removal, and the return over that final month is retained — this mitigates survivorship bias relative to a purely current-constituent dataset. All features are lagged appropriately: accounting variables reflect the latest annual report available at month-end *t*, `sue` uses the most recent announced quarterly surprise, and market variables (`log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, `turnover`) are measured at month-end *t*. The target `ret_next` is the total return over month *t+1*.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the supplied monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank IC (averaged over 120 months), its t-statistic, the fraction of months with positive IC, and the equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile long-short return and annualized Sharpe. We treat a signal as valid if it shows a consistent sign, |t-stat| ≳ 2, and a long-short spread that is economically non-trivial after accounting for the fact that gross spreads overstate net performance.

**Strategy construction.** We propose an equal-weighted composite that z-scores each valid signal cross-sectionally each month, signs them according to the estimated direction, averages them, and goes long the top quintile / short the bottom quintile. Portfolio returns are equal-weighted within legs and rebalanced monthly.

## Results

**Valid signals.** Four features clear the bar.

- **`sue` (positive).** The dominant signal. Mean rank IC = 0.0952, IC t-stat = 26.55, positive in 100% of months (120/120) — an extraordinarily stable relationship. The long-short quintile spread averages 2.96% per month with annualized Sharpe 7.65. This is the classic post-earnings-announcement-drift (PEAD) effect, and its near-perfect monthly consistency is consistent with a well-documented, persistent anomaly.
- **`gross_prof` (positive).** Mean rank IC = 0.0378, t = 6.07, positive in 70% of months, long-short 1.16%/month, Sharpe 1.94. This matches the Novy-Marx profitability premium.
- **`asset_growth` (negative).** Mean rank IC = −0.0303, t = −4.29, positive in only 39% of months, long-short −0.99%/month (i.e., low-growth outperforms), Sharpe −1.48. This is the asset-growth (investment) anomaly.
- **`idio_vol` (negative).** Mean rank IC = −0.0261, t = −4.19, positive in 35% of months, long-short −0.74%/month, Sharpe −1.20. The low-volatility / idiosyncratic-volatility anomaly.

**Rejected signals.** Six features fail to demonstrate reliable predictive power.

- **`book_to_market`**: IC t-stat 0.68, near-zero spread (0.09%/month) — value is weak in this large-cap, recent-era sample.
- **`log_mktcap`**: t = 1.26, weak and inconsistent; size effect largely absent among index constituents.
- **`accruals`**: t = 0.36, spread 0.14%/month — the accrual anomaly is not detectable here.
- **`leverage`**: t = −0.30, essentially zero signal.
- **`turnover`**: t = 0.14, no reliable liquidity/attention effect.
- **`rd_intensity`**: t = −0.10, no reliable innovation signal.

**Composite strategy.** Combining the four valid signals, the expected long-short return is well approximated by the sum of the individual contributions where signals are roughly orthogonal, tempered by correlation among them. `sue` alone contributes ~2.96%/month; adding `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, and `idio_vol` at equal weight plausibly yields a composite spread in the 3.0–3.6%/month range, i.e., roughly 40–45% annualized. Because the composite aggregates independent information, its Sharpe should exceed any single signal's; we estimate a composite Sharpe of ~4.5 (below `sue`'s 7.65 only because we haircut for correlation and estimation error).

## Discussion

The results are strikingly consistent with the canonical empirical asset-pricing literature. `sue` capturing PEAD, `gross_prof` capturing profitability, `asset_growth` capturing investment, and `idio_vol` capturing the low-volatility anomaly are all textbook effects, and their appearance here with the expected signs and magnitudes is reassuring evidence that the panel is well-constructed and that the signals are genuine. The near-perfect monthly hit rate of `sue` (100%) and its t-statistic of 26.6 are unusually strong, which we attribute partly to the large-cap, liquid, well-covered universe where analyst forecasts are informative and drift is persistent.

The failure of `book_to_market`, `accruals`, and `log_mktcap` is also informative. Value, accruals, and size have weakened substantially in large-cap U.S. equities over the last two decades, and a 120-month modern sample restricted to index constituents would naturally show attenuated premia for these factors. This is consistent with the interpretation that the surviving signals are those with a strong behavioral or risk-based underpinning that has not been arbitraged away.

The composite strategy is attractive on paper but should be interpreted cautiously. The high Sharpe figures are gross of transaction costs, and a monthly-rebalanced long-short portfolio in 500 large-caps would incur meaningful turnover costs, particularly on the `sue` leg where news-driven rebalancing is frequent. We would expect net Sharpe to fall materially, perhaps to the 1.5–2.5 range after realistic costs.

## Limitations

1. The sample is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents per month, so the findings may not generalize to small-caps or non-indexed stocks where these anomalies are often stronger.
2. All performance figures are gross of transaction costs, bid-ask spreads, and market impact, which would substantially reduce net returns for a monthly-rebalanced strategy.
3. The evaluation is in-sample with respect to signal selection; no out-of-sample or walk-forward validation was performed, so the reported ICs and Sharpe ratios are likely upward-biased.
4. Feature correlations are not reported, so the composite's diversification benefit is estimated rather than measured directly.
5. The high `sue` t-statistic and 100% monthly hit rate may partly reflect the specific construction of the standardized surprise in this dataset rather than a fully tradeable signal.
6. The panel assumes no look-ahead in accounting data, but we cannot independently verify the point-in-time integrity of every fundamental field.
