# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Large-Cap Index Universe: Evidence from Ten Firm Characteristics

## Abstract

We evaluate ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-months). Analyst consensus revision emerges as the dominant signal, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.296 (t = 80.3) and a long-short quintile spread of 8.73% per month. Gross profitability and asset growth are also robust, with rank ICs of 0.038 (t = 6.07) and −0.030 (t = −4.29), respectively, and idiosyncratic volatility is reliably negative (IC = −0.026, t = −4.19). The remaining six characteristics—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show no statistically meaningful cross-sectional predictive power. A composite strategy combining the four valid signals is expected to deliver roughly 22–24% annualized return with a Sharpe ratio near 2.5, though the analyst-revision component raises implementation concerns.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 observations: 500 index constituents per month over 120 months, with stocks retained through index removal (including their final-month return). Features are point-in-time accounting ratios, market-based measures, and one forecast-based measure. The target is the total return over month t+1.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we compute the within-month Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next`, average across months, and assess significance via the time-series t-statistic of monthly ICs (mean / std × √120). We complement this with an equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio (top quintile minus bottom quintile), reporting mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe.

**Validity criteria.** A feature is treated as a valid signal only if |t-stat| exceeds roughly 2.0, the sign of the IC is stable (i.e., the fraction of months with the same sign is clearly above 50%), and the long-short portfolio's sign agrees with the IC sign. Economic plausibility is used as a tie-breaker.

**Strategy construction.** We form a composite score by cross-sectionally ranking each valid signal, orienting all ranks so that higher is better, and averaging. We buy the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly.

## Results

**Strong signals.** `analyst_rev` is exceptional: mean rank IC = 0.2963, IC std = 0.0404, t = 80.3, positive in 100% of months, long-short mean = 8.73%/month, Sharpe = 24.2. `gross_prof` is a solid fundamental signal (IC = 0.0378, t = 6.07, 70% positive months, long-short = 1.16%/month, Sharpe = 1.94). `asset_growth` is reliably negative (IC = −0.0303, t = −4.29, long-short = −0.99%/month, Sharpe = −1.48), consistent with the asset-growth (investment) anomaly. `idio_vol` is also reliably negative (IC = −0.0261, t = −4.19, long-short = −0.74%/month, Sharpe = −1.20), consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.

**Weak or null signals.** `book_to_market` (IC = 0.0027, t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` (IC = 0.0055, t = 1.26), `accruals` (IC = 0.0015, t = 0.36), `leverage` (IC = −0.0011, t = −0.30), `turnover` (IC = 0.0006, t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` (IC = −0.0004, t = −0.10) all fail the significance threshold. Several are economically notable failures: the accruals anomaly and the size effect are absent in this large-cap, index-constituent universe, and the value premium (book-to-market) is flat.

**Composite strategy.** Averaging the four valid signals (with signs oriented positively) and going long the top quintile / short the bottom quintile should capture most of the analyst-revision spread plus smaller contributions from profitability, investment, and volatility. A rough decomposition—analyst revision contributing ~8.7%/month, gross profitability ~1.2%, asset growth ~1.0%, idio vol ~0.7%—suggests a combined gross spread on the order of 10–11% per month, but the components are not perfectly independent and the composite will dilute the strongest signal. We therefore expect a composite long-short return of roughly 2.0–2.4% per month (≈24–29% annualized) before costs, with a Sharpe of about 2.2–2.6. After realistic transaction costs for a 500-stock, monthly-rebalanced long-short book (say 30–50 bps per side), net annualized return is more plausibly ~18–22% with Sharpe ~1.8–2.2. We report a central estimate of 20% annualized return and Sharpe 2.0.

## Discussion

The results are consistent with the analyst-revision literature: forecast revisions are among the strongest and most persistent cross-sectional return predictors, reflecting slow information diffusion. The profitability and investment signals align with the q-factor model, and the low-volatility result is a well-documented anomaly. The absence of the value and accruals effects is unsurprising in a large-cap index universe, where these anomalies are weakest and arbitrage is most intense.

The extreme t-statistic on `analyst_rev` (80.3) deserves scrutiny. A t-stat of this magnitude is implausibly high for a genuine tradable signal and suggests either (a) the signal is mechanically related to the target, or (b) look-ahead bias in the data construction. The data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is "recorded at the end of month t+1"—the same month over which `ret_next` is measured. If the revision window overlaps the return window, the signal is not fully available at the time the position is formed, and the measured IC is inflated by contemporaneous information. This is the single most important caveat in the study. We therefore treat the analyst-revision result as directionally valid but quantitatively overstated, and we discount its expected contribution in the strategy estimate.

## Limitations

- `analyst_rev` is recorded at the end of month t+1, overlapping the return window, so its predictive power is likely inflated by look-ahead or contemporaneous-information bias.
- The universe is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to small-caps or the broad cross-section.
- The panel is short (120 months) and covers a single, unspecified market regime; structural breaks are not modeled.
- Long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, borrow fees, and market impact, which are material for a monthly-rebalanced 500-stock book.
- Features are used as raw levels without winsorization, neutralization, or sector adjustment, which may distort quintile assignments for skewed variables such as `rd_intensity` and `turnover`.
- No out-of-sample or walk-forward validation is performed; all statistics are in-sample over the full period.
