# Cross-Sectional Predictors of Monthly Stock Returns: Profitability, Investment, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We evaluate nine stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. We find robust evidence that gross profitability positively predicts returns, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility negatively predict returns. The remaining six characteristics do not have statistically significant rank information coefficients. A composite long-short strategy based on the three valid signals is estimated to earn about 12% per year with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.0.

## Methods
The data are a point-in-time panel of 500 stocks per month for 120 months. All accounting-based characteristics are constructed from the latest annual report available at the end of month t, so they are known before the return month. The target, `ret_next`, is the total stock return over month t+1.

For each characteristic, I compute the monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next`, then average over the 120 months to obtain the mean rank IC. The t-statistic is:

\[
t = \frac{\text{mean rank IC}}{\text{std(rank IC)} / \sqrt{120}}
\]

A characteristic is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic is greater than 2 in absolute value. I also report the equal-weighted long-short return from buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile of the characteristic each month.

## Results
Three of the nine characteristics pass the statistical significance threshold.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 |

The valid signals are:

- **Gross profitability** (`gross_prof`): positive. High gross-profit firms earn higher subsequent returns.
- **Asset growth** (`asset_growth`): negative. High asset-growth firms earn lower subsequent returns.
- **Idiosyncratic volatility** (`idio_vol`): negative. High idiosyncratic-volatility firms earn lower subsequent returns.

The other six features have rank ICs that are statistically indistinguishable from zero and are rejected as reliable standalone signals.

## Discussion
The results are broadly consistent with the quality and investment literatures. Profitable firms tend to outperform unprofitable firms, while firms that grow assets aggressively tend to underperform. The negative premium for idiosyncratic volatility is also consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.

I propose a monthly long-short strategy that combines the three valid signals. Each month, compute a composite score:

\[
\text{score} = \text{rank}(gross\_prof) - \text{rank}(asset\_growth) - \text{rank}(idio\_vol)
\]

Buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of this score, equal-weighted. Using an equal-weighted combination of the three individual long-short quintile spreads as an approximation, the expected gross return is about 0.96% per month, or roughly 12% per year, with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.0.

## Limitations
- The returns are gross of transaction costs, short-selling costs, and borrowing fees.
- The sample contains 120 months and 500 stocks per month, which may limit generalizability to other periods or universes.
- No adjustment is made for multiple testing across the nine characteristics.
- The composite strategy assumes the three signals can be combined without extreme turnover or liquidity constraints.
- The expected performance is estimated from the single-signal long-short results and may overstate the true composite Sharpe if the signals are more correlated than assumed.
