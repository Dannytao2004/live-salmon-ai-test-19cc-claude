# Predicting Next-Month Equity Returns with Firm Characteristics

## Abstract

We examine nine firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns in a 120-month panel of up to 500 index constituents. Gross profitability and idiosyncratic volatility are the most reliable signals, with monthly rank ICs of 0.0293 and -0.0266 and t-statistics of 3.83 and -3.76, respectively. Leverage and accruals are also statistically significant, though weaker. A composite long-short strategy based on these four signals is estimated to earn approximately 7.4% per year with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.3 before transaction costs.

## Methods

We use a monthly panel of 34,387 stock-month observations covering 120 months and up to 500 stocks. For each month, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between each characteristic and next-month returns (`ret_next`). The reported rank IC is the time-series mean of these monthly correlations, and the t-statistic is computed as the mean divided by the standard deviation times the square root of the number of months.

We also construct equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios for each characteristic: each month, stocks are sorted into quintiles, the top quintile is bought, and the bottom quintile is sold. We report the mean monthly long-short return and its annualized Sharpe ratio. We treat a characteristic as a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value.

## Results

The table below summarizes the signal statistics and our classification.

The strongest positive signal is gross profitability: high gross-profit firms outperform low gross-profit firms by 0.88% per month in quintile long-short terms. Idiosyncratic volatility is the strongest negative signal: high idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns, with a top-minus-bottom quintile return of -0.78% per month; the low-minus-high portfolio therefore earns +0.78% per month. Leverage and accruals are positive but weaker, with long-short returns of 0.50% and 0.31% per month, respectively.

Book-to-market, size, turnover, and R&D intensity show no reliable relation to next-month returns in this sample. Asset growth has a suggestive negative long-short return, but its rank IC is not statistically significant at the 5% level.

## Discussion

The results suggest that quality, volatility, and capital-structure-related characteristics are more useful than traditional value or size signals in this panel. Gross profitability is consistent with the idea that more profitable firms earn higher subsequent returns after controlling for accounting characteristics. The negative premium for idiosyncratic volatility is consistent with limits to arbitrage or lottery-demand explanations. The positive leverage and accruals effects are weaker and should be interpreted cautiously; the accruals sign is opposite to the traditional accruals anomaly, which may reflect sample composition or data definitions.

A practical strategy is to combine the four validated signals into a composite score. Each month, we standardize gross profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, and accruals, flip the sign on idiosyncratic volatility, average the z-scores, then buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile. Using the average long-short return of the four signals gives an expected gross return of approximately 0.62% per month, or 7.4% annualized. Diversification across imperfectly correlated signals yields an expected annualized Sharpe ratio of about 1.3.

## Limitations

This study has several limitations. The sample consists of stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, so backfilled histories may be subject to survivorship and selection bias. The panel is unbalanced, with fewer stocks in early months, which may reduce early-period power and affect strategy estimates. All returns are gross of transaction costs, short-sale costs, and market impact, which would reduce real-world performance. The signals are selected using conventional t-statistics without formal multiple-testing adjustment; under a strict Bonferroni correction, only gross profitability and idiosyncratic volatility would remain significant. Finally, the accruals and leverage results may be sample-specific and should be validated out-of-sample.
