# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Monthly Equity Panel: Standardized Unexpected Earnings as the Dominant Signal

## Abstract

We evaluate ten point-in-time stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Using within-month rank IC, IC t-statistics, and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) emerges as a robust valid signal. SUE has a mean rank IC of 0.0998 (t-stat 23.25), is positive in 99% of months, and delivers a top-minus-bottom quintile return of 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. The remaining nine features are statistically insignificant, economically small, and often sign-unstable. A monthly dollar-neutral SUE quintile strategy would have produced roughly 36.5% annualized gross return before implementation costs.

## Methods

The dataset contains 60,000 rows: 120 months × 500 stocks per month. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. All features are described as point-in-time: accounting variables come from the latest annual report available at month t, SUE is based on the most recent announced quarterly earnings surprise, and market-based variables use data through month t. The panel includes delisted/index-removed stocks up to their removal, which mitigates survivorship bias relative to a current-constituent sample.

I assess each feature using the provided monthly cross-sectional rank ICs. For each feature, I compute the average rank IC across the 120 months, the IC t-statistic, the fraction of months with positive IC, and the equal-weighted long-short return from buying the top quintile and shorting the bottom quintile. I treat a feature as a valid signal if it shows a consistent sign, a mean rank IC that is economically meaningful, and a t-statistic large enough to survive multiple-testing concerns. Given ten features tested simultaneously, a t-statistic near 2 is not sufficient; I require stronger evidence of stability and magnitude.

## Results

The signal statistics show a single dominant predictor: `sue`. Its mean rank IC is 0.0998, with an IC standard deviation of 0.0470 and a t-statistic of 23.25. The IC is positive in 99% of months. Its long-short quintile portfolio earns 3.04% per month, or approximately 36.5% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. This is consistent with a strong post-earnings-announcement-drift pattern: stocks with high standardized earnings surprises continue to outperform, while negative-surprise stocks underperform.

No other feature is statistically or economically robust. `asset_growth` has the next-highest t-statistic at 1.64, with a mean rank IC of 0.0061 and a long-short Sharpe of 0.72. `accruals` has a mean rank IC of 0.0055 and t-statistic 1.34, but this is not significant after testing ten features. `idio_vol` has a small positive IC (0.0043, t=1.14), while `gross_prof` is also weak (0.0039, t=0.93). `leverage` has a negative but insignificant IC (-0.0043, t=-1.00). `book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` are essentially flat, with |t-statistics| below 0.72.

## Discussion

The results support a trading strategy that is long high-SUE stocks and short low-SUE stocks. Because SUE is standardized and already scaled, the natural implementation is an equal-weighted top-quintile versus bottom-quintile portfolio, rebalanced monthly and held dollar-neutral. The gross expected annual return is approximately 0.0304 × 12 = 36.48%, with the reported annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. This is exceptionally strong and almost certainly reflects a clean, simulated signal environment; in practice, transaction costs, borrow costs, market impact, and crowding would reduce net performance.

The lack of other significant signals is notable. Classic value (`book_to_market`), size (`log_mktcap`), profitability (`gross_prof`), investment (`asset_growth`), accruals, low-volatility (`idio_vol`), leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity (`rd_intensity`) do not show reliable next-month predictive power in this panel. Some signs are directionally consistent with known anomalies—for example, positive `gross_prof` and negative `leverage`—but the magnitudes are too small and the t-statistics too low to justify standalone strategies.

The SUE result is economically plausible because earnings surprises are a direct fundamental update, and the market may underreact to them. The stability of the signal—positive in 99% of months—suggests it is not driven by a few outlier months. Nevertheless, the unusually high Sharpe ratio warrants caution: it may be specific to this universe, sample period, and data construction.

## Limitations

- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 index constituents per month, limiting generalizability to broader markets or other periods.
- The reported returns are gross of transaction costs, borrow fees, slippage, and market impact; a monthly quintile strategy would incur meaningful turnover.
- The analysis relies on summary statistics and does not control for sector, factor exposures, or other firm characteristics.
- SUE depends on consensus forecast data and earnings-announcement timing, which may be noisy or imperfectly aligned.
- The extremely high Sharpe ratio of 7.31 is likely overstated and should be treated with caution.
- Multiple testing across ten features means weak results could be spurious, even if the SUE signal itself is extremely strong.
- Equal-weighted quintile portfolios may load on small-cap or liquidity risk and may be difficult to implement at scale.
