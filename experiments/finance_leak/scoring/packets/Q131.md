# Predictive Signals in a Monthly Equity Panel: Profitability, Growth, and Low-Risk Effects

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a panel of 500 large-cap stocks over 120 months. Gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility are significant return predictors after accounting for cross-sectional rank correlation and long-short quintile spreads. Analyst revisions show extremely strong apparent predictive power but are recorded at the end of the return month and therefore contain look-ahead bias and are not tradable. A composite strategy that buys high gross profitability, low asset growth, and low idiosyncratic volatility stocks is estimated to produce an annualized return of approximately 11.5% with a Sharpe ratio near 2.1 before costs.

## Methods
The dataset is a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months, with ten candidate features and next-month returns. I evaluate each feature using two methods. First, I compute the monthly rank IC, defined as the Spearman correlation between the feature and next-month return across stocks within each month, averaged across months. I test significance with the t-statistic \(t = \text{mean IC} / \text{std IC} \times \sqrt{120}\). A feature is treated as a valid signal if its absolute t-statistic exceeds 2.0 and its long-short quintile spread is economically meaningful. Second, I examine the long-short quintile return, which buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the feature each month.

One feature, `analyst_rev`, is excluded from the tradable signal set despite its large t-statistic because it is defined as the change in mean FY1 EPS forecast during month \(t+1\), recorded at the end of month \(t+1\). This information is not available at the portfolio formation date at the end of month \(t\).

## Results
Three features meet the validity threshold:

| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | Positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | Negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | Negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

For `asset_growth` and `idio_vol`, the long-short return is negative when buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile, implying that high asset growth and high idiosyncratic volatility predict lower subsequent returns. These signs are economically consistent with prior research: high gross profitability is associated with higher returns, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are associated with lower returns.

The remaining features have t-statistics below conventional significance thresholds: `book_to_market` (t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` (t = 1.26), `accruals` (t = 0.36), `leverage` (t = -0.30), `turnover` (t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` (t = -0.10). Although `analyst_rev` has a mean rank IC of 0.2963 and a t-statistic of 80.29, it is rejected as nontradable because the signal is measured during the return month itself.

I construct a composite score for each stock each month by averaging the cross-sectional percentile ranks of `gross_prof`, negative `asset_growth`, and negative `idio_vol`:

\[
\text{score}_i = \frac{1}{3}\left[ \text{pct}(\text{gross\_prof}) + \text{pct}(-\text{asset\_growth}) + \text{pct}(-\text{idio\_vol}) \right].
\]

The strategy buys the top quintile of this composite score and sells the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly. Based on the historical long-short performance of the underlying signals, the expected monthly long-short return is approximately 0.96%, corresponding to an annualized return of about 11.5%. The expected annualized Sharpe ratio is approximately 2.1 before transaction costs.

## Discussion
The results indicate that a parsimonious set of fundamental and risk-based characteristics can predict next-month returns. Gross profitability is the strongest fundamental signal, supporting the view that more profitable firms earn higher subsequent returns. Asset growth has a negative coefficient, consistent with the asset-growth anomaly: firms that expand assets aggressively tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility is also a negative predictor, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly and lottery-demand explanations.

The rejection of `analyst_rev` is important. Its near-perfect monthly IC stability and enormous long-short Sharpe ratio are classic symptoms of look-ahead bias. Because the revision is measured during the future return month, it contains information that would not be available to an investor forming the portfolio at the end of month \(t\). Including it would severely overstate realistic strategy performance.

A composite signal based on the three valid features is likely to be more robust than any single signal because it diversifies across fundamental and risk-based sources of return. The expected Sharpe ratio of about 2.1 is high but plausible for an equal-weight large-cap long-short strategy before costs; after transaction costs and implementation frictions the realized Sharpe would likely be lower.

## Limitations
Several limitations apply. First, the analysis uses only provided summary statistics rather than full portfolio-level backtests, so the composite strategy performance is an approximation. Second, transaction costs, market impact, short-sale constraints, and liquidity are not modeled. Third, the sample is limited to 500 large-cap index constituents, so results may not generalize to small-cap or international equities. Fourth, the t-statistics assume independent monthly observations, which may overstate significance if signal returns are serially correlated. Fifth, the rejected signals are rejected only in linear rank-correlation form; nonlinear or interaction effects are not examined.
