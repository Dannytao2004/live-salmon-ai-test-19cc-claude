# Report Q068

# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a 500-Stock Monthly Panel: Evidence from Nine Fundamental and Market-Based Signals

---

## Abstract

We examine nine stock characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents (34,387 stock-months). Three signals exhibit statistically reliable rank information coefficients (ICs): gross profitability (mean IC = 0.0293, t = 3.83), idiosyncratic volatility (IC = −0.0266, t = −3.76), and leverage (IC = 0.0137, t = 2.56). Accruals shows a weaker but marginally significant positive IC (0.0130, t = 2.06), while asset growth is negative and borderline (IC = −0.0164, t = −1.87). Book-to-market, log market cap, turnover, and R&D intensity show no reliable predictive power. A composite long-short strategy tilting toward high gross profitability, low idiosyncratic volatility, and high leverage yields an estimated annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.5 with modest turnover. We conclude that quality, risk, and financing-based characteristics carry the most robust cross-sectional information in this panel.

---

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 34,387 monthly observations on 500 stocks over 120 months. Coverage is unbalanced and grows over time (139 stocks in month 1 to 500 in month 120), reflecting index membership entry. All features are observable at the end of month *t*; the target is the total return over month *t+1*.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the provided monthly Spearman rank IC (cross-sectional correlation of feature rank with next-month return rank), averaged across the 120 months, with a t-statistic computed as mean(IC)/std(IC) × √120. We also use the reported equal-weighted quintile long-short (top minus bottom quintile) mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio. A signal is treated as valid if |t| ≳ 2 and the sign is economically interpretable; signals with |t| < ~1.5 or unstable signs are rejected.

**Feature construction notes.** Because the panel is survivorship-conditioned (constituents of the index at month 120), and because fundamentals are lagged annual-report values, features are slow-moving and largely point-in-time. Idiosyncratic volatility uses 60 daily residuals; turnover averages 3 months.

**Strategy.** We form a composite z-score (cross-sectional, equal-weighted) of the valid signals, orienting each so higher composite = higher expected return, then go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly. Expected performance is estimated by combining individual long-short returns with a diversification haircut for imperfect signal correlation.

---

## Results

### Valid signals

| Feature | Mean rank IC | t-stat | L/S monthly | L/S Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gross_prof | +0.0293 | 3.83 | +0.88% | 1.19 |
| idio_vol | −0.0266 | −3.76 | −0.78% | −1.15 |
| leverage | +0.0137 | 2.56 | +0.50% | 0.99 |
| accruals | +0.0130 | 2.06 | +0.31% | 0.50 |
| asset_growth | −0.0164 | −1.87 | −0.64% | −0.78 |

**Gross profitability** is the strongest signal, consistent with the quality/profitability premium: profitable firms outperform. IC is positive in 61% of months, and the long-short spread earns 0.88%/month.

**Idiosyncratic volatility** is strongly negative: high-volatility stocks underperform, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly and limits-to-arbitrage. IC is positive in only 37% of months, and the L/S portfolio loses 0.78%/month when bought high-vol.

**Leverage** is positively related to next-month returns (IC = +0.0137, t = 2.56). This is a somewhat non-standard sign (leverage is often a distress proxy), but in this index-constituent, mostly large-cap universe, higher leverage may reflect disciplined capital structure or a value tilt rather than distress.

**Accruals** is positive and marginally significant (t = 2.06), opposite to the classic Sloan (1996) accruals anomaly. Given the weak t-stat and the well-documented decay/reversal of the accruals anomaly in large caps, we treat this as a weak, secondary signal with low confidence.

**Asset growth** is negative (t = −1.87), consistent with the investment/asset-growth anomaly (firms that grow assets underperform). It is borderline significant and we include it as a weak valid signal.

### Rejected signals

- **book_to_market** (IC = 0.0029, t = 0.54): no reliable cross-sectional information in this large-cap, index-constituent sample.
- **log_mktcap** (IC = −0.0043, t = −0.67): size effect is absent, unsurprising given the universe is dominated by large index members.
- **turnover** (IC = 0.0042, t = 0.64): liquidity/turnover carries no reliable next-month signal here.
- **rd_intensity** (IC = 0.0018, t = 0.30): no reliable predictive power; R&D is noisy and slow-moving.

### Strategy performance

Combining the three high-conviction signals (gross profitability long, idiosyncratic volatility short, leverage long) into an equal-weighted composite:

- Individual monthly L/S returns: 0.88%, −0.78% (inverted to +0.78% for low-vol tilt), and 0.50%.
- Assuming modest pairwise correlations among signals (0.1–0.3), the composite L/S portfolio is expected to earn roughly **0.9–1.1% per month** with lower volatility than any single leg.
- Estimated annualized return: **~11–13%**; estimated annualized Sharpe: **~1.4–1.6**.

Adding the weak accruals and asset-growth tilts provides marginal incremental diversification but also adds noise; we report the three-signal core.

---

## Discussion

The results align with the modern empirical asset-pricing literature. Profitability (gross profits-to-assets) is a canonical quality signal and emerges as the most robust predictor. The low-volatility/idiosyncratic-volatility effect is strong and negative, reflecting both leverage constraints and investor preference for lottery-like stocks. Leverage's positive sign is more unusual and may be sample-specific: in a large-cap index universe, high leverage often coincides with stable, mature, cash-generative firms rather than distressed ones.

The failure of book-to-market and size is notable and consistent with the well-documented weakening of the value and size premia in large-cap U.S. equities over the last two decades, and with the fact that this universe is conditioned on index membership at month 120 (a survivorship filter that removes the smallest, most distressed names where value and size effects are strongest).

The positive accruals sign contradicts the classic anomaly, but its t-stat is weak and the anomaly is known to have attenuated post-2000, especially among large caps. We therefore treat it as low-confidence. Asset growth's negative sign is consistent with the investment anomaly and adds economic coherence.

A practical strategy would tilt toward profitable, low-volatility, moderately levered firms. Because the signals are slow-moving (annual fundamentals, 60-day vol), turnover is low, implying favorable net-of-cost economics relative to fast-moving signals.

---

## Limitations

- The universe is survivorship-conditioned on index membership at month 120, which biases results toward large, successful firms and likely understates value/size/distress effects.
- We cannot verify data quality, point-in-time integrity, or look-ahead bias in the fundamentals; annual-report lag assumptions are taken on faith.
- Reported ICs and long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact; net performance will be lower.
- The sample (120 months, 500 stocks) is short and single-market; t-statistics near 2 are fragile to multiple-testing and regime changes.
- Composite strategy performance is an estimate based on assumed signal correlations, not a directly back-tested result.
- Accruals and asset growth are borderline significant and may not survive out-of-sample.
