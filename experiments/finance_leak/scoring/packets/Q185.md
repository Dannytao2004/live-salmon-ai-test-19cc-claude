# Cross-Sectional Predictors of Stock Returns in a Broad Index Panel

## Abstract
I evaluate nine stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a panel of 500 broad-index stocks over 120 months. Using monthly cross-sectional rank ICs and quintile long-short portfolios, I identify four significant signals: gross profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, and accruals. A composite quintile long-short strategy based on these signals is estimated to earn roughly 7% per year with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.3 before trading costs. These results are in-sample and may be affected by index-membership and survivorship biases.

## Methods
The dataset contains 34,387 stock-month observations from 500 stocks over 120 months. The prediction target is next month's total return, `ret_next`. The nine candidate features are `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

I evaluate each feature using the monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next`, averaged across months. The reported t-statistic is the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation times the square root of the number of months. I classify a signal as valid if its absolute IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0. I also examine equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios formed on each feature.

## Results
Four features pass the significance threshold:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Quintile long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | 0.0293 | 3.83 | 0.88% | 1.19 |
| `idio_vol` | -0.0266 | -3.76 | -0.78% | -1.15 |
| `leverage` | 0.0137 | 2.56 | 0.50% | 0.99 |
| `accruals` | 0.0130 | 2.06 | 0.31% | 0.50 |

The signs imply that stocks with higher gross profitability, lower idiosyncratic volatility, higher leverage, and higher accruals tend to have higher next-month returns. The negative `idio_vol` long-short return means that buying high idiosyncratic volatility and selling low idiosyncratic volatility loses money; reversing that portfolio makes it positive.

The remaining features are not significant. `asset_growth` has the expected negative sign but is only marginal, with an IC t-statistic of -1.87, below the chosen 2.0 threshold.

A composite score using the four valid signals is proposed:
`score = rank(gross_prof) - rank(idio_vol) + rank(leverage) + rank(accruals)`.

An equal-weighted combination of the four corresponding long-short portfolios would have earned about 0.62% per month, or 7.4% annualized, before costs. Accounting for likely correlation among signals, I estimate the composite strategy's gross annualized Sharpe ratio at approximately 1.3.

## Discussion
The positive predictive power of `gross_prof` is consistent with the quality-profitability literature: more profitable firms tend to earn higher subsequent returns. The negative `idio_vol` effect is also well documented and is often attributed to lottery demand and limits to arbitrage. The positive coefficient on `leverage` may reflect a risk premium or a value tilt. The positive `accruals` result is notable because it is opposite to the traditional accruals anomaly; this may be sample-specific or driven by the operating-accruals definition, so it should be treated with caution. Combining the four signals improves diversification relative to any single signal.

## Limitations
These results are in-sample and may not persist out-of-sample. No transaction costs, bid-ask spreads, or short-sale constraints are considered. The universe is constructed from index constituents at the end of month 120, which may introduce survivorship and selection biases. Multiple testing is not fully adjusted, and the `accruals` signal is only marginally significant.
