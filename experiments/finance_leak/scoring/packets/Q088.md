# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: Only Leverage Survives

## Abstract
I evaluate nine stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. Using monthly rank ICs, t-statistics, and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, I find that only `leverage` has a statistically reliable positive relation with `ret_next`. Its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0164 with a t-statistic of 2.74, and a high-minus-low leverage quintile portfolio earns 0.52% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. The remaining eight features have rank IC t-statistics below 1.2 in absolute value and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.75, so I treat them as noise in this sample. A leverage-only long-short strategy has modest expected gross performance, approximately 6.2% annualized before transaction costs.

## Methods
The panel contains 34,708 stock-month observations for 500 stocks over months 1 to 120, with the number of stocks increasing from 143 to 500 because the universe is defined as index constituents at the end of month 120. For each feature, I use the supplied monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank IC between the feature and `ret_next`, averaged across all 120 months. The reported t-statistic is `mean(IC) / std(IC) * sqrt(120)`. I classify a feature as a valid signal if its mean rank IC is statistically distinguishable from zero at conventional levels, specifically `|t| >= 2`, and if the long-short portfolio direction is consistent with the sign of the IC. Long-short returns are equal-weighted portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month. All returns are gross of transaction costs, and no raw data were available for independent replication.

## Results
Only `leverage` passes the validity threshold. It has a positive mean rank IC of 0.0164, an IC t-statistic of 2.74, and positive ICs in 59% of months. The high-minus-low leverage quintile portfolio earns 0.52% per month, or approximately 6.24% annualized arithmetic, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92.

Several other features have positive but weak point estimates: `asset_growth` has mean rank IC 0.0055 with t = 1.16 and long-short monthly return 0.37%; `accruals` has IC 0.0059 with t = 1.03; and `idio_vol` has IC 0.0056 with t = 0.86. These are not statistically reliable at conventional thresholds. `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` are all effectively indistinguishable from zero, with absolute t-statistics below 0.3 and very low long-short Sharpe ratios.

## Discussion
The positive leverage-return relation suggests that, within this sample, higher-leverage firms subsequently outperform lower-leverage firms. This could reflect a risk premium, distress/cyclicality exposure, or an artifact of the survivorship-biased index-constituent universe. The effect is economically modest: a monthly long-short spread of 0.52% and a Sharpe ratio of 0.92 are not strong enough to support a high-conviction standalone strategy. The very low rank IC also implies substantial cross-sectional noise, so implementation would require broad diversification, careful risk controls, and attention to turnover and borrowing costs. Given that the other eight signals fail to show reliable predictive power, a sensible strategy is to trade only `leverage`, going long high-leverage stocks and short low-leverage stocks, while recognizing that the edge is fragile.

## Limitations
The universe is defined by index membership at the end of month 120, creating survivorship bias and likely overstating historical performance. The panel expands from 143 to 500 stocks over time, so early-period estimates are based on fewer names and may not be comparable to later periods. The leverage result is only one significant finding among nine tested signals, raising multiple-testing concerns. Transaction costs, shorting costs, liquidity, and turnover are not reported, so net performance is likely lower than the gross estimates. The accounting features are based on annual reports, which may be stale and subject to look-ahead timing issues in real-time implementation. Finally, no out-of-sample test or factor-neutralization is possible with the provided summary statistics.
