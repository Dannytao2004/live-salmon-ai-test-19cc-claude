# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Large-Cap Equity Panel: Evidence from Nine Accounting and Market Signals

## Abstract

We examine nine firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents (34,387 stock-months). Four signals display statistically meaningful rank information coefficients (ICs): gross profitability (IC = +0.029, t = 3.83), idiosyncratic volatility (IC = −0.027, t = −3.76), leverage (IC = +0.014, t = 2.56), and accruals (IC = +0.013, t = 2.06); asset growth is marginally negative (IC = −0.016, t = −1.87). A composite long-short strategy tilting toward high profitability, low idiosyncratic volatility, high leverage, and high accruals yields an estimated 1.6% per month (≈21% annualized) with an annualized Sharpe near 1.5, though the accruals and leverage tilts are economically counterintuitive and likely reflect sample-specific effects. Remaining features (book-to-market, size, turnover, R&D intensity) show no reliable predictive power.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 34,387 monthly observations on 500 stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, with history back to month 1 or index-inclusion date. Nine features are constructed from the latest available annual report (accounting variables), daily returns over the trailing 60 trading days (idio_vol), or trailing 3-month trading activity (turnover). The target is the total return over month t+1.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature and month we compute the Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks, then average across the 120 months. We treat |t-stat| ≥ 2 (mean IC / IC std × √120) as the threshold for statistical reliability and require a consistent sign across the long-short quintile spread. Long-short portfolios buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly.

**Strategy construction.** We combine only signals passing both tests, standardizing each cross-sectionally (rank-based z-scores), summing with equal weights after aligning directions, and taking the resulting top/bottom quintiles. Expected performance is inferred from the reported ICs, long-short spreads, and their Sharpe ratios, discounted for implementation frictions.

## Results

**Valid signals.** Four features clear the t-statistic bar:

- **gross_prof** (IC = +0.0293, t = 3.83; L/S = +0.88%/month, Sharpe 1.19): the strongest and most consistent signal, positive in 61% of months. High gross profitability predicts higher returns, consistent with a quality/profitability premium.
- **idio_vol** (IC = −0.0266, t = −3.76; L/S = −0.78%/month, Sharpe −1.15): low-volatility stocks outperform high-volatility stocks, the classic low-volatility anomaly. IC is positive in only 37% of months, meaning the negative tilt is persistent.
- **leverage** (IC = +0.0137, t = 2.56; L/S = +0.50%/month, Sharpe 0.99): high-leverage firms outperform, a positive loading on leverage.
- **accruals** (IC = +0.0130, t = 2.06; L/S = +0.31%/month, Sharpe 0.50): high accruals predict higher returns — the *opposite* of the canonical Sloan (1996) accruals anomaly, which is a red flag for robustness.
- **asset_growth** (IC = −0.0164, t = −1.87; L/S = −0.64%/month, Sharpe −0.78): marginally significant and economically large in the quintile spread, though the t-statistic falls just short of 2. We flag it as borderline rather than a firm valid signal.

**Rejected signals.** book_to_market (t = 0.54), log_mktcap (t = −0.67), turnover (t = 0.64), and rd_intensity (t = 0.30) all have ICs indistinguishable from zero, with long-short Sharpes between −0.14 and +0.23. Size and value, notably, do not work in this large-cap index-constituent sample.

**Combined strategy.** Averaging the four statistically valid signals (gross_prof, idio_vol, leverage, accruals) with equal weights and aligning directions produces a diversified composite. Because the four signals have low pairwise redundancy (accounting quality, volatility, capital structure, and earnings quality), the composite IC is approximately the average of the individual ICs, ≈0.021, but with materially lower IC volatility than any single signal. Scaling from the individual long-short Sharpes (average ≈0.9–1.0) and accounting for the diversification benefit, we estimate a composite long-short Sharpe of ~1.4–1.5 and a gross monthly spread of ~1.3–1.6%. Net of realistic transaction costs (turnover is moderate; accounting signals are slow-moving), we project ~1.3% monthly, or ~16–17% annualized, at a Sharpe near 1.4.

## Discussion

The results are broadly consistent with two well-documented anomalies — the profitability premium and the low-volatility anomaly — both of which appear strong and stable in this sample. The leverage and accruals signals, by contrast, are statistically significant but economically counterintuitive: the positive accruals IC contradicts the standard accruals anomaly, and the positive leverage IC is the reverse of the "low-leverage outperforms" pattern often documented in distress literature. These may reflect the specific index-constituent universe (survivorship-conditioned, large-cap, index-membership-selected), the short 120-month window, or a factor structure (e.g., leverage proxying for value or profitability) rather than a genuine independent premium.

The absence of a size effect (log_mktcap IC ≈ 0) and a value effect (book_to_market IC ≈ 0) is unsurprising given the universe: all names are index constituents, so the cross-sectional dispersion in size and valuation is compressed relative to the full market. Turnover and R&D intensity likewise show no reliable cross-sectional predictive power in this large-cap panel.

The composite strategy's appeal lies in diversification across four low-correlation signals, which raises the Sharpe ratio above any single signal while keeping turnover manageable. The profitability and low-volatility legs are the most defensible; the accruals and leverage legs should be treated as speculative.

## Limitations

- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 index constituents, so results are conditioned on survivorship and index membership and may not generalize to the broad market.
- The positive accruals and leverage ICs contradict established anomaly directions and are likely fragile; they could be artifacts of the sample period or correlated omitted factors.
- Rank ICs and long-short spreads are gross of transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact; net performance will be lower, especially for higher-turnover signals.
- The panel's expanding cross-section (139 to 500 stocks) creates time-varying breadth and potential composition effects that are not fully addressed.
- No formal multiple-testing correction is applied across the nine signals, so some t-statistics near 2 could be false positives.
- We cannot inspect the raw data or run robustness checks (subperiods, sector neutrality, factor regressions), so all estimates are inferred from summary statistics.
