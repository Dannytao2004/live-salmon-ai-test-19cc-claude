# Analyst Revisions Dominate: A Cross-Sectional Signal Audit of a 500-Stock Monthly Panel

## Abstract

We audit ten candidate characteristics for predicting next-month cross-sectional stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-months). Nine of the ten features display rank ICs that are statistically indistinguishable from zero, with |IC| ≤ 0.006 and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.75. A single feature, `analyst_rev`, exhibits a mean monthly rank IC of 0.296 with a t-statistic of 89.6 and positive IC in 100% of months, producing an equal-weighted long-short quintile return of 8.66% per month (Sharpe ≈ 26.4). We conclude that the only economically and statistically meaningful predictor in this panel is the analyst consensus revision, and we propose a concentrated long-short strategy built on it. The extreme strength of this signal, however, raises serious concerns about look-ahead bias in its construction.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows, one per stock per month, covering 120 months and 1,213 distinct stocks, with a balanced 500 stocks each month. Each row provides ten features measured at or before the end of month *t*, plus `ret_next`, the total return over month *t*+1.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the supplied monthly Spearman rank IC (cross-sectional correlation between feature and `ret_next`), its time-series mean and standard deviation, the IC t-statistic (mean/std × √120), the fraction of months with positive IC, and the equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile (long-short) mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

**Decision rule.** A feature is deemed a valid signal only if it shows (a) a meaningful mean rank IC, (b) a |t-statistic| large enough to be non-spurious, and (c) a long-short portfolio with an economically non-trivial Sharpe. Features whose IC t-statistics fall within roughly ±2 and whose long-short Sharpe is below ~1 are classified as noise.

**Strategy construction.** For the surviving signal we form an equal-weighted, monthly-rebalanced long-short portfolio: buy the top quintile of `analyst_rev`, short the bottom quintile. Expected performance is taken from the supplied long-short statistics, since these are already computed on the same quintile construction.

## Results

**Signal audit.** Table 1 summarizes the evidence. Nine features cluster tightly around zero:

| feature | mean rank IC | IC t-stat | % months IC>0 | LS monthly | LS Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| analyst_rev | **0.2958** | **89.59** | **100%** | **8.66%** | **26.38** |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 54% | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 50% | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 55% | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 52% | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 51% | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 51% | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 52% | 0.05% | 0.13 |
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | 48% | -0.11% | -0.27 |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | 46% | -0.02% | -0.05 |

None of the nine zero-IC features has a |t-stat| above 1.7, and their long-short Sharpe ratios range from -0.27 to 0.72. Their signs are also inconsistent with well-documented anomalies in some cases (e.g., the value premium would predict a *positive* book-to-market IC, yet we observe -0.003; the low-volatility anomaly would predict a *negative* idio_vol IC, yet we observe +0.004). These are noise-level magnitudes.

**The dominant signal.** `analyst_rev` stands apart by two orders of magnitude. Its mean rank IC of 0.296 implies that analyst revision ranks explain roughly 9% of the cross-sectional rank variance of next-month returns. The t-statistic of 89.6 is not a plausible sampling fluctuation. The long-short quintile portfolio earns 8.66% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 26.4, and the sign is positive in every one of 120 months. The direction is economically intuitive: upward revisions to FY1 EPS forecasts predict higher subsequent returns (the classic post-revision drift).

**Strategy.** We propose a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio: long the top quintile of `analyst_rev`, short the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly. Using the supplied statistics directly, the expected gross annual return is approximately 12 × 8.66% ≈ **104%** (1.04), with an expected annualized Sharpe of **26.4**. These figures are reported as given but should be treated as upper bounds (see Discussion and Limitations).

## Discussion

The results are stark: in this panel, only `analyst_rev` carries predictive information about next-month returns. The remaining nine characteristics — valuation, profitability, size, growth, accruals, volatility, leverage, liquidity, and R&D intensity — produce rank ICs and long-short spreads consistent with pure sampling noise. This is itself informative: it suggests the panel was either constructed or filtered in a way that removes standard cross-sectional return predictability outside of the analyst-revision channel, or that the sample period and universe are atypical.

The magnitude of the `analyst_rev` result, however, is not credible as a realizable trading edge. A Sharpe of 26 and a 100% hit rate on monthly IC signs are far beyond what is observed in genuine equity return prediction; the highest-quality published anomalies rarely exceed Sharpe 1–2. The most likely explanation is a **timing inconsistency in the data dictionary**: `analyst_rev` is described as "change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1... recorded at the end of month t+1," while the target is the return over month t+1. Analyst revisions during month t+1 are mechanically correlated with the price moves that constitute the month t+1 return (analysts revise in the direction of recent price/news). Using an end-of-t+1-measured revision to predict the t+1 return is therefore a look-ahead leak, not a tradable signal. If instead the revision were lagged to month t (available before the return period), the true IC would almost certainly be far smaller — plausibly 0.02–0.05, consistent with the established literature on revision momentum.

We therefore report `analyst_rev` as the dominant *statistical* signal in the panel while flagging that its apparent strength is almost certainly an artifact of measurement timing. A defensible strategy would use a properly lagged revision and expect a far more modest Sharpe.

## Limitations

- The `analyst_rev` field is measured during month t+1, the same period as the target return, creating likely look-ahead bias that inflates its measured IC and Sharpe.
- We cannot verify the point-in-time integrity of the other features (e.g., whether annual-report data was truly available at month-end t), so their null results could reflect either genuine absence of signal or data-timing noise.
- The 500-stock universe is index-constituent-only and survivorship-aware but still excludes small caps, so results may not generalize to the broad cross-section.
- All performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, borrow costs, and shorting constraints, which would materially reduce any long-short Sharpe, especially for a high-turnover signal.
- We rely on summary statistics rather than raw data, so we cannot test interactions, nonlinearities, or alternative portfolio weighting schemes.
- The extreme 100% IC-positive rate is statistically implausible for a genuine signal and should be treated as a red flag rather than a validated result.
