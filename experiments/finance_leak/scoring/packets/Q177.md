# Cross-Sectional Predictors of Monthly Stock Returns in an Index-Constituent Panel

## Abstract
We examine nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using 34,387 monthly observations on 500 index constituents over 120 months. Using monthly Spearman rank ICs and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find robust positive predictive power for gross profitability, accruals, and leverage, and robust negative predictive power for idiosyncratic volatility. A composite strategy that longs high-quality/low-volatility stocks and shorts low-quality/high-volatility stocks yields an estimated annualized return of about 7.5% and Sharpe ratio of about 1.2. However, the universe is defined by end-of-sample index membership, so survivorship bias and multiple-testing concerns limit causal interpretation.

## Methods
The data form a monthly panel of 500 stocks over months 1–120, with one row per stock-month and a next-month return target (`ret_next`). All accounting variables (`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `rd_intensity`) are taken from the latest annual report available at the end of month t, so they are observable before `ret_next`. Market-based variables (`log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, `turnover`) are also measured through the end of month t. Thus the signal-formation timing does not mechanically use future information, though annual report data can be stale by up to twelve months.

For each feature, we evaluate the average monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank IC with `ret_next`, its t-statistic, and the performance of an equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile. A feature is treated as a valid signal if the absolute IC t-statistic is at least 2, the long-short return is economically meaningful, and the direction is consistent with the IC. We then form a composite score at the end of each month: cross-sectional ranks of gross profitability, accruals, and leverage are added, and the rank of idiosyncratic volatility is subtracted. The strategy buys the top quintile and shorts the bottom quintile of the composite score, equal-weighted, with monthly rebalancing.

## Results
Four features meet the validity threshold:

| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | Positive | 0.0293 | 3.83 | 0.88% | 1.19 |
| `accruals` | Positive | 0.0130 | 2.06 | 0.31% | 0.50 |
| `idio_vol` | Negative | -0.0266 | -3.76 | -0.78% (high minus low) | -1.15 |
| `leverage` | Positive | 0.0137 | 2.56 | 0.50% | 0.99 |

For `idio_vol`, the negative IC and negative long-short return mean that a profitable implementation would short high-volatility stocks and buy low-volatility stocks. The strongest signals are gross profitability and idiosyncratic volatility, both with |t| > 3.7. Accruals and leverage are weaker but pass the |t| ≥ 2 threshold.

The remaining features are not significant. `book_to_market` has a mean rank IC of 0.0029 (t = 0.54), `log_mktcap` has -0.0043 (t = -0.67), `turnover` has 0.0042 (t = 0.64), and `rd_intensity` has 0.0018 (t = 0.30). `asset_growth` is marginal: its mean rank IC is -0.0164 with t = -1.87 and a long-short mean of -0.64% per month, but it falls just short of the conventional significance threshold.

A simple equal-weight combination of the four valid signals in their profitable directions gives an expected monthly return of approximately (0.88% + 0.31% + 0.78% + 0.50%) / 4 = 0.62%, or about 7.4% annualized. Accounting for moderate diversification across signals, we estimate an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.2.

## Discussion
The results are broadly consistent with several well-known return predictors, but with important caveats. Gross profitability behaves as a quality signal: more profitable firms outperform. Idiosyncratic volatility behaves as a low-volatility anomaly: high-volatility stocks underperform. Leverage has a positive coefficient in this sample, which may reflect a risk premium or sample-specific effects rather than a universal distress anomaly. Accruals have a positive sign, opposite to the classical accruals anomaly, so this result should be treated cautiously despite its marginal statistical significance.

The composite strategy is attractive in this panel, but its estimated performance is likely optimistic. The universe consists only of stocks that were index constituents at the end of month 120, which introduces survivorship bias: stocks that failed or were removed before the end of the sample are excluded. This can inflate both individual signal performance and the composite. In addition, the strategy assumes frictionless monthly rebalancing, no shorting constraints, and no transaction costs. With nine signals tested, multiple-testing noise is also a concern, especially for the weaker signals such as accruals.

## Limitations
- The universe is defined by end-of-sample index membership, creating survivorship and look-ahead bias.
- Transaction costs, shorting constraints, and capacity are not modeled.
- Multiple testing across nine signals means some apparent predictors may be false positives.
- Accounting features may be stale because they use the latest annual report available at month t.
- The composite Sharpe is an approximation because signal correlations are not directly estimated.
- Results are in-sample; no out-of-sample validation is provided.
