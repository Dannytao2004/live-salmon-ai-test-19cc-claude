# A Note on the Predictive Structure of the Monthly Equity Panel: One Signal Dominates

## Abstract

I analyze a monthly panel of 500 index constituents followed over 120 months (60,000 stock-months, 1,217 distinct stocks) with ten candidate characteristics and a one-month-ahead return target. Of the ten features, only three carry statistically meaningful cross-sectional predictive content: analyst_rev (mean rank IC 0.296, t = 80.3), gross_prof (IC 0.038, t = 6.1), and asset_growth (IC −0.030, t = −4.3), with idio_vol as a marginal fourth (IC −0.026, t = −4.2). The remaining six features — book_to_market, log_mktcap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and rd_intensity — show rank ICs indistinguishable from zero. Because analyst_rev is recorded at the end of month t+1 while the target spans month t+1, this feature embeds a severe look-ahead problem and cannot be traded as specified. A composite strategy built only on the tradeable signals (long high gross_prof, short high asset_growth) is projected to earn roughly 5–6% annually with a Sharpe near 1.0, far below the headline analyst-revision numbers but defensible.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains one row per stock per month for months 1–120, with 500 stocks in every month (a point-in-time index universe). Stocks that leave the index remain in the data through their final month, so survivorship bias from delisting is largely avoided — a genuine strength for return prediction work. The target `ret_next` is the total return over month t+1.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature I use the provided within-month Spearman rank IC, averaged across the 120 months, with the t-statistic computed as mean(IC)/std(IC) × √120. I treat |t| ≳ 2 as evidence of a real cross-sectional relationship. I also use the equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile long-short return and its annualized Sharpe as an economic-magnitude check. Rank IC (an ordinal, outlier-robust measure) and the quintile spread are complementary: IC tests monotonicity of the whole cross-section, the spread tests the tails.

**Signal validation.** Beyond statistical significance I apply two filters: (i) *timing consistency* — the feature must be observable at the end of month t, before the return window begins; and (ii) *plausibility* — the sign should match an economic story. The data dictionary is decisive here: `analyst_rev` is "change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1... recorded at the end of month t+1." Its window overlaps the target window, so its enormous IC is mechanical foresight, not a tradeable edge.

**Strategy construction.** For the tradeable composite I equal-weight the standardized ranks of the surviving signals with signs set by their IC direction, form quintile portfolios each month, and go long the top quintile / short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted. I estimate expected return from the component long-short means and Sharpe from the reported signal Sharpes, adjusted for diversification across two weakly correlated signals.

## Results

**Valid signals.** Three features clear the significance bar and one is marginal:

- **analyst_rev** — positive, mean rank IC 0.296, t = 80.3, positive in 100% of months, long-short mean 8.73%/month, Sharpe 24.2. Statistically overwhelming but **not tradeable as specified** (see Discussion).
- **gross_prof** — positive, IC 0.038 (t = 6.1), positive in 70% of months, long-short 1.16%/month, Sharpe 1.94. Consistent with the profitability premium: high gross-profit firms outperform.
- **asset_growth** — negative, IC −0.030 (t = −4.3), positive in only 39% of months, long-short −0.99%/month, Sharpe −1.48. Consistent with the asset-growth/overinvestment anomaly: fast asset expanders underperform.
- **idio_vol** — negative, IC −0.026 (t = −4.2), positive in 35% of months, long-short −0.74%/month, Sharpe −1.20. The low-volatility anomaly; statistically real but economically modest and partly overlapping with the growth/size complex.

**Rejected signals.** book_to_market (IC 0.003, t = 0.68), log_mktcap (0.006, t = 1.26), accruals (0.002, t = 0.36), leverage (−0.001, t = −0.30), turnover (0.001, t = 0.14), and rd_intensity (−0.000, t = −0.10) all have t-statistics well inside noise. Notably, book_to_market and accruals — classic value and accrual-anomaly variables — show essentially zero IC here, and leverage and turnover are flat. The sample is a single index universe, which mechanically truncates the extreme small-cap/high-turnover tails where several of these anomalies are strongest, plausibly explaining their decay.

**Composite strategy.** Excluding analyst_rev, I combine gross_prof (long) and asset_growth (short), with idio_vol (short) as an optional third leg. Their long-short monthly means sum to roughly 0.9–1.9% depending on weighting; assuming modest positive correlation among the growth/volatility legs, a two-signal equal-weight composite retains most of the gross_prof Sharpe. Net of realistic transaction costs on a 500-name monthly-rebalanced quintile book (turnover of perhaps 30–50% per side per month), I haircut the gross spread by roughly 40%. The projected net figures are ~5% annual return and Sharpe ~0.9–1.0.

## Discussion

The dominant empirical fact is the near-perfect predictive power of analyst_rev — and the equally dominant fact that it is an artifact. An IC of 0.296 with a t-statistic of 80 and a 100% hit rate is not a signal; it is a timestamp leak. Because the revision is measured over month t+1 and scaled by contemporaneous price, it is mechanically correlated with the month t+1 return. Any backtest that "trades" analyst_rev would be trading on information it could not have had. I therefore classify it as a *valid but non-investable* feature: statistically real, economically untradeable as specified. A properly specified version would lag the revision to the end of month t (revisions observable before the return window); the literature suggests such lagged revisions retain a real, if much smaller, IC of perhaps 0.02–0.04.

Among the tradeable signals, the results form a coherent story: profitability (gross_prof) and conservative investment (low asset_growth) both earn premia, and low idiosyncratic volatility earns a modest premium — the classic quality/low-risk cluster. These three are economically related (profitable, slow-growing, stable firms), which limits diversification benefit but also means the composite is a single coherent "quality-minus-junk" bet rather than three independent edges.

The null results are as informative as the positive ones. Value (book_to_market) and accruals, two of the most celebrated anomalies, are flat in this index-constituent panel. This is consistent with the view that these effects are concentrated in micro-caps and less-liquid names excluded from a 500-stock index universe, and with post-publication decay.

## Limitations

- **The analyst_rev leak dominates the table**; excluding it, only two-to-three signals survive, and my composite performance estimate is sensitive to the assumed correlation and cost haircut, which the summary statistics do not pin down.
- **No transaction-cost data are provided**, so the net return and Sharpe figures are judgment-based haircuts rather than measured quantities.
- **Signals are evaluated independently**; I cannot observe pairwise or multivariate correlations among features, so the composite's true diversification and any redundancy (e.g., idio_vol vs. asset_growth) are inferred, not estimated.
- **The universe is a single 500-name index**, truncating the small-cap and illiquid tails where value, accruals, and turnover anomalies are typically strongest, so the null results may not generalize to the broad market.
- **The sample is 120 months of one regime**; IC stability across sub-periods cannot be assessed from the aggregate statistics, and the reported t-statistics assume independent monthly observations.
- **Feature definitions are taken on faith**; the analyst_rev timing problem is inferable only from the data dictionary, and other subtler timing mismatches cannot be ruled out.
