# Report Q147

# Point-in-Time Validation of Monthly Equity Signals

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. Raw standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) shows exceptionally strong backtest performance, but this signal is not point-in-time because earnings are announced after the fiscal quarter ends. After requiring point-in-time availability, no feature reaches conventional statistical significance. We therefore reject all ten features as valid tradable signals in this dataset and recommend no active long-short deployment based on the reported statistics.

## Methods
We use a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 120 months and 500 stocks per month. The prediction target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. For each feature, we assess predictive power using monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlations with `ret_next`, summarized by mean rank IC, IC standard deviation, and t-statistic. We also examine equal-weighted top-minus-bottom quintile long-short portfolios, with monthly mean returns and annualized Sharpe ratios.

A feature is considered a valid signal only if it is available at the end of month t without lookahead and has a statistically reliable rank IC. We use a conventional t-statistic threshold of approximately 2.0.

## Results
Raw `sue` has the strongest apparent signal: mean monthly rank IC of 0.0998, IC t-statistic of 23.25, 99% of months with positive IC, a long-short mean return of 3.04% per month, and an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. However, the data dictionary states that firms announce quarterly earnings in the month after the fiscal quarter ends. The `sue` variable is defined using the most recent fiscal quarter ending on or before the end of month t. If that quarter ended during month t, its actual EPS would not be announced until month t+1, so `sue` would not be available at the end of month t. The reported strategy would therefore be front-running the earnings announcement. This lookahead bias invalidates the raw `sue` signal as reported.

All other features show weak or negligible predictive power. Their rank IC t-statistics are all below conventional significance thresholds: `asset_growth` has the largest at 1.64, followed by `accruals` at 1.34 and `idio_vol` at 1.14. None has a t-statistic near 2.0, and long-short Sharpe ratios are all below 0.75. We therefore reject them as valid signals.

## Discussion
The dramatic raw SUE result is consistent with lookahead rather than a tradable post-earnings-announcement drift strategy. A properly lagged SUE signal would likely have a positive but much weaker return, because the announcement return would already be realized before the signal is used. The other characteristics, including value, profitability, size, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity, do not provide reliable standalone predictive power in this index universe.

Given the absence of valid point-in-time signals, the proposed strategy is to avoid trading these features. Any deployment based on the raw summary statistics would likely overstate expected performance and may lose money after transaction costs.

## Limitations
This analysis is based only on summary statistics; we cannot construct properly lagged SUE or test combined factors. Transaction costs, short-sale constraints, and capacity are not included. The universe is limited to 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize. Multiple testing and data-snooping may also affect the weak observed t-statistics.
