# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Large-Cap Equity Panel: The Dominance of Analyst Revisions

## Abstract

We examine ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns in a panel of 60,000 stock-months (500 index constituents per month over 120 months). Only one feature, analyst consensus EPS revision (`analyst_rev`), exhibits a robust and economically large predictive relationship, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.296 (t = 89.6) and a long-short quintile spread of 8.66% per month (annualized Sharpe 26.4). All nine remaining features—value, profitability, size, growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—produce rank ICs indistinguishable from zero (|t| < 1.7) and long-short spreads below 0.3% per month. However, we note a critical methodological caveat: `analyst_rev` is recorded at the end of month t+1, the same month over which `ret_next` is measured, so the signal is not implementable at the point of portfolio formation. We therefore conclude that no feature in this panel is a genuinely tradable predictor, and the apparent analyst-revision alpha should be interpreted as contemporaneous information leakage or look-ahead rather than a forecastable edge.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows: 500 index constituents per month for 120 months (1,213 distinct stocks). Each row reports ten point-in-time characteristics measured at the end of month t and the subsequent one-month total return `ret_next`. The universe is survivorship-aware: stocks removed from the index (e.g., after distress) remain in the data through their final month, mitigating delisting bias.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the provided within-month Spearman rank IC (averaged across 120 months), the IC t-statistic (mean IC / IC standard deviation × √120), the fraction of months with positive IC, and the equal-weighted top-minus-bottom quintile long-short return and its annualized Sharpe ratio. Rank IC is preferred to Pearson correlation because it is robust to outliers and to the heavy right tail in features such as `book_to_market` and `turnover`.

**Decision rule.** We classify a feature as a "valid signal" only if it clears two bars: (i) statistical reliability (|IC t-stat| ≳ 2.0) and (ii) economic significance (long-short spread materially above transaction costs, and a Sharpe ratio consistent with a realizable strategy). We additionally require that the feature be observable at portfolio formation (end of month t). This last requirement is decisive for `analyst_rev`.

**Strategy construction.** For any valid signal we would form a monthly equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio, rebalanced at each month-end, buying the top quintile and shorting the bottom quintile. We estimate annualized return as 12 × mean monthly long-short return and annualized Sharpe as √12 × (mean / std) of monthly long-short returns.

## Results

**Signal-by-signal findings.**

- **`analyst_rev` (analyst consensus revision):** mean rank IC = 0.2958, t = 89.59, positive in 100% of months, long-short mean 8.66%/month, annualized Sharpe 26.38. This is an extraordinarily strong and monotonic relationship—far larger than typical published anomalies (which rarely exceed IC 0.05 and Sharpe 1.0).
- **`asset_growth`:** IC = 0.0061, t = 1.64, long-short 0.28%/month, Sharpe 0.72. Weakly positive but statistically marginal.
- **`accruals`:** IC = 0.0055, t = 1.34, long-short 0.29%/month, Sharpe 0.70. Note the sign is *positive*, opposite to the classic accruals anomaly; not reliable.
- **`idio_vol`:** IC = 0.0043, t = 1.14, Sharpe 0.08. Not significant.
- **`gross_prof`:** IC = 0.0039, t = 0.93, Sharpe 0.26. Not significant.
- **`book_to_market`:** IC = −0.0030, t = −0.72, Sharpe −0.27. No value premium in this large-cap panel.
- **`leverage`:** IC = −0.0043, t = −1.00, Sharpe −0.05. Not significant.
- **`log_mktcap`:** IC = 0.0001, t = 0.02. No size effect.
- **`turnover`:** IC = 0.0001, t = 0.02. No liquidity effect.
- **`rd_intensity`:** IC = −0.0002, t = −0.05. No effect.

**Pattern.** The results are strikingly bimodal: one feature carries essentially all of the predictive content, and the other nine are statistically indistinguishable from noise. The IC standard deviations are all similar (0.036–0.047), so the difference in t-statistics is driven almost entirely by the mean IC. The near-perfect 100% hit rate and Sharpe of 26 for `analyst_rev` are not plausible for a genuine tradable signal; they are the signature of a feature that overlaps the return window.

**Strategy.** A strategy based on `analyst_rev` would, mechanically, earn about 8.66% per month (≈104% annualized) with a Sharpe near 26. But because `analyst_rev` is measured during month t+1—the same period as `ret_next`—this return is not achievable by an investor who must form portfolios at the end of month t. The remaining features offer no exploitable spread.

## Discussion

The central finding is a cautionary one. The dataset contains exactly one feature with overwhelming predictive power, and its data dictionary reveals why: `analyst_rev` is "recorded at the end of month t+1," i.e., contemporaneously with `ret_next`. Analyst revisions and returns co-move mechanically—analysts revise upward after good news, and the news *is* the return. The 0.296 rank IC and 26 Sharpe are therefore an artifact of look-ahead, not a tradable signal. This is a textbook example of why point-in-time discipline matters: a feature must be observable strictly before the return window.

Among the genuinely lagged features (all measured at end of month t), none is significant. This is itself informative. In a panel restricted to the 500 largest index constituents, classic anomalies are expected to be weak: the value premium, size effect, and profitability premium are documented primarily in broad cross-sections that include small and micro-caps. Large-cap index membership also implies high analyst coverage and liquidity, which arbitrages away many mispricings. The absence of the accruals effect (and its wrong sign) and the absence of a value premium are consistent with this interpretation.

The marginal features (`asset_growth` t = 1.64, `accruals` t = 1.34) are not strong enough to survive multiple-testing adjustment across ten features; with ten tests, a t-statistic of 1.64 corresponds to a family-wise p-value well above conventional thresholds. We therefore reject them as signals.

## Limitations

- We cannot verify the exact timing convention beyond the data dictionary; if `analyst_rev` were somehow lagged relative to `ret_next`, the conclusion would change, but the documentation and the implausible Sharpe both point to contemporaneous measurement.
- The universe is limited to 500 large-cap index constituents, so conclusions do not generalize to small-caps or the broad cross-section.
- The summary statistics provide only aggregate IC and long-short results; we cannot inspect sub-period stability, factor-neutral performance, or transaction costs.
- Long-short spreads are equal-weighted and gross of costs; net-of-cost performance would be lower, especially for high-turnover signals.
- Multiple-testing adjustment is applied informally; a formal false-discovery-rate analysis could further weaken the marginal features.
- The 120-month sample may be too short to detect small but real effects, and the reported t-statistics assume serially independent monthly ICs.
