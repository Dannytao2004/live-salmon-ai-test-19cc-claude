# Profitability, Investment, and Low-Volatility Signals in a Large-Cap Index Panel

## Abstract
We examine 60,000 monthly stock observations covering 500 index constituents per month over 120 months. Using monthly Spearman rank ICs and quintile long-short portfolios, we find three reliable return predictors: gross profitability (positive), asset growth (negative), and idiosyncratic volatility (negative). Other characteristics—book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show weak or statistically insignificant predictive power in this large-cap universe. A composite long-short strategy that buys high-profitability, low-asset-growth, low-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks and shorts the opposite is expected to earn roughly 11–12% annualized gross return with an annualized Sharpe near 1.8–1.9. Gross profitability is the strongest single signal, while investment and low-vol signals provide additional diversification.

## Methods
The panel contains 60,000 rows: 500 stocks per month for 120 months, with 1,217 distinct stocks. Each row has nine point-in-time characteristics and the next-month total return `ret_next`. We evaluate signals using monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank ICs, averaged over all 120 months. The IC t-stat is computed as the mean IC divided by the time-series standard deviation of ICs, multiplied by √120. We also form equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios each month, buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile of each characteristic. For negative-IC signals, the sign is reversed for economic interpretation. The composite strategy ranks stocks each month on gross profitability, negative asset growth, and negative idiosyncratic volatility, equal-weights the three signed ranks, and goes long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile.

## Results
Three features qualify as valid signals. Gross profitability has a mean rank IC of 0.0378 (t = 6.07), with 70% of months positive; its top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns 1.16% monthly with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94. Asset growth has a mean rank IC of −0.0303 (t = −4.29), and its top-minus-bottom portfolio earns −0.99% monthly; reversing the signal gives +0.99% monthly with an annualized Sharpe of 1.48. Idiosyncratic volatility has a mean rank IC of −0.0261 (t = −4.19), and its top-minus-bottom portfolio earns −0.74% monthly; reversing the signal gives +0.74% monthly with an annualized Sharpe of 1.20.

The remaining signals are rejected. Book-to-market has a near-zero IC (0.0027, t = 0.68), log market cap is weak (0.0055, t = 1.26), accruals is insignificant (0.0015, t = 0.36), leverage is flat (−0.0011, t = −0.30), turnover is flat (0.0006, t = 0.14), and R&D intensity is flat (−0.0004, t = −0.10). None has a long-short Sharpe above 0.60, and none reaches conventional statistical significance.

An equal-weighted composite of the three valid signals earns approximately 0.96% monthly gross, or about 12.2% annualized compounded. Assuming modest positive correlation among the signals, the composite Sharpe is expected to be around 1.85. After realistic trading costs, net annualized return would likely be closer to 10–11%.

## Discussion
Gross profitability captures the quality/profitability premium: firms generating more gross profit per unit of assets tend to outperform. Asset growth reflects the investment anomaly: firms expanding their asset base aggressively often earn lower subsequent returns, consistent with overinvestment, risk-based explanations, or market mispricing. Idiosyncratic volatility reflects the low-volatility anomaly: high-idio-vol stocks underperform, potentially due to lottery-like demand, short-sale constraints, or leverage constraints.

The weak results for value, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity may reflect the large-cap index universe and the specific 120-month sample. Index constituents are relatively liquid and established, reducing the power of anomalies that are stronger in small caps or distressed firms. The composite strategy diversifies across profitability, investment, and volatility, which are economically distinct and likely have low-to-moderate correlation. Implementation would require monthly rebalancing, equal weighting within quintiles, and sufficient borrow availability for the short leg.

## Limitations
The sample covers only 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to small-cap or full-market universes. The 120-month period is relatively short and may not represent all market regimes. Transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact are not explicitly modeled, and long-short implementation may be constrained for some stocks. No industry or sector neutralization is applied, so signals may load on sector exposures. The Sharpe estimate for the composite assumes approximate independence and does not use a formal risk model. IC t-statistics ignore cross-sectional correlation and overlapping annual-report information. Nonlinearities, interactions, and time-varying factor premia are not explored.
