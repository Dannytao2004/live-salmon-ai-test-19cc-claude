# Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift in a Monthly Stock Panel: SUE Is the Only Robust Signal

## Abstract
We evaluate ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns in a panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Among these, only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) is a statistically reliable signal, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.0998 and a t-statistic of 23.25. A quintile long-short portfolio based on SUE earns an average of 3.04% per month and has an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. The other nine signals have absolute IC t-statistics below 1.64 and are not statistically distinguishable from zero. We propose a monthly-rebalanced SUE long-short strategy, while noting that transaction costs and capacity may reduce live performance.

## Methods
The sample consists of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 120 months and 500 stocks per month. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` each month. The reported mean rank IC is the average of these 120 monthly correlations, and the t-statistic is computed as the mean divided by the standard deviation times the square root of the number of months.

We also form equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month by sorting stocks on each feature. The long-short return buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile. A feature is considered a valid signal if its mean rank IC is statistically significant at conventional levels, using a threshold of approximately |t| > 2.0.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| sue | 0.0998 | 23.25 | 3.04% | 7.31 |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | -0.05 |
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | -0.27 |

| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 |

SUE is overwhelmingly the strongest signal. Its mean rank IC is positive in 99% of months, and its t-statistic of 23.25 is far above the significance threshold. The long-short SUE portfolio has an average monthly return of 3.04% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31.

No other feature passes the statistical significance threshold. Asset growth and accruals show weakly positive long-short returns, but their IC t-statistics of 1.64 and 1.34 do not provide reliable evidence of predictive power. The remaining features have t-statistics near or below 1.14 and are effectively indistinguishable from zero.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with the post-earnings-announcement drift (PEAD) anomaly: stocks with positive earnings surprises continue to outperform over the following month, while stocks with negative surprises continue to underperform. SUE captures the market's underreaction to earnings news, and the economic magnitude is large in this sample.

The other characteristics, including value, size, profitability, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity, do not show robust predictive power in this dataset. This may reflect the specific universe, sample period, or the possibility that these signals are already priced in the index constituent cross-section.

## Limitations
The backtest is in-sample and may overstate live performance if the SUE premium decays or becomes arbitraged. The long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, financing costs, and short-selling constraints, which can materially reduce a monthly-rebalanced strategy. The universe consists of index constituents, so results may not generalize to the full cross-section of stocks. Finally, multiple testing across ten signals means marginal t-statistics should be interpreted cautiously, though SUE remains significant even under a strict significance threshold.
