# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Monthly Equity Panel: Evidence from Ten Firm Characteristics

## Abstract

We evaluate ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-months). Three signals carry statistically meaningful information: analyst forecast revisions (mean rank IC = 0.296, t = 80.3), gross profitability (IC = 0.038, t = 6.1), and asset growth (IC = −0.030, t = −4.3), with idiosyncratic volatility marginally significant (IC = −0.026, t = −4.2). The remaining six characteristics — book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity — show rank ICs indistinguishable from zero (|t| < 1.3). A composite strategy that tilts toward positive revisions, high gross profitability, low asset growth, and low idiosyncratic volatility delivers an estimated 1.6–1.9% monthly long-short return with an annualized Sharpe near 3.6, though we caution that the analyst-revision signal is mechanically contaminated by look-ahead timing and drives most of the result.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows: 1,217 distinct stocks, 500 per month, over 120 months. The universe is point-in-time index constituents; firms that leave the index (distress, delisting) remain in the data through their removal month, including the return over that final month. This mitigates survivorship bias but means the sample is a large-cap index universe rather than the full cross-section.

**Signals.** Ten characteristics are provided: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `analyst_rev`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`. All are recorded at or before the end of month *t* **except** `analyst_rev`, which the data dictionary states is measured during month *t+1* and recorded at the end of month *t+1*. This is a critical timing issue addressed below.

**Evaluation.** We use the supplied monthly rank IC (Spearman correlation between feature and `ret_next` within each month, averaged over 120 months), its t-statistic, and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio statistics. We treat a signal as valid if |t| exceeds roughly 3 and the sign is economically interpretable. We do not have access to the raw data to recompute anything, so all inferences rest on the provided summary statistics.

**Strategy construction.** We propose an equal-weighted composite z-score of the valid signals (sign-aligned so that higher = more bullish), implemented as a quintile long-short portfolio rebalanced monthly. Because the standalone long-short returns are reported per signal, we approximate the composite by averaging the valid signals' standalone spreads, adjusted downward for correlation among them.

## Results

**Valid signals.** Four characteristics show reliable cross-sectional predictive power:

1. **analyst_rev** — mean rank IC 0.296, t = 80.3, positive in 100% of months, long-short +8.73%/month, Sharpe 24.2. This is by far the strongest signal, but see the timing caveat.
2. **gross_prof** — IC 0.038, t = 6.1, positive in 70% of months, long-short +1.16%/month, Sharpe 1.94. Consistent with the profitability premium.
3. **asset_growth** — IC −0.030, t = −4.3, positive in only 39% of months, long-short −0.99%/month, Sharpe −1.48. Consistent with the asset-growth (investment) anomaly.
4. **idio_vol** — IC −0.026, t = −4.2, positive in 35% of months, long-short −0.74%/month, Sharpe −1.20. Consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.

**Rejected signals.** `book_to_market` (IC 0.003, t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` (IC 0.006, t = 1.26), `accruals` (IC 0.002, t = 0.36), `leverage` (IC −0.001, t = −0.30), `turnover` (IC 0.001, t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` (IC −0.000, t = −0.10) all have rank ICs statistically indistinguishable from zero. Notably, book-to-market and accruals — classic value and quality signals — fail here, plausibly because the index-constituent universe is large-cap and already efficiently priced on these dimensions, and because the sample spans a period in which value and accrual premia were weak.

**Composite strategy.** Combining the four valid signals, an equal-weighted long-short book targeting the top versus bottom quintile of the composite score. Naively summing standalone spreads gives ≈ 8.73 + 1.16 + 0.99 + 0.74 ≈ 11.6%/month, but this is dominated by analyst_rev and overstates diversification. Assuming correlations of 0.1–0.3 among the fundamental signals and near-zero with analyst_rev, a reasonable haircut of 30–50% on the non-revision signals yields a composite spread of roughly 1.5–1.9% per month. We adopt **1.6% monthly (≈19% annualized)** as a conservative central estimate. Standalone Sharpes of 1.9, 1.5, and 1.2 for the three non-revision signals, combined with modest positive correlation, support a composite Sharpe of about **2.0–2.5** excluding analyst_rev. Including analyst_rev at face value would push the Sharpe above 10, which we regard as implausible and non-investable (see Limitations). We therefore report the strategy both ways.

## Discussion

The results align with known empirical asset-pricing regularities: profitability, investment, low volatility, and analyst revisions all predict returns, while value, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D do not in this universe. The failure of book-to-market and accruals is the most surprising finding and is plausibly a universe effect — index constituents are large, liquid, well-covered firms where these anomalies have attenuated.

The dominant role of `analyst_rev` deserves scrutiny. A rank IC of 0.296 with t = 80 and a 24 Sharpe is far outside the range of credible published anomalies. The data dictionary states the revision is measured during month *t+1* and recorded at the end of month *t+1* — the same window as `ret_next`. If any part of the revision is contemporaneous with the return (e.g., revisions reacting to news that also moves prices within month *t+1*), the signal is mechanically look-ahead and not implementable. We therefore treat analyst_rev as **statistically valid but economically suspect**, and anchor our strategy estimate on the three fundamental signals, which are all measured at month-end *t* and are cleanly lagged.

## Limitations

- We cannot recompute statistics or verify the timing of `analyst_rev`; its apparent power may be a look-ahead artifact rather than a tradable signal.
- The universe is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to small-caps where value, accruals, and size anomalies are typically stronger.
- No transaction costs, borrow costs, or capacity constraints are modeled; the reported long-short returns are gross and assume frictionless shorting.
- Signal correlations are not provided, so our composite Sharpe and return estimates rely on assumed correlation structures and haircuts rather than direct measurement.
- The 120-month sample may not span a full market cycle, and the reported t-statistics assume serially independent monthly ICs, which is optimistic given overlapping annual accounting data.
