# Look-Ahead Bias and Weak Cross-Sectional Signals in a Monthly Equity Panel

## Abstract
We examine ten stock characteristics as candidate predictors of next-month returns in a 60,000-row monthly panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months. Only analyst consensus revision (`analyst_rev`) shows strong predictive power, with a mean rank IC of 0.296 and a long-short Sharpe ratio of 26.4. However, `analyst_rev` is recorded at the end of month t+1, the same period as the target return, so it is a look-ahead signal that cannot be traded at the end of month t. All other features have mean rank ICs below 0.007 in absolute value, t-statistics below 2, and long-short returns that are economically small and statistically insignificant. We conclude that no tradable feature-based long-short strategy is supported by this panel.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations from 1,213 distinct stocks, with 500 stocks per month over 120 months. The target `ret_next` is the total return over month t+1. Features are measured at the end of month t, except `analyst_rev`, which is explicitly recorded at the end of month t+1. We evaluate each feature using the average monthly Spearman rank IC between the feature and `ret_next`, its time-series t-statistic across 120 months, and an equal-weighted top-quintile minus bottom-quintile long-short portfolio. Long-short returns are reported before transaction costs and shorting constraints.

## Results
The signal statistics are summarised below.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Long-Short Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | -0.27 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| analyst_rev | 0.2958 | 89.59 | 8.66% | 26.38 |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | -0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 |

Only `analyst_rev` appears economically and statistically large. Its IC is 0.296, positive in 100% of months, and its long-short portfolio earns 8.66% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 26.4. This is implausibly strong for a real-time predictor and reflects the fact that `analyst_rev` measures forecast revisions during month t+1, scaled by price, and is recorded at the end of month t+1. It is therefore contemporaneous with, or even a transformation of, the return it is meant to predict. At the end of month t, an investor would not know these revisions.

Among the point-in-time features, `asset_growth` (IC = 0.0061, t = 1.64) and `accruals` (IC = 0.0055, t = 1.34) are the strongest, but neither reaches conventional significance. Their signs are also opposite to the well-documented asset growth and accruals anomalies, which would predict negative ICs. The remaining features are effectively zero: `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` have t-statistics near zero, while `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `idio_vol`, and `leverage` are weak and insignificant.

## Discussion
The central finding is that the only feature with strong predictive power in this panel is not a valid tradable signal because of look-ahead bias. The other features are available at the end of month t and could in principle be used, but their estimated rank ICs are tiny and their long-short returns are small and statistically indistinguishable from zero. After accounting for multiple testing across ten features, none would survive even a lenient significance threshold.

A disciplined quantitative process should therefore avoid building a feature-based long-short strategy on this dataset. If capital must be deployed, a passive index or cash position is preferable to trading on these weak signals. A combined strategy using `asset_growth` and `accruals` might show a modest backtest Sharpe, but the economic rationale is weak, the signs are anomalous, and the result is unlikely to persist out of sample.

## Limitations
The panel is limited to 120 months and 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalise to broader universes or other periods. Transaction costs, borrow costs, and shorting constraints are ignored and would further erode any weak long-short returns. Multiple testing across ten features means even marginal t-statistics should be heavily discounted. No out-of-sample validation or risk-model adjustment was performed. Finally, `analyst_rev` is recorded contemporaneously with the return month and cannot be used in a real-time strategy.
