# Look-Ahead Bias and Weak Signals in a Monthly Equity Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. The only feature with substantial predictive power is `analyst_rev`, which exhibits a mean rank IC of 0.296 and a long-short return of 8.66% per month. However, `analyst_rev` is recorded at the end of month t+1, contemporaneous with the target return, and therefore contains look-ahead bias that makes it unusable for point-in-time forecasting. All remaining features have rank ICs of magnitude 0.006 or less and t-statistics below 1.65; none survives conventional significance thresholds or multiple-testing adjustments. Consequently, no robust tradable signal emerges from this panel, and the optimal strategy is to refrain from trading on these characteristics.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 monthly observations (120 months × 500 stocks per month). For each stock-month, we observe ten characteristics known by the end of month t, except `analyst_rev`, which is recorded at the end of month t+1. The prediction target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. We assess signal validity using three criteria: (1) point-in-time availability at the end of month t, (2) statistical significance of the mean monthly rank IC, and (3) economic significance of an equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio. The provided signal statistics include mean rank IC, IC standard deviation, IC t-statistic, percentage of months with positive IC, long-short mean monthly return, and annualized Sharpe ratio. We also consider multiple-testing inflation across ten features.

## Results
The signal statistics are summarized below:

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | LS Monthly Return | LS Annual Sharpe | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `book_to_market` | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | -0.27 | Rejected |
| `gross_prof` | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 | Rejected |
| `log_mktcap` | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 | Rejected |
| `asset_growth` | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 | Rejected |
| `accruals` | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 | Rejected |
| `analyst_rev` | 0.2958 | 89.59 | 8.66% | 26.38 | Rejected (look-ahead) |
| `idio_vol` | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 | Rejected |
| `leverage` | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | -0.05 | Rejected |
| `turnover` | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 | Rejected |
| `rd_intensity` | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 | Rejected |

`analyst_rev` stands out with an implausibly high IC t-statistic (89.59) and a long-short Sharpe of 26.38. Its data dictionary entry states it is recorded at the end of month t+1, the same period as `ret_next`. Using it at the end of month t to predict month t+1 returns is a clear look-ahead violation. Excluding `analyst_rev`, the strongest remaining signals are `asset_growth` (IC = 0.0061, t = 1.64) and `accruals` (IC = 0.0055, t = 1.34). Neither reaches the conventional 1.96 threshold, and with ten features tested, the expected maximum |t|-statistic under the null is approximately 1.5. Thus even these borderline signals are likely data-mined. `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` are all statistically indistinguishable from zero.

## Discussion
The central finding is negative: apart from a look-ahead-contaminated variable, this panel provides no reliable monthly stock-selection signal. The `analyst_rev` result is a textbook example of leakage—analyst revisions during month t+1 are mechanically correlated with contemporaneous returns, but an investor at the end of month t cannot observe them. If real-time analyst revisions measured during month t were available, they might be valuable; the provided variable does not support that use. The remaining characteristics show tiny long-short spreads (0.01%–0.29% per month) that would likely be erased by transaction costs, bid-ask spreads, and market impact. A disciplined quantitative process should not deploy capital based on these features. The optimal strategy from this analysis is no-trade (or, equivalently, holding cash or a passive market position with no active tilt).

## Limitations
- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 stocks per month, limiting statistical power and generalizability.
- The universe consists of index constituents, which may not represent the broader equity market.
- No transaction costs, borrow costs, or capacity constraints are modeled.
- The analysis relies on summary statistics rather than raw data, preventing robustness checks such as subsample stability or factor neutralization.
- Multiple testing across ten features raises the risk of false discoveries.
- `analyst_rev` is contaminated by look-ahead bias, so its strong predictive performance is not actionable.
- Other potentially useful signals (e.g., momentum, quality interactions, alternative data) are not included.
