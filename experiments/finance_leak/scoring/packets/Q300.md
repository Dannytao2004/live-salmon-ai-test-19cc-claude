# Predicting Next-Month Equity Returns: Only Earnings Surprise Survives in a 120-Month Index Panel

## Abstract
We study 10 stock characteristics in a monthly panel of 500 broad-index constituents over 120 months, evaluating each as a cross-sectional predictor of next-month returns. Using monthly Spearman rank ICs, t-statistics, and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find that only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) is a robust signal: mean rank IC = 0.0998 (t = 23.25), positive in 99% of months, and a top-minus-bottom quintile return of 3.04% per month with annualized Sharpe 7.31. All other features have insignificant rank ICs and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.75. A monthly SUE long-short strategy has an estimated arithmetic annual return of about 36.5% before costs. The results are strong but likely optimistic given in-sample estimation, no trading costs, and index-constituent biases.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations for 1,213 distinct stocks, with 500 index constituents per month from month 1 to 120. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. The candidate features are `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `sue`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

We evaluate each feature using monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank ICs between the feature and `ret_next`. The reported t-statistic is the average monthly IC divided by its monthly standard deviation, multiplied by the square root of the number of months. We also examine equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month. Annual returns are approximated by monthly long-short mean returns multiplied by 12 before costs. A feature is treated as a valid signal only if its mean rank IC is statistically reliable and economically meaningful after considering multiple testing across 10 features.

## Results
Only `sue` is a valid predictor. Its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0998 with a t-statistic of 23.25, and the IC is positive in 99% of months. The SUE long-short portfolio earns 3.04% per month, or about 36.48% annualized arithmetically, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. This is far stronger than any other feature.

The remaining features are weak and statistically indistinguishable from zero at conventional levels. `asset_growth` has mean rank IC 0.0061 (t = 1.64) and long-short return 0.28% per month, but it is not significant after multiple-testing adjustment and its positive sign is inconsistent with the standard investment-factor prior. `accruals` has mean rank IC 0.0055 (t = 1.34) and long-short return 0.29% per month, also insignificant and opposite to the usual accrual-anomaly direction. `gross_prof` has mean rank IC 0.0039 (t = 0.93). `idio_vol` has mean rank IC 0.0043 (t = 1.14). `book_to_market` has a negative mean IC of -0.0030 (t = -0.72). `leverage` has mean IC -0.0043 (t = -1.00). `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` have rank ICs essentially equal to zero, with t-statistics of 0.02, 0.02, and -0.05, respectively.

A practical strategy based on the evidence is a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio: long the top SUE quintile and short the bottom SUE quintile. Based on the provided long-short statistics, expected annual return is approximately 0.3648, or 36.48%, before costs, with expected annualized Sharpe of 7.31.

## Discussion
The results indicate that earnings surprise is the dominant cross-sectional return signal in this panel. SUE’s strength is consistent with post-earnings-announcement drift: prices underreact to earnings news, and positive surprises continue to outperform negative surprises over the next month. The high t-statistic, high fraction of positive IC months, and large long-short spread all support SUE as a genuine signal in this sample.

By contrast, the other characteristics fail to show reliable predictive power. This may reflect the large-cap, index-constituent universe, where many classic anomalies are attenuated by arbitrage, liquidity, and institutional ownership. It may also reflect the relatively short 120-month sample and multiple-testing noise. `asset_growth` and `accruals` are the only other features with even modest t-statistics, but neither crosses conventional significance thresholds, and both have signs that conflict with well-known priors. Therefore, we reject them as standalone signals.

The SUE strategy’s estimated performance is unusually strong and should be interpreted cautiously. The long-short return is gross of transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact. Earnings-surprise strategies can require high turnover around announcements, and shorts may be difficult or expensive in some names. Even so, the evidence strongly favors SUE over the other features.

## Limitations
- The analysis is in-sample and does not include a true out-of-sample holdout period.
- Transaction costs, borrow costs, slippage, and market impact are ignored.
- The universe is limited to broad-index constituents, so results may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks.
- The equal-weighted long-short portfolio assumes shortability and does not address capacity or liquidity constraints.
- Multiple testing across 10 features can inflate apparent significance; only SUE survives a conservative interpretation.
- SUE timing may be sensitive to exact earnings-announcement and consensus-forecast availability assumptions.
- Extreme returns and outliers are not winsorized, which may affect monthly IC estimates.
