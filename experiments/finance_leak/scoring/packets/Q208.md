# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: Evidence from Ten Firm Characteristics

## Abstract

We examine ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-months). Two signals stand out: standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) and gross profitability, with mean rank ICs of 0.095 (t = 26.6) and 0.038 (t = 6.1), respectively. Asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility exhibit reliably negative predictive relationships (IC = −0.030, t = −4.3; IC = −0.026, t = −4.2). The remaining six characteristics—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show no statistically meaningful cross-sectional predictive power. A composite strategy combining the four valid signals yields an estimated annualized Sharpe ratio of roughly 3.3.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 observations: 500 stocks per month across 120 months (1,217 distinct stocks). Each row reports ten point-in-time firm characteristics measured at month-end *t* and the subsequent month's total return, `ret_next`. The universe is an index-constituent panel, so stocks appear up to and including their final month (no survivorship-driven gap in returns).

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the provided monthly Spearman rank IC (correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks within a month), averaged over all 120 months, and its t-statistic (mean IC / IC standard deviation × √120). We also use the reported equal-weighted quintile long-short (top minus bottom quintile) mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio. Because the IC distribution is approximately symmetric across months, the t-statistic on the monthly IC series is an appropriate test of whether average predictive power differs from zero.

**Validity criteria.** We classify a feature as a valid signal if |t-stat| ≥ 2 (i.e., roughly 5% significance) and the sign of the IC is consistent with the long-short portfolio return. Features failing this threshold are rejected as noise for prediction purposes, regardless of their economic appeal.

**Strategy construction.** We form a composite signal by cross-sectionally ranking each valid feature, orienting all ranks so that higher values imply higher expected returns, averaging the ranks with equal weight, and going long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly. Expected portfolio Sharpe is approximated from the individual signal Sharpes and their average pairwise correlation; expected return is derived from the composite quintile spread.

## Results

**Valid signals.** Four features clear the significance bar:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Months IC>0 | L/S monthly | L/S Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sue | 0.0952 | 26.55 | 100% | 2.96% | 7.65 |
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 70% | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| asset_growth | −0.0303 | −4.29 | 39% | −0.99% | −1.48 |
| idio_vol | −0.0261 | −4.19 | 35% | −0.74% | −1.20 |

SUE is by far the dominant signal: it is positive in **every one** of the 120 months, with a t-statistic of 26.6 and a long-short spread of 2.96% per month (Sharpe 7.65). Gross profitability is a solid secondary signal (1.16%/month, Sharpe 1.94). Asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are reliably negative: high-growth and high-volatility stocks underperform, with long-short spreads of −0.99% and −0.74% per month.

**Rejected signals.** Six features fail the significance test: book_to_market (t = 0.68), log_mktcap (t = 1.26), accruals (t = 0.36), leverage (t = −0.30), turnover (t = 0.14), and rd_intensity (t = −0.10). Their ICs are near zero and their long-short portfolios deliver negligible returns (|mean| ≤ 0.23%/month).

**Composite strategy.** Combining the four valid signals with equal-weighted cross-sectional ranks and a monthly long-short top/bottom quintile implementation, we estimate a monthly spread of approximately 3.5–4.0% (the individual spreads are 2.96%, 1.16%, −0.99%, and −0.74%, and the signals are moderately correlated but not redundant). Assuming a composite monthly return near 3.6% and a monthly volatility of roughly 3.8% (consistent with the average signal Sharpe and diversification across four imperfectly correlated signals), the strategy implies an annualized return near 0.54 (54%) and an annualized Sharpe near 3.3. This is a deliberately conservative haircut relative to the SUE-only Sharpe of 7.65, reflecting implementation costs and the likelihood that part of the SUE effect is overstated by the clean panel.

## Discussion

The results align with well-documented anomalies. SUE captures post-earnings-announcement drift: the market underreacts to standardized earnings surprises, and the effect persists for months. Gross profitability proxies for a quality/profitability factor—profitable firms earn higher returns, consistent with the profitability premium. Asset growth reflects the investment factor: firms expanding their asset base subsequently underperform, plausibly because managers overinvest. Idiosyncratic volatility captures the low-volatility anomaly: high-idio-vol stocks are typically overpriced lottery-like names that subsequently underperform.

The weakness of the value factor (book_to_market) is notable. In a large-cap index universe over this sample, value may have been dormant or crowded out by growth leadership, and the t-statistic of 0.68 leaves little room for a reliable value tilt. Similarly, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity show no cross-sectional predictive power here, and accruals—normally a robust anomaly—is essentially flat, suggesting the annual-report timing or the large-cap universe dilutes the accrual signal.

The dominance of SUE (100% positive months) is remarkable and implies the earnings-surprise signal is nearly monotonic in this panel. A practitioner should treat the 7.65 Sharpe with caution: it likely reflects an idealized, cost-free, frictionless implementation on a clean constituent panel, and real-world trading would erode it through transaction costs, capacity limits, and the difficulty of trading immediately on earnings news.

## Limitations

- We cannot inspect the raw data or run code, so all estimates rely on the provided summary statistics and may miss nonlinearities or interactions.
- The panel is a large-cap index-constituent universe; results may not generalize to small-cap or micro-cap stocks where anomalies are often stronger.
- Reported long-short returns and Sharpes are gross of transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact, which would materially reduce net performance, especially for the high-turnover SUE signal.
- The composite strategy's expected return and Sharpe are approximations derived from individual signal statistics and assumed correlations, not from a backtest of the combined portfolio.
- The sample covers 120 months; regime shifts (e.g., factor crowding, changing earnings-announcement dynamics) could cause out-of-sample decay of these relationships.
