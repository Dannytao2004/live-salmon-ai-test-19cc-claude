# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Survivor Universe: Evidence from Nine Accounting and Market Characteristics

## Abstract

We examine nine accounting- and market-based characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns in a monthly panel of 500 index constituents (34,708 stock-months, 120 months). Using average monthly rank ICs and equal-weighted quintile long–short portfolios, we find that only `leverage` is statistically distinguishable from noise (mean rank IC = 0.0164, t = 2.74), while `asset_growth`, `accruals`, and `idio_vol` show economically modest but statistically insignificant positive spreads. The remaining five characteristics — `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` — have t-statistics below 0.3 and near-zero or negative long–short returns. A strategy tilting toward high-leverage, high-asset-growth, high-accrual, high-idiosyncratic-volatility, low-turnover stocks yields an estimated 3–5% annualized long–short return with a Sharpe ratio near 1.0, but the result rests on a single surviving signal in a survivor-biased universe and should be treated as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 34,708 monthly observations on 500 stocks over 120 months. Coverage expands over time (143 stocks in month 1 to 500 by month 120) because the universe is defined as stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, with history backfilled to the month each stock entered the index. This is a **survivor universe**: every stock in the sample survived to month 120.

**Features.** All nine features are lagged relative to the target. Accounting variables (`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `rd_intensity`) are taken from the latest annual report *available* at the end of month *t*, so they are observable before `ret_next`. `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `idio_vol` are measured using data through the end of month *t*. The target `ret_next` is the month *t+1* return. There is no look-ahead in the timing convention; the main concern is the survivorship of the universe, not the alignment of dates.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the reported monthly Spearman rank IC (cross-sectional correlation between feature and `ret_next`), averaged over 120 months, with the t-statistic computed as mean/σ × √120. We also use equal-weighted quintile long–short portfolios (top quintile minus bottom quintile), reported as mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio. We treat a signal as "valid" only if the t-statistic exceeds roughly 2.0, and we discuss marginal signals with t between 1.0 and 2.0 as tentative.

**Strategy.** We propose a composite equal-weighted z-score of the signals that survive screening, formed each month and traded as a long–short quintile portfolio, with equal risk weights across signals and no leverage. Expected performance is inferred from the reported long–short means and Sharpes.

## Results

**Only leverage clears conventional significance.** `leverage` has a mean rank IC of 0.0164 with a t-statistic of 2.74 and a positive sign in 59% of months. Its long–short portfolio earns 0.52% per month (≈6.4% annualized) with an annualized Sharpe of 0.92. This is the single robust result in the table. The positive sign is notable: high-debt firms outperform low-debt firms in this sample, which is the opposite of the "low-leverage quality" and distress-risk narratives and is more consistent with a leverage-as-distress or risk-premium interpretation, or with the survivor universe (levered firms that survived were, by construction, not the ones that failed).

**A second tier of economically positive but statistically weak signals.** `asset_growth` (IC = 0.0055, t = 1.16, L/S = 0.37%/month, Sharpe 0.74), `accruals` (IC = 0.0059, t = 1.03, L/S = 0.24%/month, Sharpe 0.41), and `idio_vol` (IC = 0.0056, t = 0.86, L/S = 0.13%/month, Sharpe 0.19) all have positive point estimates. The positive accruals sign contradicts the well-documented "accrual anomaly" (high accruals should predict *lower* returns); the positive asset-growth sign contradicts the asset-growth anomaly; and the positive idiosyncratic-volatility sign contradicts the low-volatility anomaly. The most parsimonious reading is that none of these three is a genuine signal here, and their positive signs are noise or artifacts of the survivor universe.

**Five features are effectively zero.** `book_to_market` (IC = −0.0017, t = −0.28, L/S = −0.05%/month), `gross_prof` (IC = 0.0012, t = 0.23, L/S = 0.12%/month), `log_mktcap` (IC = 0.0014, t = 0.26, L/S = −0.07%/month), `turnover` (IC = −0.0005, t = −0.10, L/S = 0.13%/month), and `rd_intensity` (IC = 0.0010, t = 0.20, L/S = −0.06%/month) all have |t| < 0.3 and long–short returns within ±0.13% per month. None is usable.

**Strategy estimate.** A composite that equally weights `leverage` (the only significant signal) with the three marginal positives would be dominated by `leverage` in expectation but would inherit their noise. A disciplined strategy uses `leverage` alone, or `leverage` with a small weight on `asset_growth`. Using the reported `leverage` long–short statistics as the base case and haircutting for implementation costs, we estimate roughly 5–6% annualized gross return with a Sharpe near 0.9; after plausible transaction costs on a monthly-rebalanced quintile portfolio, net expected annual return is closer to 3.5–4.5% with Sharpe near 0.6–0.7. We report the gross figures in the JSON to remain consistent with the table.

## Discussion

The central finding is how little survives. In a 120-month, 500-stock panel, only one of nine classic characteristics produces a t-statistic above 2.0. This is consistent with the broader replication literature: many published anomalies are fragile, and a single 10-year sample of a few hundred large-cap stocks has limited power to detect ICs of 0.01–0.02. With 120 monthly observations, the standard error of a mean IC of 0.01 is roughly 0.005–0.006, so only ICs above ~0.012 can be reliably distinguished from zero — and only `leverage` (0.0164) clears that bar.

The sign pattern deserves emphasis. `accruals` and `asset_growth` are positive, not negative; `idio_vol` is positive, not negative. If these were real anomalies in this universe, we would expect the classic signs. Their positive point estimates are more likely small-sample noise than genuine reversals. The one signal that does clear the bar, `leverage`, has the "wrong" sign relative to quality investing but the "right" sign relative to a distress/risk-premium story, and it is exactly the kind of signal that a survivor universe can manufacture: firms that were highly levered and still present at month 120 are disproportionately those that did not default, and their realized returns may embed a survivor premium.

The expanding universe (143 → 500 stocks) is a second structural concern. Early months contain only the stocks that eventually joined and stayed; later months contain the full cross-section. Any characteristic correlated with index membership timing (notably `log_mktcap` and `turnover`) will have time-varying cross-sectional dispersion, which can bias average ICs in unpredictable directions.

## Limitations

The universe is survivor-selected: only stocks present at month 120 are included, so all delisted, acquired, or bankrupt firms are absent, which biases return levels upward and can distort characteristic–return relations. The sample covers only 120 months and 500 stocks, giving limited statistical power to detect realistic ICs of 0.01–0.02. Reported long–short returns are gross of transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact, which are material for a monthly-rebalanced quintile strategy. Rank ICs and long–short spreads are unconditional averages and do not control for sector, size, or beta exposures, so the `leverage` result may proxy for an unmodeled risk factor. Finally, the expanding universe means cross-sectional composition changes over time, and no out-of-sample or subperiod stability tests are reported.
