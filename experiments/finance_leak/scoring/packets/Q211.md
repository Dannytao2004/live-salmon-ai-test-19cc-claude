# Report Q211

# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Survivor Universe of 500 Large-Cap Stocks: Evidence from Nine Accounting and Trading Characteristics

---

## Abstract

We examine nine firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index-constituent stocks (34,708 stock-months). Only one characteristic, `leverage`, exhibits a statistically reliable rank information coefficient (mean monthly rank IC = 0.0164, t = 2.74, positive in 59% of months) and a correspondingly positive equal-weighted long-short spread (0.52% per month, annualized Sharpe 0.92). The remaining eight characteristics produce rank ICs that are economically negligible (|IC| ≤ 0.006) and statistically indistinguishable from zero (|t| ≤ 1.16), consistent with a large-cap survivor universe in which the classic anomalies have been arbitraged away. We propose a long-only tilt toward high-leverage names financed by underweighting low-leverage names, which we estimate would have delivered roughly 6.2% annualized excess return at a Sharpe ratio near 0.9 before costs. We caution that the leverage effect is fragile: it is the best of nine correlated tests, survives no multiple-testing correction at conventional thresholds, and is plausibly a distressed-risk premium rather than a free lunch.

---

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains one row per stock per month for 120 months and 500 distinct stocks (34,708 rows). The universe is defined as the set of stocks that are index constituents at the *end* of month 120, with history backfilled to month 1 or to their index-inclusion date. This is a survivor universe: stocks that were delisted, acquired, or removed from the index before month 120 are absent. Stocks per month grow monotonically from 143 (month 1) to 500 (month 120), so early-sample cross-sections are roughly one-third the size of late-sample ones.

**Features.** All nine features are lagged and observable at the end of month *t*: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, and `rd_intensity` are computed from the latest annual report available at the end of month *t*; `idio_vol` uses the 60 trading days ending at month-end *t*; `turnover` averages the three months ending at month-end *t*. The target `ret_next` is the total return over month *t+1*. Because annual-report items are used only once "available," the accounting features embed a reporting lag of roughly 3–12 months, which is the correct implementation but also means these features are stale by construction.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we take the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month, then average across the 120 months. The t-statistic is the mean IC divided by the time-series standard deviation of the ICs, scaled by √120. We additionally report equal-weighted quintile long-short spreads (top quintile minus bottom quintile) and their annualized Sharpe ratios, as supplied in the signal statistics table.

**Validity criteria.** We treat a feature as a valid signal only if it clears three joint hurdles: (i) |mean rank IC| ≥ 0.01, which is the rough economic floor for a characteristic to survive realistic trading costs at monthly rebalancing; (ii) |t-stat| ≥ 2.0; and (iii) sign consistency, i.e. the long-short spread must agree in sign with the rank IC. Features failing any hurdle are rejected. We deliberately do not apply a formal multiple-testing correction to the *selection* of signals (see Limitations), but we do note where a result would fail one.

**Strategy construction.** For the surviving signal we form an equal-weighted long-short portfolio: buy the top quintile of `leverage`, short the bottom quintile, rebalance monthly. Expected annual return is 12 × the mean monthly long-short return; expected Sharpe is the reported annualized long-short Sharpe. We report these gross of transaction costs and note the cost drag separately.

## Results

**Only leverage survives.** The signal statistics table is reproduced in summary form below.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | t-stat | Months IC>0 | L/S monthly | L/S ann. Sharpe | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| leverage | **0.0164** | **2.74** | 59% | **+0.52%** | **0.92** | Valid |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | 54% | +0.24% | 0.41 | Rejected |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | 50% | +0.13% | 0.19 | Rejected |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | 52% | +0.37% | 0.74 | Rejected |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | 56% | −0.07% | −0.14 | Rejected |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | 50% | +0.12% | 0.22 | Rejected |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | 52% | −0.06% | −0.11 | Rejected |
| book_to_market | −0.0017 | −0.28 | 51% | −0.05% | −0.08 | Rejected |
| turnover | −0.0005 | −0.10 | 52% | +0.13% | 0.23 | Rejected |

`leverage` is the sole feature with a mean IC above 0.01 and a t-statistic above 2. Its IC is positive, meaning higher-debt firms subsequently outperform lower-debt firms, and this is corroborated by a positive long-short spread of 0.52% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 0.92. The effect is present in 59% of months, the highest hit rate in the table.

**The rejected features.** The eight rejected characteristics fall into three groups. First, several are directionally "right" but far too weak to trade: `accruals` (+0.0059, t = 1.03) and `asset_growth` (+0.0055, t = 1.16) point in the *opposite* direction to the classic anomaly literature (which predicts low accruals and low asset growth outperform), and neither is significant. Second, `idio_vol` (+0.0056, t = 0.86) is positive, contradicting the low-volatility anomaly, and insignificant. Third, the value, quality, size, and turnover features — `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, `rd_intensity` — have mean ICs between −0.0017 and +0.0014, effectively zero, with |t| ≤ 0.28 and long-short Sharpes between −0.14 and +0.23. Their long-short returns are within a few basis points of zero, well inside any plausible transaction-cost band.

**Strategy performance.** A monthly-rebalanced, equal-weighted long-short portfolio on `leverage` (long top quintile, short bottom quintile) would have earned 0.52% per month gross, or approximately **6.2% annualized**, with an annualized Sharpe of **0.92**. Because the strategy trades only when a stock crosses a quintile boundary, and leverage is a slow-moving annual-report variable, turnover should be modest — perhaps 20–40% of the portfolio per month — implying a cost drag on the order of 1–2% annualized at 10–20 bps round-trip, leaving a net Sharpe in the 0.6–0.8 range.

## Discussion

The headline result is a null result with one exception. In a universe of 500 large-cap index constituents, eight of nine well-known cross-sectional predictors have no measurable next-month predictive power. This is not surprising. The universe is (a) large-cap, where anomalies are weakest; (b) a survivor set, which mechanically removes the distressed and delisted firms that drive many anomaly long-short spreads; and (c) evaluated on a single 10-year window, which is short enough that a true IC of 0.02 would only be detected about half the time.

The `leverage` result deserves scrutiny rather than celebration. Its positive sign is the opposite of the standard "low-leverage quality" story and instead resembles a **distress or financial-risk premium**: highly levered firms are riskier, and in this sample they earned higher subsequent returns, consistent with a leverage-constrained or betting-against-beta type explanation. The economic magnitude — 52 bps per month, Sharpe 0.92 — is plausible for a risk-based premium but large enough to be suspicious. Two caveats temper it. First, with nine features tested, a t-statistic of 2.74 corresponds to a multiple-testing-adjusted p-value of roughly 0.06–0.10, so it does not clear a strict Bonferroni bar. Second, the survivor universe is exactly the setting where a leverage premium can be overstated: firms that were highly levered *and* survived to month 120 are, by selection, the ones that did not blow up, which biases the measured leverage-return relation upward relative to a point-in-time universe.

The other features' failures are informative in aggregate. The near-zero ICs on `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, and `log_mktcap` suggest that within large-cap survivors, value, quality, and size have been fully arbitraged. The weak, wrong-signed results for `accruals` and `asset_growth` are consistent with the investment and accrual anomalies being concentrated in small, illiquid, and financially distressed names that this panel excludes.

---

## Limitations

1. **Survivor bias.** The universe is the 500 index members at month 120; delisted and removed firms are absent, which inflates returns generally and the leverage premium specifically, since levered firms that failed are excluded.
2. **Single short sample.** 120 months gives limited power; a true IC of 0.02 would be detected only about half the time, so several "rejected" features may be false negatives.
3. **Multiple testing.** Nine features were screened; the leverage t-statistic of 2.74 would not survive a Bonferroni correction at the 5% level, so the one "valid" signal may be a selection artifact.
4. **No transaction costs or capacity analysis.** Reported long-short returns and Sharpes are gross; net-of-cost performance, especially for a leverage-sorted portfolio that may load on illiquid or distressed names, is unmeasured.
5. **Stale accounting inputs.** Annual-report features carry a reporting lag of up to a year, so their measured ICs understate what a more timely specification might achieve — but also mean the features are not truly point-in-time clean.
6. **No risk adjustment.** Long-short spreads are raw returns; the leverage premium may be compensation for market, distress, or beta exposure rather than alpha.
7. **No out-of-sample test.** All statistics are in-sample over the same 120 months, so the strategy's expected performance is an in-sample estimate with no holdout validation.
