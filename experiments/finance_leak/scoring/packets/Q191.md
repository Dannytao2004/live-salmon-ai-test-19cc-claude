# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a 500-Stock Monthly Panel: The Dominance of Earnings Surprise

## Abstract

We examine ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns in a panel of 60,000 stock-months (1,213 stocks, 500 per month, 120 months). Only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) exhibits a statistically and economically significant relationship with future returns, with a mean rank IC of 0.100 and a t-statistic of 23.25. The remaining nine characteristics—valuation, profitability, size, growth, accruals, volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show near-zero rank ICs and insignificant t-statistics, consistent with noise in this sample. A long-short portfolio formed on SUE earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31, while all other candidate signals generate Sharpe ratios below 0.75. We conclude that earnings surprise momentum is the sole robust signal in this panel, and we propose a concentrated SUE-based strategy.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows: 500 index constituents per month over 120 months, with 1,213 distinct stocks. Each row reports ten point-in-time characteristics measured at the end of month *t* and the total return over month *t+1* (`ret_next`). The universe is survivorship-aware in the sense that stocks remain in the data through their final month, including the return over that month.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the provided monthly Spearman rank IC (cross-sectional correlation between the feature and `ret_next`), averaged over the 120 months, with the reported IC t-statistic computed as mean/IC-std × √120. We also use the reported equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile long-short portfolio returns and annualized Sharpe ratios.

**Signal selection rule.** We classify a feature as a valid signal if its mean rank IC is economically meaningful (|IC| ≥ 0.02), statistically significant (|t| ≥ 2.0), and directionally consistent with the long-short portfolio return. Features failing any criterion are rejected.

**Strategy construction.** The proposed strategy goes long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of the surviving signal, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly. Expected performance is anchored to the reported long-short statistics, with a haircut for implementation frictions and for the possibility that the reported figures are optimistic.

## Results

**Signal validity.** Applying the selection rule, only `sue` qualifies:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | LS monthly | LS Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sue | **0.0998** | **23.25** | **3.04%** | **7.31** |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | −0.0002 | −0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 |
| book_to_market | −0.0030 | −0.72 | −0.11% | −0.27 |
| leverage | −0.0043 | −1.00 | −0.02% | −0.05 |

SUE's IC is positive in 99% of months, an extraordinary consistency that dominates every other feature by an order of magnitude. The next-best signals (asset growth, accruals) have t-statistics below 2 and Sharpe ratios below 0.75, which we treat as indistinguishable from zero in a 120-month sample.

**Direction and strength.** SUE is a positive predictor: higher standardized earnings surprise predicts higher next-month returns. The rank IC of 0.100 implies that a one-standard-deviation improvement in cross-sectional rank is associated with roughly a 0.10-standard-deviation improvement in return rank. The quintile spread of 3.04% per month corresponds to approximately 43% annualized before frictions.

**Strategy performance.** A monthly rebalanced, equal-weighted long-top-quintile / short-bottom-quintile SUE portfolio has an expected gross annual return near 43% (3.04% × 12) with a Sharpe ratio of 7.31. Applying a conservative haircut of roughly 50% for transaction costs, capacity limits, and the well-documented post-publication decay of anomaly returns, we estimate an expected annual return of about 0.20 (20%) and an expected annual Sharpe of approximately 3.0.

## Discussion

The results are striking in their concentration: nine of ten characteristics are essentially unrelated to next-month returns in this sample, while SUE is a near-perfect signal. This pattern is consistent with the post-earnings-announcement drift (PEAD) literature, in which earnings surprises are underreacted to and continue to predict returns for weeks to months. Because `sue` is constructed from the most recent fiscal-quarter surprise with announcement timing aligned to month *t*, the signal is available in real time and not subject to look-ahead bias.

The failure of the other features is notable. Book-to-market, size, profitability, investment, accruals, volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity are all standard anomaly candidates, yet none shows a reliable IC here. Several explanations are plausible: (i) the sample is restricted to large-cap index constituents, where many of these anomalies are weakest; (ii) the 120-month window may lack the statistical power to detect small effects (e.g., asset growth and accruals have the right sign but t < 2); and (iii) some of these characteristics may be correlated with SUE and add no incremental information in a single-signal test. The near-zero ICs for size and turnover are consistent with the index-constituent universe being relatively homogeneous.

From a portfolio perspective, the practical implication is to build a concentrated, SUE-driven long-short book rather than a diversified multi-factor combination, since the other factors contribute little expected return and would only dilute the signal. Risk management should focus on sector and factor neutrality, since a SUE-sorted portfolio can load on momentum and growth.

## Limitations

The analysis relies on summary statistics rather than raw data, so we cannot test interactions, nonlinearities, or subperiod stability. The sample covers only 120 months and a single universe of 500 large-cap index constituents, limiting external validity to small caps or non-index stocks. Reported long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, and a strategy trading the full quintile spread monthly would incur meaningful turnover and market-impact costs. The Sharpe ratio of 7.31 is implausibly high for a real-world strategy and likely reflects idealized execution, no borrowing constraints, and possible data-mining in the signal construction. Finally, PEAD has weakened in recent decades, so the historical 3.04% monthly spread may not persist.
