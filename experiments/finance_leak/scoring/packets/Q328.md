# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Monthly Equity Panel: Valid Signals and a Multi-Factor Long-Short Strategy

## Abstract
This paper evaluates ten stock-level characteristics for predicting next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Using monthly rank ICs, t-statistics, and quintile long-short returns, I identify four valid signals: SUE (positive), gross profitability (positive), asset growth (negative), and idiosyncratic volatility (negative). The remaining six characteristics—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—are statistically unreliable in this sample. A composite long-short strategy that overweights SUE and tilts toward high gross profitability, low asset growth, and low idiosyncratic volatility is expected to earn about 26.5% annualized gross return with an annualized Sharpe near 6.0 under moderate correlation assumptions.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 monthly observations for 1,217 stocks, with 500 stocks per month from month 1 to 120. Each row includes ten firm characteristics measured at the end of month \(t\) and the stock’s total return over month \(t+1\) (`ret_next`). I evaluate each feature using:
- **Rank IC**: Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, averaged across 120 months.
- **IC t-statistic**: mean rank IC divided by its time-series standard deviation, scaled by \(\sqrt{120}\).
- **Long-short portfolio**: equal-weighted top-quintile minus bottom-quintile return each month, with annualized Sharpe ratio.

I classify a feature as a valid signal if it has a stable, economically meaningful rank IC with \(|t| > 2\) and a long-short return consistent with the IC sign. Features with weak ICs and insignificant t-statistics are rejected.

## Results
The valid signals are summarized below. Directions are stated in terms of the feature’s predictive relationship with `ret_next`.

| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SUE | Positive | 0.0952 | 26.55 | 2.96% | 7.65 |
| Gross profitability | Positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| Asset growth | Negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| Idiosyncratic volatility | Negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

SUE is by far the strongest signal: its rank IC is positive in 100% of months, and its long-short portfolio earns 2.96% per month on average. Gross profitability also has a robust positive IC and positive long-short return. Asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility have negative ICs: high asset growth and high idiosyncratic volatility predict lower future returns, so the profitable tilts are low asset growth and low idiosyncratic volatility.

The rejected features are book-to-market (IC 0.0027, t=0.68), log market cap (IC 0.0055, t=1.26), accruals (IC 0.0015, t=0.36), leverage (IC -0.0011, t=-0.30), turnover (IC 0.0006, t=0.14), and R&D intensity (IC -0.0004, t=-0.10). None have reliable predictive power in this panel.

For the strategy, I form an IC-weighted composite of the four valid signals. Each month, I standardize the features cross-sectionally, orient them so higher scores are better (high SUE, high gross profitability, low asset growth, low idiosyncratic volatility), and weight them by absolute rank IC: SUE ~50%, gross profitability ~20%, asset growth ~16%, idiosyncratic volatility ~14%. I buy the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted within each leg, and rebalance monthly. Using the individual long-short means, the composite gross return is about 1.98% per month, or 23.8% annualized arithmetic and 26.5% compounded. Assuming a conservative 0.2 average correlation among signal returns, the annualized Sharpe is approximately 6.0; under zero correlation it would be closer to 7.4.

## Discussion
The four valid signals align with well-known return predictors. SUE captures post-earnings-announcement drift: firms with positive earnings surprises continue to outperform. Gross profitability is a quality signal: profitable firms tend to generate higher future returns. Asset growth reflects the investment anomaly: firms that expand assets aggressively often underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility captures the low-volatility anomaly: high-volatility stocks tend to have lower risk-adjusted returns. The rejected signals are either too noisy or too weakly related to returns in this sample; notably, book-to-market and accruals, which are often significant in other periods, do not show reliable predictive power here. The extremely strong SUE result should be interpreted with caution because its 100% positive-month rate and very high t-statistic may reflect a particularly clean signal, a synthetic data-generating process, or potential timing advantages in earnings information.

## Limitations
- Transaction costs, shorting costs, and slippage are ignored; net returns would be lower.
- Signal correlations and common factor exposures are unknown; the expected Sharpe assumes moderate correlation and may be optimistic.
- SUE’s extreme strength may reflect look-ahead or earnings-announcement timing issues that should be verified point-in-time.
- The sample covers only 500 index constituents per month over 120 months, limiting external validity.
- Equal-weighted quintile portfolios ignore capacity, liquidity, and shorting constraints.
