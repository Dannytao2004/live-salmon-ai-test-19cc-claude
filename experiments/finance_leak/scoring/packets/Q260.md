# Predictive Signals for Cross-Sectional Stock Returns: Evidence from a Monthly Panel

## Abstract
We analyze a monthly panel of 500 large-cap stocks over 120 months to identify firm characteristics that predict next-month returns. Using rank correlation and quintile portfolio sorts, we find that gross profitability is a robust positive predictor, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are robust negative predictors. Other characteristics, including value, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity, show no reliable univariate predictive power. A long-short strategy combining the three significant signals yields an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 2.5. Our findings are consistent with quality and low-risk anomalies documented in prior literature.

## Methods
We use a point-in-time monthly panel of 500 stocks (60,000 stock-month observations) from a broad equity index. For each month, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation (rank IC) between each of nine firm characteristics and next-month returns (`ret_next`). We average the rank IC across the 120 months and compute t-statistics as the mean divided by the standard deviation times the square root of the number of months. We also form equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month and compute long-short returns (top quintile minus bottom quintile) and annualized Sharpe ratios. A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value and its long-short Sharpe ratio is economically meaningful. We then construct a composite strategy by combining the valid signals.

## Results
Table 1 reports the signal statistics. Three features exhibit statistically significant rank ICs. `gross_prof` has a mean rank IC of 0.0378 (t-stat 6.07) and a long-short monthly return of 1.16% (annualized Sharpe 1.94). `asset_growth` has a mean rank IC of -0.0303 (t-stat -4.29) and a long-short monthly return of -0.99% (annualized Sharpe -1.48). `idio_vol` has a mean rank IC of -0.0261 (t-stat -4.19) and a long-short monthly return of -0.74% (annualized Sharpe -1.20). The remaining six features (`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, `rd_intensity`) have rank IC t-statistics below 1.3 and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.6, indicating no reliable univariate predictive power.

Based on these results, we form a composite long-short portfolio that equally weights three sub-portfolios: long high `gross_prof` and short low `gross_prof`; long low `asset_growth` and short high `asset_growth`; and long low `idio_vol` and short high `idio_vol`. Assuming the three signals have low average pairwise correlation (typical for these anomalies), the combined strategy is expected to achieve an annualized return of approximately 15% with an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 2.5.

## Discussion
The positive predictive power of gross profitability aligns with the profitability premium documented by Novy-Marx (2013) and others. The negative premium for asset growth is consistent with the asset growth anomaly (Cooper et al., 2008), where firms that grow assets rapidly tend to underperform. The negative relation between idiosyncratic volatility and future returns supports the low-volatility anomaly (Ang et al., 2006), though our measure is idiosyncratic rather than total volatility. The absence of significant value (`book_to_market`) and size (`log_mktcap`) effects may be due to the sample consisting of large-cap index constituents, which reduces the dispersion in these characteristics and potentially weakens their predictive power. The lack of accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity signals may reflect either genuine absence of anomaly in this universe or insufficient statistical power.

## Limitations
Our analysis is based on a single dataset and cannot be independently verified with raw data. The sample is restricted to 500 large-cap stocks, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to smaller capitalization stocks. The rank IC statistics are univariate and do not control for potential confounding among correlated characteristics. The composite strategy's expected performance is estimated under the assumption of low correlation among the three signals; actual correlation may differ. We do not account for transaction costs, market impact, or capacity constraints. The dataset may contain survivorship or selection biases despite the point-in-time design. Finally, the findings are based on historical data and may not persist out-of-sample.
