# Title: No Valid Point-in-Time Signals in a Monthly Stock Panel: Evidence from Rank IC and Long-Short Portfolios

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns using a panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Only `analyst_rev` exhibits a strong Spearman rank IC (0.296) and long-short return (8.66% monthly), but it is recorded at the end of month t+1, making it unavailable at portfolio formation and therefore invalid for real-time prediction. All other features have rank ICs below 0.01 and t-statistics under 2, failing to reject the null of no predictive power. We conclude that no valid tradable signals exist in this dataset, and a passive or no-trade strategy is appropriate.

## Methods
We analyze a monthly panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months, with 10 firm characteristics and a next-month return target (`ret_next`). For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank IC with `ret_next` each month, average over 120 months, and calculate the t-statistic as mean IC divided by IC standard deviation times the square root of 120. We also consider equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature. A feature is deemed a valid signal only if it is both point-in-time (available at the end of month t) and statistically significant. `analyst_rev` is explicitly recorded at the end of month t+1, the same period as the target return, so it fails the point-in-time requirement.

## Results
The signal statistics are summarized below. `analyst_rev` shows an extreme mean rank IC of 0.2958, t-stat 89.59, 100% of months with positive IC, long-short mean monthly return 8.66%, and annualized Sharpe 26.38. However, this is a look-ahead artifact. Among the remaining features, `asset_growth` has the highest t-stat (1.64) with IC 0.0061 and long-short return 0.28% per month (Sharpe 0.72), followed by `accruals` (IC 0.0055, t=1.34, return 0.29%, Sharpe 0.70). `idio_vol` (IC 0.0043, t=1.14), `gross_prof` (IC 0.0039, t=0.93), `leverage` (IC -0.0043, t=-1.00), `book_to_market` (IC -0.0030, t=-0.72), `log_mktcap` (IC 0.0001, t=0.02), `turnover` (IC 0.0001, t=0.02), and `rd_intensity` (IC -0.0002, t=-0.05) are all statistically indistinguishable from zero. None of these point-in-time features provide reliable predictive power.

## Discussion
The only strong statistical relationship is `analyst_rev`, but its construction uses information from month t+1 to predict returns over month t+1. This is a classic look-ahead bias and cannot be exploited in a real-time strategy. The remaining features are properly lagged but show negligible economic and statistical significance. Some signs (e.g., positive `asset_growth` and `accruals`) contradict well-documented anomalies, suggesting the observed ICs are noise. Given 10 features and 120 months, multiple testing further weakens any marginal evidence. Therefore, we reject all features as valid signals and recommend a no-trade or passive strategy.

## Limitations
- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 stocks per month, limiting statistical power.
- All portfolios are equal-weighted, ignoring market-cap weighting or transaction costs.
- The analysis uses rank IC and quintile spreads but does not control for sector, size, or other risk factors.
- `analyst_rev` is contemporaneous with the target, but no lagged version is provided for a proper predictive test.
- Data quality and potential errors in annual report variables cannot be verified from summary statistics alone.
