# Title
**SUE Is the Signal: Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Monthly Equity Panel**

## Abstract
I evaluate ten stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns in a panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 120 months and roughly 500 index constituents per month. Only standardized unexpected earnings (`sue`) has robust predictive power: its average monthly rank IC is 0.0998, its IC t-statistic is 23.25, and its equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. The other nine characteristics have rank ICs near zero and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.75, and none survive reasonable multiple-testing thresholds. I propose a monthly long-short strategy buying high-SUE stocks and shorting low-SUE stocks, with an estimated gross annual return of 36.5% and gross annual Sharpe of 7.31. These results are strong but likely overstated net of costs, capacity constraints, and implementation frictions.

## Methods
The dataset is a monthly stock panel with one row per stock per month. Each month contains 500 stocks that were constituents of a broad equity index in that month. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month \(t+1\). I evaluate ten features: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `sue`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

For each feature and month, I compute the Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks. The monthly rank ICs are averaged over 120 months. The reported t-statistic is \( \text{mean IC} / \text{std IC} \times \sqrt{120} \). I also consider equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month. A feature is treated as a valid signal only if it has both statistical significance after accounting for multiple testing and economically meaningful long-short performance. With ten features, a Bonferroni-style threshold requires t-statistics near or above 2.8 for conventional significance; only `sue` clearly exceeds this.

## Results
`SUE` is the only feature with strong and consistent predictive power. Its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0998, its IC standard deviation is 0.0470, and its t-statistic is 23.25. The IC is positive in 99% of months. The equal-weighted long-short portfolio earns 3.04% per month, or 36.48% annualized using simple monthly aggregation, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. This is an exceptionally strong result and dominates all other features by a wide margin.

The remaining features show weak or inconsistent evidence:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | -0.27 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | -0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 |

`asset_growth` and `accruals` are the least weak among the rejected features, but their t-statistics are only 1.64 and 1.34, below standard significance thresholds and well below multiple-testing-adjusted thresholds. Moreover, their positive signs are opposite to the classical asset-growth and accruals anomalies, suggesting they are likely noise in this sample. `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` have near-zero average rank ICs and economically negligible long-short returns.

## Discussion
The dominance of `sue` is consistent with the earnings momentum and post-earnings-announcement-drift literatures. Standardized unexpected earnings capture firm-specific fundamental news that the market appears to underreact to at the monthly horizon. The signal is not only statistically strong but also broadly consistent: it is positive in 99% of months and produces a large equal-weighted long-short spread.

The failure of the other characteristics may reflect several factors. First, the universe is composed of index constituents, which are relatively large and liquid; many classical anomalies are weaker in large-cap universes. Second, the sample is only 120 months, so weak signals may not have enough power. Third, several features may be correlated with risk exposures or have been arbitraged away in this particular index-constituent sample. Finally, some weak positive signs for `asset_growth` and `accruals` contradict well-known anomaly directions, reinforcing the view that they are not reliable signals here.

I propose a simple implementable strategy: each month, rank stocks by `sue`, buy the top quintile equal-weighted, and short the bottom quintile equal-weighted. The strategy is rebalanced monthly. Based on the historical panel, the estimated gross annual return is 36.48% and the estimated gross annual Sharpe ratio is 7.31. These figures should be interpreted as gross, pre-cost, and pre-capacity estimates.

## Limitations
- The sample covers only 120 months and about 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to small-cap, non-index, or international stocks.
- The long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, bid-ask spreads, market impact, and short-borrow fees.
- The strategy assumes frictionless monthly rebalancing and no short-sale constraints or crowding effects.
- The analysis does not control for factor exposures, risk models, or interactions among the ten characteristics.
- Multiple-testing concerns remain, although `sue` has an exceptionally large t-statistic that is unlikely to be a chance result.
- The reported Sharpe ratio of 7.31 is likely overstated and not achievable at scale.
- `SUE` depends on consensus forecasts and announcement timing, and the data do not allow a full audit of potential look-ahead or revision effects.
