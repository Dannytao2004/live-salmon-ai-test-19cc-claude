# Evaluating Ten Cross-Sectional Equity Signals: SUE Dominates in a 120-Month Index Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using a 60,000-row monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Signals are assessed by average monthly Spearman rank IC, IC t-statistics, and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio returns. Only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) is a robust valid signal, with mean rank IC 0.0998 (t = 23.25), positive in 99% of months, and a top-minus-bottom quintile return of 3.04% per month (annualized Sharpe 7.31). The other nine features have weak, statistically insignificant, or economically negligible predictive power. A simple SUE long-short strategy is the only allocation supported by this sample.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations, covering 1,213 distinct stocks and 120 months with 500 index constituents per month. The point-in-time universe includes stocks until removal, so delisting returns are retained and survivorship bias is reduced. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1.

For each feature, I use the provided monthly Spearman rank IC with `ret_next`, averaged across all 120 months. The t-statistic is computed as mean IC divided by IC standard deviation, multiplied by the square root of 120. I also use the equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature. A feature is deemed a valid signal if it has a two-sided t-statistic above 1.96 in absolute value, a consistent economic direction, and a meaningful long-short return. The proposed strategy uses SUE only: long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly.

## Results
The signal statistics are summarized below.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

SUE is the only signal that clears the significance threshold. Its mean rank IC of 0.0998 is large for a monthly cross-sectional signal, and its IC is positive in 99% of months. The long-short portfolio earns 3.04% per month, or 36.48% annualized by multiplying the monthly mean by 12, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. Asset growth and accruals are the next strongest, but their t-statistics are below 1.96 and their long-short Sharpe ratios are below 1.0. All other features are statistically indistinguishable from zero.

## Discussion
The results point to earnings momentum as the dominant effect in this panel. SUE captures the market’s delayed response to earnings surprises, commonly interpreted as post-earnings-announcement drift. The extreme strength of SUE—99% positive IC months and a Sharpe above 7—suggests either a particularly strong anomaly in this index universe and sample period, or implementation assumptions that are not captured in the summary statistics. The remaining characteristics may be arbitraged away, attenuated in large-cap index constituents, or too noisy to detect over 120 months. The point-in-time universe and inclusion of delisting returns are strengths, but the SUE result should be treated with caution because the data dictionary notes that earnings are announced in the month after the fiscal quarter ends; proper lagging is essential to avoid look-ahead bias.

## Limitations
The analysis relies on summary statistics rather than raw data, so I cannot verify feature construction, outlier treatment, or exact lagging. The backtest estimates ignore transaction costs, borrow fees, market impact, and capacity constraints. Equal-weighted quintile portfolios may load on small or illiquid stocks, and shorting constraints could limit implementability. The 120-month sample is moderate and may be period-specific. SUE results could be overstated if earnings information is not strictly available at the end of month t. No multi-factor risk adjustment or out-of-sample validation was performed.
