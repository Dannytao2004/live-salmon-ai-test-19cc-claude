# Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a Point-in-Time Index Panel: Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We examine ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a point-in-time panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Three signals are statistically reliable: gross profitability has a positive premium, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility have negative premia. Analyst consensus revision has the strongest raw rank IC, but it is recorded at the end of the return month and therefore cannot be used in real time. A monthly long-short quintile strategy combining the three valid signals has an expected gross annual return of approximately 12% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 2.4.

## Methods
The dataset is a monthly panel of 500 index constituents per month for 120 months, with ten candidate features and next-month stock returns. For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank IC with next-month returns each month, average it over the 120 months, and form a t-statistic as the mean divided by the standard deviation divided by the square root of the number of months. We treat a feature as a valid signal if its rank IC is statistically significant with |t| > 2. We also examine equal-weighted top-minus-bottom quintile long-short portfolios to assess economic magnitude. We require predictors to be available at the portfolio formation time at the end of month t.

## Results
Three features pass the statistical threshold. Gross profitability has a positive mean rank IC of 0.0378 (t = 6.07), with a top-minus-bottom quintile return of 1.16% per month and an annualized Sharpe of 1.94. Asset growth has a negative mean rank IC of -0.0303 (t = -4.29), with a top-minus-bottom quintile return of -0.99% per month; reversing the portfolio would have a positive annualized Sharpe of 1.48. Idiosyncratic volatility has a negative mean rank IC of -0.0261 (t = -4.19), with a top-minus-bottom quintile return of -0.74% per month and a reversed-portfolio annualized Sharpe of 1.20.

Analyst revision has an extremely high mean rank IC of 0.2963 (t = 80.29), but it is recorded at the end of month t+1 and is contemporaneous with the return. It is not a valid point-in-time predictor. The remaining features—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—have rank ICs near zero and t-statistics below 1.3 in absolute value, so they are rejected.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with several known cross-sectional anomalies: profitable firms tend to outperform unprofitable firms, high asset growth predicts lower future returns, and high idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower future returns. A practical strategy is to combine these signals into a single composite score each month: rank stocks on gross profitability ascending, asset growth descending, and idiosyncratic volatility descending, then average the percentile ranks. Buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile equal-weighted would capture all three premia. Based on the individual long-short returns, an equal-weighted composite has an expected gross return near 0.96% per month, or about 11.6% annualized, with diversification across the three signals supporting an annualized Sharpe ratio around 2.4 before transaction costs.

## Limitations
The performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, short-sale constraints, and price impact. The sample is limited to 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks. Composite strategy performance is inferred from summary statistics rather than estimated on an independent out-of-sample period. No formal multiple-testing correction is applied, although the three accepted signals have absolute t-statistics above 4.
