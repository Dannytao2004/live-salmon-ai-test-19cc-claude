# Predicting Cross-Sectional Equity Returns in an Index Universe: Evidence from a 120-Month Panel

## Abstract

We examine ten accounting- and market-based characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns in a panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months (60,000 stock-month observations). Only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) exhibits an economically and statistically significant relationship with future returns, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.0998 (t-stat = 23.25) and a long-short quintile spread of 3.04% per month (annualized Sharpe 7.31). All other candidate signals—book-to-market, gross profitability, size, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—produce rank ICs of 0.006 or smaller in absolute value and are statistically indistinguishable from zero. We conclude that the panel's cross-sectional return predictability is dominated by the earnings-surprise signal, and we propose a long-short strategy concentrated in SUE. A caveat is that the SUE result is unusually strong relative to the published literature, suggesting either a highly efficient earnings-announcement channel in this universe or a data-construction artifact.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows: 500 index constituents per month for 120 months, with each stock present until index removal. Features are point-in-time: annual-report variables (book_to_market, gross_prof, asset_growth, accruals, leverage, rd_intensity) are taken from the latest available annual report at month-end *t*; SUE uses the most recent fiscal quarter ending on or before month-end *t*, with announcements occurring in the following month; idio_vol uses the trailing 60 trading days; turnover uses the trailing 3 months. The target, ret_next, is the total return from month-end *t* to month-end *t+1*.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we computed the cross-sectional Spearman rank IC each month, then averaged across the 120 months. Significance is assessed via the IC t-statistic (mean IC / IC standard deviation × √120). We also report an equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio that buys the top quintile and shorts the bottom quintile of each feature, with its mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

**Signal availability check.** All features are lagged to be observable at month-end *t* before ret_next is realized, so there is no look-ahead in the timing convention as described. This matters most for SUE, which is the only feature whose information arrives close to the trading month; its documented announcement lag (one month after fiscal quarter end) is consistent with the panel's construction.

## Results

The signal statistics table separates the features cleanly into one strong signal and nine weak ones.

**SUE dominates.** SUE has a mean rank IC of 0.0998 with an IC standard deviation of 0.0470, giving a t-statistic of 23.25. The IC is positive in 99% of the 120 months—an extraordinarily consistent sign. The long-short quintile portfolio earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. This is the only feature that would survive any reasonable multiple-testing adjustment.

**Everything else is noise.** The remaining nine features have mean rank ICs between −0.0043 (leverage) and +0.0061 (asset_growth), with |t-stat| ≤ 1.64. None reaches conventional significance. The best of the rest, asset_growth, has a positive IC (0.0061, t = 1.64) and a long-short return of 0.28% per month (Sharpe 0.72), but this is marginal. Accruals shows a positive IC (0.0055, t = 1.34) and long-short return of 0.29% per month (Sharpe 0.70)—the sign is opposite to the classic accruals anomaly, further suggesting these are weak or unstable effects rather than robust anomalies. Book-to-market is slightly negative (IC −0.0030, long-short −0.11%/month), leverage slightly negative, and log_mktcap, turnover, and rd_intensity essentially zero.

**Correlation structure.** We cannot compute cross-feature correlations from the summary, but the near-zero ICs for most features imply that a multi-factor combination would be dominated by SUE; adding the other signals would mostly add noise and turnover.

**Strategy.** Given the results, the natural strategy is a SUE-based long-short portfolio: each month, rank the 500 constituents by SUE, buy the top quintile (≈100 stocks) equal-weighted, and short the bottom quintile equal-weighted. The reported long-short mean monthly return of 3.04% and annualized Sharpe of 7.31 are the relevant estimates. Translating the monthly return to annual terms: (1.0304)^12 − 1 ≈ 43.2% gross annual return before costs. The Sharpe of 7.31 is implausibly high for a real-world strategy and should be treated as an in-sample, gross-of-cost upper bound.

## Discussion

The central finding is the overwhelming dominance of SUE. Earnings surprise is a well-documented predictor (post-earnings-announcement drift), so a positive sign is expected, but the magnitude here is far larger than typical published estimates. A rank IC near 0.10 and a Sharpe above 7 would be exceptional in a liquid large-cap index universe. Three interpretations are possible.

First, the panel may be constructed such that SUE is unusually clean and the universe unusually inefficient with respect to earnings news, producing a genuine but universe-specific effect. Second, the SUE variable may embed some forward-looking information or the announcement timing may not be fully respected, creating mild look-ahead that inflates the IC. The data dictionary states announcements occur in the month after the fiscal quarter ends, which is the correct convention, but the strength of the result warrants caution. Third, the 500-stock universe with monthly rebalancing and equal weighting may concentrate the strategy in smaller, less liquid names where the effect is strongest; the reported return is gross and does not account for the trading costs of monthly quintile turnover.

The failure of the classic value, profitability, investment, accruals, low-volatility, and liquidity signals is notable. In a large-cap index universe, these anomalies are often weak or arbitraged away, which is consistent with what we observe. The accruals sign is positive rather than negative, contradicting the standard accruals anomaly; this is more likely a weak/noisy estimate than a genuine reversal.

For practice, the results argue for a concentrated SUE strategy rather than a diversified multi-signal portfolio. Combining nine near-zero signals with one strong signal would dilute the strong signal and add turnover without expected return. A more defensible approach is to use SUE as the core alpha and treat the other features as risk controls or neutralization variables rather than return forecasts.

## Limitations

- The SUE result is anomalously strong relative to the published literature; it may reflect look-ahead, universe selection, or a data artifact rather than a tradable effect.
- All performance figures are gross of transaction costs, and a monthly equal-weighted quintile strategy in 500 stocks would incur substantial turnover costs, especially in the SUE long-short.
- The panel covers only 120 months and a single index universe; results may not generalize to other periods, markets, or cap segments.
- We cannot verify feature timing or compute cross-feature correlations from the summary statistics alone; the availability check relies on the data dictionary.
- The long-short Sharpe of 7.31 is not credible as a live trading expectation and should be interpreted as an in-sample upper bound.
