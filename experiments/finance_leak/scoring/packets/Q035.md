# Cross-Sectional Predictors of Monthly Stock Returns: Evidence from a 120-Month Equity Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Using monthly rank ICs and equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios, we identify three robust tradable signals: gross profitability (positive), asset growth (negative), and idiosyncratic volatility (negative). Analyst revision has an exceptionally high rank IC, but its timing makes it contemporaneous with the target return and therefore non-tradable at month t; we reject it as a look-ahead signal. A composite long-short strategy using the three valid signals is estimated to earn roughly 10% annualized net of costs with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.8. The remaining characteristics show weak, unstable, or insignificant predictive power.

## Methods
We analyze a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 120 months and 1,217 distinct stocks. Each month contains 500 index constituents, and the data include points-in-time firm characteristics measured at the end of month t and the subsequent month’s return, `ret_next`.

We assess each feature using two main statistics:
1. **Monthly rank IC**: the Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, averaged across the 120 months.
2. **Long-short quintile portfolio**: an equal-weighted portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the feature each month.

We treat a signal as valid if it is economically plausible, point-in-time available at month t, has a rank IC t-statistic greater than 2 in absolute value, and shows a non-negligible long-short spread. `analyst_rev` is explicitly rejected on timing grounds because the data dictionary states it is recorded at the end of month t+1, while the target is the return over month t+1. Thus, it is not available at portfolio formation.

For the proposed strategy, we form a composite score:
\[
\text{Score} = z(\text{gross\_prof}) - z(\text{asset\_growth}) - z(\text{idio\_vol}),
\]
where \(z(\cdot)\) denotes a cross-sectional rank-normalized score. Each month, we buy the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weight within each leg, and rebalance monthly.

## Results
### Valid tradable signals
Three features pass the validity criteria.

| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gross_prof | Positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| asset_growth | Negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| idio_vol | Negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

`gross_prof` is the strongest tradable signal: its IC is positive in 70% of months, and its long-short portfolio earns 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94. High gross profitability predicts higher next-month returns.

`asset_growth` is a robust negative signal. Its long-short spread is -0.99% per month, meaning that buying low-asset-growth stocks and shorting high-asset-growth stocks would have earned about +0.99% per month. Its IC is positive in only 39% of months, confirming the negative direction.

`idio_vol` is also a robust negative signal. High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower next-month returns. The raw long-short spread is -0.74% per month; reversing the trade to exploit the negative signal yields about +0.74% per month.

### Rejected signals
The remaining features do not provide reliable tradable predictive power.

- `book_to_market`: rank IC 0.0027, t-stat 0.68, long-short 0.09% per month. Too weak and insignificant.
- `log_mktcap`: rank IC 0.0055, t-stat 1.26, long-short 0.23% per month. Weak and statistically insignificant.
- `accruals`: rank IC 0.0015, t-stat 0.36, long-short 0.14% per month. Essentially no signal.
- `analyst_rev`: rank IC 0.2963, t-stat 80.29, long-short 8.73% per month. However, this feature is recorded at the end of month t+1, so it uses information from the same period as the target return. It is not available at month t and is therefore a look-ahead signal, not a tradable predictor.
- `leverage`: rank IC -0.0011, t-stat -0.30, long-short 0.02% per month. No reliable signal.
- `turnover`: rank IC 0.0006, t-stat 0.14, long-short 0.02% per month. No reliable signal.
- `rd_intensity`: rank IC -0.0004, t-stat -0.10, long-short 0.13% per month. No reliable signal.

### Proposed strategy and expected performance
The composite strategy combines the three valid signals with equal weight:
- Long: high gross profitability, low asset growth, low idiosyncratic volatility.
- Short: low gross profitability, high asset growth, high idiosyncratic volatility.

Using the individual directional monthly spreads, the average monthly spread is approximately:
\[
\frac{1.16\% + 0.99\% + 0.74\%}{3} \approx 0.96\%.
\]
This implies a gross annualized return of about 12%. After plausible transaction costs and shorting frictions, a net expected annual return of roughly 10% is reasonable. Assuming moderate positive correlation among the three signals, the combined annualized Sharpe ratio is expected to be around 1.8.

## Discussion
The three valid signals align with well-documented equity anomalies. Gross profitability is a quality/profitability signal: profitable firms tend to outperform. Asset growth captures the investment anomaly: firms that expand their asset base aggressively tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility reflects the low-volatility anomaly: high-volatility stocks tend to deliver lower risk-adjusted returns than low-volatility stocks.

The analyst revision result is instructive. Its rank IC of 0.2963 and monthly long-short return of 8.73% are implausibly strong for a tradable signal. The data dictionary resolves the puzzle: `analyst_rev` is recorded at the end of month t+1, contemporaneous with `ret_next`. Using it in a month-t portfolio would require future information. If lagged by one month, its predictive power would likely decline substantially, and it should not be included in a real-time strategy without careful point-in-time alignment.

The other features are either economically weak in this sample or estimated imprecisely. Value (`book_to_market`), size (`log_mktcap`), accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity do not show robust next-month predictive power. This does not mean they are universally irrelevant, but in this panel they do not meet the bar for inclusion.

## Limitations
- The analysis relies on summary statistics and cannot inspect raw data, non-linearities, outliers, or feature correlations.
- `analyst_rev` is contaminated by look-ahead timing and is rejected, but a properly lagged version could not be tested with the available information.
- The strategy’s expected Sharpe assumes moderate correlations among signals; we do not observe the full covariance matrix.
- Transaction costs, borrow fees, market impact, and capacity constraints are not modeled precisely.
- The universe consists of index constituents, which may introduce reconstitution and survivorship-related biases despite the point-in-time inclusion of delisted stocks.
- The sample covers 120 months and 500 stocks per month, so results may not generalize to other periods or markets.
- Multiple testing across ten features may inflate the apparent significance of some signals.
