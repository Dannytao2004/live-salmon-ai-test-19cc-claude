# Cross-Sectional Return Signals in a Monthly Index Stock Panel

## Abstract
I evaluate ten stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns in a 120-month panel covering 500 index constituents per month. Three signals are statistically and economically valid: gross profitability is positively related to future returns, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negatively related. Analyst revisions show an extraordinary apparent rank IC, but I reject them because they are recorded at the end of month t+1 and are therefore contemporaneous with the target return rather than available at month t. A monthly equal-weighted long-short composite of the three valid signals is expected to earn about 11.6% annualized with a Sharpe ratio near 2.0 gross of costs.

## Methods
The panel contains 60,000 stock-month observations from 120 months, with 500 stocks per month and 1,217 distinct stocks. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. I assess predictive validity using monthly Spearman rank IC, its t-statistic (`mean IC / std IC × sqrt(120)`), quintile long-short portfolio returns, and annualized Sharpe ratios. A signal is treated as valid only if it is available at the end of month t, is statistically reliable, and has a plausible economic interpretation. For the composite strategy, I orient each valid signal in its predictive direction and equal-weight the three long-short legs: long high `gross_prof`, long low `asset_growth`, and long low `idio_vol`, with the opposite sides shorted.

## Results
Three features are valid predictors. `gross_prof` has a mean rank IC of 0.0378 (t = 6.07), is positive in 70% of months, and its top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns 1.16% monthly with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94. `asset_growth` has a mean rank IC of -0.0303 (t = -4.29), and its high-minus-low quintile return is -0.99% monthly, meaning low-growth stocks outperform; the oriented low-minus-high Sharpe is 1.48. `idio_vol` has a mean rank IC of -0.0261 (t = -4.19), and its high-minus-low quintile return is -0.74% monthly, meaning low-volatility stocks outperform; the oriented low-minus-high Sharpe is 1.20.

The other non-timing-contaminated features are not reliable in this sample. `book_to_market` has IC 0.0027 (t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` 0.0055 (t = 1.26), `accruals` 0.0015 (t = 0.36), `leverage` -0.0011 (t = -0.30), `turnover` 0.0006 (t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` -0.0004 (t = -0.10). None reaches conventional significance.

`analyst_rev` is a special case. Its mean rank IC is 0.2963 (t = 80.29), it is positive in 100% of months, and its long-short portfolio earns 8.73% monthly with an annualized Sharpe of 24.23. However, the data dictionary states that it is the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, recorded at the end of month t+1. It therefore uses information from the same month as `ret_next` and is not available at the end of month t. I reject it as a tradable signal because it embeds look-ahead bias.

For the valid-signal composite, the equal-weighted oriented monthly return is approximately `(1.16% + 0.99% + 0.74%) / 3 = 0.96%`. This corresponds to roughly 11.6% simple annualized return, or about 12.2% compounded, before transaction costs. Allowing for factor correlation and implementation frictions, I use a conservative expected annualized Sharpe of about 2.0.

## Discussion
The results support three classic cross-sectional effects in this index-constituent universe: profitability, investment, and low-volatility. High gross profitability may proxy for durable competitive advantage or cash-generation quality. High asset growth is often associated with overinvestment and subsequent underperformance. High idiosyncratic volatility may reflect lottery-like demand, limits to arbitrage, or distress risk. Combining them equal-weighted improves diversification relative to any single signal and produces a plausible long-short strategy.

The most important practical finding is negative: `analyst_rev` looks spectacular in the summary table but is not usable as a predictive signal in the stated form. Its timing makes it a contemporaneous variable, not a forecast variable. This illustrates why signal validation must begin with point-in-time availability, not just with IC magnitude. The rejected accounting and market features are either too weak in this sample or already reflected in prices for large-cap index constituents.

## Limitations
The analysis relies on summary statistics rather than the raw panel, so I cannot estimate correlation among signals, portfolio turnover, or transaction costs directly. The composite Sharpe estimate is therefore approximate and likely optimistic before costs. The universe is limited to index constituents, which may exclude smaller or less liquid stocks where some anomalies are stronger. The sample covers 120 months, which is sufficient for many tests but still subject to period-specific effects. Multiple testing may inflate apparent significance, although only three non-timing-contaminated signals survive conservative t-stat thresholds. All reported returns are gross and do not account for borrow costs, market impact, or implementation delay.
