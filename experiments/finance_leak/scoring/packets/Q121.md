# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Stock Panel

## Abstract
We examine a monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months to identify characteristics that predict next-month stock returns. After enforcing point-in-time availability, three signals are valid: gross profitability (positive), asset growth (negative), and idiosyncratic volatility (negative). The standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) signal is rejected because its construction uses fiscal-quarter data that may not be announced until the following month, inducing look-ahead bias. An equal-weighted composite long-short strategy based on the three valid signals is estimated to earn roughly 12% annually with a Sharpe ratio near 2.0.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations (500 stocks per month, 120 months). The target is `ret_next`, the total return in month t+1. We evaluate ten features: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `sue`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`. For each feature, we compute the monthly Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` (rank IC), its t-statistic, and the equal-weighted long-short quintile return. A signal is deemed valid if it is both statistically significant (|t| > 2) and available without look-ahead bias. Point-in-time checks: annual-report variables (`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `rd_intensity`) are available at the end of month t; `idio_vol` and `turnover` use trailing market data. `sue` is problematic because firms announce quarterly earnings in the month after the fiscal quarter ends, so the most recent quarter ending in month t is not yet public at month-end t. The composite strategy standardizes each valid signal cross-sectionally, applies its sign (`gross_prof` +, `asset_growth` −, `idio_vol` −), averages them into a score, and goes long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile with equal weights.

## Results
Three signals pass the validity screen. `gross_prof` has a mean rank IC of 0.0378 (t = 6.07), positive in 70% of months, with a long-short return of 1.16% per month and annualized Sharpe of 1.94. `asset_growth` has a mean rank IC of −0.0303 (t = −4.29), with a long-short return of −0.99% per month (i.e., shorting high-growth firms is profitable) and annualized Sharpe of −1.48. `idio_vol` has a mean rank IC of −0.0261 (t = −4.19), with a long-short return of −0.74% per month and annualized Sharpe of −1.20. The remaining signals are not statistically reliable: `book_to_market` (IC 0.0027, t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` (IC 0.0055, t = 1.26), `accruals` (IC 0.0015, t = 0.36), `leverage` (IC −0.0011, t = −0.30), `turnover` (IC 0.0006, t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` (IC −0.0004, t = −0.10). Although `sue` shows an extreme rank IC of 0.0952 (t = 26.55) and a long-short Sharpe of 7.65, we reject it due to look-ahead bias: its value at month t can incorporate earnings for a quarter ending in month t that is not announced until t+1. An equal-weighted composite of the three valid signals has an estimated monthly return of about 0.96% (annualized ~12.2%) and, assuming moderate pairwise correlations (0.2–0.4), an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 2.0.

## Discussion
The valid signals align with well-documented anomalies: the profitability premium (high gross profits outperform), the asset growth anomaly (high asset growth underperforms), and the low-volatility anomaly (high idiosyncratic volatility underperforms). Their combination is attractive because they capture distinct economic mechanisms—quality, investment, and risk—and therefore offer diversification. The rejection of `sue` is critical: its spectacular raw performance is almost certainly an artifact of using earnings information before it is public. A point-in-time version of SUE, using only quarters announced by month-end t, would likely have lower but potentially still useful predictive power. The composite strategy is simple and transparent, but it is not optimized; alternative weighting schemes or volatility scaling could improve risk-adjusted returns.

## Limitations
- Transaction costs, market impact, and shorting constraints are not modeled.
- The composite uses ad hoc equal weights rather than an optimized combination.
- The sample period may not represent future market regimes or structural changes.
- The true point-in-time predictive power of SUE is unknown because the provided signal is contaminated.
- Correlations among the valid signals are assumed, not estimated from the data.
