# Lookahead-Aware Evaluation of Cross-Sectional Return Predictors

## Abstract
We evaluate ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a point-in-time panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. After removing one variable with lookahead bias, none of the remaining nine signals reaches the conventional statistical significance threshold of |t| ≥ 2.0. The lookahead-biased variable, `analyst_rev`, has an extremely high rank IC of 0.296 and a t-statistic of 89.6 because it is measured over the same month as the target return. We conclude that the dataset contains no robust implementable cross-sectional predictor and recommend no active long-short strategy based on these signals.

## Methods
The dataset is a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months, with point-in-time fundamentals and next-month total returns. For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` each month, average it over all 120 months, and compute a t-statistic as `mean / std × sqrt(120)`. We also report equal-weighted long-short returns that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature.

A feature is considered a valid trading signal only if:
1. It is known at the end of month t, i.e., it is point-in-time with respect to the target return.
2. Its average rank IC is statistically significant at the 5% level, approximately |t| ≥ 2.0.
3. Its economic magnitude is not negligible after accounting for the noisiness of the IC.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `analyst_rev` | 0.2958 | 89.59 | 8.66% | Reject: lookahead |
| `asset_growth` | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | Not significant |
| `accruals` | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | Not significant |
| `idio_vol` | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | Not significant |
| `gross_prof` | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | Not significant |
| `log_mktcap` | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | Not significant |
| `turnover` | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | Not significant |
| `rd_intensity` | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | Not significant |
| `book_to_market` | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | Not significant |
| `leverage` | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | Not significant |

The only statistically powerful variable is `analyst_rev`. However, according to the data dictionary, it is recorded at the end of month t+1 and represents forecast revisions during month t+1. This timing makes it contemporaneous with, not predictive of, `ret_next`. Using it as a predictor would introduce severe lookahead bias, so it is rejected as an implementable signal.

Among the remaining point-in-time variables, `asset_growth` and `accruals` have the largest t-statistics, but neither reaches the 5% significance threshold. Their economic magnitudes are also modest, with annualized long-short Sharpe ratios of 0.72 and 0.70, respectively.

## Discussion
The evidence suggests that the standard accounting, size, volatility, turnover, and profitability characteristics in this panel do not contain robust cross-sectional predictive power for next-month returns. The weak positive ICs of `asset_growth` and `accruals` may be consistent with some prior literature, but they are not strong enough in this sample to support a standalone trading strategy.

The striking performance of `analyst_rev` illustrates how easy it is to overstate a signal when the feature is measured contemporaneously with the target. A properly lagged analyst revision variable—one known at the end of month t—would be required to test whether analyst revisions actually predict future returns.

Given the rejection of all point-in-time predictors at the pre-specified significance threshold, the most prudent strategy is to avoid trading these signals actively. No positive expected active return can be justified from this analysis.

## Limitations
- The sample covers only 120 months, which limits statistical power to detect weak signals.
- Transaction costs, short-selling costs, and market impact are not considered.
- The universe is a 500-stock broad index, which may not generalize to the full cross-section of equities.
- No multiple-testing adjustment is applied across the ten features.
- The `analyst_rev` variable is rejected only because of its contemporaneous timing; a properly lagged version was not available.
