# Earnings Surprise Dominates: A Monthly Cross-Sectional Analysis of Ten Equity Characteristics

## Abstract
We examine a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 1,213 stocks and ten common equity characteristics. Using cross-sectional rank information coefficients and equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios, only standardized unexpected earnings (`sue`) emerges as a robust predictor of next-month returns. Its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0998 with a t-statistic of 23.25, positive in 99% of months, and its top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. The remaining nine features have economically small and statistically insignificant rank ICs. A simple long-high-SUE / short-low-SUE strategy is therefore the only justified signal in this panel, though its gross performance is likely optimistic after trading costs and implementation frictions.

## Methods
The dataset contains one row per stock per month for 120 months, with 500 stocks per month and 1,213 distinct stocks. Features are point-in-time accounting, market, and trading characteristics: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `sue`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`. The target is `ret_next`, the stock’s total return over month t+1.

I evaluate each feature using two standard cross-sectional tests. First, I compute the monthly Spearman rank IC between the feature and `ret_next`, then average across the 120 months. The reported t-statistic is the mean IC divided by the IC standard deviation, multiplied by sqrt(120). Second, I form an equal-weighted long-short portfolio each month that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the feature, and I annualize the Sharpe ratio using the reported monthly long-short return and volatility. A feature is treated as a valid standalone signal if it has a stable sign, a mean rank IC that is economically meaningful, and |t-stat| > 2.

## Results
Only `sue` passes the validity threshold. Its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0998, its IC t-statistic is 23.25, and its IC is positive in 99% of months. The equal-weighted top-minus-bottom SUE quintile portfolio earns 3.04% per month, or roughly 36.5% simple annualized before costs, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. The direction is positive: higher earnings surprise predicts higher next-month returns.

All other features fail to produce reliable standalone signals. `asset_growth` has the next-highest t-statistic at 1.64, followed by `accruals` at 1.34 and `idio_vol` at 1.14, but none reaches conventional significance. `gross_prof` is positive but weak (t = 0.93). `leverage` is weakly negative (t = -1.00), and `book_to_market` is weakly negative (t = -0.72). `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` have rank ICs essentially indistinguishable from zero. Several signs also contradict well-known anomalies—for example, `asset_growth` and `accruals` are positive here rather than negative—which further suggests these estimates are noise or sample-specific. Thus, no feature besides `sue` justifies inclusion as a tradable signal in this dataset.

## Discussion
The dominance of `sue` is consistent with the post-earnings-announcement drift literature: stocks with positive earnings surprises tend to continue outperforming, while negative-surprise stocks underperform. The estimated long-short spread is large and unusually stable in this panel, implying a high gross Sharpe ratio. A practical strategy would rank all stocks by `sue` each month, buy the top quintile, short the bottom quintile, equal-weight within each leg, and rebalance monthly. The expected gross annual return is approximately 36.5% simple annualized, with an annualized Sharpe near 7.31. However, this estimate should be interpreted as an in-sample, pre-cost upper bound; real-world implementation would face transaction costs, shorting constraints, borrow fees, capacity limits, and potential crowding.

The other characteristics may still have value in combination with other signals or after sector/risk neutralization, but the evidence here does not support them as standalone predictors. Their weak and sometimes theoretically inconsistent signs suggest the 120-month sample, the large-cap index universe, or noisy accounting timeliness may obscure weaker effects. For this panel, the disciplined conclusion is to reject them as independent signals.

## Limitations
This analysis is based only on summary statistics and cannot verify data quality, outliers, or look-ahead timing at the individual observation level. The reported performance is in-sample and gross of transaction costs, slippage, shorting borrow costs, and market impact. The long-short portfolio is equal-weighted and does not control for sector, size, beta, or other risk exposures. The sample covers only 120 months and a large-cap index universe, limiting generalization to smaller stocks or other periods. `sue` may be affected by timing conventions around earnings announcements, consensus forecasts, and restatements. The extreme Sharpe ratio likely reflects in-sample overfitting or unmodeled frictions. Capacity and crowding risks are not assessed.
