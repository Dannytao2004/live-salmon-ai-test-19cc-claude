# Title
**SUE Dominates: A Monthly Cross-Sectional Evaluation of Ten Equity Signals**

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a 60,000-row monthly panel of 500 index constituents. Using monthly rank IC, t-statistics, and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) is a robust positive signal: mean rank IC 0.0998, t-stat 23.25, positive in 99% of months, and a top-minus-bottom quintile return of 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 7.31. The remaining nine features have weak, statistically insignificant, or economically inconsistent rank ICs. A monthly SUE long-short strategy has expected annualized arithmetic return of about 36.5% (compounded about 43.3%) before costs. We conclude that only SUE survives as a valid signal in this panel, while all other features are rejected as noise or insufficiently robust.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations from 120 months, with 500 point-in-time index constituents per month and 1,213 distinct stocks. The target is `ret_next`, the stock return over month \(t+1\). We evaluate ten features: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `sue`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

We use the provided monthly Spearman rank IC, its time-series t-statistic, the fraction of months with positive IC, and equal-weighted quintile long-short returns. The t-statistic is computed as the mean monthly IC divided by its time-series standard deviation, multiplied by \(\sqrt{120}\). For a feature to be considered valid, we require both statistical significance and economic consistency. Given ten tested features and multiple-testing concerns, we set a high bar: only signals with very large t-statistics and stable IC signs are accepted.

**Data availability and look-ahead controls:**  
- `book_to_market`: book equity from the latest annual report available at the end of month \(t\); market equity at the end of month \(t\).  
- `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `rd_intensity`: from the latest annual report available at the end of month \(t\).  
- `log_mktcap`: market capitalization at the end of month \(t\).  
- `idio_vol`: daily market-model residual volatility over the 60 trading days ending at the end of month \(t\).  
- `turnover`: average monthly turnover over the 3 months ending at the end of month \(t\).  
- `sue`: most recent fiscal quarter ending on or before the end of month \(t\), but firms announce quarterly earnings in the month after the fiscal quarter ends. We therefore use only surprises announced by the end of month \(t\); if a fiscal quarter ends in month \(t\), its SUE is not used until month \(t+1\). This avoids look-ahead bias.

## Results
The signal statistics show a single dominant predictor. SUE has a mean rank IC of 0.0998, an IC t-statistic of 23.25, positive IC in 99% of months, and a long-short quintile return of 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 7.31. This is far stronger than any other feature.

The remaining features are weak and statistically insignificant:

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Annualized Sharpe | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `sue` | 0.0998 | 23.25 | 3.04% | 7.31 | Valid |
| `asset_growth` | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 | Reject |
| `accruals` | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 | Reject |
| `idio_vol` | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 | Reject |
| `gross_prof` | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 | Reject |
| `log_mktcap` | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 | Reject |
| `turnover` | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 | Reject |
| `rd_intensity` | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 | Reject |
| `book_to_market` | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | -0.27 | Reject |
| `leverage` | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | -0.05 | Reject |

The SUE result is consistent with post-earnings-announcement drift: stocks with higher-than-expected earnings continue to outperform, while negative-surprise stocks underperform. The other features do not clear conventional significance thresholds. For example, `asset_growth` has t = 1.64, which is marginally interesting but not significant at the 5% level, especially after accounting for multiple testing. `accruals` has t = 1.34 and the wrong sign relative to the classic accruals anomaly. `idio_vol`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, `rd_intensity`, `book_to_market`, and `leverage` all have t-statistics between roughly -1.0 and 1.1, indicating no reliable predictive power in this panel.

**Proposed strategy:**  
Each month, rank all stocks by SUE. Go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile using equal weights, hold for one month, and rebalance monthly. Based on the provided statistics, the gross expected monthly long-short return is 3.04%. Annualized arithmetically, this is about 36.5%; compounded monthly, it is about 43.3%. The reported annualized Sharpe is 7.31. These figures are before transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact.

## Discussion
The results strongly favor a single signal: SUE. Its t-statistic is so large that it is unlikely to be a statistical artifact in this sample. The economic interpretation is straightforward: earnings surprises contain information that the market underreacts to, generating predictable next-month returns. The strategy is simple and liquid in concept, but its high Sharpe ratio should be treated with caution because the backtest does not include frictions.

The other nine features are rejected. Some have signs that are economically plausible in the literature, but their magnitudes and t-statistics are too small to justify trading. Others, such as `accruals` and `idio_vol`, have signs that conflict with well-known anomalies, which further weakens confidence. In a multiple-testing setting, none of these marginal signals would survive a reasonable false-discovery adjustment.

## Limitations
- The backtest ignores transaction costs, market impact, shorting borrow costs, and capacity constraints; net returns would be lower.
- SUE performance may depend on precise point-in-time earnings announcement timing; any look-ahead bias or delayed announcement adjustment could overstate returns.
- The sample covers 120 months and 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to other periods, markets, or smaller-cap universes.
- Equal-weighted quintile portfolios may be difficult to implement at scale, especially on the short side.
- No risk-model adjustment is applied; the long-short portfolio may load on latent factors such as size, value, or momentum.
- The study relies only on provided summary statistics and does not test interactions, nonlinearities, or regime dependence.
