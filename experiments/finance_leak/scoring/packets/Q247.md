# Report Q247

# Title
Earnings Surprise Dominates Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Monthly Equity Panel

# Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using 60,000 stock-month observations from 500 index constituents per month over 120 months. Using monthly rank ICs and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) shows a robust positive relation: mean rank IC = 0.0998, t-stat = 23.25, and a top-minus-bottom quintile return of 3.04% per month. The remaining nine characteristics have economically small and statistically insignificant predictive power. A monthly long-short SUE strategy earns an estimated 36.5% gross annualized return with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31, before transaction costs. The results are consistent with post-earnings-announcement drift, but they depend critically on enforcing the earnings-announcement lag.

# Methods
We use the provided monthly stock panel. Each month contains 500 index constituents, and the target is `ret_next`, the total return over month \(t+1\). We assess predictive power using two standard cross-sectional diagnostics: the average monthly Spearman rank IC between each feature and `ret_next`, and the equal-weighted return of a long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and shorts the bottom quintile of the feature each month.

## Timing and Availability
- `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, and `rd_intensity` are based on the latest annual report available at the end of month \(t\), so they are treated as observable at \(t\).
- `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `idio_vol` are computed from market data ending at month \(t\), so they are also observable at \(t\).
- `sue` requires special care. Firms announce quarterly earnings in the month after the fiscal quarter ends. Therefore, at the end of month \(t\), the tradable SUE must correspond to a fiscal quarter that ended no later than month \(t-1\) and was announced by month \(t\). I assume the dataset applies this lag. If `sue` instead uses a quarter ending in month \(t\) before announcement, the reported SUE results would contain look-ahead bias.

We classify a signal as valid if it has a consistent economic direction, a mean rank IC that is statistically distinguishable from zero (roughly \(|t| > 1.96\)), and a long-short return that is not economically negligible. Under these criteria, only SUE qualifies.

# Results
The table below summarizes the key signal statistics.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `sue` | 0.0998 | 23.25 | 3.04% | 7.31 |
| `asset_growth` | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| `accruals` | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| `idio_vol` | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| `gross_prof` | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| `log_mktcap` | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| `turnover` | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| `rd_intensity` | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 |
| `book_to_market` | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | -0.27 |
| `leverage` | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | -0.05 |

SUE is the only feature with a strong and highly stable positive relation to next-month returns. Its IC is positive in 99% of months, and its long-short return is 3.04% per month. The next-best features, `asset_growth` and `accruals`, have positive but statistically insignificant ICs. The remaining features have near-zero or inconsistent relations.

A simple tradable strategy is to go long the top quintile of SUE and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, with monthly rebalancing. Based on the reported long-short mean monthly return of 3.04%, the gross annualized return is approximately 36.5% arithmetically, or about 43.2% compounded. The reported annualized Sharpe ratio is 7.31. These figures are gross of transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact.

# Discussion
The results are dominated by SUE, which is a standard proxy for post-earnings-announcement drift. Stocks with higher-than-expected earnings continue to outperform over the next month, while stocks with negative earnings surprises underperform. The effect is large, stable, and economically meaningful in this panel.

The weakness of the other characteristics is notable. Size (`log_mktcap`), value (`book_to_market`), profitability (`gross_prof`), investment (`asset_growth`), accruals (`accruals`), idiosyncratic volatility (`idio_vol`), leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity do not show reliable cross-sectional predictive power in this sample. Some have the expected sign directionally—for example, `asset_growth` is positive and `accruals` is positive—but their t-statistics are well below conventional significance thresholds, and their long-short returns are small.

The practical implication is that the strategy should be concentrated in the SUE signal. However, the extremely high Sharpe ratio also suggests that implementation frictions and timing assumptions are central. If the SUE signal is not strictly lagged to publicly announced earnings, the strategy’s apparent performance may be overstated.

# Limitations
- The SUE result depends on strict point-in-time availability; if earnings information is recorded before announcement, the reported performance reflects look-ahead bias.
- The backtest is gross of transaction costs, shorting costs, borrow constraints, and slippage, all of which are likely material for a monthly long-short equity strategy.
- The universe is limited to 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to microcaps, non-index stocks, or international markets.
- The strategy uses equal-weighted quintiles and monthly rebalancing; performance may be sensitive to weighting schemes, quintile cutoffs, and rebalancing frequency.
- The sample covers 120 months, and factor efficacy can vary across market regimes, liquidity conditions, and sentiment cycles.
- Only linear cross-sectional predictive relationships are examined; nonlinear interactions and conditional models are not explored.
