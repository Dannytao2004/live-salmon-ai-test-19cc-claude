# Title: No Free Lunch in a Monthly Equity Panel: A Point-in-Time Signal Evaluation

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 equities. Using rank ICs, t-statistics, and long-short quintile portfolios, we find that nine characteristics have weak and statistically insignificant predictive power. The sole exception is `analyst_rev`, which exhibits an extraordinary rank IC of 0.296 and a long-short Sharpe ratio of 26.4, but it is recorded at the end of month t+1 and therefore embeds look-ahead information. After enforcing point-in-time availability, no valid tradable signals remain. We recommend a no-trade strategy and caution against using temporally misaligned features.

## Methods
We analyze a monthly stock panel of 60,000 rows, covering 120 months with 500 stocks per month. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. We define a valid signal as one that is available at the end of month t, has a consistent sign across months, and is statistically significant in both rank IC and long-short portfolio performance (e.g., |t| > 2 and >60% of months with positive IC). We use the provided Spearman rank ICs, IC t-statistics, long-short quintile returns, and annualized Sharpe ratios. For each feature, we also consider economic plausibility and multiple-testing risk across the ten candidates.

## Results
The signal statistics are summarized below:

| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | -0.05 | 46% |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 | 51% |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 | 52% |

`analyst_rev` is the only feature with strong predictive power. However, its data dictionary entry states that it is “recorded at the end of month t+1,” while the target `ret_next` also covers month t+1. Thus, it is not available at the end of month t when the portfolio would be formed. This is a classic look-ahead bias. The remaining features are point-in-time, but none reach conventional significance. `asset_growth` and `accruals` have the highest long-short Sharpe ratios (0.72 and 0.70), but their IC t-statistics are only 1.64 and 1.34, and their signs are inconsistent across months (54% and 50% positive). After adjusting for multiple testing across nine candidate features, these results are not robust.

## Discussion
The only feature with meaningful in-sample predictive power is contaminated by future information. Using `analyst_rev` would produce an unrealistic backtest and is not implementable in practice. The other features show no reliable alpha. We therefore conclude that no valid tradable signals exist in this panel. A disciplined strategy is to avoid active trading based on these characteristics. If forced to allocate, a zero-alpha market-neutral or cash position is appropriate. This result underscores the importance of point-in-time data and multiple-testing corrections in quantitative equity research.

## Limitations
- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 stocks per month, limiting statistical power.
- We only have summary statistics, so we cannot examine nonlinearities, interactions, or transaction costs.
- No out-of-sample validation is possible with the provided information.
- The data may be synthetic or simulated, and the exact point-in-time alignment of every feature cannot be independently verified.
- Multiple testing across ten features increases the risk of false discoveries.
