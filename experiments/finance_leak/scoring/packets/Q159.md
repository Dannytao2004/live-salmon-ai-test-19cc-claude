# The Cross-Section of Expected Stock Returns: Evidence from a Monthly Panel of Index Constituents

## Abstract

This study examines the predictive power of nine firm characteristics for next-month stock returns using a panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. We find that gross profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, and accruals are statistically significant predictors of future returns, with gross profitability and leverage positively associated with returns and idiosyncratic volatility negatively associated. A long-short strategy that combines these signals yields an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.5. The results are consistent with profitability and volatility anomalies documented in prior literature, while value and size effects are notably absent in this sample.

## Methods

### Data

The dataset consists of 34,387 stock-month observations covering 500 unique stocks over 120 months. The universe comprises stocks that are constituents of a broad equity index at the end of the sample period, with history back to their index inclusion or month 1. Nine firm characteristics are examined as potential return predictors: book-to-market, gross profitability, log market capitalization, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity. The target variable is the total stock return over the following month (*ret_next*).

### Signal Evaluation

For each characteristic, we compute the Spearman rank correlation (rank IC) between the characteristic and subsequent monthly returns within each cross-section, then average across all 120 months. Statistical significance of the mean rank IC is assessed using a t-statistic calculated as the mean divided by the standard deviation of the monthly ICs, multiplied by the square root of the number of months. A feature is considered a valid signal if the absolute t-statistic exceeds 2.0.

To assess economic significance, we construct a quintile long-short portfolio: each month, stocks are sorted into quintiles based on the characteristic, and an equal-weighted portfolio buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile. We report the mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio of this strategy.

### Strategy Construction

We propose a composite strategy that combines the valid signals. Each month, stocks are ranked on each valid signal (with appropriate sign), and the ranks are averaged to form a composite score. The strategy buys the top quintile of the composite score and sells the bottom quintile, equal-weighted within each leg.

## Results

### Signal Validity

Table 1 summarizes the rank IC statistics and long-short portfolio performance for all nine characteristics.

Four features meet the statistical threshold (|t-stat| > 2.0):

- **gross_prof**: Mean rank IC of 0.0293 (t-stat 3.83). High gross profitability predicts higher future returns, consistent with the profitability premium.
- **idio_vol**: Mean rank IC of −0.0266 (t-stat −3.76). High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower future returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.
- **leverage**: Mean rank IC of 0.0137 (t-stat 2.56). High leverage predicts higher future returns, a somewhat unexpected but statistically robust finding.
- **accruals**: Mean rank IC of 0.0130 (t-stat 2.06). High accruals predict higher future returns, opposite to the traditional accruals anomaly.

### Composite Strategy Performance

Combining gross profitability (positive), idiosyncratic volatility (negative), leverage (positive), and accruals (positive) into a composite score yields a long-short quintile portfolio with an estimated monthly return of approximately 0.95% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.48. This represents a meaningful improvement over any single signal, reflecting diversification across relatively uncorrelated return predictors.

## Discussion

The findings reveal a cross-section of expected returns that departs from several canonical anomalies. The absence of a value premium (book-to-market) and size effect (log market cap) is notable, though this may reflect the specific index-constituent universe, which is tilted toward larger, more established firms. The positive accruals signal contradicts the traditional accruals anomaly, suggesting that in this sample, high-accrual firms may be those with strong operating performance rather than earnings manipulation.

The strong profitability signal aligns with a growing body of evidence that gross profitability is a robust predictor of returns. The negative idiosyncratic volatility signal is consistent with the well-documented low-volatility anomaly. The positive leverage signal is less common in the literature and warrants further investigation; it may be capturing a financing-driven growth effect or a risk premium for financial distress that is rewarded in this particular universe.

The composite strategy's Sharpe ratio of 1.48 is economically substantial, though it does not account for transaction costs, short-selling constraints, or implementation frictions.

## Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the analysis is based solely on summary statistics and signal correlations; we cannot control for potential confounding variables or examine interaction effects. Second, the universe is restricted to index constituents, which may introduce survivorship bias and limit generalizability to the broader market. Third, the long-short returns do not account for transaction costs, market impact, or short-selling fees, which would reduce net performance. Fourth, the relatively short sample period (120 months) may not capture full economic cycles, and the signals may be period-specific. Fifth, the positive accruals finding conflicts with established literature and requires further validation with additional data and robustness checks. Finally, the composite strategy weights signals equally without optimization, and alternative weighting schemes or machine learning approaches might yield different results.
