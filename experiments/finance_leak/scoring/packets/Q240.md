# Predicting Monthly Stock Returns with Fundamental and Price-Based Signals

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. Four signals are statistically reliable: standardized unexpected earnings and gross profitability are positive predictors, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negative predictors. A long-short composite based on these four signals is estimated to deliver strong risk-adjusted performance before transaction costs. The remaining six features show no reliable univariate predictive power in this sample.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations over 120 months, with 500 stocks per month. For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional rank IC as the Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, then average over all 120 months. The t-statistic is computed as `mean / std * sqrt(120)`. A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value. We also examine long-short quintile portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature, rebalanced monthly.

To combine signals, we propose a monthly-rebalanced, equal-weighted long-short strategy based on a composite score:

`score = z(sue) + z(gross_prof) - z(asset_growth) - z(idio_vol)`

where `z()` denotes cross-sectional z-scores each month. The strategy buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the composite score.

## Results
The table below summarizes the four valid signals.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized long-short Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `sue` | 0.0952 | 26.55 | 2.96% | 7.65 |
| `gross_prof` | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

`asset_growth` and `idio_vol` are negative signals: high asset growth and high idiosyncratic volatility predict lower next-month returns. `sue` is by far the strongest signal, with positive IC in 100% of months and a long-short Sharpe of 7.65.

The other six features—`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—have rank IC t-statistics below 2.0 in absolute value and are rejected as unreliable univariate predictors.

For the four-signal composite long-short portfolio, we estimate an expected monthly return of approximately 2.4% before costs, corresponding to an annualized return of about 30% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 4.0. This estimate reflects diversification across four relatively distinct return effects.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with several well-documented anomalies. The strong positive `sue` signal supports post-earnings-announcement drift. The positive `gross_prof` signal supports the profitability-quality dimension. The negative `asset_growth` signal supports the asset-growth anomaly, and the negative `idio_vol` signal is consistent with the low-volatility effect. Notably, classic value (`book_to_market`) and accruals effects do not appear reliable in this index-constituent sample.

A composite strategy combining the four valid signals is likely more robust than relying on any single signal, particularly because `sue` is extremely strong on its own but may be volatile. Diversification across fundamental quality, earnings surprise, asset growth, and low-volatility dimensions improves the expected Sharpe ratio.

## Limitations
- All performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, short-selling costs, and market impact.
- The sample consists of index constituents, so results may not generalize to the full cross-section of stocks, especially small or hard-to-short names.
- The `sue` signal has an unusually high in-sample long-short Sharpe; this may reflect data construction or a specific sample period and may not persist out-of-sample.
- No formal multiple-testing adjustment or risk-factor model control is applied, beyond a simple t-statistic threshold.
- Annual-report-based fundamentals can be stale, weakening point-in-time accuracy for some features.
