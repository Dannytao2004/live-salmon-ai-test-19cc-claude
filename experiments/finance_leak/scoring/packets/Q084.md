# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Monthly Stock Panel: Evidence from Fundamental and Volatility Signals

## Abstract
Using a monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months, I evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns. Three signals are statistically and economically valid: gross profitability (positive), asset growth (negative), and idiosyncratic volatility (negative). SUE exhibits an implausibly strong rank IC (0.095) and 100% positive months, but is rejected due to a likely look-ahead bias in its availability. A composite long-short strategy combining the three valid signals yields an expected gross annual return of approximately 11% with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.0 before transaction costs. These results are consistent with the profitability, asset-growth, and low-volatility anomalies.

## Methods
The dataset comprises 60,000 stock-month observations: 1,217 distinct stocks, with 500 stocks per month for 120 months. Each row contains ten point-in-time characteristics and the subsequent monthly return `ret_next`. I evaluate signals using the provided mean rank IC (Spearman correlation between feature and `ret_next` within each month, averaged across months), its t-statistic, and the equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio return (top quintile minus bottom quintile). A signal is considered valid if it shows both statistical significance (|t-stat| > 2) and economic significance (|mean rank IC| > 0.02 and a plausible long-short Sharpe). I also scrutinize data timing: `sue` is flagged because firms announce quarterly earnings in the month after the fiscal quarter ends, so using the most recent quarter ending on or before month t may include information not yet available at month t. For the valid signals, I construct a composite score by standardizing each signal cross-sectionally and averaging them with signs oriented to predict positive returns. The strategy goes long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of the composite, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly.

## Results
Three features are valid signals:

| Feature | Direction | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short mean monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|--------|-----------|--------------|-----------|-------------------------------|-------------------|
| gross_prof | Positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| asset_growth | Negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| idio_vol | Negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

For `asset_growth` and `idio_vol`, the negative IC means the profitable strategy is to buy low-growth and low-volatility stocks. Reversing the long-short direction gives expected monthly returns of +0.99% and +0.74%, respectively.

The remaining features fail to show reliable predictive power. `book_to_market` (IC 0.0027, t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` (IC 0.0055, t = 1.26), `accruals` (IC 0.0015, t = 0.36), `leverage` (IC -0.0011, t = -0.30), `turnover` (IC 0.0006, t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` (IC -0.0004, t = -0.10) all have t-statistics well below conventional significance thresholds and negligible long-short Sharpe ratios. `sue` appears extraordinarily strong (IC 0.0952, t = 26.55, 100% positive months, Sharpe 7.65), but this is almost certainly driven by look-ahead bias: the feature uses the most recent fiscal quarter ending on or before month t, while firms announce earnings in the following month. Thus `sue` would not have been available at the portfolio formation date and is excluded from the valid signals.

## Discussion
The valid signals correspond to well-documented anomalies. Gross profitability captures the profitability premium: firms with higher gross profit relative to assets earn higher subsequent returns. Asset growth reflects the investment anomaly: firms that expand their asset base tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility captures the low-volatility anomaly: high-volatility stocks underperform on a risk-adjusted basis. A composite strategy that equally weights the three valid signals (long high profitability, low asset growth, low idiosyncratic volatility; short the opposite) would have an expected monthly return of approximately 0.96% (average of the three correctly oriented long-short returns). This translates to a gross annualized return of about 11–12% and an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.0, assuming moderate correlations among the signals. The strategy is implementable with monthly rebalancing, though it requires shorting and assumes no transaction costs or borrow constraints.

## Limitations
- Transaction costs, market impact, and shorting frictions are ignored and would reduce net returns.
- The composite strategy assumes equal weights and ignores correlations among signals, which may lower the realized Sharpe.
- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to other periods or universes.
- The `sue` signal is excluded due to a likely look-ahead bias; if the data were correctly point-in-time, its predictive power would likely be much lower.
- No out-of-sample or subperiod robustness tests are possible from the summary statistics alone.
- The analysis uses monthly rebalancing; higher-frequency or event-driven implementations may differ.
