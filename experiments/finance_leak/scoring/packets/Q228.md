# Cross-Sectional Stock Return Predictability in a Point-in-Time Index Panel: Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We evaluate nine stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. Only gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility are statistically robust signals, with absolute rank-IC t-statistics above 4.0. Gross profitability predicts returns positively, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility predict returns negatively. A composite quintile long-short strategy based on these three signals is estimated to earn about 11.5% per year before costs with an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 1.8.

## Methods
The sample consists of 60,000 stock-month observations: 120 months and 500 index constituents per month. All features are constructed point-in-time, with annual-report variables available at the end of month t and market-based variables measured at or before the end of month t. The target is total stock return over month t+1.

For each feature, I compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next` each month. The reported rank IC is the monthly average, and the t-statistic is the average divided by its standard deviation and multiplied by sqrt(120). I classify a signal as valid if its absolute t-statistic exceeds 2.0 and ideally the Bonferroni threshold for nine tests of approximately 2.77. I also examine equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios: buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile of each feature.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short mean (monthly) | Annualized Sharpe | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `gross_prof` | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 | Valid positive |
| `asset_growth` | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 | Valid negative |
| `idio_vol` | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 | Valid negative |
| `log_mktcap` | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 | Reject |
| `book_to_market` | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 | Reject |
| `accruals` | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 | Reject |
| `turnover` | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 | Reject |
| `rd_intensity` | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 | Reject |
| `leverage` | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 | Reject |

Three features are robust: `gross_prof` has a positive rank IC of 0.0378, `asset_growth` has a negative rank IC of -0.0303, and `idio_vol` has a negative rank IC of -0.0261. Each has an absolute t-statistic above 4.0 and a long-short return consistent with its IC sign. The remaining six features have absolute t-statistics below 1.3 and are not statistically distinguishable from zero in this sample.

A simple composite strategy ranks stocks monthly by the average of cross-sectional percentiles of `gross_prof`, negative `asset_growth`, and negative `idio_vol`, then buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile. The historical average long-short return from equal-weighting the three individual long-short portfolios is approximately 0.96% per month, or 11.5% annualized before trading costs. With moderate correlation among the three signals, the expected annualized Sharpe is approximately 1.8 before costs.

## Discussion
The positive gross profitability signal is consistent with the profitability premium: firms with higher gross profits relative to assets tend to earn higher subsequent returns. The negative asset growth signal is consistent with the asset growth anomaly: firms that expand assets aggressively tend to underperform. The negative idiosyncratic volatility signal is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly: high idiosyncratic volatility stocks tend to earn lower subsequent returns.

Notably, commonly studied signals such as book-to-market and size do not appear statistically significant in this index-constituent panel. This may reflect the sample period, the index universe, or the point-in-time construction of fundamentals. The three validated signals are economically meaningful and may capture partially distinct sources of return predictability.

## Limitations
The results are in-sample and do not guarantee out-of-sample performance. All performance estimates are gross of trading costs, short-selling costs, and implementational frictions. The sample is limited to 120 monthly cross-sections of 500 index constituents, so results may not generalize to other universes or periods. Annual-report data can be stale and may be revised. Multiple testing remains a concern despite the use of a Bonferroni threshold.
