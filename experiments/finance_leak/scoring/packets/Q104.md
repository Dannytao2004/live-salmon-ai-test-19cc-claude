# Earnings Surprise (SUE) as the Dominant Cross-Sectional Signal in a Monthly Equity Panel

## Abstract
We analyze a 120-month panel of 500 index-constituent stocks with 10 characteristics and next-month returns as the target. Using monthly cross-sectional rank ICs and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, only standardized unexpected earnings (`sue`) shows robust predictive power. The `sue` signal has a mean rank IC of 0.0998 (t-stat 23.25), is positive in 99% of months, and delivers a 3.04% monthly long-short return with an annualized Sharpe of 7.31. All other features are statistically insignificant and economically small, with |t-stat| below 2. A monthly SUE long-short strategy would be the only signal-supported allocation in this dataset, though its extreme reported performance warrants caution.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations from 120 months, with 500 stocks per month drawn from a broad equity index. The target is `ret_next`, the stock return over month t+1. I evaluate each feature using the provided monthly Spearman rank IC between the feature and `ret_next`, averaged across all 120 months. The reported t-stat is the mean IC divided by the IC standard deviation times the square root of 120. I also use the provided equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio returns, which buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature monthly. I treat a feature as a valid signal if it has a plausible economic interpretation and a mean rank IC with |t-stat| greater than approximately 2. No raw data or code were available, so all conclusions rely on the supplied summary statistics.

## Results
Only `sue` qualifies as a robust signal. Its mean rank IC is 0.0998, with an IC standard deviation of 0.0470 and a t-stat of 23.25. The IC is positive in 99% of months. Its equal-weighted long-short portfolio earns 3.04% per month, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. This is far stronger than any other feature.

The remaining nine features are weak and statistically insignificant. `book_to_market` has a mean rank IC of -0.0030 (t = -0.72). `gross_prof` has 0.0039 (t = 0.93). `log_mktcap` has 0.0001 (t = 0.02). `asset_growth` has 0.0061 (t = 1.64). `accruals` has 0.0055 (t = 1.34). `idio_vol` has 0.0043 (t = 1.14). `leverage` has -0.0043 (t = -1.00). `turnover` has 0.0001 (t = 0.02). `rd_intensity` has -0.0002 (t = -0.05). None has a t-stat above 2, and their long-short returns are all below 0.30% per month with annualized Sharpe ratios below 0.75.

## Discussion
The results point to earnings surprise momentum as the primary cross-sectional return predictor in this panel. `sue` captures the well-documented post-earnings-announcement drift: firms with better-than-expected earnings tend to outperform, while firms with negative surprises underperform. The signal is not only strong on average but also highly consistent across months, as indicated by the 99% positive-IC frequency.

The absence of significant value, profitability, size, accruals, volatility, leverage, turnover, or R&D effects may reflect the specific universe. The panel consists only of 500 broad-index constituents per month, which reduces cross-sectional dispersion and may exclude the smallest, most distressed, or most speculative firms where several of these anomalies are strongest. It is also possible that some signals are correlated with `sue` or are simply weak in this sample.

A practical strategy would be a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio: buy the top quintile of `sue` and short the bottom quintile of `sue`, rebalanced at each month end. Based on the reported 3.04% monthly mean return, the compounded gross annualized return would be approximately 43.2%, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. These figures are exceptionally high and should be interpreted as gross, pre-cost, and likely optimistic.

## Limitations
- The universe contains only 500 broad-index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to small-cap or microcap stocks.
- The sample covers 120 months and may be sensitive to specific market regimes or structural breaks.
- The reported SUE long-short return is gross of transaction costs, slippage, market impact, and borrow fees.
- The extreme Sharpe ratio suggests possible data artifacts, look-ahead bias, or unrealistically favorable execution assumptions.
- Equal-weighted quintile portfolios can be capacity constrained and sensitive to illiquid or extreme-return stocks.
- No raw data or code were available to verify feature construction, earnings announcement timing, or delisting-return treatment.
