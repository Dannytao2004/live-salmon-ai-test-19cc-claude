# Point-in-Time Evaluation of Cross-Sectional Equity Signals: SUE Look-Ahead Bias and the Absence of Tradeable Predictors

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) is the only feature with strong univariate predictive power, showing an average monthly rank IC of 0.0998, a t-statistic of 23.25, and a top-minus-bottom quintile return of 3.04% per month. However, the data definition reveals that SUE is not point-in-time: the most recent fiscal quarter can end in month t while earnings are announced only in month t+1, so the reported SUE uses information not yet available at the formation date. After rejecting SUE, no other feature reaches conventional statistical significance. We conclude that the dataset contains no reliably investable signal and recommend against trading the reported SUE strategy without a properly lagged, announcement-date-aligned SUE measure.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations, with 500 stocks per month for 120 months. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. Ten characteristics are evaluated as univariate predictors.

For each feature, I compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` each month, then average over the 120 months. The reported t-statistic is the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation and multiplied by sqrt(120). I also examine equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature.

A feature is treated as a valid signal only if it satisfies two conditions:
1. It is known at the end of month t, so it could be used in real time.
2. Its rank IC is statistically significant at the conventional threshold, approximately |t| > 2.0.

## Results
SUE is by far the strongest univariate signal. Its mean rank IC is 0.0998, with a t-statistic of 23.25. The long-short quintile portfolio earns 3.04% per month and has an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. In 99% of months the cross-sectional IC is positive.

However, the data dictionary states that SUE uses the most recent fiscal quarter ending on or before the end of month t, and that firms announce quarterly earnings in the month after the fiscal quarter ends. This means that when the most recent fiscal quarter ends in month t, the actual EPS and consensus forecast needed to compute SUE are not publicly known until t+1. The reported SUE therefore embeds future information and is not a tradable point-in-time signal at the end of month t. The extremely high rank IC and Sharpe ratio are consistent with this look-ahead bias.

The remaining features are all economically weak and statistically insignificant at the |t| > 2.0 threshold:

| feature | mean rank IC | IC t-stat |
|---|---:|---:|
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 |
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 |

None of these features has a statistically significant rank IC, and their long-short returns are small relative to their volatility.

## Discussion
The apparent success of SUE is likely an artifact of earnings announcement timing. In a properly point-in-time backtest, SUE would need to be constructed using only earnings data that were publicly announced before the end of month t. Because the provided data do not enforce this restriction, the reported SUE backtest cannot be used to estimate expected live performance.

After removing SUE, no other characteristic provides a reliable edge. Some features show signs that are inconsistent with commonly studied anomalies, but because the t-statistics are close to zero or well below conventional thresholds, these signs are not meaningful. The recommended strategy is therefore not to trade a long-short portfolio based on the provided features. Any future analysis should reconstruct SUE from announcement dates and require strict point-in-time availability.

## Limitations
- The analysis is univariate only; multivariate combinations were not tested.
- Transaction costs, market impact, short-sale constraints, and capacity are not considered.
- The sample has 120 monthly observations, which may limit power for weaker signals.
- Annual report-based variables may be stale for some firms even though they are labeled as available.
- SUE as provided is not point-in-time and cannot be corrected using only the summary statistics.
