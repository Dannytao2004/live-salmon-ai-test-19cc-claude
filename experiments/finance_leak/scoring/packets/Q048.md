# Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility as Cross-Sectional Return Predictors

## Abstract
We examine nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 stocks per month. Cross-sectional rank IC tests identify gross profitability as a reliably positive signal and asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility as reliably negative signals. The other six characteristics—book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not have statistically significant univariate rank ICs. A monthly rebalanced quintile long-short portfolio combining the three valid signals is expected to earn approximately 11.6% annually with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.9 before transaction costs. The findings support profitability, investment, and low-volatility anomalies.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations: 1,217 distinct stocks over 120 months, with 500 index constituents per month. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month \(t+1\). For each of the nine features, we compute the monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` and average it over the 120 months to obtain the mean rank IC. The associated t-statistic is calculated as:

\[
t = \frac{\text{mean rank IC}}{\text{std of rank IC} / \sqrt{120}}
\]

A feature is treated as a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds approximately 2.8 in absolute value, corresponding to a conservative Bonferroni correction for nine tests. We also report univariate quintile long-short returns: each month, stocks are sorted by a feature and an equal-weighted portfolio buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile.

## Results
The table below summarizes rank IC statistics and long-short performance for all features.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 |

Three features pass the significance threshold. `gross_prof` has a mean rank IC of 0.0378 with a t-statistic of 6.07, indicating that more profitable firms earn higher next-month returns. `asset_growth` has a mean rank IC of -0.0303 with a t-statistic of -4.29, indicating that high asset growth predicts lower returns. `idio_vol` has a mean rank IC of -0.0261 with a t-statistic of -4.19, indicating that high idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns. The remaining features have rank ICs statistically indistinguishable from zero and inconsistent sign behavior.

Based on the three valid signals, we construct a composite score:

\[
\text{score} = z(\text{gross\_prof}) - z(\text{asset\_growth}) - z(\text{idio\_vol})
\]

Each month we rank stocks by this score, buy the top quintile, sell the bottom quintile, and hold an equal-weighted long-short portfolio. The average of the three univariate long-short monthly returns is approximately 0.96% per month, implying about 11.6% annual return. Assuming moderate correlation among the three signal sleeves, the composite long-short strategy is expected to achieve an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.9 before transaction costs.

## Discussion
The results are broadly consistent with established anomalies. Gross profitability predicts returns positively, supporting the idea that profitable firms tend to be undervalued relative to less profitable peers. Asset growth predicts returns negatively, consistent with the investment anomaly: firms that grow assets aggressively tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility predicts returns negatively, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly and limits-to-arbitrage explanations.

Notably, book-to-market does not show a significant value effect in this sample, and size is also weak. This may reflect the index-constituent universe, the sample period, or the specific point-in-time accounting definitions. The composite strategy benefits from combining three economically distinct signals, which helps diversify signal-specific noise.

## Limitations
This analysis does not incorporate transaction costs, market impact, or short-sale constraints. The sample is limited to 500 index constituents per month and may not generalize to smaller stocks or non-index names. Accounting-based features rely on point-in-time annual data availability, but reporting lags, restatements, and differences in accounting standards could introduce measurement error. Finally, the expected strategy performance is based on in-sample statistics and may overstate out-of-sample returns.
