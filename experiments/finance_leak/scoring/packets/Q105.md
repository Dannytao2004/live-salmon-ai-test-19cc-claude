# Leverage as a Cross-Sectional Predictor of One-Month Stock Returns: Evidence from a 120-Month Current-Constituent Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns in a panel of 500 current index constituents over 120 months. Among the nine features, only leverage exhibits a statistically reliable cross-sectional relation with future returns, with a mean monthly rank IC of +0.0164 (t-stat = 2.74) and a top-minus-bottom quintile spread of 0.52% per month. A monthly rebalanced long-short leverage quintile strategy would have delivered an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92 and an expected annual return of approximately 6.24% before costs. The remaining features are not reliable standalone signals after conventional significance screening.

## Methods
The dataset contains 34,708 stock-month observations from month 1 to month 120, covering 500 distinct stocks that are constituents of a broad equity index at the end of month 120. For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with next-month returns within each month. The reported rank IC is the time-series average of these monthly correlations, and the t-statistic is computed as the mean divided by the standard deviation over the square root of the number of months. We also examine equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month. A feature is treated as valid only if its mean rank IC is statistically distinguishable from zero at conventional levels and its long-short return is directionally consistent.

## Results
Leverage is the only feature that passes the significance screen. Its mean monthly rank IC is +0.0164 with a standard deviation of 0.0654, yielding a t-statistic of 2.74. The IC is positive in 59% of months. The long-short quintile portfolio based on leverage earns 0.52% per month, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92.

The other eight features have rank IC t-statistics no larger than 1.16 in absolute value. Book-to-market, gross profitability, log market cap, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, turnover, and R&D intensity all have small or economically inconsistent return spreads. Asset growth has a relatively promising long-short Sharpe ratio of 0.74, but its rank IC is only 0.0055 with a t-statistic of 1.16, so it is not accepted as a reliable monotonic cross-sectional signal in this sample.

## Discussion
The positive relation between leverage and next-month returns suggests that, within this sample of current index constituents, high-leverage firms earned higher subsequent returns than low-leverage firms. This may reflect compensation for leverage-related fundamental risk, or it may be specific to the sample period and the current-constituent universe. The economic magnitude is moderate: a top-versus-bottom quintile spread of 52 basis points per month is comparable to many published cross-sectional return spreads. Because the signal is based only on leverage, the proposed strategy is simple and transparent: each month sort stocks into quintiles by leverage, buy the top quintile, and sell the bottom quintile.

## Limitations
The sample is limited to stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, which may introduce survivorship or selection bias. The leverage signal is significant at conventional single-test levels, but it is only borderline after conservative multiple-testing adjustments across nine features. The reported long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, taxes, short-selling fees, and market impact. Annual-report-based fundamentals may be stale or subject to point-in-time reporting assumptions. No industry, sector, or other control variables are included, and the linear rank IC framework may miss nonlinear or interaction effects.
