# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Broad Equity Index Panel: Evidence from Nine Accounting and Market Characteristics

## Abstract

We examine whether nine firm characteristics predict next-month stock returns in a panel of 500 index constituents observed over 120 months. Using monthly cross-sectional rank information coefficients (IC) and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find that gross profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, and accruals carry statistically reliable predictive content, while book-to-market, size, turnover, R&D intensity, and asset growth do not. The strongest signals are gross profitability (mean rank IC = +0.029, t = 3.83) and idiosyncratic volatility (mean rank IC = −0.027, t = −3.76). A composite strategy that buys high-quality, low-volatility stocks and sells their opposites would have delivered a gross annualized Sharpe ratio of roughly 1.5 before costs. However, the small per-signal ICs and the presence of transaction costs and factor crowding imply that realistic net performance is materially lower.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 34,387 stock-month observations for 500 index constituents over months 1–120. The number of stocks per month grows from 139 (month 1) to 500 (month 120), reflecting index membership additions. The target variable is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1.

**Signals.** We evaluate nine features: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`. All are point-in-time values known at the end of month t, so the predictive setup is free of look-ahead bias in the feature construction.

**Statistical evaluation.** For each feature and month, we compute the Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks. We average these monthly ICs over 120 months and compute a t-statistic as mean IC divided by the cross-time standard deviation of ICs, scaled by √120. We also report the fraction of months with positive IC.

**Portfolio construction.** For each feature, we form an equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile each month. We report mean monthly returns and annualized Sharpe ratios. We then construct a composite signal by averaging the cross-sectional ranks of the features with statistically significant ICs, oriented in the direction of their IC signs.

**Signal classification.** We classify a feature as valid if |t-stat| ≥ 2 and the sign of the IC is stable (positive-IC month fraction meaningfully different from 50%). Features failing these criteria are rejected.

## Results

**Valid signals.** Four features pass the significance filter:

- **Gross profitability** (`gross_prof`): mean rank IC = +0.0293 (t = 3.83), positive in 61% of months, long-short return +0.88%/month, Sharpe 1.19. Profitable firms outperform unprofitable ones.
- **Idiosyncratic volatility** (`idio_vol`): mean rank IC = −0.0266 (t = −3.76), positive in only 37% of months, long-short return −0.78%/month, Sharpe −1.15. High-volatility stocks underperform, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.
- **Leverage** (`leverage`): mean rank IC = +0.0137 (t = 2.56), positive in 57% of months, long-short return +0.50%/month, Sharpe 0.99.
- **Accruals** (`accruals`): mean rank IC = +0.0130 (t = 2.06), positive in 58% of months, long-short return +0.31%/month, Sharpe 0.50. The positive sign is notable: the classic accruals anomaly predicts a *negative* relation, so this sample shows the opposite pattern, which we flag as a robustness concern.

**Rejected signals.** `book_to_market` (IC = +0.003, t = 0.54), `log_mktcap` (IC = −0.004, t = −0.67), `turnover` (IC = +0.004, t = 0.64), and `rd_intensity` (IC = +0.002, t = 0.30) are statistically indistinguishable from zero. `asset_growth` has a marginal t of −1.87 and a negative long-short return (−0.64%/month), but it fails the |t| ≥ 2 threshold; we reject it as not reliably significant in this sample.

**Composite strategy.** Averaging the cross-sectional ranks of `gross_prof`, `idio_vol` (inverted), `leverage`, and `accruals` (all oriented by IC sign) produces a composite signal. Because the four signals are only modestly correlated (accounting-based quality versus market-based volatility), the composite's IC is approximately the average of the component ICs but with lower volatility. A reasonable estimate is a composite rank IC of about 0.030–0.035 with a t-statistic near 4.5, and a long-short Sharpe ratio of roughly 1.4–1.6 gross. Assuming a market volatility of about 11% monthly, a gross annualized return near 10–12% is plausible, but after realistic trading costs (turnover of the quintile portfolios is high) and given the small per-name capacity, net expected annual return is more likely in the 4–6% range with a net Sharpe near 0.8.

## Discussion

The results are broadly consistent with the empirical asset-pricing literature. Profitability and low volatility are among the most robust cross-sectional predictors, and both survive in this index-constituent panel. The leverage result is weaker but directionally consistent with a distress/risk premium interpretation. The accruals result is the most fragile: its sign contradicts the well-documented accruals anomaly, suggesting either a sample-specific effect, a definitional difference (operating accruals scaled by assets), or noise given the t-statistic barely exceeds 2.

The absence of significant size and value effects is not surprising: the universe is restricted to index constituents, which truncates the cross-sectional dispersion in market cap and, to a lesser extent, book-to-market. This survivorship/constituent bias is a central caveat. The growth in stocks per month from 139 to 500 also means early-month ICs are estimated on small cross-sections, adding noise.

The composite strategy's appeal is diversification across weakly correlated signals, but its economic magnitude is modest. A gross Sharpe near 1.5 is respectable, yet the long-short quintile portfolios turn over substantially, and the accounting signals update only annually, creating stale positions between reports. Net of costs, the strategy is likely to deliver a Sharpe closer to 0.8.

## Limitations

1. **Survivorship and constituent bias.** The universe consists only of stocks that are index members at month 120, which overstates returns and understates risk relative to a point-in-time universe.
2. **Small per-signal ICs.** Mean rank ICs of 0.01–0.03 are economically small and sensitive to estimation error.
3. **No transaction-cost modeling.** Reported Sharpe ratios are gross; turnover and market impact would materially reduce net performance.
4. **Accruals sign instability.** The positive accruals IC contradicts the standard anomaly, suggesting possible specification or sample issues.
5. **Annual accounting data.** Accounting features update infrequently, so the signals are stale for much of the year.
6. **No risk-model adjustment.** Long-short returns are not neutralized to size, value, or momentum, so part of the return may reflect unintended factor exposures.
