# Point-in-Time Equity Signals: Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
Using a monthly panel of 500 index stocks over 120 months, we test ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month returns. We find statistically significant positive predictive power for gross profitability and negative predictive power for asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility. Analyst revisions have the strongest statistical relation with returns but are recorded at the end of the forecast month and are therefore not investable at the time of prediction. A composite quintile long-short strategy based on the three valid signals is expected to earn about 11% per year with an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 2.0 gross of transaction costs.

## Methods
The dataset is a point-in-time monthly panel of 500 broad equity index constituents per month for 120 months. The prediction target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month, average this rank IC across all 120 months, and compute a t-statistic as mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation times the square root of the number of months. We also report equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature. A feature is treated as a valid signal only if it is available at the end of month t and has a rank IC t-statistic with absolute value above 2.0. `analyst_rev` is excluded from predictive signals because it is recorded at the end of month t+1, making it contemporaneous with the return it would predict.

## Results
Three features survive the significance and timing screens.

`gross_prof` is positively related to next-month returns, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.0378 (t-stat 6.07) and a long-short return of 1.16% per month (annualized Sharpe 1.94). `asset_growth` is negatively related to next-month returns, with a mean rank IC of -0.0303 (t-stat -4.29); reversing the high-long/short-low portfolio yields 0.99% per month (annualized Sharpe 1.48). `idio_vol` is also negatively related to returns, with a mean rank IC of -0.0261 (t-stat -4.19); reversing the high-long/short-low portfolio yields 0.74% per month (annualized Sharpe 1.20).

The remaining features are not statistically significant. `book_to_market` has a rank IC of 0.0027 (t-stat 0.68), `log_mktcap` 0.0055 (t-stat 1.26), `accruals` 0.0015 (t-stat 0.36), `leverage` -0.0011 (t-stat -0.30), `turnover` 0.0006 (t-stat 0.14), and `rd_intensity` -0.0004 (t-stat -0.10). `analyst_rev` has a very high rank IC of 0.2963 (t-stat 80.29), but this reflects contemporaneous information rather than an implementable predictive signal because it is recorded at the end of month t+1.

Combining the three valid signals into a composite score—averaging percentile ranks of gross profitability, negative asset growth, and negative idiosyncratic volatility—and forming a top-versus-bottom quintile long-short portfolio is expected to produce a gross annual return of approximately 11% with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.0.

## Discussion
The results are economically consistent with prior research. Gross profitability predicts returns positively, supporting the idea that profitable firms tend to outperform. Asset growth predicts returns negatively, consistent with the investment effect: firms that grow assets aggressively tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility predicts returns negatively, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly. The exclusion of `analyst_rev` illustrates the importance of point-in-time data discipline: a signal can be extremely strong but unusable if it is observed only after the return period begins.

The proposed composite strategy is dollar-neutral and uses only information available at the end of month t. Its expected performance is estimated from the historical single-factor long-short results and the diversification benefit of combining three relatively distinct signals.

## Limitations
The performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, and monthly quintile rebalancing may incur meaningful costs. The sample covers only 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to smaller or less liquid stocks. The expected composite Sharpe ratio is based on historical in-sample statistics and assumes the signals remain stable out of sample. Correlations among the three valid signals are not directly modeled from the provided statistics, so the composite strategy's realized volatility and return may differ.
