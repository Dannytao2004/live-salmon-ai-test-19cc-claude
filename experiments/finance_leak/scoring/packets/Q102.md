# Predicting Next-Month Returns: Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility are statistically reliable cross-sectional signals, while the apparently strong analyst revision signal is not usable because it is measured during the same month as the target return. A composite long-short strategy based on the three valid signals is expected to earn an annualized return of about 11% with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.0, gross of transaction costs.

## Methods
The dataset contains one row per stock per month for 120 months, with 500 stocks each month and a total of 60,000 stock-month observations. The prediction target is `ret_next`, the stock return over month t+1. For each feature, we compute monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlations with `ret_next` and average those rank ICs across the 120 months. We also form equal-weighted top-quintile and bottom-quintile portfolios each month and report the long-short return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

A feature is treated as a valid signal only if it is known at the end of month t based on the data dictionary and its mean rank IC has an absolute t-statistic above 2.0. The only forward-looking feature is `analyst_rev`, which is recorded at the end of month t+1 and therefore cannot be used to predict the t+1 return.

## Results
Three features pass the validity and statistical significance threshold.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

`gross_prof` is a positive signal: high gross profitability predicts higher next-month returns. `asset_growth` is negative: high asset growth predicts lower returns, so a long low-asset-growth, short high-asset-growth portfolio earns a positive premium. `idio_vol` is negative: low idiosyncratic volatility stocks outperform high idiosyncratic volatility stocks.

The remaining features are not statistically reliable. `book_to_market` has a rank IC of 0.0027 (t=0.68), `log_mktcap` 0.0055 (t=1.26), `accruals` 0.0015 (t=0.36), `leverage` -0.0011 (t=-0.30), `turnover` 0.0006 (t=0.14), and `rd_intensity` -0.0004 (t=-0.10). `analyst_rev` has an extremely high rank IC of 0.2963 and positive IC in 100% of months, but it is excluded for lookahead: the revision is measured during t+1, contemporaneous with `ret_next`.

## Discussion
The three valid signals represent economically distinct return premia. Gross profitability is a quality signal; asset growth captures the well-documented negative relation between asset expansion and future returns; and idiosyncratic volatility captures the low-volatility anomaly. Because these signals are likely imperfectly correlated, combining them should improve the stability of the long-short portfolio.

I propose a composite strategy: each month, standardize the three valid signals cross-sectionally, compute a composite score as `z(gross_prof) - z(asset_growth) - z(idio_vol)`, buy the top quintile, and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted. Based on the average single-signal long-short returns and diversification across the three signals, the expected gross monthly return is approximately 0.9%–1.0%, implying an annualized return of about 11% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 2.0.

## Limitations
The performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, short-sales constraints, and implementation costs. The sample covers only 120 months and 500 stocks, so the results may not generalize to other periods or universes. The composite Sharpe estimate depends on the unknown correlation structure among the three signals and is based on in-sample single-signal results. Multiple testing across ten features may also inflate the apparent significance of some signals.
