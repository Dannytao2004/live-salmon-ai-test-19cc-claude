# Report Q146

# No Tradable Cross-Sectional Equity Signals After Eliminating Look-Ahead Bias in Analyst Revisions

## Abstract

We evaluate ten stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Analyst revision is the only variable with a strong univariate rank IC of 0.296, but it is recorded at the end of the return month and therefore cannot be used without look-ahead bias. Among the remaining nine point-in-time characteristics, none has a statistically significant rank IC at the conventional \(|t|>2\) threshold. We conclude that this dataset contains no implementable cross-sectional return signal, and the estimated expected active strategy return is zero before costs.

## Methods

We use a panel of 60,000 stock-month observations, with 500 stocks per month for 120 months. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month \(t+1\).

For each feature, we compute the monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next`. We report the mean rank IC across 120 months and a t-statistic:

\[
t = \frac{\text{mean IC}}{\text{IC std}} \times \sqrt{120}
\]

We also compute equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature.

A feature is considered a valid signal only if:
1. It is available at the end of month \(t\), before the return period begins.
2. Its mean rank IC is statistically significant at the conventional \(|t|>2.0\) threshold.

## Results

`analyst_rev` has by far the strongest univariate association with next-month returns: mean rank IC of 0.2958, t-statistic of 89.6, positive IC in 100% of months, and a long-short quintile return of 8.66% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 26.4. However, according to the data dictionary, `analyst_rev` is the change in mean FY1 EPS forecast during month \(t+1\), recorded at the end of month \(t+1\). It is therefore contemporaneous with, rather than prior to, the return it is meant to predict. This is look-ahead bias and makes the signal unusable in a real-time strategy.

Among the remaining point-in-time features, no signal reaches statistical significance. The strongest are `asset_growth` with mean rank IC 0.0061 (t-stat 1.64) and `accruals` with mean rank IC 0.0055 (t-stat 1.34). Their long-short annualized Sharpe ratios are 0.72 and 0.70, respectively. All other features have t-statistics near or below 1.0 and economically trivial long-short returns.

## Discussion

The only apparently powerful signal in this dataset is invalid due to look-ahead bias. A naive backtest using `analyst_rev` would report extraordinary performance, but that performance would not be achievable in real time. Once this variable is removed, none of the point-in-time characteristics reliably predicts next-month returns. The borderline but insignificant performance of `asset_growth` and `accruals` could reflect noise or mild economic effects, but it is not strong enough to support a robust trading strategy.

Given the absence of valid signals, the proposed strategy is to avoid building a long-short strategy from these point-in-time features. The expected active return before costs is zero.

## Limitations

- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 stocks per month, limiting statistical power.
- The analysis is univariate; multivariate combinations are not tested.
- Transaction costs, short-selling costs, and capacity constraints are not modeled.
- Multiple testing across ten features increases the risk of false positives.
- The point-in-time availability of accounting variables depends on the accuracy of the reported data definitions.
