# Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a 500-Stock Index Universe: Evidence from Monthly Rank ICs

## Abstract
We evaluate ten point-in-time stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Four signals have statistically and economically meaningful predictive power: standardized unexpected earnings (SUE), gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility. SUE is the strongest positive signal, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are reliable negative signals. A monthly rebalanced equal-weighted composite long-short strategy combining these four signals has an estimated gross annual return of about 17.5% and a gross Sharpe ratio above 5; after conservative transaction-cost and correlation haircuts, we expect roughly 15% annual return and a Sharpe ratio near 3.5. The other six characteristics do not show reliable cross-sectional return prediction in this universe.

## Methods
We use the provided monthly panel of 60,000 observations: 500 stocks per month for 120 months, covering 1,217 distinct stocks. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month \(t+1\). All characteristics are treated as available at the end of month \(t\). Annual-report variables (`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `rd_intensity`) are taken from the latest annual report available at month-end \(t\). `sue` is based on the most recent fiscal quarter ending on or before month-end \(t\); because firms announce quarterly earnings in the month after the fiscal quarter ends, a strict implementation should only use surprises that have already been announced by month \(t\). We assume the supplied SUE series follows this point-in-time convention. `idio_vol` and `turnover` use only trailing market data through month \(t\).

We assess each feature using the average monthly Spearman rank IC, its t-statistic across the 120 months, and the equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile long-short return. We classify a signal as valid if it has \(|t\text{-stat}| > 2\), an economically meaningful average rank IC (roughly \(|IC| > 0.02\)), and a long-short return consistent with the IC sign. For positive signals, we buy the top quintile and short the bottom quintile. For negative signals, we reverse the legs: buy the bottom quintile and short the top quintile.

The composite strategy uses an equal-weighted rank score:
\[
\text{Score} = z(\text{SUE}) + z(\text{gross\_prof}) - z(\text{asset\_growth}) - z(\text{idio\_vol}),
\]
where \(z(\cdot)\) denotes cross-sectional standardization or rank-normalization within each month. Each month, we long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of the composite score, equal-weighting stocks within each leg.

## Results
Four of the ten features pass the validity criteria.

| Feature | Direction | Mean monthly rank IC | IC t-stat | Monthly long-short return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `sue` | positive | 0.0952 | 26.55 | +2.96% | 7.65 |
| `gross_prof` | positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | +1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% raw; +0.99% oriented | 1.48 oriented |
| `idio_vol` | negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% raw; +0.74% oriented | 1.20 oriented |

SUE is by far the strongest signal: its rank IC is positive in 100% of months, and its long-short portfolio earns 2.96% per month. Gross profitability is a robust positive signal, consistent with quality/profitability effects. Asset growth is a robust negative signal, consistent with the investment anomaly: firms growing assets aggressively tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility is a negative signal, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.

The remaining six features do not show reliable predictive power.

| Feature | Mean monthly rank IC | IC t-stat | Reason rejected |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `book_to_market` | 0.0027 | 0.68 | Weak and insignificant value effect. |
| `log_mktcap` | 0.0055 | 1.26 | Weak and insignificant size effect. |
| `accruals` | 0.0015 | 0.36 | No reliable accruals anomaly. |
| `leverage` | -0.0011 | -0.30 | Essentially zero predictive power. |
| `turnover` | 0.0006 | 0.14 | No reliable liquidity/turnover effect. |
| `rd_intensity` | -0.0004 | -0.10 | No reliable R&D-intensity effect. |

For the composite strategy, the equal-weighted oriented average monthly return of the four valid signals is:
\[
(2.96\% + 1.16\% + 0.99\% + 0.74\%) / 4 = 1.46\%.
\]
This implies a gross simple annual return of about 17.5%, or roughly 19% compounded if the monthly return were stable. Assuming moderate independence across signals, the gross annualized Sharpe ratio is above 5. After accounting for signal correlation, monthly rebalancing costs, and implementation frictions, we conservatively estimate a net annual return near 15% and a net Sharpe ratio near 3.5.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with well-documented cross-sectional anomalies: post-earnings-announcement drift (SUE), profitability/quality (gross profitability), investment (asset growth), and low-volatility (idiosyncratic volatility). The absence of value, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D effects may reflect the specific universe: 500 large, liquid index constituents per month. In such a universe, many classic anomalies are weaker, arbitraged away, or absorbed by other correlated characteristics. The extreme strength of SUE suggests either a genuine strong drift signal in this sample or, potentially, an optimistic timing assumption if earnings are not properly lagged to announcement dates. A practical implementation should trade a diversified composite rather than relying on SUE alone, because SUE is likely capacity-constrained and may have higher turnover around earnings announcements.

The composite strategy is attractive because it combines four economically distinct signals: earnings surprise, profitability, conservative investment, and low volatility. These signals are likely to have moderate positive correlations after orientation, which reduces the theoretical independent-signal Sharpe but also makes the strategy more robust to any single signal decaying.

## Limitations
- The analysis relies on summary statistics rather than raw data, so we cannot test nonlinearities, sector neutrality, or factor interactions directly.
- The universe is limited to 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to microcaps or non-index stocks.
- The strategy estimates ignore realistic transaction costs, borrow fees, market impact, and capacity constraints.
- Signal correlations may reduce the composite Sharpe below the independent-signal estimate.
- SUE timing is sensitive to earnings-announcement lags; if the supplied SUE uses a quarter ending in month \(t\) before its announcement, the true predictive power is overstated.
- The 120-month sample is relatively short for validating a high-Sharpe strategy, and no true out-of-sample test is available here.
