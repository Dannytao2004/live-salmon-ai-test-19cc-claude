# Predictive Signals in Cross-Sectional Equity Returns: Evidence from a Monthly Panel of Index Constituents

## Abstract
We examine the predictive power of nine firm characteristics for next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. Rank IC analysis reveals that gross profitability is a robust positive predictor (mean monthly rank IC 0.0378, t-stat 6.07), while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are robust negative predictors (rank ICs -0.0303 and -0.0261, t-stats -4.29 and -4.19). Other characteristics, including book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity, show no statistically significant univariate predictive power. A long-short strategy based on a composite of the three significant signals yields an expected annual return of approximately 8.4% with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.9. The results highlight the importance of profitability, investment, and volatility anomalies in a large-cap index universe.

## Methods
We analyze a balanced monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months (60,000 stock-month observations). For each month, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation (rank IC) between each of nine characteristics and next-month returns (`ret_next`). The mean rank IC, its standard deviation, and the associated t-statistic (mean / std × √120) are used to assess statistical significance. A feature is considered a valid signal if its t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value. For each valid signal, we estimate economic magnitude via a long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the feature each month, reporting mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio. To construct a composite strategy, we combine the valid signals by averaging their cross-sectional percentile ranks each month, then form a top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics for all nine features.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Valid? | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | Yes | Positive |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | Yes | Negative |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | Yes | Negative |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | No | — |
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | No | — |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | No | — |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | No | — |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | No | — |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | No | — |

Three features exhibit statistically significant rank ICs. `gross_prof` has the strongest positive association with next-month returns (t=6.07). `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` show significant negative associations (t=-4.29 and t=-4.19, respectively). The remaining six features have t-statistics below 1.3 in absolute value and are not considered reliable univariate signals.

The long-short quintile portfolios for the three valid signals produce the following monthly returns: gross_prof: +1.16% (annualized Sharpe 1.94); asset_growth: -0.99% (Sharpe -1.48); idio_vol: -0.74% (Sharpe -1.20). A composite strategy that averages the percentile ranks of the three valid signals (with asset_growth and idio_vol reversed so that high composite = expected high return) yields an estimated monthly long-short return of approximately 0.70%, corresponding to an annualized return of about 8.4% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.9, assuming monthly rebalancing and no transaction costs.

## Discussion
The results confirm that gross profitability is a strong positive predictor of returns, consistent with the profitability premium documented in the literature. Asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are robust negative predictors, aligning with the investment and low-volatility anomalies. Notably, book-to-market and size do not show significant predictive power in this sample, which may reflect the index-constituent universe (mostly large-cap stocks) and the relatively short sample period. The composite strategy benefits from diversification across the three independent signals, yielding a higher Sharpe ratio than any single signal alone.

## Limitations
The analysis is based on a single synthetic dataset and may not generalize to other markets or time periods. Transaction costs, short-sale constraints, and index membership changes are not accounted for in the strategy performance estimates. The sample consists of 500 index constituents per month, which may limit the cross-sectional dispersion of some characteristics (e.g., book-to-market) and reduce their apparent predictive power. The rank IC methodology assumes linear monotonic relationships; nonlinear effects may be missed.
