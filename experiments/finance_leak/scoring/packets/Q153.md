# Title: Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: Valid Signals and a Composite Strategy

## Abstract
We evaluate ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility are statistically robust and economically interpretable cross-sectional signals. Analyst revisions exhibit extremely large in-sample predictive power, but the feature is recorded at the end of the prediction month and is therefore contaminated by look-ahead bias. A composite long-short strategy built on the three valid signals is expected to earn roughly 11.6% annualized gross return with a Sharpe ratio near 2.0. The remaining characteristics show no reliable incremental predictive power.

## Methods
We use a monthly panel of 60,000 observations covering 120 months and 1,217 distinct stocks, with 500 stocks per month. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month \(t+1\). Each feature is measured at the end of month \(t\), except `analyst_rev`, which the data dictionary states is recorded at the end of month \(t+1\).

We assess each feature using its average monthly rank IC, its IC t-statistic, and the mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio of an equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile. A signal is considered valid if it is point-in-time available at portfolio formation, has a plausible economic rationale, and has a rank IC t-statistic greater than 2 in absolute value. For valid signals with negative ICs, the profitable long-short direction is reversed (buy bottom quintile, sell top quintile).

The proposed strategy forms an equal-weighted composite score: \(z(\text{gross\_prof}) - z(\text{asset\_growth}) - z(\text{idio\_vol})\). Each month, the portfolio buys the top quintile and shorts the bottom quintile of this composite, equal-weighted and rebalanced monthly.

## Results
Three features pass the validity criteria:

| Feature | Direction | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short mean monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gross_prof | Positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| asset_growth | Negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| idio_vol | Negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

The remaining features are rejected. `book_to_market` (IC 0.0027, t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` (IC 0.0055, t = 1.26), `accruals` (IC 0.0015, t = 0.36), `leverage` (IC -0.0011, t = -0.30), `turnover` (IC 0.0006, t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` (IC -0.0004, t = -0.10) are statistically indistinguishable from zero. `analyst_rev` has an extraordinary IC of 0.2963 and a long-short mean monthly return of 8.73%, but it is recorded at the end of month \(t+1\), making it unavailable at the end of month \(t\) when the portfolio is formed. It is therefore rejected as a look-ahead-contaminated signal.

A composite long-short strategy using the three valid signals is expected to earn approximately \((1.16\% + 0.99\% + 0.74\%)/3 = 0.96\%\) per month, or about 11.6% annualized, before transaction costs. Given the individual Sharpes and moderate assumed correlations, the composite Sharpe is estimated near 2.0.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with three well-documented anomalies. The positive profitability signal reflects the profitability premium: more profitable firms tend to outperform. The negative asset-growth signal captures the asset-growth anomaly: firms that expand their balance sheets aggressively subsequently underperform. The negative idiosyncratic-volatility signal reflects the low-volatility anomaly: high-idio-vol stocks tend to have poor risk-adjusted returns.

The analyst-revision signal is a cautionary case. Its in-sample performance is almost too good, and the data dictionary reveals why: it is measured during the prediction month. In a real-time strategy, this information would not be available at portfolio formation. Excluding it is essential for a valid backtest.

The composite strategy diversifies across three economically distinct signals, which should improve risk-adjusted performance relative to any single signal. The expected gross return is attractive, but net performance will depend on turnover, trading costs, and borrow availability.

## Limitations
- The analysis assumes no transaction costs, borrow costs, or shorting constraints, which would reduce net returns.
- The sample covers only 120 months and a specific index-constituent universe, so results may not generalize to other periods or markets.
- The equal-weighted composite ignores market-cap weighting and potential correlations among signals.
- `analyst_rev` is excluded due to clear look-ahead bias, but other features could still contain subtle timing issues.
- No out-of-sample validation is performed; in-sample signal selection may overstate expected performance.
