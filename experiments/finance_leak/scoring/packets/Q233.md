# Report Q233

# Monthly Cross-Sectional Equity Signal Study: Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract

Using a point-in-time panel of 60,000 monthly observations across 1,217 index constituents, I evaluate nine stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns. Gross profitability is a robust positive signal, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are robust negative signals. The remaining six characteristics have average rank ICs close to zero and statistically insignificant t-statistics. A composite long-short strategy that buys profitable, low-asset-growth, low-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks and shorts the opposite earns approximately 11.6% annualized gross return, with an estimated Sharpe ratio near 2.0. These results are consistent with the profitability, investment, and low-volatility anomalies.

## Methods

**Data and universe.** The panel contains 120 months and 500 stocks per month. The universe is point-in-time: stocks remain in the sample until removal from the broad equity index, and their final-month returns are included. This mitigates survivorship bias but reflects index-constituent selection.

**Feature availability.** At the end of month \(t\), all features are observable. Market-based variables—`log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, and `turnover`—are computed through month \(t\). Accounting variables—`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, and `rd_intensity`—use the latest annual report available at the end of month \(t\). The target `ret_next` is the total return from the end of month \(t\) to the end of month \(t+1\). Thus, no future information is used in signal formation.

**Signal evaluation.** For each month, I compute the Spearman rank correlation between each feature and `ret_next` across stocks. The average monthly rank IC and its t-statistic are reported, where \(t = \text{mean IC} / \text{IC std} \times \sqrt{120}\). I also use equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios: long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly. For negative signals, the sign is reversed when building the composite strategy.

**Strategy construction.** The composite score is:

\[
\text{Score} = z(\text{gross\_prof}) - z(\text{asset\_growth}) - z(\text{idio\_vol})
\]

Stocks are ranked each month. The strategy buys the top quintile and shorts the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, and rebalances monthly.

## Results

### Valid signals

| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short mean monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | Positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | Negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | Negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

`gross_prof` has a positive and highly significant rank IC. Its long-short portfolio earns 1.16% per month, with IC > 0 in 70% of months. `asset_growth` has a negative and significant rank IC; the high-minus-low portfolio loses 0.99% per month, implying that going long low-asset-growth stocks and short high-asset-growth stocks earns about 0.99% per month. `idio_vol` is also negative and significant; the high-minus-low portfolio loses 0.74% per month, so the sign-adjusted low-volatility trade earns about 0.74% per month.

### Rejected signals

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `book_to_market` | 0.0027 | 0.68 | No reliable value effect in this panel. |
| `log_mktcap` | 0.0055 | 1.26 | No statistically reliable size effect. |
| `accruals` | 0.0015 | 0.36 | No reliable accrual anomaly. |
| `leverage` | -0.0011 | -0.30 | No reliable leverage effect. |
| `turnover` | 0.0006 | 0.14 | No reliable turnover/liquidity signal. |
| `rd_intensity` | -0.0004 | -0.10 | No reliable R&D intensity signal. |

These features have average rank ICs near zero and t-statistics well below conventional significance thresholds. Their long-short returns are also small and inconsistent.

### Composite strategy estimate

Sign-adjusted monthly long-short returns are:

- `gross_prof`: +1.16%
- `asset_growth`: +0.99% (long low growth, short high growth)
- `idio_vol`: +0.74% (long low idio vol, short high idio vol)

An equal-weight composite of these three sign-adjusted portfolios has an estimated monthly gross return of approximately:

\[
(1.16\% + 0.99\% + 0.74\%) / 3 \approx 0.96\%
\]

This corresponds to about 11.6% annualized gross return before transaction costs and financing. Individual annualized Sharpe ratios are 1.94, 1.48, and 1.20. Assuming moderate positive correlation among the signals, the composite Sharpe is plausibly around 1.9–2.1. I use 2.0 as a central estimate.

## Discussion

The three valid signals have well-known economic interpretations. High gross profitability is associated with higher future returns, consistent with quality and profitability premiums. High asset growth predicts lower returns, consistent with the investment anomaly and q-theory: firms that expand assets aggressively may be overinvesting or experiencing deteriorating expected returns. High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly and lottery-demand explanations.

The rejected features do not show reliable cross-sectional predictive power in this sample. This may reflect the specific index-constituent universe, the 120-month sample, the monthly horizon, or the lack of interaction and nonlinear modeling. Accounting variables may also be stale, since they are updated annually, and their information may already be reflected in prices by the time they become available.

The composite strategy improves diversification relative to any single signal. It remains a gross-return estimate: actual implementation would face trading costs, shorting constraints, borrow fees, and capacity limits, especially for high-volatility and small-cap names.

## Limitations

- Transaction costs, market impact, and borrow fees are not modeled, so net returns would be lower than the gross estimates.
- The strategy requires shorting, which may be constrained or costly for some stocks, especially high-idiosyncratic-volatility names.
- Accounting variables are updated only annually and can be stale by up to 12 months.
- The sample contains only 500 large index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to microcaps or non-index stocks.
- No sector, industry, or risk-factor neutralization is applied, leaving exposure to unintended systematic biases.
- The 120-month sample is relatively short for validating anomaly persistence and may contain period-specific effects.
- Signal correlations are unknown, so the composite Sharpe estimate is approximate and could be lower if the signals are highly correlated.
