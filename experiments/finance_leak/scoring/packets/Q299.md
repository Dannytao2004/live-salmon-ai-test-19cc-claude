# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Survivor Universe: Evidence from 120 Monthly Stock Panels

## Abstract
I evaluate nine stock-level characteristics for predicting next-month returns using 34,387 stock-month observations from a 500-stock survivor universe. Monthly rank ICs and equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios show that gross profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, and accruals are the most reliable signals. Gross profitability and leverage are positively related to future returns, while idiosyncratic volatility is negatively related; accruals have a weaker positive relation. A composite long-short strategy using these four signals has an estimated gross annual return of about 7.5% with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.5. Value, size, asset growth, turnover, and R&D intensity do not pass conventional significance thresholds in this sample.

## Methods
The panel contains one row per stock per month for months 1–120. The universe is the 500 stocks that are constituents of a broad equity index at the end of month 120, with history from month 1 or from index inclusion. All characteristics are known at the end of month `t`: accounting variables are from the latest annual report available at `t`, `idio_vol` is computed from the 60 trading days ending at `t`, and `turnover` is averaged over the 3 months ending at `t`. The target, `ret_next`, is the total return over month `t+1`, so the predictive setup is point-in-time.

I evaluate each feature using the provided monthly Spearman rank IC, averaged over 120 months, with t-stat = mean IC / IC standard deviation × √120. I also use equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month; for negative signals, the economically signed strategy buys the bottom quintile and sells the top quintile. I classify a signal as valid if it has |t-stat| ≈ 2 or greater and economically consistent long-short performance. For the composite strategy, I rank each valid signal, sign-aligns it so higher scores predict higher returns, average the ranks, and trade the top versus bottom quintile each month.

## Results
The signal statistics are summarized below.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | LS mean monthly return | Annualized Sharpe | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| gross_prof | 0.0293 | 3.83 | +0.88% | 1.19 | Valid positive |
| idio_vol | -0.0266 | -3.76 | -0.78% | -1.15 | Valid negative |
| leverage | 0.0137 | 2.56 | +0.50% | 0.99 | Valid positive |
| accruals | 0.0130 | 2.06 | +0.31% | 0.50 | Valid positive |
| asset_growth | -0.0164 | -1.87 | -0.64% | -0.78 | Not primary |
| book_to_market | 0.0029 | 0.54 | -0.06% | -0.11 | Rejected |
| log_mktcap | -0.0043 | -0.67 | -0.09% | -0.14 | Rejected |
| turnover | 0.0042 | 0.64 | +0.15% | 0.23 | Rejected |
| rd_intensity | 0.0018 | 0.30 | +0.12% | 0.20 | Rejected |

Gross profitability is the strongest signal: high gross-profit firms outperform low gross-profit firms, with an average rank IC of 0.0293 and a long-short return of 0.88% per month. Idiosyncratic volatility is the strongest negative signal: high-idio_vol stocks underperform, so a low-minus-high portfolio earns 0.78% per month. Leverage and accruals are weaker but statistically positive: leverage earns 0.50% per month and accruals earns 0.31% per month in long-short quintiles. Asset growth is directionally negative and economically plausible, but its t-stat of -1.87 is just below the conventional 5% threshold, so I do not include it in the primary composite. Book-to-market, log market cap, turnover, and R&D intensity have near-zero ICs and weak long-short performance.

An equal-weighted composite of the four valid signals has an estimated average long-short return of approximately 0.62% per month, or about 7.5% annualized, before transaction costs. Assuming modest cross-signal correlation of roughly 0.2, the composite Sharpe ratio is approximately 1.5 annualized. The strategy is long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of the composite score, rebalanced monthly.

## Discussion
The results are broadly consistent with two well-known cross-sectional effects: a profitability premium and a low-volatility anomaly. Firms with high gross profits tend to earn higher subsequent returns, and firms with high idiosyncratic volatility tend to earn lower subsequent returns. Leverage and accruals are positive in this sample, which is less conventional. The positive accruals relation is opposite to the classic accruals anomaly, suggesting either sample-specific effects, the survivor universe, or a different accounting composition among index constituents. Leverage may be positive because the sample contains only index survivors, so high-leverage firms may not face the same distress risk as in a broader universe.

The absence of a value or size effect is notable. Book-to-market and log market cap have small ICs and long-short returns close to zero. Turnover and R&D intensity also fail to predict returns in this panel. The composite strategy benefits from combining the four valid signals, but most of the return comes from gross profitability and low idiosyncratic volatility. The strategy is likely to be more robust than any single signal, but it remains a gross long-short backtest with no trading-cost or borrow-cost assumptions.

## Limitations
- The universe is built from stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, which introduces survivorship bias and may inflate returns or distort anomaly signs.
- The analysis is in-sample over a single 120-month panel, so the reported ICs and Sharpe ratios are subject to data-snooping and multiple-testing concerns.
- Transaction costs, market impact, shorting fees, and borrow constraints are not modeled; monthly rebalancing would reduce net returns.
- The number of stocks per month grows from 139 to 500, so early-month portfolios are less diversified and may have different risk properties.
- Accounting variables are annual and lagged, so they may miss more timely fundamental changes.
- The strategy assumes equal-weighted quintile portfolios without a risk model, so it may carry unintended factor exposures.
