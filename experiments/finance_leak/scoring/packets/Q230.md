# Leverage as the Only Robust Cross-Sectional Signal in a Broad Equity Index Panel

## Abstract
We examine nine stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Using monthly cross-sectional rank ICs and quintile long-short portfolios, only leverage is a statistically significant signal, with a mean rank IC of 0.0164 and a t-statistic of 2.74. A strategy that buys the top leverage quintile and sells the bottom quintile earns an average of 0.52% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. All other characteristics have t-statistics below conventional significance thresholds and are rejected. The results indicate that high-leverage stocks outperformed low-leverage stocks in this sample.

## Methods
The dataset consists of 34,708 stock-month observations covering 500 stocks and 120 months. The target is `ret_next`. For each of the nine features, we compute the monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next`, average it over 120 months, and calculate a t-statistic as the mean divided by the standard deviation times the square root of 120. A feature is considered valid if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value. We also evaluate equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile each month.

## Results
Only `leverage` passes the significance screen. Its mean rank IC is 0.0164, with a standard deviation of 0.0654 and a t-statistic of 2.74; 59% of monthly ICs are positive. The long-short top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio formed on `leverage` earns 0.52% per month, which annualizes to approximately 6.24%, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. The positive sign indicates that higher-leverage stocks tended to earn higher next-month returns.

The remaining features are not significant. `asset_growth` and `accruals` have the largest t-statistics among the rejected signals at 1.16 and 1.03, respectively, but their IC magnitudes are small and their long-short Sharpe ratios are modest. `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` all have t-statistics below 1.0 and near-zero rank ICs.

## Discussion
The finding that leverage is the only robust signal suggests a risk-based explanation: higher-leverage firms are riskier and may earn higher expected returns. However, the effect is modest in economic terms, and the lack of significance in other well-known characteristics may reflect the specific index universe, the sample period, or the construction of the panel. Because only one signal survives the univariate screen, the strategy should be interpreted as a simple long-short tilt on leverage rather than a diversified multi-signal model.

## Limitations
The universe is constructed from index constituents at the end of month 120, which may introduce survivorship and backfill bias. Transaction costs, market impact, and short-selling constraints are not included in the performance estimates. The analysis is univariate and does not formally adjust for multiple testing across the nine features. The results are specific to this sample period and may not generalize out-of-sample.
