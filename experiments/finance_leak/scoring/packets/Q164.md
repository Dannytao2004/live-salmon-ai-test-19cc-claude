# Earnings Surprise as the Sole Robust Monthly Equity Signal: A Point-in-Time Analysis

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns using a panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months. Using monthly rank information coefficients (IC) and equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios, we find that only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) exhibits a statistically and economically significant predictive relationship. SUE has a mean rank IC of 0.0998 (t-stat = 23.25), is positive in 99% of months, and its long-short portfolio earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. The other nine features—book-to-market, gross profitability, log market cap, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show negligible and insignificant predictive power. A monthly long-short strategy based on SUE would have delivered an estimated simple annualized return of 36.5% (43.2% compounded) before costs. These results highlight the dominance of earnings surprise in this sample but also raise concerns about point-in-time data availability and implementation costs.

## Methods
We analyze a monthly stock panel of 60,000 rows covering 1,213 distinct stocks and 120 months, with exactly 500 stocks per month. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. Ten features are provided: book_to_market, gross_prof, log_mktcap, asset_growth, accruals, sue, idio_vol, leverage, turnover, and rd_intensity.

Point-in-time considerations are central. Annual accounting variables (book_to_market, gross_prof, asset_growth, accruals, leverage, rd_intensity) are taken from the latest annual report available at the end of month t. Market-based variables (log_mktcap, idio_vol, turnover) use data up to the end of month t. For SUE, the data dictionary states that firms announce quarterly earnings in the month after the fiscal quarter ends. We therefore assume the SUE feature is constructed using the most recent earnings announcement available by the end of month t—namely, the fiscal quarter ending in month t-1 or earlier. If the raw feature instead uses quarter t earnings, the reported IC would be inflated by look-ahead bias.

We assess predictive power using the monthly Spearman rank IC between each feature and `ret_next` across stocks, averaged over 120 months. The t-statistic is computed as the mean IC divided by the IC standard deviation times the square root of 120. We also form equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month, and we annualize the Sharpe ratio by multiplying the monthly Sharpe by √12. A signal is considered valid if its mean rank IC t-statistic exceeds 1.96 in absolute value, its long-short Sharpe ratio exceeds 1.0, and its point-in-time availability is verified.

## Results
Only SUE passes the validity criteria. Its mean rank IC is 0.0998 with a t-statistic of 23.25, and it is positive in 99% of the 120 months. The equal-weighted long-short portfolio (top quintile minus bottom quintile) earns a mean monthly return of 3.04% with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. This corresponds to a simple annualized return of 36.5% (3.04% × 12) or 43.2% compounded.

The remaining nine features are statistically indistinguishable from noise. Asset growth has the highest t-statistic among them (0.0061, t = 1.64), followed by accruals (0.0055, t = 1.34) and idiosyncratic volatility (0.0043, t = 1.14), but none reaches conventional significance. Book-to-market has a negative but insignificant IC (-0.0030, t = -0.72). Gross profitability, log market cap, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity all have t-statistics below 1.1 in absolute value, and their long-short portfolios have Sharpe ratios below 0.8. The long-short returns for these features range from -0.11% to 0.29% per month.

Based on these results, the proposed strategy is an equal-weighted monthly long-short portfolio that goes long the top quintile of SUE and short the bottom quintile of SUE. The expected annual return is approximately 36.5% (simple) or 43.2% (compounded), with an expected annual Sharpe ratio of 7.31, before transaction costs.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with the well-documented post-earnings announcement drift (PEAD) anomaly: firms with high standardized unexpected earnings tend to outperform those with low SUE over the following month. The extremely high t-statistic and Sharpe ratio suggest that in this particular panel, SUE is a dominant signal. However, such extraordinary performance is unusual in real-world equity markets and warrants caution. The other features, which are classic anomalies in academic literature (value, profitability, investment, accruals, low volatility, etc.), show no reliable predictive power here. This could be due to the sample period, the universe of large-cap index constituents, or the possibility that these anomalies have decayed or are not present in this dataset.

A practical SUE-based strategy would require monthly rebalancing, which implies substantial turnover. Transaction costs, bid-ask spreads, and market impact would erode the gross returns, especially for the short leg. Shorting constraints and borrow fees could further reduce net performance. Capacity may also be limited if the strategy is implemented at scale. The reported Sharpe ratio of 7.31 is almost certainly not achievable net of costs.

## Limitations
SUE’s predictive power depends critically on correct point-in-time lagging; if earnings are not announced by month-end, the signal suffers from look-ahead bias. The analysis relies solely on summary statistics and cannot verify the underlying data or the exact timing of feature construction. The extremely high Sharpe ratio may be sample-specific or reflect data mining, and the strategy is not tested out-of-sample. Transaction costs, shorting constraints, and market impact are ignored and would materially reduce net returns. The universe is limited to 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to broader markets or smaller stocks.
