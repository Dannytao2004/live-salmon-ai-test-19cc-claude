# Point-in-Time Cross-Sectional Signals in a Monthly Equity Panel: Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
I evaluate ten stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Three signals are valid on point-in-time and statistical grounds: gross profitability is positively related to future returns, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negatively related. Analyst forecast revisions show extremely strong predictive statistics but are recorded at the end of month t+1, making them look-ahead and unusable for predicting month t+1 returns. A composite long-short strategy that buys high gross profitability, low asset growth, and low idiosyncratic volatility is expected to earn about 12% annually with a Sharpe ratio near 2.0, before transaction costs.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations from 120 months, with 500 stocks per month. The target is `ret_next`, the stock’s total return over month t+1. Features are stock characteristics observed at or before the end of month t, except where the data dictionary indicates forward-looking information. I classify a feature as a valid signal if it is available at the end of month t, has a plausible economic rationale, and shows a statistically reliable rank IC and long-short return spread. The main statistics are the average monthly Spearman rank IC, its t-statistic across months, the equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile mean monthly return, and the annualized Sharpe ratio of that long-short portfolio.

The rejected features are those with weak or insignificant rank IC t-statistics, or those with a timing mismatch that makes them unavailable at the forecast date. For the composite strategy, I orient each valid signal in its predictive direction: high `gross_prof`, low `asset_growth`, and low `idio_vol`. Each month, stocks are ranked cross-sectionally on each signal, converted to equal-weighted ranks, and combined into a composite score. The strategy goes long the top quintile of the composite and short the bottom quintile, with equal weights within each leg and monthly rebalancing.

## Results
The strongest statistically valid point-in-time signal is `gross_prof`, with a mean rank IC of 0.0378 (t = 6.07), positive IC in 70% of months, and a long-short mean monthly return of 1.16% (annualized Sharpe 1.94). This indicates that firms with higher gross profits relative to assets tend to outperform.

`asset_growth` is a valid negative signal. Its mean rank IC is -0.0303 (t = -4.29), with IC positive in only 39% of months and a top-minus-bottom long-short return of -0.99% per month (annualized Sharpe -1.48). Oriented correctly, low asset growth outperforms high asset growth, consistent with the investment anomaly.

`idio_vol` is also a valid negative signal. Its mean rank IC is -0.0261 (t = -4.19), with IC positive in 35% of months and a top-minus-bottom long-short return of -0.74% per month (annualized Sharpe -1.20). Low idiosyncratic volatility stocks outperform high idiosyncratic volatility stocks.

`analyst_rev` has by far the strongest reported statistics: mean rank IC 0.2963, t = 80.29, positive in 100% of months, and a long-short Sharpe of 24.23. However, the data dictionary states that it is the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, recorded at the end of month t+1. It is therefore contemporaneous with the target return and cannot be used at the end of month t. I reject it as a look-ahead-biased signal.

The remaining features are statistically weak: `book_to_market` (IC 0.0027, t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` (IC 0.0055, t = 1.26), `accruals` (IC 0.0015, t = 0.36), `leverage` (IC -0.0011, t = -0.30), `turnover` (IC 0.0006, t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` (IC -0.0004, t = -0.10). None has sufficient standalone evidence in this panel to be treated as a valid next-month return predictor.

A simple equal-weighted composite of the three valid signals would have a monthly long-short mean of roughly 0.96% if the individual spreads combine with modest diversification. After accounting for likely positive but imperfect correlations among quality and low-risk signals, I estimate an expected annual return of about 12% and an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.0 for the composite long-short strategy, before costs.

## Discussion
The results are economically coherent. Gross profitability is a quality signal: profitable firms generate stronger cash flows and may be mispriced if investors underreact to fundamental profitability. Asset growth captures the investment channel: firms that expand their asset base aggressively often subsequently earn lower returns, consistent with overinvestment or empire-building behavior. Idiosyncratic volatility captures the low-volatility anomaly: high-volatility stocks tend to be overpriced or subject to lottery-like demand, leading to lower subsequent returns.

The analyst revision result is a useful caution. It is the most powerful column in the panel, but its power comes from using information from month t+1 to explain a return realized over month t+1. Including it would produce an implausibly high Sharpe ratio and would not survive a real-time implementation test. The other classic characteristics—value, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not show reliable next-month predictive power in this particular panel, despite being economically motivated in other samples.

The composite strategy focuses on three signals that are observable at month-end, economically distinct, and statistically significant. It is dollar-neutral and equal-weighted, which reduces reliance on a single factor. Because I cannot observe the correlation matrix of the signal returns, the combined performance estimate is approximate. Still, the standalone Sharpe ratios of the valid signals are between 1.20 and 1.94, so a diversified composite should plausibly achieve a Sharpe ratio around 2.0 with an annual return near 12% before frictions.

## Limitations
The analysis is based only on summary statistics, so I cannot inspect outliers, missing data, or nonlinear relationships. The sample covers 500 index constituents per month, which may not generalize to microcaps or non-index stocks. Transaction costs, shorting borrow fees, market impact, and capacity constraints are not modeled and would reduce net performance. The composite Sharpe estimate assumes no access to the full cross-sectional correlation matrix and is therefore approximate. The panel contains only 120 months, so estimates may be period-specific and subject to data mining. Index membership rules may introduce selection effects even though delisted stocks remain in the data through their removal. Finally, `analyst_rev` is excluded for look-ahead bias, but other subtle timing or restatement issues could remain in annual-report-based features.
