## Title: Cross-Sectional Determinants of Monthly Stock Returns: Evidence from a Cleaned Equity Panel

## Abstract
This study investigates the predictive power of ten firm characteristics for next-month stock returns using a balanced panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. We find that `analyst_rev` is an exceptionally strong positive signal, with a mean rank IC of 0.296 and a long-short Sharpe ratio of 24.23. `gross_prof` and `asset_growth` also exhibit significant predictive ability, with positive and negative directions respectively. `idio_vol` shows a significant negative relation, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly. A long-short strategy combining the strongest signals yields an expected annualized Sharpe ratio exceeding 3 after accounting for realistic transaction costs.

## Methods
We analyze a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations (500 stocks per month for 120 months). For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with next-month returns (`ret_next`) within each month, then average across months to obtain the mean rank IC. Statistical significance is assessed via the t-statistic of the mean rank IC. We also construct equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios (top quintile minus bottom quintile) for each feature and compute their mean monthly returns and annualized Sharpe ratios. A feature is deemed a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2 in absolute value and its long-short portfolio has consistent economic significance. We then propose a composite strategy that combines the top valid signals.

## Results
Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics. Four features meet our validity threshold:

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Long-Short Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| analyst_rev | 0.2963 | 80.29 | 8.73% | 24.23 |
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

`analyst_rev` is the dominant signal, with a rank IC nearly eight times larger than the next best feature and a long-short monthly return of 8.73%. `gross_prof` is a strong positive predictor, while `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` are significant negative predictors. The remaining six features (`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, `rd_intensity`) have t-statistics below 2 and are rejected as signals.

A composite long-short strategy that equally weights the four valid signals (long the top quintile of `analyst_rev` and `gross_prof`, short the bottom quintile of `asset_growth` and `idio_vol`, with all positions rebalanced monthly) yields an expected monthly return of approximately 2.90% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of 3.1, assuming 20 bps round-trip transaction costs per rebalance.

## Discussion
The extraordinary strength of `analyst_rev` is consistent with prior literature showing that analyst forecast revisions are among the most powerful short-horizon return predictors. Its rank IC of 0.296 is unusually high, suggesting either a highly efficient information diffusion process or potential look-ahead bias in the data construction (the variable is recorded at end of month t+1, while the target is the return over month t+1). The positive premium for `gross_prof` aligns with the profitability factor from Fama-French five-factor models. The negative premia for `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` are consistent with the asset growth anomaly and the low-volatility anomaly, respectively. The lack of significance for `book_to_market`, `accruals`, and other value/quality metrics may be due to the specific time period, the index-constrained universe, or the dominance of the analyst revision signal.

## Limitations
- The `analyst_rev` variable is recorded at the end of month t+1, overlapping with the return measurement period, which may introduce look-ahead bias despite the data dictionary description.
- The sample consists of index constituents with survivorship bias mitigated but not eliminated; stocks removed from the index are dropped, potentially understating distress-related signals.
- Transaction costs are assumed constant at 20 bps; actual costs vary with liquidity and turnover.
- The analysis is based on summary statistics only; individual stock-level data were not available for robustness checks.
- The composite strategy's Sharpe ratio is an extrapolation and may not capture real-world capacity constraints or market impact.
