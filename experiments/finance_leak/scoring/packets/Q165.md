# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: Evidence from Ten Firm Characteristics

## Abstract

We examine ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-months). Analyst forecast revisions dominate all other signals, with a mean rank IC of 0.296 (t = 80.3) and a long-short quintile spread of 8.73% per month. Gross profitability and asset growth are moderately strong and statistically reliable signals, while idiosyncratic volatility is a weaker but consistent negative predictor. The remaining six characteristics—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show rank ICs indistinguishable from zero and are rejected as standalone signals. A composite strategy combining the four valid signals is expected to deliver roughly 70–80% annualized return with a Sharpe ratio near 3, though this estimate is optimistic because it ignores transaction costs and the suspiciously strong analyst-revision effect.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 observations: 500 index constituents per month over 120 months, with each stock's characteristics measured at month-end *t* and returns measured over month *t+1*. Because the universe is point-in-time and includes stocks through their final month (including distress/delisting returns), the sample is largely free of survivorship bias.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we used the provided monthly Spearman rank IC (correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks within a month), averaged over 120 months, with a t-statistic computed as mean(IC)/std(IC) × √120. We treat |t| ≥ 2 as statistically reliable. We also used the reported equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile long-short returns and their annualized Sharpe ratios to gauge economic magnitude.

**Validity criteria.** A feature is deemed a valid signal if (i) the mean rank IC is economically non-trivial (|IC| ≳ 0.02), (ii) the t-statistic exceeds 2 in absolute value, and (iii) the sign of the long-short return is consistent with the sign of the IC. Features failing any criterion are rejected.

**Strategy construction.** We form a composite score by cross-sectionally standardizing (z-scoring) each valid signal within month and summing with signs aligned to the predicted direction: +gross_prof, +analyst_rev, −asset_growth, −idio_vol. We then buy the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly. Expected performance is approximated by summing the individual long-short spreads (adjusted for correlation) and by scaling Sharpe ratios by √12 for annualization.

**Important caveat on analyst_rev.** The data dictionary states `analyst_rev` is the change in the FY1 EPS forecast during month *t+1*, scaled by price, recorded at the end of month *t+1*. The target is the return over month *t+1*. This means the signal and the return are measured over the *same* month, so the feature is not fully known at the start of the return window. This creates look-ahead contamination that almost certainly inflates its measured predictive power.

## Results

**Signal-level results.** Four features passed our validity screen:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | L/S monthly | L/S Sharpe | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| analyst_rev | 0.2963 | 80.29 | 8.73% | 24.23 | positive |
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 | positive |
| asset_growth | −0.0303 | −4.29 | −0.99% | −1.48 | negative |
| idio_vol | −0.0261 | −4.19 | −0.74% | −1.20 | negative |

The analyst-revision signal is extraordinary: positive in 100% of months, an IC t-stat of 80, and a long-short spread of 8.73% per month. Such uniformity is implausible for a genuinely tradeable signal and reinforces the look-ahead concern noted above. Gross profitability (positive) and asset growth (negative) behave exactly as the asset-pricing literature predicts (profitability premium; asset-growth/investment anomaly), with IC t-stats of 6.1 and −4.3 respectively. Idiosyncratic volatility is a reliable negative predictor, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.

**Rejected signals.** Six features showed no reliable relationship: book_to_market (IC 0.0027, t = 0.68), log_mktcap (0.0055, t = 1.26), accruals (0.0015, t = 0.36), leverage (−0.0011, t = −0.30), turnover (0.0006, t = 0.14), and rd_intensity (−0.0004, t = −0.10). None reached |t| = 2, and all had long-short spreads below 0.25% per month.

**Composite strategy.** Combining the four valid signals (with analyst_rev down-weighted to reflect contamination), we estimate a long-short spread of roughly 5–7% per month. If we naively sum the four spreads, we get ~8.2% monthly; adjusting for the high correlation between analyst_rev and the return window and for signal correlation, a more conservative 5.5–6.5% monthly spread (≈ 65–90% annualized) is reasonable, with a Sharpe ratio of roughly 2.5–3.5 after diversification across four imperfectly correlated signals.

## Discussion

The results align well with established empirical asset pricing. Profitability (gross_prof) and investment (asset_growth) are two of the most robust anomalies in the literature, and both appear here with the expected signs and meaningful t-statistics. The low-volatility effect (idio_vol) is also well documented. The failure of book-to-market, accruals, and leverage to predict returns in this sample is notable—value and accrual anomalies have weakened in recent decades, and this panel (likely large-cap index constituents) may be too homogeneous for them to show through.

The dominant finding—analyst revisions—must be interpreted with caution. Its perfect monthly hit rate is a red flag for look-ahead bias, since the feature is recorded contemporaneously with the return window. A realistic implementable version of the signal (using only revisions known before month *t+1*) would likely show a much smaller IC, perhaps 0.03–0.06, consistent with the academic literature on analyst revision momentum.

A composite strategy would likely be dominated by analyst_rev, making it fragile to that signal's contamination. A cleaner, more defensible strategy would weight gross_prof, asset_growth, and idio_vol more heavily, targeting a lower but more credible Sharpe ratio around 1.5–2.0.

## Limitations

1. **Look-ahead contamination in analyst_rev**: the feature is measured over the same month as the target return, so its predictive power is not implementable in real time.
2. **No transaction costs**: monthly rebalancing of quintile portfolios, especially in smaller/high-turnover names, would erode a substantial fraction of gross returns.
3. **Limited universe**: only 500 large-cap index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to small caps or to non-index stocks.
4. **No risk-factor adjustment**: long-short spreads are raw returns, not alphas; some of the profit may be compensation for market, size, or value exposures.
5. **Single sample period**: 120 months is a moderate window, and the apparent strength or weakness of signals may reflect period-specific conditions rather than stable relationships.
6. **No out-of-sample test**: all statistics are in-sample, and the strategy's expected performance is an extrapolation.
