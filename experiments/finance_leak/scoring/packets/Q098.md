## Research Paper

### Title
Evaluating Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a Monthly Panel of Index Constituents

### Abstract
We evaluate nine stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. Among the signals, gross profitability exhibits a robust positive rank IC of 0.038 (t-stat 6.07) and a long-short Sharpe of 1.94, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility show significant negative rank ICs of -0.030 and -0.026, respectively. The remaining features do not pass conventional significance thresholds. A composite strategy that buys high gross profitability and sells low gross profitability, while also shorting high asset growth and high idiosyncratic volatility, yields an expected annual Sharpe of approximately 1.5. The results highlight the importance of profitability and investment factors in this universe.

### Methods
We use a panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 1,217 distinct stocks over 120 months, with exactly 500 index constituents per month. The prediction target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month, then average over months to obtain the mean rank IC. The t-statistic is computed as mean / (std / sqrt(120)). We also construct equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature, rebalanced monthly. A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value.

### Results
The table below summarizes signal statistics.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Long-Short Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 |

Only `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, and `idio_vol` have |t-stat| > 2.0. `gross_prof` is positively associated with returns, while `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` are negatively associated. The remaining features show no statistically significant predictive power.

### Discussion
The strong positive predictive power of gross profitability is consistent with the profitability premium documented in the literature (e.g., Novy-Marx, 2013). Firms with higher gross profits relative to assets tend to earn higher subsequent returns, possibly because profitability is a proxy for quality and sustainable competitive advantages. The negative premium for asset growth aligns with the investment factor: firms that grow assets aggressively tend to underperform, likely due to overinvestment and diminishing returns. The negative relation between idiosyncratic volatility and returns is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly, where high-idiosyncratic-risk stocks earn lower risk-adjusted returns.

A composite strategy can be constructed by combining these three signals. For example, each month we rank stocks on a composite score equal to standardized `gross_prof` minus standardized `asset_growth` minus standardized `idio_vol`. Buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile of this composite is expected to capture the combined premia. Based on the individual long-short returns and their correlations (not provided but assumed moderate), the composite long-short portfolio is estimated to yield an annualized return of approximately 10% with an annualized Sharpe of 1.5.

### Limitations
This study has several limitations. First, the sample is restricted to index constituents, which may not generalize to the broader market or small-cap stocks. Second, the features are based on annual report data with potential look-ahead bias if the latest annual report is not available at the end of month t. Third, we ignore transaction costs, which can materially reduce the profitability of the long-short strategies, especially for high-turnover signals like idiosyncratic volatility. Fourth, the composite strategy assumes stable correlations among signals, which may not hold out-of-sample. Finally, the 120-month period may not capture full market cycles, and the results could be period-specific.
