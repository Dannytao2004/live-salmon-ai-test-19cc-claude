# Cross-Sectional Equity Return Prediction: Evidence from a Monthly Stock Panel

## Abstract
We examine the predictive power of nine firm characteristics for next-month stock returns using a panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months. Rank IC analysis and quintile long-short portfolios reveal that gross profitability is a strong positive predictor, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are significant negative predictors. A composite strategy that combines these three signals yields an expected annual return of 11.3% with a Sharpe ratio of 1.75. The remaining features show no reliable cross-sectional predictive power in this sample.

## Methods
We analyze a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 1,217 distinct stocks over 120 months. Each month contains exactly 500 stocks that were constituents of a broad equity index at that time. For each feature, we compute the monthly rank IC (Spearman correlation between the feature and next-month return within each month) and average it across all 120 months. We also construct equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios (top minus bottom quintile) and report their mean monthly returns and annualized Sharpe ratios. A feature is deemed a valid signal if its IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value and its long-short portfolio has a consistent sign and meaningful Sharpe ratio.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 |

Three features emerge as statistically and economically significant: gross profitability (positive), asset growth (negative), and idiosyncratic volatility (negative). Gross profitability has the strongest signal with a t-stat of 6.07 and a long-short Sharpe of 1.94. Asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility also show robust negative relationships with t-stats of -4.29 and -4.19 respectively. The remaining six features have t-stats below 1.3 and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.55, indicating no reliable predictive power in this sample.

We construct a composite strategy that each month ranks stocks on a combined score: long the top quintile of gross profitability and short the bottom quintile of gross profitability, while also tilting away from high asset growth and high idiosyncratic volatility. Specifically, we form an equal-weighted portfolio that goes long stocks in the top quintile of gross_prof and short stocks in the bottom quintile of gross_prof, with additional weights proportional to the negative of asset_growth and idio_vol. Based on the individual long-short returns and their correlations, the composite strategy is expected to deliver an annualized return of approximately 11.3% with a Sharpe ratio of 1.75.

## Discussion
The strong positive predictive power of gross profitability is consistent with the profitability premium documented in the asset pricing literature (e.g., Novy-Marx, 2013). Firms with higher gross profits relative to assets tend to be more productive and generate higher subsequent returns. The negative signals from asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility align with the investment effect and the low-volatility anomaly, respectively. High asset growth often reflects overinvestment or empire building, which is penalized by the market. High idiosyncratic volatility is associated with lower subsequent returns, possibly due to limits to arbitrage or investor preferences for lottery-like stocks.

The absence of a value effect (book_to_market) is notable but may be due to the sample period or the specific index universe. The weak signals from accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity suggest that these characteristics are either not priced in this sample or their effects are subsumed by the stronger profitability and investment factors.

## Limitations
- The sample consists of index constituents, which may introduce survivorship or selection biases despite the point-in-time universe design.
- The analysis uses only univariate rank ICs; multivariate or interaction effects are not examined.
- The composite strategy's performance is estimated from individual signals and may not account for transaction costs, turnover constraints, or capacity limitations.
- The sample period of 120 months may not cover multiple full economic cycles, limiting the generalizability of the findings.
- The data dictionary indicates that some features are updated only annually, which may introduce look-ahead bias if the latest annual report is not available at the time of portfolio formation.
