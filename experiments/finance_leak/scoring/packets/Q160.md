# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: Evidence from Nine Accounting and Market Characteristics

## Abstract

Using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-months), I evaluate nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month returns. Only two signals display statistically reliable cross-sectional predictive power: gross profitability (mean rank IC = 0.038, t = 6.07) and asset growth (mean rank IC = −0.030, t = −4.29), with idiosyncratic volatility a third, weaker but reliable negative predictor (mean rank IC = −0.026, t = −4.19). The remaining six characteristics—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show rank ICs indistinguishable from zero. A composite long-short strategy that buys high gross profitability, low asset growth, and low idiosyncratic volatility would have earned roughly 1.5–2.0% per month on an equal-weighted basis, with an annualized Sharpe near 1.5–1.8. The results are consistent with the profitability, investment, and low-risk anomalies documented in the asset-pricing literature.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 observations: 500 stocks per month over 120 months (1,217 distinct stocks). Each month's universe is the point-in-time set of index constituents, with delisted/distressed names retained through their final month, so the panel is free of the survivorship bias that would inflate the performance of strategies tilting toward small, distressed, or volatile stocks.

**Features.** Nine characteristics are available, all lagged to the end of month *t*: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`. The target is the total return over month *t+1* (`ret_next`).

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature I use the pre-computed monthly rank IC (Spearman correlation between feature and `ret_next` within a month), averaged across the 120 months. The t-statistic is the mean IC divided by the IC standard deviation, scaled by √120. I treat |t| ≥ 2 as evidence of a genuine signal and also require a consistent sign across the long-short portfolio returns. Long-short returns are equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile portfolios formed monthly.

**Magnitudes.** Because rank ICs are scale-free, I map them to portfolio performance using the reported long-short mean monthly returns and annualized Sharpe ratios, which are internally consistent with the ICs (e.g., gross_prof: IC 0.038 → 1.16%/month, Sharpe 1.94).

**Composite strategy.** I combine the surviving signals into a single score, standardizing each feature cross-sectionally each month and averaging, with signs set by the estimated direction. Because I cannot re-simulate the composite, I approximate its performance from the component long-shorts, accounting for the imperfect correlation among them (profitability, investment, and volatility signals are only weakly related).

## Results

**Valid signals.** Three features clear the reliability bar:

- **Gross profitability** (`gross_prof`): mean rank IC = +0.038 (t = 6.07), positive in 70% of months, long-short +1.16%/month, Sharpe 1.94. This is the strongest and most consistent signal in the panel.
- **Asset growth** (`asset_growth`): mean rank IC = −0.030 (t = −4.29), positive in only 39% of months, long-short −0.99%/month, Sharpe −1.48. High asset growth predicts low future returns (the investment anomaly).
- **Idiosyncratic volatility** (`idio_vol`): mean rank IC = −0.026 (t = −4.19), positive in 35% of months, long-short −0.74%/month, Sharpe −1.20. High-volatility stocks underperform (the low-risk/betting-against-beta anomaly).

**Rejected signals.** Six features fail to show reliable predictive power:

- `book_to_market`: IC = 0.003 (t = 0.68), long-short Sharpe 0.23 — no reliable value premium in this panel.
- `log_mktcap`: IC = 0.006 (t = 1.26), Sharpe 0.55 — weak and insignificant size effect.
- `accruals`: IC = 0.002 (t = 0.36), Sharpe 0.36 — no accrual anomaly.
- `leverage`: IC = −0.001 (t = −0.30), Sharpe 0.05 — no reliable distress signal.
- `turnover`: IC = 0.001 (t = 0.14), Sharpe 0.06 — no liquidity/turnover effect.
- `rd_intensity`: IC = −0.000 (t = −0.10), Sharpe 0.32 — no innovation premium.

**Composite strategy.** Combining the three valid signals with equal weights and correct signs, the composite long-short should earn approximately the average of the component returns, adjusted upward for diversification because the signals are only weakly correlated (profitability is a quality signal, asset growth an investment signal, and idio_vol a risk signal). A reasonable estimate is **~1.5% per month (~18–19% annualized)** with an **annualized Sharpe of ~1.6**. This is slightly below the sum of the parts (2.89%/month) due to overlap and slightly above the average (0.97%/month) due to diversification.

## Discussion

The results align closely with the modern empirical asset-pricing literature. Gross profitability is the canonical "quality" signal (Novy-Marx), asset growth is the canonical "investment" signal (Cooper, Gulen, and Schill), and idiosyncratic volatility is the canonical "low-risk" signal (Ang et al.). Their persistence across 120 months, with t-statistics above 4, suggests these are robust features of the cross-section rather than data-mined artifacts.

The absence of a value premium (`book_to_market`) and of the accrual anomaly is notable. Both are well-documented in long-sample historical data, so their failure here may reflect the specific 10-year sample window, the index-constituent universe (which excludes many small, distressed value stocks), or the fact that these anomalies have attenuated in recent decades. The weak size and turnover results are similarly consistent with post-2000 evidence that these effects have largely disappeared.

For practitioners, the implication is that a concentrated, quality-and-investment-focused strategy—long high-profitability, low-asset-growth, low-volatility stocks; short the opposite—would have been the most reliable source of cross-sectional alpha in this panel, while value, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D tilts would have added little.

## Limitations

- The panel covers only 120 months and a single (index-constituent) universe; results may not generalize to other periods or to small-cap/global stocks.
- Signals are evaluated individually; the composite strategy's performance is estimated from component statistics rather than directly simulated, so its Sharpe and return figures are approximations.
- Rank ICs and long-short returns are equal-weighted and ignore transaction costs, borrow costs, and capacity constraints, which would reduce net performance.
- The data provide no information on sector, country, or macro regime, so I cannot test whether the signals are robust across sub-samples or conditions.
- Survivorship is handled (delisted names retained), but the index-constituent filter may still introduce a selection bias relative to the full CRSP-style universe.
