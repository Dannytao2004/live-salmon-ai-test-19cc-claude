# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a 500-Stock Monthly Panel: The Dominance of Earnings Surprise

## Abstract

Using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-month observations, 1,213 distinct stocks), I evaluate ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month returns. Only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) exhibits a robust, economically large cross-sectional relationship with future returns: mean monthly rank IC of 0.100 (t = 23.3), with a positive IC in 99% of months and a top-minus-bottom quintile long-short spread of 3.04% per month (annualized Sharpe 7.31). The remaining nine characteristics — value, profitability, size, growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity — show rank ICs indistinguishable from zero (|t| < 1.7) and long-short portfolios with annualized Sharpes below 0.75. I propose a strategy that tilts toward high-SUE stocks, optionally hedged against the bottom SUE quintile, and estimate 36.5% annualized return with a Sharpe near 7.3 before costs, with meaningful degradation expected under realistic turnover and capacity constraints.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 500 stocks per month for 120 months, drawn from the point-in-time constituents of a broad equity index. Stocks are retained through their final month (including delisting/distress returns), which mitigates survivorship bias. The prediction target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1.

**Signal evaluation.** For each of the ten features, I use the provided within-month Spearman rank IC averaged across the 120 months, its cross-month standard deviation, and the t-statistic computed as mean/σ × √120. I also use the equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile long-short monthly return and its annualized Sharpe ratio. A signal is deemed *valid* only if it clears a joint bar: |t-stat| ≥ ~2 (roughly 5% significance), a long-short Sharpe materially above zero, and directional consistency across months (IC sign frequency far from 50%).

**Strategy construction.** The proposed strategy is a long-only tilt toward the top SUE quintile, with an explicit hedged variant (long top quintile, short bottom quintile) for context. Expected performance is anchored on the reported long-short statistics, with a haircut for transaction costs and implementation frictions discussed in Limitations.

**Interpretation of the data.** Because the panel is a fixed 500-stock index universe, all signals are cross-sectional (relative, not time-series) and the strategies are implicitly market-neutral in the hedged form and market-beta-exposed in the long-only form.

## Results

**Signal screening.** Only SUE survives screening. Its mean rank IC is 0.0998 with a t-statistic of 23.25 and a positive IC in 99% of the 120 months — an extraordinarily stable relationship for a monthly equity signal. The corresponding long-short portfolio earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 7.31, implying an annualized gross spread of roughly 36.5%.

**Rejected signals.** Every other feature fails. `asset_growth` (IC 0.0061, t = 1.64) and `accruals` (IC 0.0055, t = 1.34) are the strongest of the remainder but fall short of conventional significance and produce long-short Sharpes of only 0.72 and 0.70. `gross_prof` (IC 0.0039, t = 0.93) and `idio_vol` (IC 0.0043, t = 1.14) are weak and unstable. `book_to_market` (IC −0.0030, t = −0.72), `leverage` (IC −0.0043, t = −1.00), `log_mktcap` (IC 0.0001, t = 0.02), `turnover` (IC 0.0001, t = 0.02), and `rd_intensity` (IC −0.0002, t = −0.05) are statistically indistinguishable from noise. Notably, several of these are classic anomalies (value, profitability, accruals, low-volatility) that appear in the literature; their failure here is consistent with the panel being a large-cap index universe where such premia are weak or arbitraged, and with the sample size (120 months) limiting power.

**Strategy performance.** A long-top-SUE-quintile portfolio captures roughly half the long-short spread as raw alpha, or about 1.5% per month, before considering market beta. The market-neutral long-short version earns 3.04% per month gross (36.5% annualized) at a Sharpe near 7.3. Given that the long-short return series has a monthly standard deviation implied by the Sharpe of roughly 4.2%, drawdowns are small in sample but the signal will not persist at this magnitude out of sample.

## Discussion

The results are striking in their concentration: a single accounting-based signal, SUE, carries essentially all of the cross-sectional predictive power in this panel, while nine other well-known characteristics are flat. This is consistent with the post-earnings-announcement drift (PEAD) literature, in which earnings surprises predict returns for weeks to months after announcement. The 99%-of-months hit rate and t-statistic of 23 are unusually high for a monthly equity signal and suggest the panel may have been constructed with a strong, possibly idealized, PEAD effect. The absence of value, profitability, size, and low-volatility premia is plausible for a large-cap index universe, where these effects are known to be attenuated, but the near-zero ICs (t < 1) for all nine are also consistent with a deliberately sparse signal design.

From a portfolio perspective, the practical takeaway is to concentrate exposure on SUE. A simple long-only tilt is easy to implement but retains market beta; the hedged long-short version isolates the signal but requires shorting and bears the associated costs. Given the equal-weighted quintile construction and a 500-stock universe, turnover is likely moderate (earnings surprises decay over quarters), which supports the strategy's viability net of costs, though a full 36.5% annualized gross return is almost certainly an upper bound.

## Limitations

- The panel covers only 120 months and a fixed 500-stock large-cap universe; results may not generalize to small caps or other periods.
- The reported statistics are in-sample and pre-cost; no transaction costs, borrow costs, or slippage are modeled, and the implied Sharpe of 7.3 is implausibly high for a live strategy.
- All signals are evaluated in isolation; no multivariate regression or correlation analysis among features is available, so overlapping or redundant signals cannot be diagnosed.
- SUE depends on analyst consensus forecasts, which may be unavailable, stale, or biased for some firms, and the data may embed look-ahead or idealized announcement timing.
- The long-short quintile returns are equal-weighted with no capacity, liquidity, or shorting-constraint analysis, so realized performance for a sizeable portfolio would be lower.
