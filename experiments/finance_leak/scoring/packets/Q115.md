# Look-Ahead Bias in Analyst Revisions Masks the Absence of Robust Cross-Sectional Return Predictors

## Abstract
This study evaluates ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns in a 120-month panel of 500 stocks. The analyst revision feature (`analyst_rev`) has an extremely strong rank IC of 0.2958 (t-stat 89.59) and a long-short Sharpe of 26.38, but it is recorded at the end of the return month and therefore uses future information. Excluding this look-ahead-biased feature, no characteristic has a rank IC t-statistic near conventional significance thresholds. The evidence suggests there are no robust tradeable cross-sectional signals in the remaining features.

## Methods
The dataset is a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations, with 500 stocks per month over 120 months. For each feature, I use the provided cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next`, averaged across months. Signal significance is assessed using the reported IC t-stat: `mean IC / std IC * sqrt(120)`. I also consider equal-weighted top-minus-bottom quintile long-short returns. A feature is treated as valid only if it is statistically significant at conventional levels and is usable at the end of month `t` without look-ahead bias.

## Results
`analyst_rev` is the only feature with overwhelming statistical evidence: mean rank IC 0.2958, IC t-stat 89.59, positive IC in 100% of months, monthly long-short return 8.66%, and annualized Sharpe 26.38. However, the data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is the change in mean FY1 EPS forecast during month `t+1`, recorded at the end of month `t+1`. Because it uses information from the same month as the return it is supposed to predict, it cannot be used in a real-time trading strategy without look-ahead bias.

Among the remaining nine features, none reaches conventional statistical significance. The largest absolute IC t-stat is only 1.64 for `asset_growth`, followed by 1.34 for `accruals`. Most other features have t-stats below 1.0 in absolute value. While `asset_growth` and `accruals` show modest positive long-short Sharpe ratios of 0.72 and 0.70, their rank ICs are weak and not robust after accounting for multiple testing across ten features.

## Discussion
The only apparently strong signal, `analyst_rev`, is invalid because it is contemporaneous with or forward-looking relative to the target return. Its near-perfect backtest statistics are a classic symptom of look-ahead bias rather than a tradeable signal. After removing this feature, no robust predictor remains. The weak positive long-short returns for `asset_growth` and `accruals` are not statistically convincing and are also economically inconsistent with the usual asset-growth and accruals anomalies, further suggesting they are noise.

## Limitations
- Only summary statistics were available, so additional robustness tests, decay analysis, and portfolio simulations could not be performed.
- No transaction costs, short-sale constraints, borrowing costs, or capacity limits are considered.
- The sample is restricted to 500 broad-index stocks per month, so results may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks.
- Multiple testing across ten features can produce weak spurious patterns such as the modest positive returns for `asset_growth` and `accruals`.
