# Leverage as a Cross-Sectional Predictor of Next-Month Equity Returns

## Abstract
I evaluate nine stock characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns in a 120-month panel of up to 500 index stocks. Using monthly rank ICs and quintile long-short portfolios, only leverage has a statistically significant average rank IC of 0.0164 (t-stat = 2.74). All other features have rank IC t-statistics below conventional significance thresholds. A monthly long-short quintile strategy based on leverage earns 0.52% per month, or 6.24% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92.

## Methods
The dataset contains 34,708 stock-month observations from 500 stocks over 120 months. The prediction target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over the next month. I use two methods to evaluate each feature. First, for each month I compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next`; I then average these monthly rank ICs over the 120 months and compute a t-statistic as `mean IC / (IC std / sqrt(120))`. Second, I form equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month and compute the return of the top quintile minus the bottom quintile. A feature is considered a valid signal if its average rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| book_to_market | -0.0017 | -0.28 | -0.05% | -0.08 |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | 0.12% | 0.22 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | -0.07% | -0.14 |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | 0.37% | 0.74 |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | 0.24% | 0.41 |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | 0.13% | 0.19 |
| leverage | 0.0164 | 2.74 | 0.52% | 0.92 |
| turnover | -0.0005 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.23 |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | -0.06% | -0.11 |

Only `leverage` passes the significance threshold. Its positive average rank IC indicates that stocks with higher total debt to total assets tend to have higher next-month returns. Although `asset_growth`, `accruals`, and `idio_vol` show positive long-short returns, their rank IC t-statistics are well below 2.0, so they cannot be statistically distinguished from noise in this sample.

## Discussion
The positive predictability of leverage is consistent with a risk-based interpretation: higher-leverage firms may earn higher expected returns as compensation for financial distress or default risk. However, the effect is modest, and the other widely studied characteristics do not show statistically significant rank ICs in this sample. This may reflect the specific index universe, the sample period, or the backfill/survivorship structure of the data.

## Limitations
The leverage signal is only modestly significant and would be marginal under conservative multiple-testing adjustments. Performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, short-selling costs, and market impact. The sample is limited to stocks that are index constituents at the end of the sample, which may introduce survivorship and backfill biases.
