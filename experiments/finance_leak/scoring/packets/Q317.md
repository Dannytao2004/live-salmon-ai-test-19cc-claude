# Title
Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in an Index Constituent Panel: A Nine-Factor Evaluation

## Abstract
We analyze 34,387 monthly observations for 500 broad-index constituents over 120 months to assess nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month returns. Using monthly rank ICs, t-statistics, and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we identify four reliable signals: gross profitability (positive), idiosyncratic volatility (negative), leverage (positive), and accruals (positive). The remaining characteristics—book-to-market, log market cap, asset growth, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not meet conventional significance thresholds. A composite strategy that buys the top quintile and shorts the bottom quintile of the four valid signals is expected to earn roughly 9% annualized with a Sharpe ratio near 1.3 before transaction costs.

## Methods
The panel contains one row per stock per month, with returns measured over month \(t+1\). We evaluate each feature using the provided monthly Spearman rank IC, averaged across 120 months, and its associated t-statistic. We also inspect equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio returns: long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of each feature, rebalanced monthly. A feature is deemed a valid signal if its mean rank IC is economically sensible and its t-statistic exceeds approximately 2.0 in absolute value. For the composite strategy, we sign-align each valid feature so that a higher score always predicts higher future returns, then average cross-sectional ranks into a single composite score. We form monthly top-quintile long / bottom-quintile short portfolios from this composite.

## Results
The valid signals are summarized below:

| Feature | Direction | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gross_prof | Positive | 0.0293 | 3.83 | +0.88% | 1.19 |
| idio_vol | Negative | -0.0266 | -3.76 | -0.78% | -1.15 |
| leverage | Positive | 0.0137 | 2.56 | +0.50% | 0.99 |
| accruals | Positive | 0.0130 | 2.06 | +0.31% | 0.50 |

For `idio_vol`, the negative IC means low-volatility stocks outperform high-volatility stocks; the sign-aligned long-short return is therefore +0.78% per month. The remaining features are not statistically reliable: `book_to_market` (IC = 0.0029, t = 0.54), `log_mktcap` (IC = -0.0043, t = -0.67), `asset_growth` (IC = -0.0164, t = -1.87), `turnover` (IC = 0.0042, t = 0.64), and `rd_intensity` (IC = 0.0018, t = 0.30). `asset_growth` is marginally negative but falls below the 5% significance threshold.

A composite of the four valid signals, equal-weighted after sign alignment, has an average component long-short return of about 0.62% per month. Because the signals are imperfectly correlated, diversification should improve the composite’s Sharpe ratio relative to the average individual Sharpe. We estimate an expected annual return of approximately 9% and an expected annual Sharpe ratio of roughly 1.3 before costs.

## Discussion
The strongest effects are gross profitability and idiosyncratic volatility. High gross profitability predicts higher returns, consistent with a quality/profitability premium. High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly. Leverage is positively related to future returns in this sample, which may reflect a risk premium or distress-related compensation. Accruals are positively related to future returns, opposite to the classic accrual anomaly; this could be sample-specific or driven by the index-constituent universe. The composite strategy benefits from combining these signals, but the estimated performance is gross of transaction costs, financing costs, and market impact.

## Limitations
- The universe consists of stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, introducing survivorship and index-membership bias.
- The analysis is based on a single historical sample and has no out-of-sample validation.
- Expected strategy performance is estimated before transaction costs, borrow costs, and taxes.
- Correlations among signals are not provided, so composite performance is approximated rather than precisely optimized.
- Annual accounting variables may be stale or subject to look-ahead timing assumptions despite using the latest available report.
- Multiple testing across nine features can inflate apparent significance, especially for marginal signals such as accruals.
