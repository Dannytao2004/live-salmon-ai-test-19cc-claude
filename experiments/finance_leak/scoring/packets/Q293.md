# Title: Valid Cross-Sectional Signals in a Monthly Equity Panel: Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We analyze a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 500 index constituents per month from month 1 to 120. Using rank information coefficients (ICs) and equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios, we identify three statistically and economically valid return predictors: gross profitability (positive), asset growth (negative), and idiosyncratic volatility (negative). Several other characteristics, including book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity, show weak or insignificant predictive power. Analyst revision has an implausibly strong relation to next-month returns because it is recorded at the end of month t+1 and therefore embeds look-ahead information; we reject it as a tradable signal. A composite long-short strategy combining the three valid signals has an estimated annualized return of about 11.5% and an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.9 before costs.

## Methods
We use the provided monthly stock panel with 120 months and a point-in-time universe of 500 index constituents per month. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. For each feature, we evaluate the average Spearman rank IC across stocks within each month, its t-statistic, the fraction of months with positive IC, and the mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio of an equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile.

We classify a feature as a valid signal if it is (i) available at the end of month t, (ii) statistically significant with |t-stat| above roughly 2, and (iii) economically meaningful in the long-short portfolio. We reject features that are unavailable at formation, statistically insignificant, or economically negligible. For the composite strategy, we equal-weight the valid signals after aligning their directions: long high `gross_prof`, long low `asset_growth`, and long low `idio_vol`, while shorting the opposite quintiles.

## Results
The strongest valid signals are:

- **gross_prof**: mean rank IC = 0.0378, t-stat = 6.07, positive direction. The top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94.
- **asset_growth**: mean rank IC = -0.0303, t-stat = -4.29, negative direction. The raw top-minus-bottom quintile return is -0.99% per month, so a negative-direction strategy (long low growth, short high growth) earns +0.99% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.48.
- **idio_vol**: mean rank IC = -0.0261, t-stat = -4.19, negative direction. The raw top-minus-bottom quintile return is -0.74% per month, so a negative-direction strategy (long low volatility, short high volatility) earns +0.74% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.20.

Several features fail our validity criteria. `book_to_market` has a rank IC of 0.0027 (t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` has 0.0055 (t = 1.26), `accruals` has 0.0015 (t = 0.36), `leverage` has -0.0011 (t = -0.30), `turnover` has 0.0006 (t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` has -0.0004 (t = -0.10). None of these are statistically significant. `analyst_rev` shows an extraordinary rank IC of 0.2963, t-stat of 80.29, monthly long-short return of 8.73%, and Sharpe of 24.23, but it is recorded at the end of month t+1 and is therefore not available when forming portfolios at the end of month t. It is a look-ahead signal and must be rejected for predicting `ret_next`.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with well-documented anomalies. Gross profitability captures a quality premium: profitable firms outperform unprofitable ones. Asset growth captures the investment anomaly: firms that grow assets aggressively tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility captures the low-volatility anomaly: high-volatility stocks underperform on a risk-adjusted basis. These three signals are plausibly distinct: one is a profitability/quality signal, one is an investment/discount-rate signal, and one is a risk/volatility signal.

A simple equal-weighted composite long-short strategy using these three valid signals would have earned roughly (1.16% + 0.99% + 0.74%) / 3 ≈ 0.96% per month, or about 11.5% annualized before costs. Assuming modest positive correlation among the signals, the combined annualized Sharpe is approximately 1.9. This is attractive but should be treated as an in-sample estimate. The analyst revision result is a useful diagnostic: its implausible strength and perfect monthly hit rate signal a timing mismatch rather than a tradable edge.

## Limitations
- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 large-cap index constituents per month, limiting generalizability to broader markets.
- The composite strategy is evaluated in-sample and does not account for transaction costs, borrow costs, or liquidity constraints.
- Correlations among the valid signals are not provided, so the combined Sharpe ratio is an approximation.
- The analyst revision feature illustrates the importance of point-in-time data verification; its apparent predictive power is not tradable at month t.
- Results may be affected by multiple testing, and no out-of-sample validation is performed.
