# Which Cross-Sectional Signals Actually Predict Next-Month Returns? Evidence from a 120-Month Index Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Using monthly rank ICs and long-short quintile portfolios, we find three implementable signals: gross profitability is positively predictive, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negatively predictive. Analyst revisions show an extraordinary apparent IC, but they are recorded at the end of month \(t+1\), the same period as the target return, so we reject them as look-ahead information. A composite long-short strategy using the three valid signals earns an estimated 0.96% per month, or about 11.6% annualized, with an estimated Sharpe ratio near 2.2 before costs. The remaining value, size, accrual, leverage, turnover, and R&D signals are weak and statistically unreliable in this panel.

## Methods
The panel contains 60,000 stock-month observations, 1,217 distinct stocks, and 120 months, with exactly 500 stocks per month. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month \(t+1\). All features except `analyst_rev` are constructed from information available at the end of month \(t\), so they are implementable for predicting month \(t+1\) returns. `analyst_rev` is explicitly recorded at the end of month \(t+1\), so it is contemporaneous with the target and not available when the portfolio would be formed.

We assess each feature using the provided monthly Spearman rank IC, its t-statistic, the percentage of months with positive IC, and the mean return and annualized Sharpe ratio of an equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile portfolio. We treat a feature as valid if it has a plausible timing convention and its average rank IC is statistically reliable, with \(|t|>2\). For negative signals, the implementable strategy reverses the long-short portfolio: long the bottom quintile and short the top quintile. The composite strategy equal-weights the three valid signals after orienting them in the predicted direction.

## Results
Three features are valid and implementable.

| Feature | Direction | Mean monthly rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

Gross profitability is the strongest valid signal: high-profitability stocks outperform low-profitability stocks by 1.16% per month, with positive IC in 70% of months. Asset growth is robustly negative: high-asset-growth firms underperform, so the implementable version earns 0.99% per month by buying low-growth and shorting high-growth stocks. Idiosyncratic volatility is also negative: low-volatility stocks outperform high-volatility stocks, earning 0.74% per month in the reversed long-short portfolio.

`analyst_rev` has a mean rank IC of 0.2963, a t-statistic of 80.29, and positive IC in 100% of months. However, it is recorded at the end of month \(t+1\), after the return period has already occurred. It is therefore not a valid predictor of `ret_next`; it is a look-ahead-biased signal.

The remaining features are weak. `book_to_market` has IC 0.0027 (t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` has IC 0.0055 (t = 1.26), `accruals` has IC 0.0015 (t = 0.36), `leverage` has IC -0.0011 (t = -0.30), `turnover` has IC 0.0006 (t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` has IC -0.0004 (t = -0.10). None reaches conventional significance.

A composite strategy that equal-weights the three valid signals—long high `gross_prof`, short high `asset_growth`, and short high `idio_vol`—has an estimated monthly return of \((1.16\% + 0.99\% + 0.74\%)/3 = 0.96\%\). Annualized, this is approximately 11.6%. Assuming moderate diversification across the three signals, the combined annualized Sharpe ratio is estimated near 2.2 before transaction costs.

## Discussion
The results align with well-documented equity anomalies: profitability, investment/asset growth, and low-volatility or idiosyncratic-volatility effects. Gross profitability captures quality, asset growth captures aggressive investment that tends to be followed by weaker returns, and idiosyncratic volatility captures a low-volatility premium or mispricing in high-risk stocks. The equal-weighted composite is attractive because the three signals have different economic drivers and may provide diversification benefits.

The analyst revision result is the most important caution. Its enormous apparent predictive power is exactly what one would expect from a variable measured during the same month as the return. In a real investment process, analyst revisions during month \(t+1\) cannot be used to trade at the end of month \(t\). A properly lagged analyst revision signal might still be useful, but this panel does not provide it.

The weak signals should not be interpreted as proof that value, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, or R&D are irrelevant in all settings. In this 500-stock index universe and 120-month sample, they simply do not produce reliable monthly cross-sectional return predictions.

## Limitations
This analysis relies on summary statistics rather than the raw panel, so nonlinearities, interactions, sector effects, and outliers cannot be examined directly. Transaction costs, turnover, borrow costs, shorting constraints, and capacity are not modeled; the reported returns are gross and may overstate net performance. The composite Sharpe estimate assumes moderate diversification and may be overstated if the three valid signals are more highly correlated than assumed. The sample covers a specific 120-month period and a 500-stock index universe, so results may not generalize to other periods, countries, or smaller stocks. Finally, multiple testing is a concern because ten features were evaluated without formal false-discovery-rate adjustment.
