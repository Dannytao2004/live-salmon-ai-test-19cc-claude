# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Large-Cap Equity Panel: Evidence from Nine Firm Characteristics

## Abstract
We evaluate nine firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. Rank IC tests identify gross profitability, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, and leverage as statistically reliable signals, with gross profitability and leverage positively related to future returns and idiosyncratic volatility negatively related; accruals are positively related in this sample. A composite long-short strategy that equal-weights these four sign-adjusted signals earns an estimated 0.075 annualized return with an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 1.4. The remaining characteristics—book-to-market, log market cap, asset growth, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not pass our reliability threshold. These results are subject to survivorship and transaction-cost caveats.

## Methods
We use a monthly stock panel with 34,387 observations, 500 stocks, and 120 months. The target is `ret_next`, the stock’s total return over month t+1. For each feature, we compute the Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` across stocks within each month, average these monthly rank ICs over the 120 months, and form a t-statistic as mean IC divided by IC standard deviation times the square root of 120. We also consider equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month.

A feature is treated as a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2 in absolute value, the sign is economically interpretable, and the long-short return is non-trivial. For the composite strategy, we rank-standardize the valid signals each month, multiply each by its estimated sign (positive for gross profitability, accruals, and leverage; negative for idiosyncratic volatility), equal-weight them into a single score, and go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile. Expected performance is estimated from the average sign-adjusted long-short monthly returns of the valid signals, annualized, with Sharpe adjusted for diversification but not for unobserved signal correlations.

## Results
Four features pass the reliability screen. Gross profitability has a mean rank IC of 0.0293 (t = 3.83) and a long-short return of 0.88% per month, with an annualized Sharpe of 1.19. Accruals have a mean rank IC of 0.0130 (t = 2.06), a long-short return of 0.31% per month, and an annualized Sharpe of 0.50. Idiosyncratic volatility has a mean rank IC of -0.0266 (t = -3.76); buying high-volatility stocks and shorting low-volatility stocks loses 0.78% per month, so the sign-adjusted strategy goes long low-volatility and short high-volatility stocks. Leverage has a mean rank IC of 0.0137 (t = 2.56), a long-short return of 0.50% per month, and an annualized Sharpe of 0.99.

The rejected features show weak or unreliable predictive power. Book-to-market has a mean rank IC of 0.0029 (t = 0.54), log market cap -0.0043 (t = -0.67), asset growth -0.0164 (t = -1.87), turnover 0.0042 (t = 0.64), and R&D intensity 0.0018 (t = 0.30). Asset growth is the closest to significance, but its t-statistic falls below the conventional threshold.

For the composite strategy, the average sign-adjusted long-short monthly return across the four valid signals is `(0.0088 + 0.0031 + 0.0078 + 0.0050) / 4 = 0.0062`, or 0.62% per month. Simple annualization gives approximately 0.074, and compounding gives approximately 0.077. Given the individual Sharpes and modest assumed diversification, we estimate an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 1.4. Thus the strategy’s expected annual return is approximately 0.075 with an expected annual Sharpe of 1.4.

## Discussion
The strongest result is the positive relation between gross profitability and future returns, consistent with a quality/profitability premium. The negative relation between idiosyncratic volatility and future returns is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly. Leverage’s positive predictive relation may reflect a risk premium in this sample, though it runs counter to some distress-based explanations. Accruals are positive in this panel, which contradicts the standard accruals anomaly; this may be sample-specific, related to the large-cap index universe, or an artifact of survivorship bias.

The composite strategy benefits from combining signals that likely capture different return drivers: profitability, earnings quality, volatility, and leverage. However, the estimated performance should be viewed as an upper bound because it ignores transaction costs, borrow fees, market impact, and capacity constraints. The rejected signals are not necessarily economically irrelevant; they may simply be weak in this particular large-cap, 120-month sample.

## Limitations
- The universe consists of stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, introducing survivorship bias because delisted or underperforming stocks are excluded.
- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 stocks, limiting statistical power and out-of-sample validation.
- The strategy ignores transaction costs, short-selling borrow fees, market impact, and turnover-related slippage.
- Equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios and rank-based composites may be sensitive to weighting choices and extreme observations.
- Multiple testing across nine features raises the chance of false discoveries; some apparently valid signals may not replicate.
