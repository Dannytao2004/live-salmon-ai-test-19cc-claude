# Predicting Monthly Equity Returns: Profitability, Investment, and Volatility Signals in a 120-Month Panel

## Abstract
We analyze a monthly stock panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months with ten firm characteristics. Using rank information coefficients (ICs) and equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios, we identify three valid return predictors: gross profitability (positive), asset growth (negative), and idiosyncratic volatility (negative). Analyst consensus revisions show an implausibly high IC of 0.2963, but they are invalid because they are recorded at the end of month t+1 and are therefore contemporaneous with the target return. A composite strategy equal-weighting the three valid signals is expected to earn roughly 11.6% annually with a Sharpe ratio near 2.0 before transaction costs. The remaining characteristics exhibit weak or statistically insignificant predictive power.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 monthly observations for 1,217 stocks, with 500 stocks per month from month 1 to 120. Each row includes ten features measured at the end of month t and the target `ret_next`, the stock return over month t+1. We evaluate predictive strength using the mean monthly Spearman rank IC, its t-statistic, and the mean monthly return of an equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of each feature.

A feature is considered valid only if it is point-in-time available at the end of month t and shows a statistically significant and economically meaningful relation to `ret_next`. We reject `analyst_rev` on timing grounds: the data dictionary states it is recorded at the end of month t+1, so it cannot be known when forming portfolios at the end of month t. This makes its 0.2963 IC and 8.73% monthly long-short return a look-ahead artifact rather than a tradable signal.

For the proposed strategy, we combine the valid signals with equal weights. We go long the top quintile of `gross_prof`, long the bottom quintile of `asset_growth`, and long the bottom quintile of `idio_vol`; we short the opposite quintiles. The portfolio is rebalanced monthly.

## Results
Three features qualify as valid signals:

| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short mean monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | Positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | Negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | Negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

For `asset_growth` and `idio_vol`, the negative long-short returns mean the profitable trade is the reverse: buy the bottom quintile and sell the top quintile. Thus the implementable monthly returns are approximately 0.99% for `asset_growth` and 0.74% for `idio_vol`.

The remaining non-analyst features are not statistically reliable. `book_to_market` has IC 0.0027 (t=0.68), `log_mktcap` 0.0055 (t=1.26), `accruals` 0.0015 (t=0.36), `leverage` -0.0011 (t=-0.30), `turnover` 0.0006 (t=0.14), and `rd_intensity` -0.0004 (t=-0.10). `analyst_rev` has an extreme IC of 0.2963 (t=80.29) and a long-short return of 8.73% per month, but it is rejected because it is not available at the portfolio formation date.

Equal-weighting the three valid signals gives a composite monthly return of approximately (1.16% + 0.99% + 0.74%) / 3 = 0.96%. Annualized arithmetically, this is about 11.6%. Assuming moderate cross-signal correlation, the composite Sharpe ratio is estimated near 2.0.

## Discussion
The valid signals correspond to well-documented equity anomalies. Gross profitability captures a quality/profitability premium: more profitable firms outperform. Asset growth reflects the investment effect: firms that expand their balance sheets aggressively tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility captures the low-volatility anomaly: high-volatility stocks underperform on a risk-adjusted basis.

The analyst revision signal is the most striking result but also the most dangerous. Its IC is 0.2963 with positive IC in 100% of months and an annualized Sharpe of 24.23. Such performance is far beyond realistic monthly stock prediction. The timing convention explains it: the signal is updated during month t+1, the same period as `ret_next`. At the end of month t, an investor cannot know the revision that will occur in month t+1. This is a textbook look-ahead bias, and including it would produce spuriously high backtest returns.

The composite strategy diversifies across three economically distinct signals. Its expected annual return of about 11.6% and Sharpe near 2.0 are attractive before costs, but they rely on the historical ICs persisting and on the signals being only moderately correlated. The weak signals may be noisy in this sample or already arbitraged away.

## Limitations
- Analyst revisions are excluded due to look-ahead bias, but their apparent strength underscores the need for strict point-in-time data.
- Expected performance ignores transaction costs, market impact, borrowing fees, and shorting constraints.
- The composite Sharpe assumes moderate cross-signal correlation and equal weighting, which may not hold out of sample.
- The universe is limited to 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to microcaps or non-index stocks.
- Monthly rebalancing and simple quintile portfolios ignore risk management, sector neutrality, and factor timing.
