# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a 500-Stock Monthly Panel: A Signal-Screening Study

## Abstract

We evaluate ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns in a monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months (60,000 stock-months). Using within-month Spearman rank ICs, IC t-statistics, and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find that one signal—analyst FY1 EPS revision (`analyst_rev`)—dominates all others, with a mean rank IC of 0.296, a t-statistic of 89.6, positive IC in 100% of months, and an annualized long-short Sharpe ratio of 26.4. The remaining nine characteristics show economically negligible and statistically insignificant predictive power, with |mean IC| ≤ 0.006 and |t| ≤ 1.64. We conclude that the panel's return predictability is concentrated almost entirely in the analyst-revision signal, and we caution that its extraordinary t-statistic is consistent with a look-ahead or timing artifact rather than a tradable edge.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows: 500 stocks per month across 120 months, with a point-in-time index universe that retains stocks through their final month (including the return over that month), which mitigates survivorship bias in the target. Each row provides ten characteristics measured at the end of month *t* and the subsequent month's total return, `ret_next`.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the provided within-month Spearman rank IC (feature rank vs. `ret_next` rank across the 500 stocks), averaged over 120 months, with the reported IC t-statistic = mean IC / IC std × √120. We also use the reported equal-weighted quintile long-short (top-minus-bottom quintile) mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio. We classify a signal as *valid* only if it shows a mean IC that is economically meaningful relative to its cross-sectional dispersion and a t-statistic comfortably above conventional thresholds (|t| ≳ 2), consistent with the long-short evidence.

**Timing/point-in-time check.** We scrutinized when each variable becomes observable relative to the start of the return window. All accounting variables (`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `rd_intensity`) and market variables (`log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, `turnover`) are measured at the end of month *t* and are therefore strictly lagged relative to `ret_next`. `analyst_rev` is the exception: it is defined as the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast *during month t+1*, i.e., it is recorded contemporaneously with the return it is used to predict.

**Strategy construction.** We form a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio in the valid signal(s), rebalanced monthly at month-end, with no transaction-cost model applied in the reported gross figures.

**Analyst revisions.** `analyst_rev` is the only signal with a first-order effect. Its mean rank IC of 0.296 implies that a one-standard-deviation improvement in revision rank is associated with roughly a 0.3-standard-deviation improvement in return rank, and the sign is positive in every one of the 120 months. The top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile portfolio earns 8.66% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 26.4. The t-statistic of 89.6 is far beyond anything plausibly generated by a genuine, tradable monthly equity signal.

**Remaining characteristics.** The other nine features are indistinguishable from noise. The strongest of them, `asset_growth` (IC 0.0061, t = 1.64) and `accruals` (IC 0.0055, t = 1.34), carry the "right" signs relative to the literature (high asset growth and high accruals predict lower returns), but their long-short spreads (0.28% and 0.29% per month) and Sharpes (0.72 and 0.70) are marginal. Value (`book_to_market`) and leverage show weakly negative ICs, the opposite of the classic value and leverage effects; size (`log_mktcap`) and turnover are flat; profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, and R&D intensity are economically trivial.

**Strategy.** A long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile of `analyst_rev` and shorts the bottom quintile, equal-weighted and rebalanced monthly, is the only strategy the data support. Gross of costs, the reported spread is 8.66% per month, i.e., roughly 104% per year, with an annualized Sharpe of 26.4. We regard this as not credible as a net-of-cost, implementable return (see Discussion).

## Discussion

The central finding is a stark concentration of predictability: nine of ten characteristics are effectively inert, while one—analyst EPS revision—appears to explain the cross-section almost perfectly. Two interpretations are possible.

The first is that analyst revisions are a genuinely powerful short-horizon signal, consistent with the well-documented post-revision drift: analysts update slowly, and prices underreact to revisions, producing continuation. Under this reading, the sign (positive) and the monotonicity (positive IC in 100% of months) are economically sensible.

The second, and more likely, interpretation is a timing artifact. The data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is the change in the FY1 consensus *during month t+1*, recorded at the end of month t+1—the same window over which `ret_next` is measured. The signal is therefore not known at the start of the return window; it is contemporaneous with the return. An IC of 0.30 with a t-statistic near 90 and a 100% positive-month hit rate is the signature of a variable that is mechanically co-determined with the target (analysts revise upward after prices rise), not of a forecast. A tradable version would require lagging the signal to information available at the end of month *t*, which the data do not permit us to construct.

The weak performance of the fundamental signals is itself informative. In a 500-name, large-cap index universe, classic anomalies (value, size, profitability, accruals, asset growth) are known to be attenuated relative to broad-market samples, and the small ICs we observe (|IC| < 0.007) are consistent with that. The near-zero size and turnover ICs, and the slightly *negative* value and leverage ICs, suggest that within this universe these characteristics carry little cross-sectional information over the sample.

## Limitations

- The `analyst_rev` signal is recorded contemporaneously with the return window, so its measured IC and Sharpe almost certainly reflect look-ahead bias rather than tradable predictive power; we cannot construct a properly lagged version from the summary provided.
- We observe only summary statistics (mean IC, IC std, t-stat, long-short means), not the raw panel, so we cannot control for cross-signal correlation, sector, or factor exposures.
- All long-short figures are gross; no transaction costs, borrow costs, or capacity constraints are modeled, which is especially material for a signal with an implausibly high turnover-driven Sharpe.
- The universe is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents, so the weak fundamental signals may not generalize to small-cap or broad-market samples.
- The sample spans 120 months only, and the reported t-statistics assume serially independent monthly ICs, which may understate standard errors for persistent signals.
- The point-in-time universe retains delisted names through their final month, which reduces but does not eliminate delisting-return measurement concerns.
