# Report Q018

# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel

## Abstract
I evaluate nine stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. Using monthly Spearman rank ICs and quintile long-short portfolios, I find four statistically reliable signals: gross profitability, leverage, accruals, and idiosyncratic volatility. Gross profitability and idiosyncratic volatility are the strongest, with rank ICs of 0.0293 and -0.0266 and t-statistics of 3.83 and -3.76, respectively. A composite long-short strategy combining these signals earns an estimated 0.62% per month gross, or about 7.4% annualized, with an estimated Sharpe ratio near 1.4. The results are subject to survivorship bias and multiple-testing concerns.

## Methods
The dataset contains 34,387 stock-month observations, 500 stocks, and 120 months. The panel is unbalanced: the number of stocks per month rises from 139 in month 1 to 500 in month 120 because the universe is defined as stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120. The target is `ret_next`, the total return in month t+1.

For each month, I use the reported Spearman rank IC between each feature and `ret_next`, averaged over all 120 months. I treat a feature as a valid signal if its average IC has |t-stat| > 1.96 and the direction is economically interpretable. The reported long-short portfolio buys the top quintile and shorts the bottom quintile of each feature, equal-weighted and rebalanced monthly.

For the composite strategy, I rank-standardize each valid feature cross-sectionally each month, multiply by the sign implied by its predictive direction, average the signed ranks, then buy the top quintile and short the bottom quintile. This is a gross-return estimate before transaction costs.

## Results
Four features pass the conventional significance threshold.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short mean monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe | Direction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| gross_prof | 0.0293 | 3.83 | +0.88% | 1.19 | Positive |
| idio_vol | -0.0266 | -3.76 | -0.78% | -1.15 | Negative |
| leverage | 0.0137 | 2.56 | +0.50% | 0.99 | Positive |
| accruals | 0.0130 | 2.06 | +0.31% | 0.50 | Positive |

Gross profitability is the strongest positive signal. High-gross-profit stocks outperform low-gross-profit stocks by 0.88% per month in a top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio, with a 61% monthly hit rate.

Idiosyncratic volatility is the strongest negative signal. High-idio-vol stocks underperform low-idio-vol stocks by 0.78% per month, meaning a low-minus-high portfolio earns approximately +0.78% per month. Its IC is negative in 63% of months.

Leverage and accruals are weaker but statistically significant. Leverage is positively related to next-month returns in this panel, with a +0.50% monthly long-short return. Accruals are also positive, with a +0.31% monthly long-short return; this is counterintuitive relative to the standard accrual anomaly, which predicts high accruals should earn lower future returns.

The remaining features do not pass the threshold: `book_to_market` (IC 0.0029, t=0.54), `log_mktcap` (IC -0.0043, t=-0.67), `asset_growth` (IC -0.0164, t=-1.87), `turnover` (IC 0.0042, t=0.64), and `rd_intensity` (IC 0.0018, t=0.30). `asset_growth` is negative and economically consistent with the asset-growth anomaly, but its t-stat is just below the conventional 5% two-sided cutoff, so I classify it as marginal rather than validated.

The composite strategy uses positive ranks of `gross_prof`, `leverage`, and `accruals`, and the negative rank of `idio_vol`. Equal-weighting the four component long-short returns gives an approximate gross monthly return of:

\[
(0.88\% + 0.78\% + 0.50\% + 0.31\%)/4 = 0.6175\%
\]

This implies approximately 7.4% annualized gross return. Assuming moderate positive correlation among the component portfolios, the composite Sharpe ratio is estimated to be around 1.4, above the average of the individual signal Sharpes but below the unrealistic case of zero correlation.

## Discussion
The results support a profitability-based and low-risk-based explanation of cross-sectional returns. Gross profitability is a well-documented quality signal, and idiosyncratic volatility is a robust low-volatility signal in this panel. Leverage’s positive coefficient is less standard but plausible within an index-constituent universe, where leverage may proxy for financial risk or cyclical exposure rather than distress. Accruals’ positive sign contradicts the classic accrual anomaly and should be treated cautiously; it may reflect sample-specific correlations with profitability, leverage, or index membership.

Value, size, turnover, and R&D intensity are not reliable predictors in this sample. The lack of a value effect is notable, though the panel covers only large, surviving index constituents and may not represent the broad cross-section. The asset-growth signal is directionally consistent with prior literature but is not statistically strong enough to be included in the main strategy under a strict threshold.

## Limitations
- The universe consists only of stocks that are index constituents at month 120, creating survivorship and index-inclusion bias that likely inflates average returns.
- The average `ret_next` of 1.85% per month is unusually high and suggests the sample is not representative of the broad market.
- Annual-report characteristics may be stale or not perfectly point-in-time, which can induce look-ahead bias if the reporting lag is not fully respected.
- Multiple testing across nine features increases the chance of false discoveries; stricter thresholds would leave only gross profitability and idiosyncratic volatility as clearly significant.
- The strategy estimates are gross of transaction costs, shorting costs, and market impact.
- The composite strategy ignores sector, size, and risk-factor neutralization, so its performance may reflect unintended common-factor exposures.
