# Leverage as the Only Robust Cross-Sectional Signal in a Monthly Index Panel

## Abstract
We examine nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns in a monthly panel of up to 500 index constituents over 120 months. Among these characteristics, only leverage shows a statistically reliable cross-sectional relation: its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0164 with a t-statistic of 2.74, and a top-minus-bottom quintile spread of 0.52% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 0.92. The other eight characteristics—book-to-market, gross profitability, size, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, turnover, and R&D intensity—are not statistically significant predictors in this sample. A monthly-rebalanced long-short leverage strategy is expected to earn approximately 6.2% per year gross of costs.

## Methods
The dataset is a monthly panel of 34,708 stock-month observations from month 1 to 120, covering up to 500 stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120. For each characteristic, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with next-month returns within each month, then average these correlations over all 120 months. The t-statistic is computed as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation and multiplied by √120. We also form equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month and compute the top-minus-bottom quintile long-short return. A feature is classified as a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value.

## Results
The table below summarizes the predictive performance of each feature.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| book_to_market | -0.0017 | -0.28 | -0.05% | -0.08 |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | 0.12% | 0.22 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | -0.07% | -0.14 |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | 0.37% | 0.74 |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | 0.24% | 0.41 |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | 0.13% | 0.19 |
| leverage | 0.0164 | 2.74 | 0.52% | 0.92 |
| turnover | -0.0005 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.23 |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | -0.06% | -0.11 |

Only leverage meets the pre-specified significance threshold. Its positive rank IC and positive long-short return indicate that high-leverage stocks tend to outperform low-leverage stocks over the next month.

## Discussion
The positive predictive power of leverage is consistent with a risk-based interpretation: highly levered firms are riskier and therefore may offer higher expected returns. The absence of reliable value, profitability, asset growth, accruals, and volatility effects in this sample may reflect the specific period, the index-constrained universe, or the sample construction.

Based on these findings, the proposed strategy is to sort stocks monthly by leverage, buy the top quintile, and sell the bottom quintile with equal weighting and monthly rebalancing. The historical gross long-short spread is 0.52% per month, implying an expected annual return of approximately 6.24% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92.

## Limitations
This study has several limitations. The sample is conditioned on index membership at the end of month 120, which introduces survivorship and backfill bias. All performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, market impact, and shorting costs. The statistical tests do not fully adjust for multiple testing across the nine features. The sample covers only 120 months, so the results may be regime-dependent. Finally, the fundamental data may not be strictly point-in-time and could contain restatement or look-ahead bias.
