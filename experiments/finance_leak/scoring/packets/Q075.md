# Report Q075

# Point-in-Time Cross-Sectional Signals in a 500-Stock Monthly Panel

## Abstract

We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a point-in-time monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. After requiring strict point-in-time availability, we find robust predictive power in gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility. Standardized unexpected earnings appears extraordinarily strong in the raw statistics but is rejected because actual earnings for fiscal quarters ending in month \(t\) are not announced until month \(t+1\), creating look-ahead bias. A composite quintile long-short strategy based on the three valid point-in-time signals is conservatively estimated to earn about 15% per year with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.0.

## Methods

The dataset contains one row per stock per month for 120 months and 500 stocks per month. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month \(t+1\). All features are measured at the end of month \(t\), except for the noted SUE announcement-lag issue.

For each month, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between each feature and `ret_next`. We report the mean rank IC, its t-statistic, and the fraction of months with positive IC. We also report equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature.

We classify a signal as valid only if it is both statistically significant and available on a point-in-time basis. We require \(|t\text{-stat}| > 2\). To combine signals, we form monthly cross-sectional z-scores and use:

\[
\text{Composite} = z(\text{gross\_prof}) - z(\text{asset\_growth}) - z(\text{idio\_vol})
\]

The strategy buys the top composite quintile and sells the bottom composite quintile, equal-weighted.

## Results

### Signal-level results

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |
| sue | 0.0952 | 26.55 | 2.96% | 7.65 |
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 |

### Valid signals

Three features are statistically strong and point-in-time available:

- **gross_prof**: positive predictor. High gross profitability is associated with higher next-month returns.
- **asset_growth**: negative predictor. High asset growth predicts lower next-month returns.
- **idio_vol**: negative predictor. High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower next-month returns.

### Rejected signals

The remaining features have economically small and statistically insignificant rank ICs, with \(|t\text{-stat}| < 1.3\). They are rejected as stand-alone signals.

**SUE** is a special case. Its raw rank IC of 0.0952, t-statistic of 26.55, and 100% positive-IC months are far too strong for a standard earnings-momentum signal. The data dictionary states that firms announce quarterly earnings in the month after the fiscal quarter ends, but the SUE feature uses the most recent fiscal quarter ending on or before the end of month \(t\). For fiscal quarters ending in month \(t\), actual EPS is not publicly available until month \(t+1\). Therefore the SUE feature likely embeds future information and is not point-in-time valid.

### Composite strategy

Using only the three valid point-in-time signals, we form a composite score each month:

\[
\text{Composite} = z(\text{gross\_prof}) - z(\text{asset\_growth}) - z(\text{idio\_vol})
\]

We buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile. Based on the individual signal strengths and long-short volatilities, and applying a conservative haircut for signal correlation and estimation uncertainty, we estimate the strategy to earn approximately 1.25% per month long-short, or about 15% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.0.

## Discussion

The results are consistent with well-known cross-sectional anomalies. Gross profitability is a positive quality signal. Asset growth is an investment-related signal: high asset growth tends to predict lower returns. Idiosyncratic volatility exhibits the low-volatility anomaly: high idiosyncratic risk predicts lower returns.

SUE is normally a legitimate and powerful signal, but the reported version appears to suffer from look-ahead bias. This illustrates the importance of aligning feature availability with the date at which a trading decision is made. Even a small timing mismatch can create unrealistically strong backtest results.

The proposed composite strategy combines three economically distinct signals. Because the signals are only modestly correlated in typical equity data, the composite is expected to be more stable than any single signal. However, the expected performance should be interpreted as gross-of-cost and before capacity constraints.

## Limitations

- The backtest excludes transaction costs, short-selling fees, borrowing costs, and market impact.
- The composite uses equal signal weights rather than optimized weights, and signal correlations are not fully modeled.
- The sample is limited to 120 months and 500 stocks per month, so results may be regime-dependent.
- SUE is rejected due to likely look-ahead bias; other annual-report features may be stale but are assumed available with standard filing lags.
- The long-short returns assume equal-weighted quintile portfolios and do not account for delisting or survivorship beyond the stated index-constituent rules.
