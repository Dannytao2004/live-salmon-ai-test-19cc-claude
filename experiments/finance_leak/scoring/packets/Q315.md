# Predicting Next-Month Equity Returns with Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns in a point-in-time panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Gross profitability is a robust positive signal, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are robust negative signals. Analyst forecast revision has extreme apparent predictive power but is not a valid signal because it is measured during the return month. A composite long-short strategy based on the three valid signals is estimated to earn about 15% annually with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.0 before transaction costs.

## Methods
The dataset consists of 60,000 stock-month observations: 500 stocks per month for 120 months. For each feature, we compute the within-month Spearman rank correlation with next-month returns, `ret_next`, and average these correlations across months. The reported t-statistic is the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation and multiplied by the square root of the number of months. We treat a signal as statistically valid if its IC t-statistic exceeds 2 in absolute value.

We also report quintile long-short portfolios: each month, stocks are sorted into quintiles on a feature; the portfolio buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly. For economically valid signals, we construct a composite score equal to the average z-score of `gross_prof`, negative `asset_growth`, and negative `idio_vol`, then form a top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio.

## Results
Three features are statistically and economically reliable predictors of next-month returns:

- **Gross profitability (`gross_prof`)** is a positive signal. Its mean rank IC is 0.0378 with a t-statistic of 6.07, and the long-short quintile portfolio earns 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94.
- **Asset growth (`asset_growth`)** is a negative signal. Its mean rank IC is -0.0303 with a t-statistic of -4.29. Low asset-growth stocks outperform high asset-growth stocks; reversing the top-minus-bottom portfolio yields 0.99% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.48.
- **Idiosyncratic volatility (`idio_vol`)** is a negative signal. Its mean rank IC is -0.0261 with a t-statistic of -4.19. Low-volatility stocks outperform high-volatility stocks; reversing the top-minus-bottom portfolio yields 0.74% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.20.

The remaining features are not significant predictors:
`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` all have absolute t-statistics below 2 and economically small long-short returns.

The `analyst_rev` feature has an extremely high mean rank IC of 0.2963, a t-statistic of 80.29, positive IC in 100% of months, and a long-short return of 8.73% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 24.23. However, this feature is recorded at the end of month t+1, meaning it uses information during the same month as the return being predicted. It is therefore not a valid predictive signal and is excluded from the strategy.

A composite long-short strategy based on `gross_prof`, `-asset_growth`, and `-idio_vol` is estimated to earn approximately 1.2% per month, or about 15% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.0 before transaction costs.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with several well-known cross-sectional return patterns. Gross profitability predicts higher returns, supporting a quality-profitability interpretation. High asset growth predicts lower returns, consistent with the asset-growth anomaly. High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly. The weakness of book-to-market and accruals in this sample may reflect the specific index, period, or portfolio construction.

The `analyst_rev` result highlights the importance of point-in-time data. Although analyst revisions are genuinely powerful signals in practice, the version provided here contains look-ahead bias and cannot be used in a realistic backtest.

Combining the three valid signals improves diversification relative to any single signal. The expected performance of the composite long-short strategy is conservative: it does not include transaction costs, shorting costs, or the possible capacity constraints of a 500-stock equity universe.

## Limitations
- The analysis is based on summary statistics only; no additional out-of-sample or portfolio-level robustness tests are available.
- Expected returns and Sharpe ratios are gross of transaction costs, market impact, financing costs, and short-sale constraints.
- The sample covers a single index and a specific 120-month period, so results may not generalize to other markets or periods.
- No multiple-testing correction is applied, though the significance threshold reduces the risk of false positives.
- The invalid `analyst_rev` feature cannot be used, but a properly point-in-time analyst revision measure might still be valuable.
