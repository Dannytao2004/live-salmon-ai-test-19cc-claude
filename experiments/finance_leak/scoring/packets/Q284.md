# Earnings Surprise Momentum in a Monthly Equity Panel: SUE as the Dominant Predictor

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns in a 60,000-observation monthly panel of 500 index constituents. Only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) exhibits a strong, stable predictive relation: its average monthly rank IC is 0.0998, t-statistic 23.25, and IC is positive in 99% of months. A long-short quintile portfolio based on SUE earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. All other features have economically small and statistically insignificant rank ICs. We conclude that SUE is the primary valid signal in this dataset, while the remaining characteristics should be rejected as standalone predictors.

## Methods
We use a monthly panel with 120 months and 500 stocks per month. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month \(t+1\), in decimals. The candidate features are `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `sue`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

For each month, we compute the Spearman rank correlation between each feature and `ret_next` across stocks. We average these monthly rank ICs over all 120 months and compute a t-statistic as \(\text{mean IC} / \text{IC std} \times \sqrt{120}\). We also examine equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature, rebalanced monthly. A feature is considered a valid signal only if it shows a meaningful rank IC, statistical significance, and consistency across months. Transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact are ignored in the reported statistics.

## Results
SUE is the only feature with clear predictive power. Its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0998, with an IC standard deviation of 0.0470 and a t-statistic of 23.25. The IC is positive in 99% of months. The SUE long-short quintile portfolio earns 3.04% per month, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. This is an exceptionally strong and stable result.

The other features are far weaker. `asset_growth` has a mean rank IC of 0.0061 (t = 1.64) and a long-short return of 0.28% per month, Sharpe 0.72. `accruals` has a mean rank IC of 0.0055 (t = 1.34), long-short return 0.29% per month, Sharpe 0.70. `idio_vol` has IC 0.0043 (t = 1.14), `gross_prof` has IC 0.0039 (t = 0.93), `log_mktcap` has IC 0.0001 (t = 0.02), `turnover` has IC 0.0001 (t = 0.02), `book_to_market` has IC −0.0030 (t = −0.72), `leverage` has IC −0.0043 (t = −1.00), and `rd_intensity` has IC −0.0002 (t = −0.05). None of these reaches conventional statistical significance, and their long-short Sharpe ratios are all below 0.75.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with the earnings momentum and post-earnings-announcement-drift literature: firms with positive earnings surprises continue to outperform, and firms with negative surprises continue to underperform. SUE is both statistically and economically significant in this panel, with a very high hit rate across months. The other characteristics may be weak in this particular universe and sample period, or their known premia may require different horizons, conditioning variables, or risk adjustments to appear. Their signs are also sometimes inconsistent with well-known anomalies, further suggesting they are noisy here.

A practical strategy is to rank stocks each month by SUE, buy the top quintile equal-weighted, and short the bottom quintile equal-weighted. Based on the reported statistics, the arithmetic annualized expected return is approximately \(0.0304 \times 12 = 0.365\), or 36.5% per year, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. Compounded monthly, the return would be approximately 43.2%, though the arithmetic figure is more consistent with the table’s monthly mean. This strategy should be treated as a research baseline rather than a live trading recommendation because the reported performance excludes costs and implementation frictions.

## Limitations
The sample covers only 120 months and 500 stocks per month, so the results may not generalize to other periods, markets, or universes. The reported long-short returns ignore transaction costs, shorting borrow costs, market impact, and capacity constraints, all of which would reduce net performance. The SUE signal may be especially sensitive to earnings announcement timing and data alignment, and any look-ahead or restatement issues would inflate its apparent power. The extremely high Sharpe ratio is likely optimistic and should not be expected to persist out of sample. Finally, we test ten features without a formal multiple-testing correction, so some weaker signals may be false positives even though we reject them here.
