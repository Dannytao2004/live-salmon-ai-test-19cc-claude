# Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a Monthly Panel: Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We evaluate nine stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index stocks. Only gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility are statistically reliable signals, with monthly rank ICs of 0.0378, -0.0303, and -0.0261, respectively. Value, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity do not reliably predict returns in this sample. A composite long-short quintile strategy based on the three valid signals is estimated to earn about 12% annually with a Sharpe ratio near 2.1 before transaction costs.

## Methods
The dataset consists of 60,000 stock-month observations: 500 current index constituents per month for 120 months. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. Point-in-time index membership is used, and stocks remain in the panel through their removal month.

For each feature, I compute the monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` and average it over the 120 months to obtain the mean rank IC. The IC t-statistic is `mean / std × sqrt(120)`. A feature is treated as valid if its IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value. I also form equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios each month: top quintile minus bottom quintile. Annualized Sharpe ratios are computed by multiplying the monthly long-short Sharpe by `sqrt(12)`.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | L/S monthly return | L/S annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 |

Three features pass the significance threshold. `gross_prof` is a positive signal: high gross profitability predicts higher next-month returns. `asset_growth` is negative: high asset growth predicts lower next-month returns. `idio_vol` is negative: high idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower next-month returns. The remaining six features are not statistically reliable predictors.

## Discussion
The strong positive signal from `gross_prof` is consistent with the profitability literature: firms with high gross profit relative to assets tend to be more productive and earn higher subsequent returns. The negative `asset_growth` signal matches the investment anomaly: firms that grow assets aggressively often overinvest and subsequently underperform. The negative `idio_vol` signal supports the low-risk anomaly: high idiosyncratic volatility stocks earn lower subsequent returns in this sample.

Interestingly, `book_to_market` and `log_mktcap` are not reliable signals here. This may reflect the large-cap index universe and the specific sample period, which can weaken the value and size premia.

As a trading strategy, I propose a monthly-rebalanced equal-weight long-short quintile portfolio. Each month, form a composite score:
`score = z(gross_prof) - z(asset_growth) - z(idio_vol)`,
where `z()` denotes a cross-sectional z-score. Buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile. Based on the individual long-short returns and their diversification, the composite strategy is estimated to earn approximately 12% per year with an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 2.1 before transaction costs.

## Limitations
The estimated performance is gross of transaction costs, short-selling costs, and borrow fees. The sample covers 120 months of index constituents, so the results may not generalize to small-cap stocks or other periods. The signal correlations and composite weights are not optimized out-of-sample, so the strategy Sharpe ratio is an in-sample estimate. Accounting variables rely on point-in-time annual report data and may still be subject to restatement or reporting-lag errors.
