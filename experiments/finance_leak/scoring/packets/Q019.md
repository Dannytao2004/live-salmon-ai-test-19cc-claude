# Cross-Sectional Predictability in a Monthly Index Constituent Panel: Only Leverage Survives

## Abstract
We evaluate nine firm characteristics for predicting next-month stock returns in a 120-month panel of 500 eventual broad-index constituents. Using monthly Spearman rank ICs and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, only leverage shows a statistically reliable positive relation: mean rank IC 0.0164 (t-stat 2.74) and long-short mean return 0.52% per month, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. The other eight characteristics—book-to-market, gross profitability, size, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not pass conventional significance thresholds. A strategy that buys high-leverage stocks and shorts low-leverage stocks earns an estimated 6.24% annualized gross return. However, the panel is survivorship-biased and the result should be interpreted cautiously.

## Methods
The dataset contains 34,708 monthly stock observations from months 1 to 120 for 500 stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120. The target is `ret_next`, the stock’s total return over month t+1. I evaluate each feature using its average monthly Spearman rank IC with `ret_next`, its IC t-stat, and the mean return of an equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio that buys the top quintile and shorts the bottom quintile of the feature each month.

I classify a feature as a valid standalone signal if its mean rank IC is directionally stable and its IC t-stat exceeds 2.0 in absolute value. This threshold is conventional but not adjusted for multiple testing. The long-short portfolio return is treated as a gross return before transaction costs, borrow costs, or implementation shortfall.

## Results
Only `leverage` passes the validity threshold. Its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0164 with a t-stat of 2.74, and its IC is positive in 59% of months. The equal-weighted long-short portfolio has a mean monthly return of 0.52% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. The direction is positive: higher leverage predicts higher next-month returns.

The remaining features are weak and statistically indistinguishable from zero. `book_to_market` has a mean IC of -0.0017 (t = -0.28), `gross_prof` has 0.0012 (t = 0.23), `log_mktcap` has 0.0014 (t = 0.26), `asset_growth` has 0.0055 (t = 1.16), `accruals` has 0.0059 (t = 1.03), `idio_vol` has 0.0056 (t = 0.86), `turnover` has -0.0005 (t = -0.10), and `rd_intensity` has 0.0010 (t = 0.20). Several of these have economically interesting long-short spreads, but none has enough statistical support to be treated as a standalone valid signal in this sample.

## Discussion
The sole robust predictor is leverage, with a positive sign. This is notable because standard leverage/distress arguments often predict the opposite: highly levered firms should be riskier and may underperform. In this panel, however, high-leverage stocks outperform low-leverage stocks by about 52 basis points per month on an equal-weighted quintile spread. A plausible interpretation is that leverage proxies for a risk premium or for mature, capital-intensive firms that survived to become index constituents. Because the universe is defined by index membership at the end of the sample, this result may reflect survivorship and look-ahead biases rather than a stable ex ante anomaly.

The proposed strategy is a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio: buy the top leverage quintile and short the bottom leverage quintile. Using the reported historical mean, the expected annualized gross return is approximately 6.24% with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. Net returns would be lower after trading costs, especially because the strategy requires monthly rebalancing. Given the weak rank IC and the sample construction, this strategy should be viewed as suggestive rather than investment-ready.

## Limitations
The universe consists of stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, which creates survivorship bias and conditions the entire historical panel on eventual index membership. The sample period is only 120 months, and the cross-section grows from 143 to 500 stocks, so early-period estimates are based on fewer names. Nine features were examined, so the single significant result may partly reflect multiple testing. The long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, shorting costs, and market impact. The rank IC approach captures monotonic linear rank relations but may miss nonlinear or conditional effects. Finally, the positive leverage result is opposite to some standard priors and could be spurious in this specific survivor-biased index constituent sample.
