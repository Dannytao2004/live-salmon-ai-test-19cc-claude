# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a 500-Stock Index Panel: Evidence from Ten Accounting, Market, and Earnings Signals

## Abstract

We evaluate ten candidate characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-month observations). Only standardized unexpected earnings (`sue`) exhibits an economically and statistically robust relationship with future returns: its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0998 (t-stat = 23.25), it is positive in 99% of months, and its quintile long-short portfolio earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. All nine other features have rank ICs indistinguishable from zero (|t| ≤ 1.64) and long-short Sharpes below 0.75. We propose a concentrated, `sue`-ranked long-short strategy that is expected to deliver very high risk-adjusted returns, though the magnitude of the estimated Sharpe ratio should be interpreted with caution given the possibility of implementation frictions, data-snooping, and the unusual strength of the earnings-surprise signal in this sample.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows: 500 index constituents per month for 120 months (1,213 distinct stocks over the full sample). Each row provides ten point-in-time characteristics measured at the end of month *t* and the total return over month *t+1* (`ret_next`). The universe is point-in-time and survivorship-bias-free in the sense that firms removed from the index remain in the panel through their removal month, including their final-month return, which mitigates the classic delisting bias.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the provided monthly Spearman rank IC (cross-sectional correlation between feature and `ret_next`), averaged over 120 months, along with its cross-month standard deviation and t-statistic (mean / std × √120). We complement this with equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio statistics (buy top quintile, sell bottom quintile) and annualized Sharpe ratios.

**Decision rule.** A feature is deemed a valid signal if its mean rank IC is economically meaningful (|IC| ≳ 0.02), statistically reliable (|t| ≳ 2.0), and directionally consistent across months (IC sign agreement clearly above 50%). Features failing these criteria are rejected.

**Strategy construction.** For the surviving signal we form a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio: long the top quintile of `sue`, short the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly. Expected annual return is approximated as 12 × the mean monthly long-short return; expected Sharpe is taken from the reported annualized long-short Sharpe.

**The dominant signal: `sue`.** Standardized unexpected earnings is the only feature with a large, precisely estimated rank IC. A mean IC of 0.0998 is roughly 16–25 times larger than any other feature's IC, and the t-statistic of 23.25 is far beyond conventional thresholds. The signal is positive in 99% of the 120 months, indicating remarkable temporal stability rather than a few lucky episodes. The quintile spread portfolio earns 3.04% per month (≈ 43% annualized before costs) with an annualized Sharpe of 7.31.

**Substantive but insignificant candidates.** `asset_growth` (IC = 0.0061, t = 1.64) and `accruals` (IC = 0.0055, t = 1.34) show the expected signs — asset growth negatively related to future returns and accruals negatively related — but their long-short spreads (0.28% and 0.29% monthly) are not statistically reliable and their Sharpe ratios (~0.7) are modest.

**Weak or absent signals.** `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` all have |IC| ≤ 0.0043 and |t| ≤ 1.14, with sign-agreement shares near 50%. Their long-short returns are economically negligible (|monthly| ≤ 0.11%).

**Proposed strategy.** Rank all 500 stocks each month by `sue`; go long the top quintile (100 stocks) and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, dollar-neutral. Expected annual return ≈ 12 × 3.04% ≈ 36.5% gross; expected annualized Sharpe ≈ 7.3. Given the extreme Sharpe estimate, we haircut the expected Sharpe substantially for realism in the JSON block (to roughly 3.0) and report the raw gross return expectation with the caveat that costs and capacity constraints will erode it.

## Discussion

The results are striking in their selectivity: among ten well-known anomaly candidates, only the earnings-surprise signal survives. This is consistent with the post-earnings-announcement-drift (PEAD) literature, which documents that prices underreact to earnings news and continue to drift in the direction of the surprise for weeks to months. The `sue` construction here — (actual EPS − consensus) / σ(past surprises) — is a standard PEAD proxy, and its dominance in this panel suggests that in this index universe, fundamental earnings news is the primary driver of cross-sectional return predictability.

The null results for the other features are informative. `book_to_market` and `gross_prof` (value and quality) show essentially no predictive power in this sample, which may reflect the sample period, the large-cap index composition, or the fact that these characteristics are already priced. The near-zero ICs for `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `idio_vol` suggest no meaningful size, liquidity, or volatility effects at the monthly horizon in this universe. The positive-but-insignificant coefficients on `asset_growth` and `accruals` point in the theoretically expected direction but lack the statistical power to be traded on their own.

The economic magnitude of the `sue` strategy — 3.04% per month, 7.31 Sharpe — is implausibly large relative to typical published anomaly returns, which usually fall in the 0.5–1.5% monthly range with Sharpes below 2. Several explanations are possible: (i) the panel is a curated index universe that may over-represent firms with clean, well-followed earnings; (ii) the `sue` signal may embed look-ahead timing advantages if the consensus forecast is measured with perfect timing; (iii) the equal-weighted quintile spread may concentrate in small, illiquid names within the index; and (iv) the sample may be a favorable period for PEAD. A realistic implementation would apply transaction costs, capacity limits, and possibly a shrinkage of the signal.

## Limitations

- The Sharpe ratio of 7.31 for the `sue` strategy is far above typical published anomaly Sharpes and is likely inflated by data construction, timing, or universe-selection effects.
- Transaction costs, market impact, borrow costs on the short leg, and turnover are not modeled; net returns would be materially lower, especially for an equal-weighted 200-name portfolio rebalanced monthly.
- The analysis relies on summary statistics and rank ICs rather than the raw data; we cannot verify outliers, missing values, or the exact timing of the consensus forecast relative to `ret_next`.
- The 120-month sample and 500-stock universe may not generalize to other periods, markets, or smaller/larger capitalization segments.
- Because only one signal survives, the strategy is undiversified across factors and exposed to the risk of PEAD decay or crowding.
- We cannot rule out that the `sue` result partly reflects look-ahead bias if the consensus estimate used in the numerator was not fully available at the end of month *t*.
