# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Broad Equity Index Panel: Evidence from Nine Monthly Signals

## Abstract
We analyze a monthly panel of 34,387 stock-month observations covering 500 index constituents over 120 months. Using cross-sectional Spearman rank ICs and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we test nine accounting and market-based characteristics for predicting next-month returns. Four signals are statistically significant at conventional levels: gross profitability (positive), accruals (positive), leverage (positive), and idiosyncratic volatility (negative). A composite equal-weighted long-short strategy combining these four signals has an estimated gross annual return of approximately 7.4% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 1.35. Book-to-market, log market cap, asset growth, turnover, and R&D intensity do not provide reliable return prediction in this panel.

## Methods
The data contain one row per stock per month, with returns over the next month (`ret_next`) as the target. The universe consists of 500 stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, with history back to month 1 or index inclusion. I evaluate each feature using two statistics: the average monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank IC and the equal-weighted quintile long-short return formed by buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile of the feature each month. The IC t-statistic is computed as the mean IC divided by the IC standard deviation times the square root of 120 months. I classify a signal as valid if the absolute IC t-statistic is at least 2.0, approximately a 5% significance threshold.

For the composite strategy, I sign-adjust each valid feature according to its IC direction: positive for gross profitability, accruals, and leverage; negative for idiosyncratic volatility. I then average cross-sectional ranks of these features with equal weights, rebalancing monthly, and form a long-short quintile portfolio. Expected performance is estimated from the individual sign-adjusted sleeve returns and their volatilities, assuming moderate positive pairwise correlations among sleeves.

## Results
Four features are statistically significant return predictors.

| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short mean monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gross_prof | Positive | 0.0293 | 3.83 | 0.88% | 1.19 |
| accruals | Positive | 0.0130 | 2.06 | 0.31% | 0.50 |
| idio_vol | Negative | -0.0266 | -3.76 | -0.78% | -1.15 |
| leverage | Positive | 0.0137 | 2.56 | 0.50% | 0.99 |

Gross profitability is the strongest signal: stocks with higher gross profits relative to assets tend to outperform. Leverage also has a positive predictive relationship, and accruals have a weaker but significant positive IC. Idiosyncratic volatility is negatively related to future returns; the raw top-minus-bottom quintile return is -0.78% monthly, so the tradable direction is long low-volatility stocks and short high-volatility stocks, yielding an equivalent +0.78% monthly return.

Five features fail the significance threshold. Book-to-market has a mean rank IC of 0.0029 with t-stat 0.54, showing no reliable value premium. Log market cap has an IC of -0.0043 with t-stat -0.67, indicating no significant size effect. Asset growth has a negative IC of -0.0164 and t-stat -1.87, which is suggestive of an investment effect but marginally below the 5% threshold. Turnover has an IC of 0.0042 and t-stat 0.64, and R&D intensity has an IC of 0.0018 and t-stat 0.30; neither provides reliable next-month return prediction.

An equal-weight composite of the four significant signals has an estimated gross monthly return of approximately 0.62%. This corresponds to about 7.4% simple annual return, or roughly 7.7% compounded. Using individual sleeve volatilities and assuming average pairwise correlations between 0.3 and 0.4, the composite annualized Sharpe ratio is estimated at approximately 1.3 to 1.4. The main drivers are gross profitability and low idiosyncratic volatility, with leverage and accruals adding smaller diversification benefits.

## Discussion
The results align partially with known asset-pricing patterns. Gross profitability is a well-documented quality signal, and its positive IC is consistent with profitable firms earning higher subsequent returns. The negative idiosyncratic volatility relationship is also consistent with the low-volatility anomaly: high-volatility stocks underperform on a risk-adjusted basis. Leverage’s positive coefficient is less standard; in this index-constituent panel, higher-leverage firms earn slightly higher future returns, perhaps because leverage proxies for value or financial risk in a way not captured by book-to-market.

The positive accruals coefficient is notable because it is opposite to the classic accruals anomaly, which predicts lower returns for high-accrual firms. In this sample, high accruals are associated with modestly higher next-month returns. This may reflect the specific index universe, accounting definitions, or sample period, and it warrants caution. The absence of book-to-market and size effects is also notable, though not unprecedented in large-cap index constituent samples or shorter samples.

A practical strategy would combine gross profitability, accruals, leverage, and low idiosyncratic volatility into a single cross-sectional score. The composite should be rebalanced monthly, with long positions in the top quintile and short positions in the bottom quintile. Expected gross performance is attractive, but net performance would be lower after transaction costs, shorting costs, and implementation frictions.

## Limitations
The sample is limited to 120 months and 500 eventual index constituents, so survivorship and index-inclusion biases may affect the results. The analysis is based on in-sample signal statistics and does not test out-of-sample stability. Transaction costs, borrow costs, market impact, and turnover are ignored, so the strategy’s net return and Sharpe ratio would likely be lower. Multiple testing across nine signals increases the chance of false discoveries. The composite performance estimate relies on assumed correlations among signal sleeves, which may not hold in live trading. Finally, asset growth is marginally significant and could be included under a looser significance threshold, but it is excluded from the primary composite.
