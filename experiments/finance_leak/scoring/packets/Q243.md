# Cross-Sectional Predictors of Monthly Equity Returns: Evidence from a Survivorship-Biased Index Panel

## Abstract
This study evaluates nine stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Using monthly rank information coefficients and quintile long-short returns, only leverage emerges as a statistically reliable signal: it has a mean rank IC of 0.0164 (t-stat = 2.74) and a top-minus-bottom quintile return of 0.52% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 0.92. The other eight features have weak or statistically insignificant rank ICs despite some positive long-short returns. A dollar-neutral quintile strategy based on leverage is proposed, with an expected gross annual return near 6.2% and Sharpe ratio near 0.9, though the evidence is tempered by backfill and survivorship biases.

## Methods
The dataset consists of 34,708 stock-month observations covering 500 stocks and 120 months. The universe is defined as stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, with their monthly history backfilled to month 1 or to the month they joined the index. The target variable is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1.

For each feature, I compute the monthly rank information coefficient (Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks within each month), average it over all 120 months, and compute its t-statistic as:

\[
t = \frac{\text{mean rank IC}}{\text{std rank IC}} \times \sqrt{120}.
\]

I also use the reported long-short portfolio results, which are equal-weighted top-quintile minus bottom-quintile returns rebalanced monthly. A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic is at least 2 in absolute value and its long-short return has the same sign as the rank IC.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics for all nine features.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| book_to_market | -0.0017 | -0.28 | -0.05% | -0.08 |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | 0.12% | 0.22 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | -0.07% | -0.14 |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | 0.37% | 0.74 |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | 0.24% | 0.41 |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | 0.13% | 0.19 |
| leverage | 0.0164 | 2.74 | 0.52% | 0.92 |
| turnover | -0.0005 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.23 |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | -0.06% | -0.11 |

Only `leverage` satisfies the validity criterion. It has a positive mean rank IC of 0.0164 with a t-statistic of 2.74, and 59% of monthly ICs are positive. The corresponding long-short portfolio earns 0.52% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 0.92. The direction is positive: stocks with higher leverage tend to have higher next-month returns.

`asset_growth` shows a positive long-short return of 0.37% per month and an annualized Sharpe of 0.74, but its rank IC t-statistic is only 1.16, so it is not statistically reliable by the pre-specified criterion. `accruals`, `idio_vol`, and the remaining features have even weaker rank IC statistics.

## Discussion
The leverage result is consistent with the interpretation that balance-sheet risk is priced in the cross-section: more levered firms may carry higher distress risk and therefore offer higher expected returns. However, the magnitude of the signal is modest: a rank IC of 0.0164 is small in economic terms, and the long-short spread is 52 basis points per month before costs.

The evidence is also statistically fragile. With nine features tested, a Bonferroni-adjusted significance threshold would require a t-statistic near 2.8, so the leverage t-statistic of 2.74 would be considered marginal. The positive long-short returns for `asset_growth` could reflect nonlinearity or outlier months rather than a stable monotonic signal, as its rank IC is not significant.

The proposed strategy is a monthly-rebalanced, dollar-neutral quintile portfolio: rank stocks on `leverage`, buy the top quintile, and sell the bottom quintile with equal weights within each quintile. Based on the reported long-short returns, the expected gross annual return is approximately 6.2%, with an expected annualized Sharpe of 0.92.

## Limitations
- The sample is subject to survivorship and backfill bias because the universe consists of stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, with early history backfilled.
- The sample covers only 120 months, so statistical power is limited.
- The leverage signal is only marginally significant and would not survive a strict Bonferroni multiple-testing correction.
- All reported returns are gross and do not account for transaction costs, short-sale constraints, or turnover.
- Equal-weight quintile portfolios may overweight small or illiquid stocks, so realized capacity-adjusted performance could be lower.
