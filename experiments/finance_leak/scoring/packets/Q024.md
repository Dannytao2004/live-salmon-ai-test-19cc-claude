# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: Only Leverage Survives Statistical Scrutiny

## Abstract
We evaluate nine stock characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. Signal strength is measured by average monthly rank IC, its t-statistic, and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio performance. Only `leverage` has a reliably positive rank IC of 0.0164 with a t-statistic of 2.74, and its long-short portfolio earns 0.52% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. The remaining eight features have small, statistically insignificant rank ICs and inconsistent long-short evidence. A market-neutral long-short leverage strategy therefore has modest but positive expected gross performance, with meaningful caveats around multiple testing, survivorship, and trading costs.

## Methods
The panel contains 34,708 stock-month observations from months 1 to 120, covering 500 stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120. For each feature, I use the provided monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank IC against `ret_next`, averaged over all months. The associated t-statistic is computed as mean IC divided by IC standard deviation times the square root of 120 months. I also use the provided equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios, which buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month. I classify a feature as a valid signal if its mean rank IC is economically meaningful and statistically reliable at conventional levels, i.e. |t| approximately greater than 2. I also consider sign consistency and long-short Sharpe as supporting evidence. Because nine features are tested, I note that a single t-statistic near 2.7 is only marginal after Bonferroni-style adjustment.

## Results
Only `leverage` meets the reliability threshold. Its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0164, with t = 2.74, positive IC in 59% of months, and a long-short portfolio mean monthly return of 0.52%. Its annualized long-short Sharpe ratio is 0.92. The sign is positive: higher leverage predicts higher next-month returns in this panel.

The other eight features are weak or unreliable. `asset_growth` has the next strongest t-statistic at 1.16, with mean rank IC 0.0055 and a long-short Sharpe of 0.74, but it does not reach conventional significance. `accruals` has t = 1.03 and mean rank IC 0.0059, but its positive sign is contrary to the usual accruals anomaly and is not robust. `idio_vol` has mean rank IC 0.0056 but t = 0.86. `log_mktcap` and `gross_prof` have t-statistics below 0.3, and `book_to_market`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` have near-zero rank ICs and t-statistics below 0.3 in absolute value. None of these rejected features provides a dependable standalone predictive signal in this dataset.

A simple implementable strategy is a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile of `leverage` and shorts the bottom quintile. Based on the provided long-short statistics, it is expected to earn about 0.52% per month gross, or 0.0624 annualized, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. This is a modest gross premium and would be lower after realistic trading costs.

## Discussion
The main finding is that most standard anomaly-style characteristics do not have reliable cross-sectional predictive power in this panel. The only statistically credible signal is financial leverage, and its positive direction is notable: rather than high leverage predicting distress and poor returns, high-leverage stocks in this sample subsequently outperform low-leverage stocks. This could reflect a risk premium, a sample-specific leverage effect, or an omitted factor correlated with leverage. The weak results for `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `accruals`, `asset_growth`, and `idio_vol` may be due to the relatively short 120-month sample, the limited 500-stock universe, an unbalanced panel with increasing coverage over time, or the fact that many characteristics are slow-moving accounting variables. The strategy implied by these results is deliberately narrow: a leverage-based long-short quintile portfolio. Its expected Sharpe of 0.92 is attractive in isolation, but the statistical evidence is only marginally significant after accounting for multiple testing, so the strategy should be treated as a candidate signal rather than a confirmed anomaly.

## Limitations
- The universe consists of stocks that are index constituents at month 120, which introduces survivorship bias.
- The sample covers only 120 months and up to 500 stocks, limiting statistical power.
- Nine features are tested, so the single significant leverage result is marginal after multiple-testing adjustment.
- The expected strategy return is gross and ignores transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact.
- Characteristics such as book-to-market, profitability, accruals, and leverage are based on annual reports and may be stale or backward-looking.
- The panel is unbalanced, with the number of stocks rising from 143 to 500, which may affect cross-sectional ranks and portfolio breadth over time.
- The analysis uses only the provided summary statistics and cannot examine subsample stability, sector neutrality, or factor exposures.
