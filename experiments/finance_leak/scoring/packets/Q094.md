# Point-in-Time Equity Signals in a Monthly Stock Panel: Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
Using a monthly panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months, I evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns. After enforcing point-in-time availability, three signals are statistically and economically valid: gross profitability is positively predictive, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negatively predictive. The analyst revision variable has a spectacular apparent IC but is recorded at the end of month t+1, making it contemporaneous with the target and therefore unusable in a live strategy. A simple equal-weighted composite long-short strategy using the three valid signals is expected to earn roughly 11.5% annualized with a Sharpe ratio near 2.1. The remaining characteristics show weak or insignificant predictability in this sample.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations from 120 months, with 500 stocks per month and 1,217 distinct stocks. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. I use the provided monthly rank ICs, IC t-statistics, and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio statistics to judge signal validity.

A feature is treated as valid only if it is available at the end of month t or earlier, has a plausible economic mechanism, and shows a statistically meaningful relation to `ret_next`. I require a t-statistic above roughly 2 in absolute value and a long-short portfolio with a non-trivial Sharpe ratio. I estimate signal strength using the mean monthly rank IC and the long-short spread. For the strategy, I combine the valid signals into an equal-weighted composite score, orienting each signal in its predictive direction.

## Results
Three features pass the validity screen.

| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short mean monthly return | Long-short Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | Positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | Negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | Negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

Gross profitability is the strongest valid signal. Its rank IC is positive in about 70% of months, and the top-minus-bottom quintile spread is 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94. Asset growth is a robust negative predictor: high-asset-growth stocks underperform, and the reversed long-low/short-high portfolio earns about 0.99% per month. Idiosyncratic volatility is also negatively related to future returns; the reversed low-volatility portfolio earns about 0.74% per month.

The analyst revision signal (`analyst_rev`) has by far the largest apparent predictive power: mean rank IC of 0.2963, t-statistic of 80.29, and an 8.73% monthly long-short return. However, the data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is recorded at the end of month t+1, while `ret_next` is the return over month t+1. This makes the feature contemporaneous with the target and introduces severe look-ahead bias. It is therefore rejected despite its statistical performance.

The remaining features are weak or insignificant. `book_to_market` has a near-zero IC (0.0027) and a t-statistic of 0.68. `log_mktcap` has a small positive IC (0.0055) but a t-statistic of only 1.26. `accruals` has an IC of 0.0015 and t-statistic 0.36. `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` all have insignificant ICs and negligible long-short Sharpe ratios.

## Discussion
The valid signals align with well-known equity anomalies. Gross profitability captures a quality/profitability premium: profitable firms tend to outperform. The negative asset-growth signal reflects the investment anomaly: firms that grow assets aggressively often subsequently underperform. The negative idiosyncratic-volatility signal is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly: high-volatility stocks tend to deliver lower risk-adjusted returns.

A composite strategy that equal-weights the three valid signals—long high `gross_prof`, short high `asset_growth`, and short high `idio_vol`—should improve diversification relative to any single signal. Using the individual long-short spreads as a rough guide, the equal-weighted composite has an expected monthly return near 0.96%, or about 11.5% annualized. Because the signals capture different economic effects, the combined Sharpe ratio should exceed the average individual Sharpe; I estimate an annualized Sharpe near 2.1.

The most important practical lesson is that the analyst revision signal, despite its enormous IC, is not tradeable as specified. Any backtest that includes it would be optimistic and would not represent a live strategy.

## Limitations
This analysis relies on summary statistics rather than the raw panel, so outlier treatment, missing-data handling, and exact portfolio construction cannot be independently verified. The results are in-sample and may overstate out-of-sample performance due to data snooping, regime dependence, and factor crowding. Transaction costs, shorting costs, and market impact are ignored, which is especially important for high-turnover monthly rebalancing. The universe is limited to 500 index constituents per month, so capacity and breadth may differ from a broader equity universe. The composite Sharpe estimate assumes diversification benefits that depend on correlations not reported in the summary. Finally, while `analyst_rev` is rejected for obvious look-ahead bias, other features could have subtler timing issues not visible in the provided summary.
