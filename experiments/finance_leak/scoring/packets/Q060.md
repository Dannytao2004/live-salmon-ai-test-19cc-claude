# Cross-Sectional Equity Return Predictors: Evidence from a Point-in-Time Monthly Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a point-in-time panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Using monthly cross-sectional rank ICs, we identify gross profitability (positive), asset growth (negative), and idiosyncratic volatility (negative) as robust return signals. Analyst forecast revisions have an extremely high measured rank IC but are not tradable because they are recorded at the end of the return month. A composite quintile long-short strategy based on the three valid signals has an estimated annual return of about 12% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 2.0 before costs.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations with ten candidate features and next-month returns. For each month, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between each feature and `ret_next`. The reported rank IC is the time-series average of these monthly correlations. The IC t-statistic is computed as the mean IC divided by its standard deviation times the square root of the number of months.

A feature is treated as a valid signal if its rank IC is statistically significant and it is available at the end of month t, before the return month. We apply a conventional significance threshold of approximately |t| > 2. Long-short portfolios are equal-weighted top-quintile minus bottom-quintile portfolios rebalanced monthly.

One feature, `analyst_rev`, requires special treatment. It is defined as the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, recorded at the end of month t+1. Since it is not known at the end of month t, it cannot be used to predict the month t+1 return without look-ahead bias. We therefore exclude it as a tradable signal despite its high measured IC.

## Results
Three features emerge as statistically robust signals:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | 0.0378 | 6.07 | +1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% (raw) | -1.48 (raw) |
| `idio_vol` | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% (raw) | -1.20 (raw) |

For tradable strategies, the signs of `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` are reversed: buying low asset-growth stocks and selling high asset-growth stocks produces about +0.99% monthly, and buying low idiosyncratic-volatility stocks while selling high idiosyncratic-volatility stocks produces about +0.74% monthly.

The remaining features are not statistically significant. `book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` all have rank IC t-statistics below 1.3 in absolute value. `analyst_rev` has a rank IC of 0.2963 and a t-statistic of 80.29, but this is not usable at month t because of its t+1 recording date.

## Discussion
Gross profitability is positively related to future returns, consistent with profitability-based asset pricing and quality-investing evidence. High asset growth predicts lower returns, consistent with the investment anomaly and q-factor literature. High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly and limits-to-arbitrage explanations.

Because the three valid signals are economically distinct, combining them may improve diversification. I propose forming a composite signal each month by averaging the cross-sectional percentile ranks of `gross_prof`, negative `asset_growth`, and negative `idio_vol`, then buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile. Based on the univariate long-short results, this composite strategy is expected to earn approximately 12% per year with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.0 before transaction costs.

## Limitations
These results are based on summary statistics and do not include transaction costs, short-sale costs, or liquidity constraints. The sample covers 120 months, so the findings may be period-specific. The composite strategy performance is inferred from univariate long-short results rather than estimated from a full combined backtest. Multiple testing may also affect inference, although the three valid signals have t-statistics well above conventional thresholds.
