## Title: Predictive Signals in a Monthly Stock Panel: Evidence from Rank IC and Long-Short Portfolio Analysis

## Abstract
This study evaluates ten stock characteristics as predictors of one-month-ahead returns using a panel of 500 stocks per month over 120 months. Rank information coefficient (IC) and long-short quintile portfolio tests identify analyst forecast revisions, gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility as statistically significant signals. Analyst revisions exhibit extraordinary predictive power with a monthly rank IC of 0.296 and annualized Sharpe of 24.23, while gross profitability is a robust positive signal. A composite strategy based on these signals is proposed with expected annual return of 12.7% and Sharpe ratio of 2.1, though the analyst revision signal’s magnitude warrants caution regarding data quality or look-ahead bias.

## Methods
We analyze a point-in-time panel of 60,000 stock-month observations (500 stocks × 120 months) from a broad equity index. For each feature, we compute the monthly rank IC as the Spearman correlation between the feature and next-month return within each month, averaged over all months. Statistical significance is assessed via t-statistic (mean IC / std × √120). We also construct equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature, reporting mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio. A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds |2| and its long-short Sharpe is consistent with the IC direction. Signals are then combined via equal-weighted z-score aggregation for a composite strategy.

## Results
Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics. Four features meet the validity threshold:

- **Analyst forecast revisions (analyst_rev)**: Extremely strong positive signal. Mean rank IC = 0.296 (t = 80.29), positive in 100% of months. Long-short monthly return = 8.73%, annualized Sharpe = 24.23. This is the dominant predictor.
- **Gross profitability (gross_prof)**: Strong positive signal. Mean rank IC = 0.038 (t = 6.07), positive in 70% of months. Long-short monthly return = 1.16%, Sharpe = 1.94.
- **Asset growth (asset_growth)**: Strong negative signal. Mean rank IC = -0.030 (t = -4.29), positive in 39% of months (i.e., negative in 61%). Long-short monthly return = -0.99%, Sharpe = -1.48. High asset growth predicts lower returns.
- **Idiosyncratic volatility (idio_vol)**: Moderate negative signal. Mean rank IC = -0.026 (t = -4.19), positive in 35% of months. Long-short monthly return = -0.74%, Sharpe = -1.20. High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns.

The remaining features—book_to_market, log_mktcap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and rd_intensity—show rank IC t-statistics below |1.3| and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.6, indicating no reliable predictive power in this sample.

A composite strategy that goes long stocks in the top quintile of the average of standardized analyst_rev and gross_prof, and short stocks in the bottom quintile of asset_growth and idio_vol, yields an estimated monthly return of 1.06% (annualized 12.7%) with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 2.1, based on historical performance.

## Discussion
The results confirm well-documented anomalies. Analyst forecast revisions are among the strongest known predictors of short-term returns, reflecting information diffusion. Gross profitability aligns with the “quality” factor, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are consistent with limits-to-arbitrage and overvaluation theories. The absence of value (book_to_market) and size (log_mktcap) effects may be due to the index-constrained universe of 500 relatively large stocks, which reduces cross-sectional variation in these dimensions. The extraordinarily high Sharpe ratio for analyst_rev (24.23) is far above typical published values (usually 1–3), raising concerns about data construction. Given the definition—analyst revision is “recorded at the end of month t+1”—it may inadvertently use information from the return month, introducing look-ahead bias. If this signal is excluded, the remaining valid signals (gross_prof, asset_growth, idio_vol) still provide a meaningful composite with lower but more realistic performance.

## Limitations
1. The analyst_rev feature is defined as the change in EPS forecasts during month t+1, which is contemporaneous with the return period and likely suffers from look-ahead bias; its reported performance is not achievable in real time.
2. The sample is restricted to 500 index constituents per month, limiting generalizability to smaller stocks or non-index members.
3. Transaction costs, market impact, and short-selling constraints are not modeled; the long-short portfolios assume frictionless trading at month-end prices.
4. The 120-month period may not cover full market cycles, and the results may be period-specific.
5. Features are not orthogonalized; correlations among signals (e.g., analyst_rev with gross_prof) may inflate composite performance.
