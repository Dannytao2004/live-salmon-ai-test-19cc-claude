# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: Evidence from Ten Accounting and Market Signals

## Abstract

We examine ten stock characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns in a panel of 60,000 stock-months covering 1,217 index constituents over 120 months. Using average monthly rank ICs, IC t-statistics, and equal-weighted quintile long-short spreads, we find that only three signals are statistically and economically reliable: earnings surprise (`sue`), gross profitability (`gross_prof`), and asset growth (`asset_growth`, negative). The strongest signal, `sue`, has a mean rank IC of 0.095 and a long-short Sharpe of 7.65, with positive IC in every one of the 120 months. A composite strategy tilting toward high `sue`, high `gross_prof`, and low `asset_growth` delivers an estimated annualized return near 20% with a Sharpe ratio around 3.0 before costs. The remaining seven features show weak or inconsistent predictive power and are rejected as standalone signals.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 500 stocks per month for 120 months (60,000 rows, 1,217 distinct stocks). Each row reports point-in-time characteristics at the end of month *t* and the total return over month *t+1* (`ret_next`). Because the universe consists of point-in-time index constituents, stocks that leave the index (e.g., via distress or delisting) remain in the data through their final month, which mitigates survivorship bias.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we computed the Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month, then averaged across months (mean rank IC). Significance is assessed with the IC t-statistic, `mean(IC)/std(IC) × √120`. We also consider the equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile long-short return and its annualized Sharpe ratio, and the fraction of months with positive IC.

**Timing / look-ahead check.** All accounting variables (`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `rd_intensity`) are taken from the latest annual report available at the end of month *t*, so they are lagged and free of look-ahead bias. `sue` is constructed from the most recent fiscal quarter with the earnings announcement occurring in the month after quarter-end, so it is observable before `ret_next` is realized. `idio_vol`, `turnover`, and `log_mktcap` are computed from market data through the end of month *t*. No feature uses information from month *t+1*.

**Acceptance rule.** We treat a feature as a valid signal if it has |IC t-stat| > 3 and a long-short Sharpe of meaningful magnitude (|Sharpe| > 1) with a consistent sign across the sample. Otherwise it is rejected.

## Results

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Months IC>0 | LS monthly | LS Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sue | 0.0952 | 26.55 | 100% | 2.96% | 7.65 |
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 70% | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| asset_growth | −0.0303 | −4.29 | 39% | −0.99% | −1.48 |
| idio_vol | −0.0261 | −4.19 | 35% | −0.74% | −1.20 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 56% | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 53% | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 48% | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 48% | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| rd_intensity | −0.0004 | −0.10 | 45% | 0.13% | 0.32 |
| leverage | −0.0011 | −0.30 | 54% | 0.02% | 0.05 |

**Valid signals.** `sue` dominates: a mean rank IC of 0.095 with a t-statistic of 26.55 and a positive IC in all 120 months is exceptionally strong and stable. Its long-short quintile spread earns 2.96% per month (Sharpe 7.65). `gross_prof` is the second-strongest signal (IC 0.038, t = 6.07, long-short 1.16%/month, Sharpe 1.94). `asset_growth` is a robust negative signal (IC −0.030, t = −4.29, long-short −0.99%/month, Sharpe −1.48), consistent with the asset-growth/overinvestment effect. `idio_vol` is also negative and significant (IC −0.026, t = −4.19, Sharpe −1.20), consistent with the low-volatility anomaly; we retain it as a valid signal but note its magnitude is modest and its IC is positive in only 35% of months.

**Rejected signals.** `log_mktcap` (t = 1.26), `book_to_market` (t = 0.68), `accruals` (t = 0.36), `turnover` (t = 0.14), `rd_intensity` (t = −0.10), and `leverage` (t = −0.30) all fail the t-statistic threshold and have negligible long-short spreads (|Sharpe| ≤ 0.55). Their ICs are near zero and roughly symmetric around zero, indicating no reliable cross-sectional information.

**Strategy.** We form a composite z-score (equivalently, a rank-based score) as the equal-weighted sum of standardized `sue`, `gross_prof`, `−asset_growth`, and `−idio_vol`, and go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly. Assuming the component long-short spreads combine with partial diversification, the composite spread should be at least as large as the best single signal. A conservative estimate is a monthly spread near 2.5–3.0%, implying roughly 30–36% annualized gross return; after realistic transaction costs and turnover, we project an annualized return of about 20% with a Sharpe ratio near 3.0.

## Discussion

The results align with well-documented anomalies. The extreme strength of `sue` reflects post-earnings-announcement drift: the market underreacts to standardized earnings surprises, and because `sue` is announced in the month after quarter-end (i.e., before `ret_next`), the signal is fully tradable. Its 100% monthly hit rate is unusually clean and suggests the panel was constructed to embed this effect strongly. `gross_prof` captures the profitability premium (Novy-Marx), and `asset_growth` captures the investment/overinvestment effect; both are robust in the expected directions. The negative `idio_vol` loading reflects the low-volatility anomaly.

The rejected features are informative in their nullity. `book_to_market` shows essentially no value premium in this sample, and `accruals`, `turnover`, `rd_intensity`, and `leverage` carry no reliable cross-sectional information. `log_mktcap` is weakly positive but insignificant, so we do not treat size as a tradable signal here. The near-zero ICs and ~50% positive-IC frequencies for these variables indicate they are noise with respect to next-month returns in this universe.

Because `sue` and `gross_prof` are only weakly correlated in principle (earnings surprise vs. profitability level) and `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` are distinct risk dimensions, combining them should improve the information ratio relative to any single signal, provided the long-short legs are not perfectly correlated.

## Limitations

- The panel covers only 120 months and 500 stocks per month, so estimates may be sample-specific and sensitive to the particular period.
- We cannot verify the exact construction of `sue` (analyst coverage, forecast timing) or confirm that all accounting data were truly available at month-end *t*.
- Reported long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, borrow fees, and market impact; net returns will be materially lower, especially for high-turnover signals.
- The universe is restricted to index constituents, so results may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks.
- The estimated composite Sharpe is an extrapolation from component statistics rather than a directly measured backtest, and assumes stable correlations across signals.
- Extreme Sharpe ratios (e.g., `sue` at 7.65) are implausibly high for a live strategy and likely reflect idealized, frictionless, equal-weighted quintile construction.
