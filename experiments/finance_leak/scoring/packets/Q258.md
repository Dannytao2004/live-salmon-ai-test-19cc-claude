# Report Q258

# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Large-Cap Equity Panel: Evidence from Nine Accounting and Market Signals

---

## Abstract

We examine nine point-in-time stock characteristics for their ability to predict next-month returns in a panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months (60,000 stock-month observations, 1,217 distinct stocks). Using monthly cross-sectional rank information coefficients (IC) and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find that only three signals carry statistically meaningful predictive power: gross profitability (positive), asset growth (negative), and idiosyncratic volatility (negative). Each achieves an IC t-statistic above 4 in absolute value. The remaining six signals—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show t-statistics below 1.5 and are rejected as standalone return predictors. A composite strategy that buys high-profitability, low-asset-growth, low-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks and sells the opposite is expected to earn roughly 9–10% annualized with a Sharpe ratio near 1.5–1.8, net of the simplifying assumptions we state.

---

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains one row per stock per month for 120 months, with a fixed universe of 500 index constituents each month. Because the universe is a point-in-time index membership list, stocks that leave the index (distress, delisting, acquisition) remain in the data through their final month, including the final-month return. This avoids survivorship bias in the target variable but means the universe is not a constant set of 500 names over time (1,217 distinct stocks total).

**Features.** All nine features are lagged and point-in-time: accounting variables come from the latest annual report available at month-end *t*, and market variables (log market cap, idiosyncratic volatility, turnover) are measured at or before month-end *t*. The target `ret_next` is the total return from month-end *t* to month-end *t+1*. No look-ahead is present by construction.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use two complementary statistics reported in the panel:

1. **Rank IC**: the Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` computed *within* each month across the ~500 stocks, then averaged over the 120 months. The t-statistic is `mean(IC) / std(IC) × √120`. This measures monotonic predictive content and is robust to outliers and to the cross-sectional return distribution.
2. **Long-short quintile portfolio**: equal-weighted long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of each feature, rebalanced monthly. We report the mean monthly return and the annualized Sharpe ratio (mean/std × √12).

**Decision rule.** We treat a signal as valid if |IC t-stat| ≳ 2.5–3 (a conservative bar given 120 months and multiple testing across nine signals) *and* the long-short portfolio has the same sign and a Sharpe of roughly 0.5 or better. Signals failing either condition are rejected.

**Composite strategy.** For the valid signals we form a simple equal-weighted z-score composite, oriented so that higher composite values predict higher returns (gross profitability positive; asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility negated). We estimate performance by combining the individual long-short spreads, acknowledging that correlations between signals will reduce the combined volatility relative to the naive sum.

---

## Results

**Signal-level results.** The table below summarizes the evidence (reproduced from the panel statistics).

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | IC>0 share | LS monthly return | LS annualized Sharpe | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gross_prof | +0.0378 | 6.07 | 70% | +1.16% | 1.94 | Valid (positive) |
| asset_growth | −0.0303 | −4.29 | 39% | −0.99% | −1.48 | Valid (negative) |
| idio_vol | −0.0261 | −4.19 | 35% | −0.74% | −1.20 | Valid (negative) |
| log_mktcap | +0.0055 | 1.26 | 56% | +0.23% | 0.55 | Rejected |
| book_to_market | +0.0027 | 0.68 | 53% | +0.09% | 0.23 | Rejected |
| accruals | +0.0015 | 0.36 | 48% | +0.14% | 0.36 | Rejected |
| leverage | −0.0011 | −0.30 | 54% | +0.02% | 0.05 | Rejected |
| turnover | +0.0006 | 0.14 | 48% | +0.02% | 0.06 | Rejected |
| rd_intensity | −0.0004 | −0.10 | 45% | +0.13% | 0.32 | Rejected |

**Gross profitability** is the strongest signal. Its IC of 0.038 with a t-stat of 6.07 and a 70% hit rate on the sign of the IC is highly consistent across months. The long-short spread of 1.16% per month (≈14.9% annualized, gross) with a Sharpe of 1.94 is the single best-performing signal. This is consistent with the well-documented profitability premium.

**Asset growth** is the strongest negative signal. The IC of −0.030 (t = −4.29) and a long-short spread of −0.99% per month (≈−11.2% annualized) confirm the asset-growth (investment) anomaly: firms that expand their balance sheets aggressively subsequently underperform.

**Idiosyncratic volatility** is also reliably negative. The IC of −0.026 (t = −4.19) and a long-short spread of −0.74% per month (≈−8.5% annualized) reproduce the low-volatility anomaly. Its Sharpe of −1.20 is economically meaningful.

**Rejected signals.** Book-to-market shows a positive but economically trivial IC (0.0027) and a Sharpe of only 0.23, failing both bars. Log market cap is weakly positive (IC 0.0055, t = 1.26) and its long-short Sharpe of 0.55 is below our threshold; in a 500-name large-cap index universe, size has little cross-sectional dispersion to exploit. Accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity all have |t| < 0.4 and long-short Sharpes below 0.4, indistinguishable from noise.

**Composite strategy.** Combining the three valid signals, each oriented correctly, produces a composite whose expected long-short return is approximately the average of the three spreads (since they are roughly equally weighted and not perfectly correlated): (1.16% − 0.99% − 0.74%)/3 ≈ −0.19% as a naive average of signed spreads—but this arithmetic is misleading because the signals are *individually* oriented. Properly oriented, the composite long-short spread is the average of the absolute spreads, ≈ (1.16 + 0.99 + 0.74)/3 ≈ 0.96% per month gross, or ≈11.6% annualized. Because the three signals are imperfectly correlated (profitability, investment, and volatility capture distinct effects), the composite volatility is materially lower than the average of the individual volatilities, so the composite Sharpe is plausibly higher than the average of the individual Sharpes. Taking the individual Sharpes of 1.94, 1.48, and 1.20 and assuming pairwise signal correlations of roughly 0.2–0.3, the composite Sharpe is approximately 1.5–1.8. We therefore estimate an expected annualized return of ~9–10% after a modest haircut for implementation and an expected annualized Sharpe of ~1.5.

---

## Discussion

The results align closely with established empirical asset-pricing findings. Profitability, investment (asset growth), and idiosyncratic volatility are three of the most robust cross-sectional predictors in the academic literature, and they emerge here with t-statistics above 4 in a clean, survivorship-bias-free panel. The failure of book-to-market, accruals, and leverage is more surprising relative to the classic value and accrual anomalies, but is explainable by the universe: a 500-stock broad index is dominated by large, mature, relatively homogeneous firms, which compresses the cross-sectional dispersion in valuation ratios, accruals, and leverage and attenuates their predictive power. Turnover and R&D intensity likewise have limited dispersion in this universe and show no reliable signal.

The economic interpretation is straightforward. Gross profitability captures quality: highly profitable firms earn persistent economic rents and are rewarded. Asset growth captures the investment channel: managers who over-expand tend to do so when their stock is overvalued, and subsequent returns disappoint. Idiosyncratic volatility captures the low-volatility anomaly, which may reflect leverage constraints, lottery-seeking behavior, or limits to arbitrage.

A practical composite strategy would be long high-gross-prof, low-asset-growth, low-idio-vol stocks and short the opposite. Because the three signals are only weakly correlated, the combined portfolio diversifies idiosyncratic signal noise and should deliver a higher risk-adjusted return than any single signal. Turnover in the composite is moderate (monthly rebalancing of quintiles), and the index-constituent universe keeps transaction costs and capacity concerns manageable.

---

## Limitations

1. **Multiple testing.** Nine signals were evaluated; with a t-statistic threshold of ~2.5–3, the three "valid" signals survive, but we cannot rule out that some of the weaker signals would appear significant in a longer sample or different universe.
2. **Universe specificity.** The 500-name large-cap index universe compresses dispersion in valuation, accrual, and leverage variables, so our rejections may reflect limited cross-sectional variation rather than a true absence of predictive power in the broader market.
3. **No transaction costs or capacity analysis.** The reported long-short returns are gross; net-of-cost performance, especially for the higher-turnover idiosyncratic-volatility leg, would be lower.
4. **Equal-weighted quintile portfolios** ignore market-cap weighting, borrow costs on the short side, and any constraints on shorting.
5. **No factor-neutralization.** The signals are evaluated raw; part of the profitability and low-vol spreads may be explained by exposures to size, value, or momentum, which we do not control for.
6. **Regime dependence.** The 120-month sample may span a single dominant macro regime; the stability of these signals out of sample is unknown.
