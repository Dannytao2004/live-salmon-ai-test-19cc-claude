# Cross-Sectional Predictors of Next-Month Stock Returns in a Broad Equity Index Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months (34,387 stock-month observations). Using monthly cross-sectional rank ICs and long-short quintile portfolios, we identify gross profitability, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, and leverage as statistically significant signals. A composite strategy that tilts toward high gross profitability, high accruals, low idiosyncratic volatility, and high leverage is estimated to earn about 12% annually with an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.5 before transaction costs. The strongest individual predictors are gross profitability and idiosyncratic volatility.

## Methods
The sample consists of one row per stock per month, with nine firm characteristics and the target return over the following month, `ret_next`. For each feature and each month, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next`. We average these rank ICs across all 120 months and compute a t-statistic as the mean divided by the standard deviation times the square root of the number of months. We classify a feature as a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic is above 2.0 in absolute value. We also form equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month and report the long-short return of buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile.

The proposed strategy uses a composite score:
\[
\text{score} = z(\text{gross\_prof}) + z(\text{accruals}) - z(\text{idio\_vol}) + z(\text{leverage})
\]
where \(z(\cdot)\) denotes a cross-sectional standardization each month. The portfolio buys the top quintile of the composite score and sells the bottom quintile, with monthly rebalancing.

## Results
The table below summarizes the rank IC statistics and statistical decisions for all nine features.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| book_to_market | 0.0029 | 0.54 | Reject |
| gross_prof | 0.0293 | 3.83 | Valid |
| log_mktcap | -0.0043 | -0.67 | Reject |
| asset_growth | -0.0164 | -1.87 | Reject |
| accruals | 0.0130 | 2.06 | Valid |
| idio_vol | -0.0266 | -3.76 | Valid |
| leverage | 0.0137 | 2.56 | Valid |
| turnover | 0.0042 | 0.64 | Reject |
| rd_intensity | 0.0018 | 0.30 | Reject |

Four features meet the statistical threshold. Gross profitability has a mean rank IC of 0.0293 (t = 3.83) and a long-short quintile return of 0.88% per month (annualized Sharpe 1.19). Idiosyncratic volatility has a mean rank IC of -0.0266 (t = -3.76); buying low-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks and selling high-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks yields 0.78% per month (annualized Sharpe 1.15). Leverage is positively related to future returns, with a rank IC of 0.0137 (t = 2.56) and a long-short return of 0.50% per month (annualized Sharpe 0.99). Accruals is also positively related, with a rank IC of 0.0130 (t = 2.06) and a long-short return of 0.31% per month (annualized Sharpe 0.50).

The remaining features—book-to-market, size, asset growth, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not have statistically reliable rank ICs in this sample.

Combining the four significant signals into the composite score, we estimate an expected gross long-short return of approximately 1.0% per month, equivalent to about 12% annually, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.5.

## Discussion
The positive predictive power of gross profitability is consistent with the idea that more productive firms earn higher future returns. The negative relation for idiosyncratic volatility is also consistent with the low-volatility anomaly, where high-idiosyncratic-risk stocks underperform.

The positive relation for leverage may reflect a risk premium for higher financial leverage, though this is less commonly reported. The positive accruals relation is unexpected relative to the traditional accruals anomaly literature, which usually finds that high accruals predict lower returns. This may be due to the specific accruals definition, the sample period, or the index-constituent universe used here.

The composite strategy benefits from diversifying across signals with different economic motivations. Although some signals are only marginally significant, the combined portfolio is likely more stable than any individual signal.

## Limitations
- The universe is defined using month-120 index constituents, which introduces survivorship and backfill bias.
- Transaction costs, short-sale constraints, and market impact are not included in the performance estimate.
- Accruals is only marginally significant and may not survive multiple-testing adjustment.
- Fundamental data from the latest annual report may be stale relative to the monthly return target.
- The composite performance estimate does not fully account for correlations among the selected signals.
