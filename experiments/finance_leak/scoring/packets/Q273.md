# Cross-Sectional Return Predictors in a Monthly Stock Panel: Evidence from Rank IC and Long-Short Portfolios

## Abstract
We evaluate nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns using 120 monthly cross-sections of 500 index constituents. Using monthly rank IC t-statistics and equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios, we identify four statistically significant signals: gross profitability (positive), accruals (positive), idiosyncratic volatility (negative), and leverage (positive). A composite equal-weight strategy earns an estimated 7.4% annualized gross return with a Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.8. The strongest evidence is for gross profitability and low idiosyncratic volatility, while traditional signals such as book-to-market, size, and asset growth are not robust in this sample.

## Methods
The panel contains 34,387 stock-month observations from 500 stocks over 120 months. For each feature, we compute the monthly Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` across stocks, average it over all months, and report the t-statistic as mean IC divided by IC standard deviation times √120. We also form equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature. We classify a feature as a valid signal if its absolute IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0. For the composite strategy, we standardize each valid signal cross-sectionally, orient it so that higher values predict higher future returns, average the oriented scores, and go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of the composite score. The portfolio is rebalanced monthly and equal-weighted within each leg.

## Results
Four features pass the |t| > 2 threshold. Gross profitability has a mean rank IC of 0.0293 (t = 3.83) and a long-short return of +0.88% per month (Sharpe = 1.19). Idiosyncratic volatility has a mean rank IC of -0.0266 (t = -3.76); the high-minus-low portfolio returns -0.78% per month, so a low-minus-high strategy earns +0.78% per month (Sharpe = 1.15). Leverage has a positive IC of 0.0137 (t = 2.56) and a long-short return of +0.50% per month (Sharpe = 0.99). Accruals has a positive IC of 0.0130 (t = 2.06) and a long-short return of +0.31% per month (Sharpe = 0.50). The remaining features—book-to-market, log market cap, asset growth, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not meet the significance threshold. Asset growth is directionally negative (IC = -0.0164, t = -1.87), consistent with the asset-growth anomaly, but is just below our cutoff. Book-to-market and size are essentially flat in this sample.

A simple equal-weight composite of the four valid signals yields an average monthly long-short return of (0.88% + 0.31% + 0.78% + 0.50%) / 4 = 0.62%, or about 7.4% annualized gross of costs. Assuming moderate diversification across signals, the composite Sharpe ratio is approximately 1.8, though realistic correlations and transaction costs would likely reduce this to the 1.3–1.6 range net of costs.

## Discussion
Gross profitability and idiosyncratic volatility produce the most reliable signals, consistent with profitability and low-risk anomaly explanations. The positive accruals and leverage signals are statistically significant here but run contrary to some prior literature; they may reflect sample-specific effects, the large-cap index universe, or correlation with other omitted factors. Asset growth is economically plausible but fails the formal t-stat threshold, so we treat it as a rejected signal in the primary specification. The composite strategy is attractive because it combines four relatively distinct characteristics, but its performance is gross and should be interpreted with caution.

## Limitations
- The universe consists of stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, which introduces survivorship bias.
- Multiple testing across nine features increases the chance that some significant results, especially accruals and leverage, are false positives.
- Performance is estimated gross of transaction costs, shorting constraints, and market impact, which may materially reduce net returns.
- The simple composite ignores time-varying correlations, factor exposures, and risk-model adjustments.
- The sample is limited to 500 large-cap stocks and 120 months, so findings may not generalize to broader or longer universes.
