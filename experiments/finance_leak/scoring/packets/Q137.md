# Evaluating Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a Monthly Stock Panel

## Abstract
I evaluate nine stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a panel of 500 index stocks over 120 months. Rank IC tests identify gross profitability, accruals, and leverage as positive signals and idiosyncratic volatility as a negative signal; the remaining five characteristics, including book-to-market and size, are not statistically reliable. An equal-weighted composite of the four valid signals produces an estimated long-short return of about 0.62% per month, or 7.4% annually, with an estimated gross annualized Sharpe ratio of about 1.2. These results are gross of transaction costs and subject to index-membership and sample-specific biases.

## Methods
For each month, I compute cross-sectional Spearman rank correlations between each characteristic and next-month returns. A feature is considered a valid signal if its mean rank IC has a t-statistic with absolute value above 2.0, corresponding to approximately 5% two-sided significance over 120 months. I also examine equal-weighted top-minus-bottom quintile long-short portfolios.

For the composite strategy, each valid feature is cross-sectionally z-scored each month and sign-adjusted so that positive values represent expected higher returns. The four adjusted z-scores are averaged into a composite score; the strategy buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile equal-weight.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics and decisions.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Direction | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| gross_prof | 0.0293 | 3.83 | Positive | Valid |
| idio_vol | -0.0266 | -3.76 | Negative | Valid |
| leverage | 0.0137 | 2.56 | Positive | Valid |
| accruals | 0.0130 | 2.06 | Positive | Valid |
| asset_growth | -0.0164 | -1.87 | Negative | Rejected, marginal |
| book_to_market | 0.0029 | 0.54 | None | Rejected |
| log_mktcap | -0.0043 | -0.67 | None | Rejected |
| turnover | 0.0042 | 0.64 | None | Rejected |
| rd_intensity | 0.0018 | 0.30 | None | Rejected |

The strongest signals are gross profitability and idiosyncratic volatility. Leverage and accruals are statistically significant but economically weaker. Asset growth is directionally consistent with a negative premium but does not meet the conventional significance threshold.

The individual long-short monthly returns for the four valid signals are: gross_prof 0.88%, idio_vol -0.78%, leverage 0.50%, and accruals 0.31%. An equal-weighted combination of these four signal legs gives an estimated gross return of roughly 0.62% per month, or about 7.4% per year. Diversification across the four signals gives an estimated gross annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.2.

## Discussion
The results support a multi-signal equity strategy that combines profitability, low idiosyncratic risk, moderate leverage, and positive accruals exposure in this sample. Unlike some classic value and size anomalies, book-to-market and market capitalization do not show significant predictive power here. The composite approach is expected to be more stable than any single signal because the four valid signals are not perfectly correlated and capture different economic dimensions.

## Limitations
This analysis has several limitations. First, the sample is conditioned on stocks that are index constituents at month 120, which may create survivorship and backfill biases. Second, all returns are gross of transaction costs, market impact, short-selling fees, and borrow constraints. Third, signal correlations and multiple testing may lead to overstated Sharpe ratios. Finally, the early sample contains fewer stocks, which may make early quintile portfolio returns noisier.
