# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a 500-Stock Monthly Panel: The Dominance of Analyst Revisions

## Abstract

We examine ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-months). Only one feature, `analyst_rev` (analyst consensus EPS revision scaled by price), exhibits a robust and economically large relationship with `ret_next`, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.296 (t = 89.6) and a long-short quintile spread of 8.66% per month (annualized Sharpe 26.4). All other nine features display negligible rank ICs (|IC| < 0.007) and t-statistics below 1.7, consistent with the absence of exploitable cross-sectional signal. We propose a long-short strategy that buys the top quintile and shorts the bottom quintile of `analyst_rev`, rebalanced monthly. The central caveat is that `analyst_rev` is recorded at the end of month t+1, meaning the signal overlaps the prediction window and likely embeds look-ahead information; the strategy's headline performance should therefore be treated as an upper bound rather than a tradable result.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows: 500 stocks per month for 120 months (1,213 distinct stocks). Each row reports ten firm characteristics observed at the end of month *t*, and `ret_next`, the stock's total return over month *t+1*.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the provided monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank IC (correlation between feature rank and `ret_next` rank within each month), averaged across the 120 months, with an associated t-statistic. We also use the equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio return (long top quintile, short bottom quintile) and its annualized Sharpe ratio. We treat a feature as a valid signal if it shows a consistent sign, a large t-statistic, and a material long-short spread.

**Strategy construction.** The strategy takes equal-weighted long positions in the top quintile of the chosen signal and equal-weighted short positions in the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly. Expected annual return is approximated as 12 × the mean monthly long-short return; the Sharpe is taken from the reported annualized figure.

`analyst_rev` is the only feature with a meaningful relationship to `ret_next`. Its rank IC is positive in **all 120 months**, its t-statistic is extreme, and its long-short portfolio earns 8.66% per month with a Sharpe of 26.4. No other feature clears even a t-statistic of 2, and their long-short spreads (≤ 0.29%/month) are within noise. We therefore designate `analyst_rev` as the sole valid signal and reject the remaining nine.

## Discussion

The results are starkly concentrated: analyst revisions dominate, while classic value, quality, growth, accrual, volatility, size, turnover, and R&D signals show no cross-sectional predictive power here. This pattern is economically plausible—analyst revisions are known to be among the strongest short-horizon return predictors—but the magnitude is implausibly large for a genuinely tradable signal. The data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is the change in the FY1 EPS forecast *during month t+1*, recorded *at the end of month t+1*, while `ret_next` is the return *over month t+1*. The signal and the target therefore share the same information window, so the measured IC almost certainly reflects contemporaneous (look-ahead) information rather than a lagged, tradable prediction. The near-perfect IC (0.296 with 100% positive months) is consistent with this interpretation. If the feature were instead lagged to be observable at the end of month *t*, its predictive power would plausibly fall by one or more orders of magnitude toward the range of ordinary analyst-revision effects (rank IC on the order of 0.02–0.05).

## Limitations

- `analyst_rev` is timestamped at the end of month *t+1*, so it overlaps the return window and likely embeds look-ahead bias; the reported IC and long-short returns are not a tradable result.
- No transaction costs, slippage, borrow costs, or capacity constraints are modeled; the long-short spread is a gross, frictionless figure.
- The universe is limited to 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks.
- The panel is short (120 months) and covers a single (unspecified) market regime, so statistical stability across cycles is untested.
- The remaining features are evaluated only through summary IC and quintile statistics; nonlinear effects, interactions, and conditional signals are not explored.
