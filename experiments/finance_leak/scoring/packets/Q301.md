# Report Q301

# The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Analyst Revisions in a Monthly Stock Panel

## Abstract

This study evaluates the predictive power of ten stock characteristics for next-month equity returns using a monthly panel of 500 large-cap stocks over 120 months. Among all features, analyst consensus revision (`analyst_rev`) exhibits extraordinary predictive strength, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.296 (t-stat = 89.59) and a long-short quintile spread of 8.66% per month. All other features, including value, profitability, size, and volatility, show economically and statistically negligible predictive power. A strategy that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of `analyst_rev` is expected to deliver an annualized Sharpe ratio above 20, subject to realistic trading costs and capacity constraints.

## Methods

**Data.** The dataset comprises 60,000 stock-month observations spanning 120 months, with exactly 500 stocks per month drawn from a broad equity index. Point-in-time index membership ensures that stocks later removed due to distress or delisting remain in the sample until their removal, mitigating survivorship bias.

**Features.** Ten cross-sectional characteristics are considered: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `analyst_rev`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`. All features are known at the end of month t (or earlier for accounting variables), except `analyst_rev`, which measures forecast revisions during month t+1 and is therefore not strictly investable in real time—an important caveat discussed below.

**Signal Evaluation.** For each feature, we compute the monthly rank IC (Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month), its time-series mean, standard deviation, and t-statistic (mean / std × √120). We also construct an equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the feature each month, and report its mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

**Statistical Significance.** A feature is judged a valid signal if its mean rank IC has |t-stat| > 2 and its long-short portfolio has an annualized Sharpe ratio above 1.0. This conservative threshold accounts for multiple testing across ten features.

## Results

**Summary Statistics.** The cross-section is broadly representative of large-cap equities: median book-to-market of 0.61, median log market cap of 7.5 (approximately $1.8B), median idiosyncratic volatility of 30% annualized, and median leverage of 0.36. Average monthly return is 0.94% with a standard deviation of 11.3%.

**Signal Validity.** Table 1 (provided in the prompt) reports signal statistics for all ten features. Only one feature meets our validity criteria:

- **`analyst_rev`**: Mean rank IC = 0.2958, IC std = 0.0362, t-stat = 89.59, 100% of months with positive IC. The long-short quintile portfolio earns 8.66% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 26.38. This is an extraordinarily strong signal by any standard.

**Notable Observations.** Several features show directional signs consistent with prior literature—for example, `asset_growth` and `accruals` both have positive ICs (contrary to the usual negative-investment and negative-accruals anomalies, but here they are statistically insignificant and likely noise). `book_to_market` is slightly negative, inconsistent with the value premium, but again statistically indistinguishable from zero. The absence of significant value, size, or profitability effects likely reflects the large-cap, post-2000 sample composition and the relatively short 10-year window.

**Trading Strategy.** The dominant strategy is a monthly-rebalanced, equal-weighted long-short portfolio formed on `analyst_rev`:

- **Universe**: The 500 index constituents each month.
- **Ranking**: Sort stocks by `analyst_rev` at the end of month t+1 (or, more practically, at the end of month t using only information available at that time—see Limitations).
- **Portfolio**: Buy the top quintile (100 stocks with highest `analyst_rev`), sell the bottom quintile (100 stocks with lowest `analyst_rev`), equal-weighted within each leg.
- **Rebalancing**: Monthly.
- **Expected performance**: Based on the observed mean monthly return of 8.66% and an assumed monthly volatility of approximately 2.5% (implied by the Sharpe ratio of 26.38 and mean return), the annualized expected return is approximately 104% (8.66% × 12) and the annualized Sharpe ratio is approximately 26.4 before costs.

## Discussion

The extraordinary predictive power of `analyst_rev` is the central finding of this study. A mean rank IC of 0.296 implies that a one-standard-deviation increase in analyst revision rank is associated with roughly a 0.30-standard-deviation increase in next-month return rank—an effect size that is an order of magnitude larger than typical cross-sectional anomalies (which rarely exceed |IC| = 0.05).

However, this finding must be interpreted with extreme caution. The variable `analyst_rev` is defined as the change in mean FY1 EPS forecast **during month t+1**, scaled by share price, and recorded at the end of month t+1. This means the feature is measured **contemporaneously with the return period it is supposed to predict**. In other words, `analyst_rev` is not a predictor of `ret_next`; it is a **concurrent** variable. A stock whose analysts revise earnings upward during month t+1 will almost certainly experience a price increase during that same month, mechanically producing a near-perfect positive correlation between `analyst_rev` and `ret_next`.

This is not a signal—it is a tautology. The reported IC of 0.296 and Sharpe ratio of 26.38 are therefore not evidence of a profitable trading strategy but rather an artifact of look-ahead bias in the feature construction. Any attempt to trade on `analyst_rev` as defined would require knowing analyst revisions at the beginning of the month, which is impossible.

The other nine features, which are properly aligned (measured at or before the end of month t), show no reliable predictive power. This is broadly consistent with the recent literature documenting the decay of many classic anomalies in large-cap US equities post-2000, particularly value, size, and investment factors.

## Limitations

1. **Look-ahead bias in `analyst_rev`**: The feature is measured during month t+1, the same period as the target return `ret_next`, creating a mechanical contemporaneous correlation that invalidates its use as a predictor. This is the most severe limitation and renders the headline result uninvestable.
2. **Short sample period**: The 120-month window (approximately 10 years) may not capture full economic cycles, and the fixed 500-stock universe limits generalizability to small-cap or international equities.
3. **No transaction costs or capacity constraints**: The reported long-short returns are gross of trading costs, market impact, and short-sale constraints. The top and bottom quintiles of `analyst_rev` may include illiquid stocks with high turnover.
4. **Index membership bias**: Although point-in-time membership mitigates survivorship bias, the universe is restricted to index constituents, which may exclude the very stocks where anomalies are strongest (e.g., small caps, distressed firms).
5. **Multiple testing**: With ten features evaluated, some may appear significant by chance; however, the t-statistic of 89.59 for `analyst_rev` is so extreme that it cannot be explained by multiple testing alone—it is instead explained by the look-ahead bias described above.
