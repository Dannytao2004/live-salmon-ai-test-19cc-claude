# Post-Earnings Announcement Drift in a Monthly Equity Panel: SUE as the Dominant Cross-Sectional Signal

## Abstract
We evaluate ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Using monthly Spearman rank ICs, t-statistics, and equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios, only standardized unexpected earnings (`sue`) is a statistically and economically robust signal. SUE has a mean monthly rank IC of 0.0998, an IC t-statistic of 23.25, positive ICs in 99% of months, and a top-minus-bottom quintile return of 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. A monthly strategy that buys high-SUE stocks and shorts low-SUE stocks has an estimated gross annualized return of 36.48%. All other characteristics are weak, unstable, or statistically indistinguishable from noise.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations, 1,213 distinct stocks, and 120 months. Each month has 500 stocks. The target is `ret_next`, the stock return over month t+1. The ten candidate features are `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `sue`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

Signal evaluation uses the provided monthly cross-sectional rank ICs. For each feature, the mean rank IC is the average Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks within a month. The t-statistic is computed as mean IC divided by IC standard deviation, multiplied by the square root of 120 months. Long-short portfolios are equal-weighted top-quintile minus bottom-quintile portfolios, rebalanced monthly. Annualized Sharpe ratios are provided as monthly mean divided by monthly volatility, scaled by √12.

I classify a signal as valid if it has a clear economic mechanism, a mean rank IC with |t-stat| above approximately 2, consistent sign across months, and a long-short Sharpe ratio that is economically meaningful. Because ten features are tested, I also require the evidence to be strong enough to survive multiple-testing concerns.

## Results
Only `sue` passes the validity thresholds. Its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0998, its IC t-statistic is 23.25, and its IC is positive in 99% of months. The equal-weighted top-minus-bottom SUE quintile portfolio earns 3.04% per month, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. The direction is positive: higher standardized unexpected earnings predict higher next-month returns.

The remaining features have weak and statistically insignificant predictive power. `asset_growth` has a mean rank IC of 0.0061 and a t-statistic of 1.64, with a long-short Sharpe of 0.72. `accruals` has a mean rank IC of 0.0055 and a t-statistic of 1.34, with a long-short Sharpe of 0.70. Neither is significant at conventional levels. `gross_prof` has a mean IC of 0.0039 and t-statistic of 0.93. `idio_vol` has a mean IC of 0.0043 and t-statistic of 1.14. `book_to_market` and `leverage` have small negative ICs, but their t-statistics are only -0.72 and -1.00. `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` have essentially zero mean rank ICs and negligible long-short performance.

## Discussion
The strong performance of `sue` is consistent with the post-earnings announcement drift literature: prices underreact to earnings surprises, so positive-surprise firms continue to outperform and negative-surprise firms continue to underperform. The effect is unusually strong in this panel, with an IC t-statistic above 23 and positive ICs in 99% of months.

The strategy implication is straightforward: each month, form an equal-weighted long portfolio of the highest-SUE quintile and an equal-weighted short portfolio of the lowest-SUE quintile. The reported long-short mean monthly return is 3.04%, implying a simple annualized gross return of approximately 36.48%. The reported annualized Sharpe ratio is 7.31. These figures are gross of transaction costs, borrowing costs, and market impact.

The other features are rejected. Some, such as `asset_growth` and `accruals`, show marginally positive long-short returns, but their t-statistics are below 2 and their Sharpe ratios are below 1. Given the number of tested signals, these are not reliable standalone predictors. Value, profitability, size, volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity show no robust next-month return predictability in this sample.

## Limitations
The sample covers only 120 months and 500 stocks per month, so the results may be period-specific or universe-specific. The reported returns and Sharpe ratios are gross of transaction costs, shorting costs, and market impact, which are likely material for a monthly long-short strategy. SUE construction depends on earnings announcement timing and may be affected by look-ahead bias if not perfectly lagged. The strategy is equal-weighted, which may tilt toward smaller or less liquid stocks and may not be scalable. The extremely high Sharpe ratio is implausible for a live strategy and likely reflects in-sample optimization or data limitations. Finally, the analysis does not control for common risk factors, sector exposures, or multiple-testing corrections beyond qualitative judgment.
