# Earnings Surprise as the Dominant Cross-Sectional Predictor of Next-Month Stock Returns

## Abstract
We examine ten stock characteristics in a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months to identify predictors of next-month returns. Standardized unexpected earnings (`sue`) is the only robust signal: its monthly rank IC is 0.0998 with a t-statistic of 23.25, and a top-minus-bottom quintile strategy earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 7.31. All other features have rank ICs near zero and t-statistics below conventional significance thresholds. We propose a monthly-rebalanced long-short strategy based on `sue` and discuss important implementation caveats.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations with ten candidate features and one target, `ret_next`. For each feature and each month, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next`. The reported rank IC is the time-series mean of these monthly correlations, and the t-statistic is computed as the mean divided by the standard deviation of monthly ICs multiplied by the square root of the number of months. Long-short returns are formed by buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile of each feature each month, equal-weighted within each quintile.

We use a conventional significance threshold of approximately |t| > 2.0 for a single signal. With ten signals tested, a Bonferroni-adjusted threshold would be stricter; the key conclusion is unchanged because only one signal is highly significant.

## Results
Among the ten features, `sue` is the clear outlier. Its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0998, its IC t-statistic is 23.25, and 99% of monthly ICs are positive. The top-minus-bottom quintile spread earns an average of 3.04% per month, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31.

The remaining nine features all have economically small and statistically insignificant rank ICs. `asset_growth` has the largest t-statistic among them at 1.64, followed by `accruals` at 1.34 and `idio_vol` at 1.14. `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` all have t-statistics near or below 1.0 in absolute value. None of these features produce long-short Sharpe ratios above 0.75, and most are close to zero.

The results suggest that the earnings surprise signal captured by `sue` is the dominant and essentially the only reliable cross-sectional return predictor in this panel.

## Discussion
The strong positive relation between `sue` and next-month returns is consistent with the post-earnings-announcement drift literature: firms with positive earnings surprises tend to outperform firms with negative surprises over the following month. The economic magnitude of the signal is large, with a monthly top-bottom quintile spread of 3.04% before costs.

The absence of reliable value, size, profitability, investment, leverage, volatility, turnover, and R&D intensity effects may reflect the specific sample, the construction of the features, or the short one-month horizon. However, given the reported t-statistics, none of these features can be confidently treated as valid signals in this dataset.

## Limitations
The reported performance is gross of transaction costs, short-selling fees, borrow constraints, and market impact; net performance would be lower. The extremely high Sharpe ratio of the `sue` strategy is likely overstated and may reflect in-sample properties of the data. The sample covers only 120 months of index constituents, so the results may not generalize to other periods or universes. The analysis is univariate and does not control for industry, sector, or interactions with other signals.
