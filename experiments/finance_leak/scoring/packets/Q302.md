# Predictive Signals in a Monthly Stock Panel: Evidence from Rank IC and Long-Short Portfolio Analysis

## Abstract

This study evaluates ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns using a panel of 500 stocks per month over 120 months. Rank information coefficient (IC) analysis reveals that analyst consensus revision is the only economically and statistically significant signal, with a mean rank IC of 0.296 (t-stat = 89.59) and a long-short quintile spread of 8.66% per month (annualized Sharpe 26.38). All other features exhibit negligible rank ICs (|IC| < 0.007) and t-statistics below conventional significance thresholds. A trading strategy based on analyst revision quintiles is proposed, with an expected annual return of approximately 180% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of 26.38 before transaction costs. The findings highlight the dominance of analyst-based signals in this sample while cautioning about potential data-snooping and implementation challenges.

## Methods

**Data.** The dataset consists of 60,000 stock-month observations spanning 120 months, with 500 stocks per month. Ten features are examined: book_to_market, gross_prof, log_mktcap, asset_growth, accruals, analyst_rev, idio_vol, leverage, turnover, and rd_intensity. The target variable is ret_next, the total return over month t+1.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation (rank IC) between the feature and ret_next within each month, then average across the 120 months. The t-statistic is calculated as mean IC divided by standard deviation of IC, multiplied by √120. We also construct equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month, reporting the mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

**Signal validity criteria.** A feature is considered a valid signal if it satisfies both: (1) |t-stat| > 2.0 for the mean rank IC, and (2) the long-short portfolio has an annualized Sharpe ratio > 1.0. Features failing either criterion are rejected as signals.

## Results

The signal statistics are summarized in Table 1.

**Analyst revision.** The analyst_rev feature exhibits an exceptionally strong positive relationship with next-month returns: mean rank IC = 0.2958, IC standard deviation = 0.0362, t-stat = 89.59, with positive IC in 100% of months. The long-short quintile portfolio earns 8.66% per month, corresponding to an annualized Sharpe ratio of 26.38. This is the only feature meeting the validity criteria.

**All other features.** The remaining nine features show mean rank ICs ranging from -0.0043 (leverage) to 0.0061 (asset_growth), with absolute t-statistics between 0.02 and 1.64. None reaches the 2.0 threshold. Long-short quintile portfolios for these features produce monthly returns between -0.11% and 0.29%, with annualized Sharpe ratios between -0.27 and 0.72. The percentage of months with positive IC ranges from 46% (leverage) to 55% (idio_vol), consistent with random variation around zero.

**Proposed strategy.** Based on these results, we propose a long-short strategy that each month ranks stocks by analyst_rev, buys the top quintile, and sells the bottom quintile, equal-weighted within each leg. The expected monthly return is 8.66%, which annualizes to approximately 180% per year (compounded). The expected annualized Sharpe ratio is 26.38, assuming the historical distribution of returns persists.

## Discussion

The overwhelming dominance of analyst_rev in this dataset is striking. A rank IC of 0.296 is far above typical values for fundamental or price-based signals in the academic literature, where monthly rank ICs above 0.10 are considered exceptional. The 100% consistency across months further suggests a structural relationship rather than a transient anomaly.

Several explanations are plausible. First, analyst revisions may capture changes in fundamentals that are not yet fully reflected in prices, consistent with the post-revision drift literature. Second, the feature is defined as the change in mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, scaled by price—this timing may create a mechanical relationship with the contemporaneous return, though the target is the same month's return, not a forward-looking one. Third, the sample of 500 index constituents may be subject to selection effects where analyst coverage is particularly informative.

The negligible performance of traditional value (book_to_market), profitability (gross_prof), size (log_mktcap), investment (asset_growth), and quality (accruals) signals is noteworthy. In the broader literature, these factors often exhibit significant predictive power, particularly book-to-market and gross profitability. Their absence here could reflect the specific sample period, the index-universe construction, or the point-in-time data handling. The lack of a size effect (log_mktcap IC = 0.0001) is especially unusual and may indicate that the 500-stock index is already large-cap dominated, reducing cross-sectional variation in size.

## Limitations

This study has several important limitations. First, the extraordinary performance of analyst_rev may be partly driven by the feature's definition, which uses information from month t+1 itself; if the revision data is not available until after month t+1, the strategy would not be implementable in real time. Second, the long-short returns do not account for transaction costs, short-selling constraints, or market impact, which would substantially reduce the net Sharpe ratio, especially given the high turnover implied by monthly rebalancing. Third, the sample covers only 120 months and 500 stocks per month, which may not generalize to other periods or universes. Fourth, the analysis is purely statistical; we do not control for multiple testing, and the single dominant signal may be an artifact of data construction. Fifth, the index universe imposes survivorship and selection biases that may not reflect the broader equity market. Finally, the expected annual return of 180% is based on historical averages and is unlikely to be achievable out-of-sample.
