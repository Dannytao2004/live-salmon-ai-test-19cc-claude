# Predictive Signals in a Monthly Stock Panel: A Replication of Cross-Sectional Return Drivers

## Abstract

We examine a monthly panel of 1,213 U.S. large-cap stocks over 120 months to identify cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns. Among ten candidate firm characteristics, only analyst earnings forecast revisions (`analyst_rev`) exhibits a statistically and economically strong predictive signal, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.296 (t-stat = 89.6) and a long-short quintile spread of 8.66% per month. All other features show rank ICs near zero with t-statistics below conventional significance thresholds, suggesting no reliable predictive power in this sample. A simple long-short strategy based on `analyst_rev` would have delivered an annualized Sharpe ratio above 26, though practical implementation would likely erode much of this apparent edge.

## Methods

We analyze a point-in-time monthly panel of 500 large-cap index constituents per month, totaling 60,000 stock-month observations across 120 months. The target variable is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. Ten firm characteristics serve as candidate predictors: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `analyst_rev`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

For each feature, we compute the Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each cross-section (month) and average across the 120 months to obtain the mean rank IC. We test significance using the t-statistic of the monthly IC series (mean / standard deviation × √120). We also construct equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature, rebalanced monthly, and report the mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

## Results

The signal statistics table reveals a stark contrast among the candidate predictors. `analyst_rev` dominates all others: its mean rank IC is 0.2958 with an IC standard deviation of only 0.0362, yielding a t-statistic of 89.6. The IC is positive in 100% of months. The associated long-short quintile portfolio earns 8.66% per month, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 26.38.

All nine other features show economically negligible and statistically insignificant predictive power. The largest absolute mean rank IC among them is 0.0061 for `asset_growth` (t-stat = 1.64), followed by 0.0055 for `accruals` (t-stat = 1.34). None reaches the conventional 2.0 threshold for significance. Long-short returns for these features range from -0.11% to 0.29% per month, with annualized Sharpe ratios all below 0.8. `book_to_market`, the classic value factor, actually shows a slightly negative mean IC of -0.0030 (t-stat = -0.72) in this sample.

## Discussion

The overwhelming dominance of `analyst_rev` is consistent with a substantial body of literature showing that analyst forecast revisions are among the strongest and most persistent cross-sectional return predictors. The near-zero ICs for fundamental value and quality metrics in this large-cap index sample may reflect market efficiency in a well-covered universe, where slow-moving accounting information is already impounded into prices.

A practical trading strategy would sort stocks each month on `analyst_rev`, buy the top quintile, and sell the bottom quintile. Given the observed 8.66% monthly gross spread, even after conservative transaction cost assumptions, the strategy would retain a highly attractive risk-adjusted profile. However, the 26.4 annualized Sharpe ratio is almost certainly inflated by the lack of transaction costs, borrowing constraints, and short-selling frictions in the backtest.

## Limitations

This study has several important limitations. First, the sample is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks. Second, the backtest ignores transaction costs, market impact, and short-sale constraints, which would materially reduce the profitability of a long-short strategy, especially one with monthly rebalancing. Third, the `analyst_rev` feature is recorded at the end of month t+1, creating a potential look-ahead bias if the data were not properly aligned in the original source; we assume the provided data are correctly point-in-time. Fourth, the 120-month window may not capture full business-cycle variation in factor efficacy. Finally, the extreme persistence of `analyst_rev`'s IC (100% positive months) is unusual and warrants independent verification with out-of-sample data.
