# Report Q110

# Look-Ahead Bias in Analyst Revisions and the Absence of Robust Cross-Sectional Return Predictors

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 stocks. Only analyst consensus revision shows a large cross-sectional correlation with next-month returns, but it is recorded at the end of the target return month and is therefore not available at portfolio formation. After excluding this look-ahead signal, no characteristic has a statistically significant rank IC. We conclude that there is no robust tradable cross-sectional signal in this dataset and recommend a passive equal-weight portfolio as the baseline.

## Methods
We assess predictive power using monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlations between each characteristic and `ret_next`, averaged over 120 months. The associated t-statistic is computed as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation, scaled by the square root of the number of months. We also report equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each characteristic. A feature is considered a valid signal only if it is known at the end of month `t`, the formation month, and its rank IC is statistically significant at conventional levels. Given ten features, we treat |t| > 1.96 as the minimum threshold, with stronger caution for multiple testing.

## Results
`analyst_rev` has an average rank IC of 0.2958, an IC t-statistic of 89.59, and a long-short return of 8.66% per month. However, the data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is the EPS forecast revision during month `t+1`, recorded at the end of month `t+1`. Since it uses information from the same month as the target return, it is not available at the end of month `t` and cannot be used in a real-time strategy.

All other features have economically small and statistically insignificant rank ICs. `asset_growth` has the largest t-statistic at 1.64, followed by `accruals` at 1.34. None exceed conventional significance thresholds, and their long-short Sharpe ratios are modest and consistent with noise. `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` all show no reliable predictive power.

## Discussion
The only strong predictor is contaminated by look-ahead bias. Once that variable is excluded, the panel contains no robust cross-sectional return predictor. The marginally positive long-short returns for `asset_growth` and `accruals` are not statistically reliable after accounting for multiple testing. A practical strategy is therefore a passive equal-weight portfolio of the 500-stock universe. The panel average monthly return is 0.936%, implying an annualized return of approximately 11.2%. The monthly return standard deviation is 0.1132, implying an annualized volatility of approximately 39.2% and a passive Sharpe ratio of roughly 0.29.

## Limitations
- The sample covers 120 months and 500 stocks per month, which limits power to detect weak anomalies.
- Reported long-short returns do not account for transaction costs, short-selling constraints, or liquidity.
- Accounting variables may still contain reporting-lag or restatement issues despite point-in-time conventions.
- Only linear rank correlations are tested; nonlinear or interaction effects are not considered.
- Multiple testing further weakens the already marginal evidence for `asset_growth` and `accruals`.
