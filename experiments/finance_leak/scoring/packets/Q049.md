# Standardized Unexpected Earnings as the Dominant Cross-Sectional Signal in a Monthly Stock Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. The only statistically reliable predictor is standardized unexpected earnings (`sue`), with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.0998 (t-stat = 23.25) and positive IC in 99% of months. A monthly rebalanced long-short portfolio that buys the top `sue` quintile and sells the bottom `sue` quintile earns a mean monthly return of 3.04% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31 before costs. The other nine characteristics do not meet the significance threshold. The evidence is consistent with post-earnings announcement drift.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations from months 1 to 120, with exactly 500 stocks per month. The universe consists of broad equity index constituents, and stocks that are later removed remain in the sample through their final month. The prediction target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1.

For each feature, I compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month. I then average these rank ICs over the 120 months and compute a t-stat as the mean divided by the standard deviation times the square root of the number of months. I classify a feature as a valid signal only if the absolute t-stat exceeds 2.0. I also evaluate economic magnitude using equal-weighted top-minus-bottom quintile long-short portfolios rebalanced monthly.

## Results
Standardized unexpected earnings (`sue`) is the only feature that passes the statistical threshold. It has a mean rank IC of 0.0998, an IC standard deviation of 0.0470, a t-stat of 23.25, and positive IC in 99% of months. Its top-minus-bottom quintile long-short portfolio earns a mean monthly return of 3.04% and an annualized Sharpe of 7.31.

The next strongest candidates are `asset_growth` (mean rank IC = 0.0061, t-stat = 1.64) and `accruals` (mean rank IC = 0.0055, t-stat = 1.34), but neither meets the significance threshold. The remaining features have absolute t-stats below 1.15: `book_to_market` (-0.72), `gross_prof` (0.93), `log_mktcap` (0.02), `idio_vol` (1.14), `leverage` (-1.00), `turnover` (0.02), and `rd_intensity` (-0.05).

## Discussion
The positive and highly significant relation between `sue` and `ret_next` indicates that stocks with recent positive earnings surprises tend to outperform over the following month, while stocks with negative earnings surprises tend to underperform. This is consistent with the well-documented post-earnings announcement drift anomaly. In this sample, the effect is economically large: a simple quintile spread on `sue` has historically generated a very high risk-adjusted return before costs. Other commonly studied characteristics such as value, profitability, investment, and idiosyncratic volatility do not appear to be reliable standalone signals here.

## Limitations
The reported performance is gross of transaction costs, market impact, and short-selling fees, which would materially reduce net returns. The results are based on a single sample and may not generalize out-of-sample or to live trading. No formal multiple-testing adjustment is applied beyond the t-stat threshold, though the SUE result is strong enough to survive conventional adjustments. The proposed strategy is univariate and does not incorporate risk models or interaction effects with other characteristics.
