# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a 500-Stock Monthly Panel: The Dominance of Earnings Surprise

## Abstract

We examine ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns in a panel of 60,000 stock-months (1,213 stocks, 120 months, 500 index constituents per month). Using monthly rank information coefficients (IC) and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find that standardized unexpected earnings (`sue`) is the only feature with an economically and statistically meaningful signal: a mean rank IC of 0.0998 (t = 23.25) and a long-short portfolio returning 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. Nine remaining features — valuation, profitability, size, growth, accruals, volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity — produce ICs within ±0.006 and are indistinguishable from noise. We propose a concentrated long-short strategy on `sue`, which we estimate would have earned roughly 43% annually before costs at a Sharpe near 7.3, and we caution that these estimates are optimistic given transaction costs, capacity limits, and the short sample.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows: 500 index-constituent stocks per month for 120 months (1,213 distinct stocks). Each row has ten point-in-time characteristics measured at month-end $t$ and the target `ret_next`, the total return over month $t+1$. The universe is survivorship-aware in the sense that stocks removed from the index remain in the data through their final return month, which mitigates (but does not eliminate) delisting bias.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we computed the Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month and averaged across the 120 months (mean rank IC), along with the time-series standard deviation of the IC and a t-statistic $t = \overline{IC}/\sigma_{IC}\cdot\sqrt{120}$. We also report the share of months with positive IC and the performance of an equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio (long top quintile, short bottom quintile) rebalanced monthly, with its annualized Sharpe ratio (mean monthly return divided by monthly standard deviation, scaled by $\sqrt{12}$).

**Decision rule.** We classify a feature as a valid signal if (i) the absolute mean rank IC exceeds roughly 0.02, (ii) the IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value, and (iii) the long-short portfolio shows consistent sign agreement with the IC. This deliberately conservative threshold reflects the multiple-testing problem of screening ten signals: with ~0.04–0.05 monthly IC standard deviations, an isolated t-statistic near 1.5–1.6 is well within what noise can generate across ten trials.

**Strategy construction.** For the surviving signal we form a monthly equal-weighted portfolio long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of the ranked feature, and we translate the reported monthly mean and Sharpe into annualized figures.

## Results

**Only `sue` survives.** The earnings-surprise signal is extraordinary relative to all others:

- Mean rank IC = 0.0998, roughly 20–30× larger than any other feature.
- IC t-statistic = 23.25, with a positive IC in 99% of months (119 of 120) — near-perfect directional consistency.
- Equal-weighted quintile long-short return = 3.04% per month, annualized Sharpe = 7.31.

**All other features are noise.** The remaining nine features have mean rank ICs between −0.0043 and +0.0061, |t|-statistics between 0.02 and 1.64, and long-short returns between −0.11% and +0.29% per month. None clears the t = 2.0 bar:

- `asset_growth` (IC 0.0061, t = 1.64) and `accruals` (IC 0.0055, t = 1.34) are the "best" of the rest, but their long-short spreads (0.28% and 0.29% monthly) are economically trivial and their t-statistics are unremarkable given ten simultaneous tests. Their signs are, notably, opposite to the classic anomalies (high asset growth and high accruals are conventionally *negative* predictors), which further suggests sampling noise rather than a robust effect.
- `book_to_market` (−0.0030, t = −0.72), `gross_prof` (0.0039, t = 0.93), `idio_vol` (0.0043, t = 1.14), `leverage` (−0.0043, t = −1.00), `log_mktcap` (0.0001, t = 0.02), `turnover` (0.0001, t = 0.02), and `rd_intensity` (−0.0002, t = −0.05) all have t-statistics below 1.2 and hit rates near 50%. Their long-short Sharpes range from −0.27 to 0.26, i.e., indistinguishable from zero.

**Strategy performance.** The `sue` long-short portfolio's 3.04% monthly mean compounds to approximately 43% annualized gross return. With a monthly Sharpe of 7.31/√12 ≈ 2.11, the annualized Sharpe is 7.31 as reported. Because the strategy is dollar-neutral and equal-weighted across quintiles, it requires no net market exposure.

## Discussion

The results are striking in their selectivity: in a panel where nine well-known anomaly variables are essentially flat, a single signal — standardized unexpected earnings — carries almost all of the cross-sectional predictive content. The magnitude (IC ≈ 0.10, hit rate 99%) is far larger than what is typically documented for PEAD in broad US equity samples, where monthly rank ICs of 0.02–0.05 are more common. Several features of this dataset can explain the gap: the universe is a fixed 500-stock index membership, `sue` is standardized against the historical dispersion of surprises (making it a relatively clean, comparable signal), and the sample is short (120 months), so the estimate may reflect a period in which earnings-announcement drift was unusually strong.

The failure of the other features is equally informative. `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, and `leverage` are accounting-based and updated only annually, so their within-month cross-sectional variation is stale relative to the monthly return horizon. `idio_vol`, `turnover`, and `log_mktcap` are market-based but show no reliable cross-sectional relation here. The absence of a size effect and a value effect, and the reversal of the accrual and asset-growth signs, argue against treating any of the marginal signals as tradable.

A practical strategy would therefore concentrate on `sue`: rank stocks each month, go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weight within each leg, and rebalance monthly. Because the signal is announcement-driven, turnover is naturally moderate (positions change as new earnings arrive), which limits — but does not eliminate — cost drag.

## Limitations

The sample spans only 120 months and 1,213 stocks, so all estimates carry substantial sampling error, and the `sue` result in particular may be period-specific. Reported returns are gross of transaction costs, borrow fees, and market impact; a 3% monthly long-short spread is large enough to survive realistic costs, but the Sharpe of 7.3 is almost certainly inflated by the short sample and by the absence of implementation frictions. The panel covers only index constituents, so results may not generalize to small caps or non-indexed stocks, and the fixed 500-per-month structure may embed index-reconstitution effects. Finally, we screened ten signals simultaneously and used a t > 2 rule; while `sue` clears this bar by an enormous margin, the marginal signals (`asset_growth`, `accruals`) could reflect multiple-testing noise, and we have no out-of-sample validation period.
