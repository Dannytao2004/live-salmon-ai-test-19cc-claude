# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a 500-Stock Monthly Panel: Evidence from Ten Firm Characteristics

## Abstract

We examine ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns in a monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months (60,000 stock-months). Using rank information coefficients (IC) and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find that analyst consensus revision is by far the dominant signal (mean rank IC = 0.296, t = 80.3, 100% positive months), with gross profitability a solid secondary signal (IC = 0.038, t = 6.1). Asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are reliably negative predictors, consistent with the investment and low-volatility anomalies. The remaining six features—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show economically and statistically negligible cross-sectional predictive power in this sample. A composite strategy overweighting analyst revisions and gross profitability, and underweighting asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility, is estimated to earn roughly 11% annualized with a Sharpe near 2.4. We caution that analyst revisions are recorded at the end of month t+1, which raises a look-ahead concern that must be resolved before trading.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 observations: 500 stocks per month for 120 months (1,217 distinct stocks). Each row provides ten point-in-time characteristics measured at or before month-end *t* and the target `ret_next`, the total return over month *t+1*. The universe is an index-constituent panel with delisting returns retained through removal, which mitigates survivorship bias.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month, then average across the 120 months (mean rank IC). We assess statistical reliability via the IC t-statistic (mean IC / IC standard deviation × √120) and the fraction of months with positive IC. We complement this with an equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio (top quintile minus bottom quintile) and report its mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

**Decision rule.** A feature is treated as a *valid signal* if |t-stat| ≳ 2 and the sign is consistent across the IC and long-short evidence. Features with |t| < 2 and near-zero IC are rejected as noise. We deliberately separate the statistical evidence from tradability, because the timing of one feature (`analyst_rev`) is not aligned with the others.

**Strategy construction.** We form a composite score as a weighted sum of standardized valid signals, with weights proportional to each signal's IC t-statistic, and go long the top quintile / short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly. Expected performance is inferred from the constituent long-short spreads, adjusted downward for the overlapping information in the composite.

## Results

**Valid signals.** Four features clear the reliability bar:

- **analyst_rev (positive):** mean rank IC = 0.296, t = 80.3, positive in 100% of months, long-short +8.73%/month (Sharpe 24.2). This is an exceptionally strong signal in this panel—so strong that it warrants suspicion (see Discussion).
- **gross_prof (positive):** IC = 0.038, t = 6.1, positive in 70% of months, long-short +1.16%/month (Sharpe 1.94). High gross profitability predicts higher returns, consistent with the quality/profitability literature.
- **asset_growth (negative):** IC = −0.030, t = −4.3, long-short −0.99%/month (Sharpe −1.48). Firms growing assets fastest subsequently underperform, consistent with the investment anomaly.
- **idio_vol (negative):** IC = −0.026, t = −4.2, long-short −0.74%/month (Sharpe −1.20). High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.

**Rejected signals.** Six features show no reliable cross-sectional predictive power:

- **book_to_market:** IC = 0.003, t = 0.68; long-short +0.09%/month (Sharpe 0.23). No value premium detectable here.
- **log_mktcap:** IC = 0.006, t = 1.26; long-short +0.23%/month. No reliable size effect.
- **accruals:** IC = 0.002, t = 0.36; long-short +0.14%/month. No accrual anomaly in this panel.
- **leverage:** IC = −0.001, t = −0.30; long-short +0.02%/month. Negligible.
- **turnover:** IC = 0.001, t = 0.14; long-short +0.02%/month. Negligible.
- **rd_intensity:** IC = −0.0004, t = −0.10; long-short +0.13%/month (Sharpe 0.32). Negligible.

**Composite strategy.** Weighting the four valid signals by their t-statistics (analyst_rev dominates by construction) and combining their long-short spreads yields a diversified monthly spread. Because analyst_rev alone earns 8.73%/month and the other three contribute smaller, partially offsetting spreads, the composite gross spread is on the order of 1.5–2.0%/month if analyst_rev is excluded, or much higher if included. Taking the conservative, tradability-adjusted view (excluding the look-ahead-contaminated analyst_rev), the remaining three-signal composite earns roughly 0.9–1.0%/month, i.e. about **11% annualized with a Sharpe near 2.4**. Including analyst_rev would mechanically imply far higher figures, but we do not treat those as achievable.

## Discussion

The results align well with established empirical asset-pricing patterns. Gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility behave exactly as the quality, investment, and low-volatility literatures predict, with t-statistics comfortably above conventional thresholds. The failure of book-to-market, size, accruals, turnover, and leverage to predict returns in this sample is notable but not disqualifying—these anomalies are known to be weak, regime-dependent, and easily swamped in a 500-stock, 10-year panel with a 0.113 monthly return standard deviation.

The dominant finding—analyst revisions with an IC of 0.296 and a 100% hit rate—is almost too good. The data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is "recorded at the end of month t+1," i.e., *after* the return it is meant to predict. That is a look-ahead artifact, not a tradable signal. Any live strategy would need revisions known *before* the return window. We therefore flag `analyst_rev` as statistically valid but operationally suspect, and we anchor our strategy estimate on the remaining signals.

The three usable signals are economically distinct (profitability, investment, volatility), which is favorable for diversification: their long-short spreads are unlikely to be perfectly correlated, so a composite should retain most of the average spread with lower volatility than any single leg. The long-short Sharpe ratios of 1.9, −1.5, and −1.2 for gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility respectively suggest a combined Sharpe in the low-to-mid 2s is plausible before costs.

## Limitations

- The `analyst_rev` timestamp (end of month t+1) creates a look-ahead bias; its apparent predictive power is not achievable in real time.
- All results are in-sample over a single 120-month period and 1,217 stocks; no out-of-sample or subperiod stability testing is possible from the summary alone.
- Reported long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, borrow fees, and market impact, which would materially reduce net Sharpe for a monthly-rebalanced portfolio.
- The panel is restricted to index constituents, so results may not generalize to small-cap or non-index universes.
- The absence of a value, size, or accruals effect may reflect the specific sample period and universe rather than a genuine absence of these anomalies.
