# Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a Monthly Index Panel: Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a point-in-time panel of 500 broad index constituents over 120 months. Gross profitability is a robust positive signal (monthly rank IC 0.038, t=6.07), while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are robust negative signals (monthly rank IC -0.030 and -0.026, t=-4.29 and -4.19). Analyst forecast revisions show an extremely strong apparent signal but are not tradable because they are measured during the return month, creating look-ahead bias. A long-short strategy combining the three real-time signals is estimated to earn about 12% gross annual return with an annualized Sharpe of approximately 1.6.

## Methods
The dataset is a monthly panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months, for a total of 60,000 stock-month observations. For each feature at month t, I compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with next-month returns, `ret_next`, and average it over the 120 months. I use the reported t-statistic, computed as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation and multiplied by sqrt(120). I also examine equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature. A signal is considered valid only if it is known at the end of month t and has a statistically reliable rank IC. The feature `analyst_rev` is excluded from the tradable signal set because it is measured during month t+1 and recorded at the end of month t+1.

## Results
Gross profitability is the strongest valid fundamental signal. Its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0378 with a t-statistic of 6.07, positive in 70% of months. The top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94.

Asset growth is a reliable negative signal. Its mean rank IC is -0.0303 with a t-statistic of -4.29. The top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns -0.99% per month, implying a low-minus-high asset growth return of 0.99% per month and an annualized Sharpe of 1.48.

Idiosyncratic volatility is also a reliable negative signal. Its mean rank IC is -0.0261 with a t-statistic of -4.19. The top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns -0.74% per month, implying a low-minus-high idiosyncratic volatility return of 0.74% per month and an annualized Sharpe of 1.20.

The remaining real-time features—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—have rank ICs that are economically small and statistically indistinguishable from zero. Analyst forecast revisions have an exceptionally high rank IC of 0.296, t=80.29, and a long-short monthly return of 8.73%, but these results are not usable because the variable is measured during the return month.

## Discussion
The evidence supports three economically interpretable signals. High gross profitability predicts higher subsequent returns, consistent with profitability-based investing. High asset growth predicts lower subsequent returns, consistent with the asset-growth anomaly. High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower subsequent returns, consistent with low-risk/quality effects.

A practical strategy is to combine these signals into a composite monthly score: rank stocks on gross profitability, negative asset growth, and negative idiosyncratic volatility, then buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile. An equal-weighted combination of the three single-signal long-short portfolios gives approximately 0.96% monthly, or roughly 12% annualized. Accounting for diversification but also likely signal correlation, an annualized Sharpe of about 1.6 is a reasonable gross estimate.

## Limitations
The performance estimates are gross of transaction costs and short-sale constraints. The sample covers only 500 broad index stocks per month, so results may not generalize to smaller or non-index stocks. Correlations among the three signals are not modeled, so the combined Sharpe is approximate. The strongest statistical signal, analyst revisions, is unusable due to look-ahead bias, which limits the economic strength of the tradable strategy.
