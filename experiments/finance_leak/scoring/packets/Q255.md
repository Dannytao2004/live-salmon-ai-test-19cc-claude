# Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift in a Monthly Cross-Section: SUE Is the Only Robust Predictor

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a point-in-time panel of 500 stocks per month over 120 months. Standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) is the only economically and statistically robust signal: its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0998 with a t-statistic of 23.25, and it is positive in 99% of months. A quintile long-short portfolio sorted on SUE earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 7.31 before costs. The other nine characteristics have weak or statistically insignificant rank ICs. We propose a monthly-rebalanced SUE long-short strategy and discuss implementation limits.

## Methods
The dataset consists of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 120 months and 1,213 distinct stocks, with 500 index constituents per month. The prediction target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. Features include book-to-market, gross profitability, log market cap, asset growth, accruals, SUE, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity.

For each feature, I compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank IC with `ret_next` each month, then average across all 120 months. The t-statistic is calculated as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation, scaled by the square root of the number of months. I use a significance threshold of |t| > 2.0. I also form equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month and report the top-minus-bottom long-short return and annualized Sharpe.

## Results
Among the ten features, only `sue` meets the validity threshold. Its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0998, with a standard deviation of 0.0470 and a t-statistic of 23.25. The signal is positive in 99% of months. A quintile long-short portfolio sorted on `sue` earns 3.04% per month, corresponding to an annualized Sharpe of 7.31. The direction is positive: stocks with higher standardized unexpected earnings outperform stocks with lower SUE.

The remaining features are statistically weak. `asset_growth` has the next-highest t-statistic at 1.64, followed by `accruals` at 1.34. Both have small mean rank ICs of 0.0061 and 0.0055, respectively, and their long-short Sharpe ratios are below 1.0. The other features—`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—have t-statistics near or below 1.2 and rank ICs near zero. None of these are considered valid signals in this sample.

## Discussion
The strong performance of `sue` is consistent with post-earnings-announcement drift: firms with positive earnings surprises continue to outperform over the following month, while negative surprises underperform. The magnitude of the effect is large in this dataset, partly because the long-short portfolio is equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly, and does not account for transaction costs or short constraints. Nonetheless, the consistency of the signal, with 99% of monthly ICs positive, suggests a highly reliable cross-sectional relation.

The failure of other characteristics is not necessarily evidence that they are irrelevant in all markets. Value, quality, and volatility signals may be period-specific, may require different definitions, or may be subsumed by the index universe and the monthly horizon used here.

## Limitations
The results are in-sample over the 120-month period and have not been tested out-of-sample. All performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, shorting fees, and market impact. The long-short SUE strategy has an extremely high annualized Sharpe of 7.31, which is unlikely to be achievable after costs or at scale. The universe contains broad-index members, so findings may not generalize to smaller or less liquid stocks. Finally, `sue` depends on analyst consensus forecasts, which may be unavailable for many stocks outside this dataset.
