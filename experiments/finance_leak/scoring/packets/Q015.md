# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Point-in-Time Equity Panel: A Look-Ahead Bias Check

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. The only characteristic with strong in-sample predictive power is analyst consensus revision, but this variable is recorded at the end of the return month and therefore suffers from look-ahead bias. After removing that variable, no remaining characteristic has a statistically significant rank IC at conventional thresholds. We conclude that these point-in-time features contain no robust cross-sectional signal and recommend no active long-short strategy based on them.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations: 500 stocks per month for 120 months. For each feature, signal strength is measured using the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month. The reported rank IC is the average of these monthly correlations, and the t-statistic is computed as `mean IC / std(IC) × sqrt(120)`.

We also consider equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios, buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile of each feature each month. A feature is treated as a valid signal only if its information is known at the end of month `t` and its rank IC t-statistic is at least 2.0 in absolute value, corresponding approximately to a 5% significance level.

## Results
The strongest in-sample signal is `analyst_rev`, with a mean rank IC of 0.2958, an IC t-statistic of 89.59, positive IC in 100% of months, and a long-short Sharpe ratio of 26.38. However, this variable is explicitly recorded at the end of month `t+1`, making it contemporaneous with the target return. It is not available at the time a portfolio would be formed and is therefore invalid.

For the remaining nine features, none reaches the conventional significance threshold:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.27 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.26 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.08 |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.70 |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.08 |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.13 |

The largest t-statistics among the valid point-in-time features are `asset_growth` (1.64) and `accruals` (1.34), but both fall below conventional significance and have signs that are not robustly consistent with prior literature. No valid signal survives.

## Discussion
The extraordinary predictive performance of `analyst_rev` is a clear artifact of look-ahead bias. Because the feature is measured during month `t+1` and recorded at the end of month `t+1`, it overlaps with the return it is supposed to predict. Any backtest using this variable would produce unrealistically high Sharpe ratios that cannot be achieved in real time.

Once this variable is removed, the other characteristics behave essentially like noise. Some weak positive associations appear for `asset_growth` and `accruals`, but their t-statistics are not strong enough to justify trading signals, especially after accounting for multiple testing. The absence of robust signal among standard characteristics suggests the dataset has no exploitable cross-sectional predictor under point-in-time constraints.

## Limitations
- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 stocks, so weak but real signals may be missed.
- No transaction costs, market impact, or short-selling constraints are incorporated.
- The significance threshold of |t-stat| ≥ 2.0 is somewhat arbitrary.
- The analysis relies on summary rank IC and long-short statistics rather than full portfolio-level controls.
- The universe is restricted to index constituents, so results may not generalize to the broader market.
