# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel

## Abstract

We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Using monthly rank information coefficients, t-statistics, and quintile long-short portfolios, we identify four robust signals: gross profitability, earnings surprise (SUE), asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility. A composite strategy that goes long high gross profitability, high SUE, low asset growth, and low idiosyncratic volatility earns an estimated gross annual return of about 19% with an expected Sharpe ratio near 3.0 before costs. The remaining six characteristics do not show reliable cross-sectional predictive power in this sample.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 stock-month observations from 1,217 distinct stocks over 120 months. Each month has exactly 500 stocks. Features are point-in-time firm characteristics observed at the end of month \(t\), and the target is the total return over month \(t+1\), `ret_next`.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature, I use the reported monthly Spearman rank IC between the feature and `ret_next`, averaged over 120 months. I also use the reported IC t-statistic, the percentage of months with positive IC, and the mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio of an equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the feature.

**Validity rule.** A feature is treated as a valid signal if it has a statistically meaningful IC t-statistic (roughly \(|t| > 2\)), a consistent sign, and an economically meaningful long-short return. Features with weak t-statistics and negligible long-short performance are rejected for this sample.

**Composite strategy.** Each month, I cross-sectionally rank the valid features, sign them according to their predictive direction, and average the signed ranks into a composite score. The strategy goes long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of the composite score, equal-weighted, and rebalances monthly. Expected return is estimated from the average of the signed long-short returns of the valid signals. Expected Sharpe is estimated from the individual signal Sharpe ratios under a conservative correlation assumption.

## Results

### Valid signals

| Feature | Direction | Mean monthly rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short mean monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `sue` | positive | 0.0952 | 26.55 | 2.96% | 7.65 |
| `idio_vol` | negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

The strongest signal is `sue`: its rank IC is 0.0952, it is positive in 100% of months, and its long-short portfolio earns 2.96% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 7.65. Gross profitability is also robust, with a positive IC of 0.0378 and a long-short return of 1.16% per month. Asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are reliably negative predictors: high asset growth and high idiosyncratic volatility are associated with lower next-month returns.

### Rejected signals

| Feature | Mean monthly rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short mean monthly return | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `book_to_market` | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | Weak and statistically insignificant. |
| `log_mktcap` | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | No reliable size effect in this sample. |
| `accruals` | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | No stable accruals relation. |
| `leverage` | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | Negligible predictive power. |
| `turnover` | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | No useful turnover signal. |
| `rd_intensity` | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | Insignificant and inconsistent. |

### Composite strategy estimate

Using equal weights across the four valid signals, the signed long-short monthly returns are approximately:

- `gross_prof`: +1.16%
- `asset_growth` short: +0.99%
- `sue`: +2.96%
- `idio_vol` short: +0.74%

The equal-weighted average is about **1.46% per month**, or roughly **19.0% annualized gross return** after compounding. Assuming moderate positive correlation among the four signals, the combined Sharpe ratio is estimated at approximately **3.0**. This estimate is gross of transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact.

## Discussion

The results are consistent with several well-documented cross-sectional regularities. `sue` captures post-earnings-announcement drift: stocks with recent positive earnings surprises continue to outperform. `gross_prof` captures a quality/profitability premium: profitable firms earn higher subsequent returns. `asset_growth` captures the investment anomaly: firms that expand their asset base aggressively tend to underperform. `idio_vol` captures a low-volatility or lottery-like preference effect: high idiosyncratic volatility stocks underperform.

The rejected signals are not necessarily invalid in all markets or periods, but in this 120-month panel they lack statistical reliability. In particular, the value signal (`book_to_market`) and size (`log_mktcap`) are weak here, while accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity show almost no monotonic relation with next-month returns.

A practical implementation would combine the four valid signals into a diversified composite, preferably with industry and beta neutralization to reduce unintended risk exposures. Because `sue` is by far the strongest signal, a composite that equal-weights all four signals is conservative relative to an IC-weighted or SUE-tilted strategy, but it is also less dependent on a single anomaly.

## Limitations

- The sample covers only 120 months and a 500-stock monthly index universe, so results may not generalize to other periods, markets, or smaller stocks.
- Expected performance is gross of transaction costs, shorting costs, and market impact; net returns would likely be lower.
- The composite Sharpe estimate depends on assumed correlations among signals and may be optimistic if the signals are highly correlated in stress periods.
- The analysis does not control for industry, beta, or other common risk factors that could explain part of the returns.
- Multiple testing across ten features means some apparent signals could be sample-specific, even when individual t-statistics are high.
