# Report Q171

# Three Robust Cross-Sectional Signals in a 120-Month Equity Panel

## Abstract
We examine a monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months to identify firm characteristics that predict next-month returns. Using monthly rank information coefficients (ICs) and quintile long-short portfolios, we find that gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility are statistically robust signals. The other six characteristics—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not reliably predict returns in this sample. An equal-weight long-short composite of the three valid signals earns an estimated 11.6% annually before costs, with an annualized Sharpe ratio around 1.9 under moderate signal correlation. The results are consistent with the profitability/quality, investment, and low-volatility anomalies.

## Methods
**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 stock-month observations: 1,217 distinct stocks, 500 stocks per month, and 120 months. The universe is point-in-time index constituents. Stocks remain in the data until index removal, and their final-month return is included, which mitigates survivorship and delisting bias relative to a fixed current-constituent sample.

**Signals.** The nine features are `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`. The target is `ret_next`, the stock return over month \(t+1\).

**Signal validation.** For each feature, we use the reported mean monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank IC, its t-statistic, the mean return of an equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile portfolio, and the annualized Sharpe ratio of that long-short portfolio. A feature is treated as a valid standalone signal if it has a consistent sign, an absolute IC t-statistic above 2, and an absolute long-short Sharpe above 1. Features failing these thresholds are rejected as unreliable standalone predictors.

**Composite strategy.** We form an equal-weight long-short composite of the three valid signals: long the top quintile of `gross_prof`, long the bottom quintile of `asset_growth`, and long the bottom quintile of `idio_vol`; short the opposite quintile of each signal. The three long-short legs are equally weighted and rebalanced monthly. Expected return is the average of the three sign-adjusted long-short returns. Expected Sharpe is estimated from the individual long-short Sharpes under an assumed moderate pairwise correlation among the signals.

## Results
### Valid signals
| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | Positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | Negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | Negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

`gross_prof` is the strongest signal: higher gross profitability predicts higher next-month returns, with a positive IC in 70% of months. `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` are robust negative signals: high asset growth and high idiosyncratic volatility both predict lower next-month returns. Their long-short portfolios are negative because the top quintile underperforms; trading them in the opposite direction gives positive expected returns of roughly 0.99% and 0.74% per month, respectively.

### Rejected signals
| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe | Reason for rejection |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `book_to_market` | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 | IC statistically indistinguishable from zero; weak long-short spread |
| `log_mktcap` | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 | IC t-stat below 2; long-short Sharpe not strong enough |
| `accruals` | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 | Near-zero IC and weak long-short performance |
| `leverage` | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 | Essentially no predictive power |
| `turnover` | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 | No meaningful IC or long-short spread |
| `rd_intensity` | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 | Near-zero IC and weak long-short Sharpe |

### Composite strategy performance
The equal-weight composite of the three valid signals has an estimated monthly return of:

\[
\frac{1.16\% + 0.99\% + 0.74\%}{3} \approx 0.96\% \text{ per month}
\]

This corresponds to approximately **11.6% annualized before costs**. Using the individual long-short Sharpes (1.94, 1.48 after sign adjustment for `asset_growth`, and 1.20 after sign adjustment for `idio_vol`), and assuming moderate positive correlation among the three legs, the composite is estimated to have an annualized Sharpe ratio of roughly **1.9**. If the signals were uncorrelated, the Sharpe would be higher; if they were perfectly correlated, it would fall toward the weighted-average Sharpe of about 1.5.

## Discussion
The three valid signals have well-documented economic interpretations. Gross profitability captures firm quality: profitable firms tend to generate stronger future returns, consistent with the profitability premium. Asset growth is a proxy for investment and expansion; firms that grow their asset base aggressively tend to underperform, consistent with the investment anomaly. Idiosyncratic volatility captures the low-volatility anomaly: stocks with high idiosyncratic risk tend to deliver lower risk-adjusted returns, likely due to limits to arbitrage, lottery-like demand, or leverage constraints.

The rejected signals are not necessarily economically irrelevant in all settings, but they are weak in this particular panel. For example, book-to-market often works in broad value/growth sorts but has a small IC and low long-short Sharpe here. Log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity also fail to clear reasonable statistical and economic hurdles. This may reflect the index-constituent universe, the 120-month sample, or correlation with other omitted risk factors.

The composite strategy benefits from combining three distinct return drivers. Profitability, investment, and low-volatility signals are not perfectly correlated, so an equal-weight long-short portfolio can improve risk-adjusted performance relative to any single signal. The strategy would likely tilt toward profitable, conservatively investing, low-volatility firms and away from unprofitable, high-growth, high-volatility firms.

## Limitations
- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to other periods or broader universes.
- The reported performance is gross of transaction costs; monthly rebalancing and shorting would reduce net returns.
- The composite Sharpe estimate depends on assumed correlations among the three valid signals, which are not directly observed.
- All signals are evaluated in-sample, so some measured performance may reflect data mining or period-specific effects.
- The features are correlated with each other and with common risk factors, so standalone long-short returns may overstate incremental alpha.
- Shorting constraints, borrow costs, capacity limits, and implementation slippage are ignored.
