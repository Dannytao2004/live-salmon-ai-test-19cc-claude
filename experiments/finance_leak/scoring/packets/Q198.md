# Report Q198

# Title
Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: Evidence from Ten Firm Characteristics

## Abstract
We evaluate ten firm-level characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Using monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank ICs and long-short quintile portfolios, we find three credible point-in-time signals: gross profitability is positively predictive, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negatively predictive. Analyst revisions show an implausibly strong relation with returns, but this feature is recorded at the end of month t+1 and is therefore not available at portfolio formation at the end of month t. The remaining six characteristics show weak and statistically insignificant relations in this sample. A diversified long-short composite of the three valid signals has an estimated gross annualized return of about 11.6% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 2.3.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 monthly observations: 500 stocks per month for 120 months. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. All candidate features are treated as point-in-time information available at the end of month t, except where the data dictionary indicates otherwise.

For each month, we compute the Spearman rank correlation between each feature and `ret_next` across stocks. The reported mean rank IC is the average of these monthly cross-sectional correlations. The t-statistic is computed as the mean IC divided by the IC standard deviation, multiplied by the square root of the number of months, 120. Long-short portfolio returns buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature, equal-weighted within legs. A positive mean rank IC implies that higher feature values predict higher next-month returns; a negative IC implies the opposite.

We classify a feature as a valid signal if it has a statistically meaningful IC t-statistic (roughly |t| > 2), an economically interpretable direction, and point-in-time availability at the end of month t. Features with weak ICs, inconsistent signs, or look-ahead bias are rejected.

## Results
Three features survive the screen.

| Feature | Direction | Mean monthly rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | Positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | Negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | Negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

`gross_prof` is the strongest point-in-time signal: firms with higher gross profits relative to assets earn higher next-month returns, with positive ICs in 70% of months. `asset_growth` is negatively related to future returns, consistent with the investment anomaly: firms that have grown assets rapidly tend to underperform. `idio_vol` is also negatively related to future returns, consistent with a low-volatility effect.

`analyst_rev` has an extreme mean rank IC of 0.2963, a t-statistic of 80.29, and a long-short Sharpe ratio of 24.23. However, it is recorded at the end of month t+1, while the target return spans month t+1. It is therefore not known at the end of month t when the portfolio would need to be formed. This is a look-ahead bias, and we reject it despite its apparent predictive power.

The remaining features—`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—have small and statistically insignificant ICs in this sample. Their long-short returns are also close to zero, with annualized Sharpe ratios well below 1.

### Proposed strategy
Each month, form a composite signal from the three valid predictors:

- `gross_prof`: long high values, short low values.
- `asset_growth`: long low values, short high values.
- `idio_vol`: long low values, short high values.

Equal-weight the three rank-transformed signals after aligning their signs. Go long the top quintile of the composite and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted within each leg, and rebalance monthly.

Using the historical long-short returns, the traded versions of the three signals have approximate monthly returns of 1.16% for `gross_prof`, +0.99% for inverted `asset_growth`, and +0.74% for inverted `idio_vol`. An equal-weighted combination gives an approximate monthly return of 0.96%, or about 11.6% simple annualized. Assuming moderate diversification across the signals, the combined annualized Sharpe ratio is estimated at around 2.3, lower than the naive independent-signal estimate but above most individual signal Sharpes.

## Discussion
The results support three well-known cross-sectional return patterns. Gross profitability captures firm quality: profitable firms tend to continue outperforming. Asset growth captures overinvestment: rapidly expanding firms tend to earn lower subsequent returns. Idiosyncratic volatility captures a low-volatility or lottery-demand effect: high-volatility stocks tend to underperform.

The analyst-revision result is a useful diagnostic rather than an investable signal. Its near-perfect IC and Sharpe are mechanically inflated by the fact that the feature is measured over the same month as the return. In a live strategy, one cannot observe the full-month revision at the start of that month. Any backtest that includes it without lagging would be subject to severe look-ahead bias.

The other characteristics are not reliably predictive in this panel. Some have expected signs in the literature—for example, accruals are often negatively related to returns—but here the estimated relation is tiny and insignificant. This could reflect the particular sample, index-constituent universe, or feature construction, but based on the provided evidence they should not be used as standalone signals.

A composite strategy is preferable to relying on one characteristic. The three valid signals are economically distinct and likely provide some diversification. The combined portfolio sacrifices the highest individual return, from `gross_prof` alone, but may achieve a better risk-adjusted profile.

## Limitations
The sample is limited to 500 index constituents per month over 120 months, so the findings may not generalize to small-cap, micro-cap, or non-index stocks. Transaction costs, short borrow costs, and market impact are ignored; the monthly long-short strategy would likely face meaningful turnover and implementation costs. The combined Sharpe estimate is approximate because the correlations among the three signals are not observed directly. Accounting features are assumed to be correctly aligned point-in-time, but annual reports can be stale or restated. The analysis does not neutralize sector, size, or other risk-factor exposures, so part of the apparent alpha may reflect omitted common factors.
