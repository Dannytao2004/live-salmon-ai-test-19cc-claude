# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: Evidence from Nine Stock Characteristics

## Abstract
I evaluate nine stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. Using monthly Spearman rank ICs and equal-weighted top-minus-bottom quintile portfolios, only `leverage` shows statistically reliable predictive power: its mean rank IC is 0.0164 with a t-stat of 2.74, and its long-short portfolio earns 0.52% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. The remaining eight characteristics have mean rank ICs close to zero and t-stats below 1.2, providing no robust evidence of return predictability. A monthly long-short leverage strategy has an estimated gross annualized return of about 6.4%, but performance is likely fragile after costs and given survivorship bias in the panel.

## Methods
The dataset contains 34,708 stock-month observations for 500 stocks over months 1–120. The universe consists of stocks that are constituents of a broad equity index at the end of month 120, with monthly history back to month 1 or to index inclusion. This creates an unbalanced panel with increasing coverage over time: 143 stocks in month 1 rising to 500 in month 120.

The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. The candidate features are `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`. For each feature, I use the provided monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank IC with `ret_next`, averaged over 120 months. The t-statistic is computed as mean IC divided by IC standard deviation, multiplied by the square root of 120. I also use the provided equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and shorts the bottom quintile of each feature, rebalanced monthly.

I classify a feature as a valid signal if it has a mean rank IC with an absolute t-statistic of at least 2.0 and a consistent direction in the long-short portfolio. Features failing this threshold are treated as rejected signals. No transaction costs, borrow costs, or risk-factor adjustments are applied in the reported signal statistics.

## Results
Only `leverage` passes the validity threshold. It has a mean rank IC of 0.0164 with an IC standard deviation of 0.0654, giving a t-statistic of 2.74. The IC is positive in 59% of months. The equal-weighted long-short portfolio, long high-leverage stocks and short low-leverage stocks, earns 0.52% per month on average, corresponding to approximately 6.4% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. Economically, the effect is modest: the mean rank IC implies only weak cross-sectional discrimination.

The other eight features are not statistically reliable. `book_to_market` has a mean rank IC of -0.0017 (t = -0.28) and a long-short return of -0.05% per month. `gross_prof` has a mean rank IC of 0.0012 (t = 0.23) and a long-short return of 0.12% per month. `log_mktcap` has a mean rank IC of 0.0014 (t = 0.26) but a negative long-short return of -0.07% per month. `asset_growth` has a mean rank IC of 0.0055 (t = 1.16) and a long-short return of 0.37% per month. `accruals` has a mean rank IC of 0.0059 (t = 1.03) and a long-short return of 0.24% per month. `idio_vol` has a mean rank IC of 0.0056 (t = 0.86) and a long-short return of 0.13% per month. `turnover` has a mean rank IC of -0.0005 (t = -0.10) and a long-short return of 0.13% per month. `rd_intensity` has a mean rank IC of 0.0010 (t = 0.20) and a long-short return of -0.06% per month.

## Discussion
The main finding is that leverage positively predicts next-month returns in this sample. High-leverage firms tend to outperform low-leverage firms, consistent with a risk-compensation or leverage-premium interpretation. However, the effect is small in rank-IC terms and only marginally significant under conventional standards. With nine features tested, a t-statistic of 2.74 is not strong after strict multiple-testing correction. The long-short Sharpe ratio of 0.92 is attractive in isolation but not exceptional for an equity strategy, and it ignores implementation frictions.

The other characteristics show no reliable predictive power. Several signs are contrary to well-known anomalies: `book_to_market` is weakly negative rather than positive, and `accruals` is weakly positive rather than negative. These results likely reflect noise, the specific sample period, and the unusual survivorship-biased universe rather than robust economic relationships. The apparent weakness of size, value, profitability, investment, accruals, volatility, turnover, and R&D intensity in this panel argues against building a diversified multi-factor strategy from these summaries alone.

A simple implementable strategy is an equal-weighted monthly long-short portfolio on `leverage`: buy the top quintile of leverage and short the bottom quintile. Based on the provided statistics, its expected gross annualized return is about 6.4%, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. Net performance would be lower after trading costs, borrow costs, and shorting constraints. The strategy should be viewed as a marginal single-signal strategy rather than a high-conviction production alpha.

## Limitations
The universe is limited to stocks that survive in the index until month 120, creating survivorship bias and likely overstating historical returns and signal performance. The panel is unbalanced, with coverage growing from 143 to 500 stocks, so early-period results reflect a different and smaller cross-section. The analysis uses summary statistics only and cannot inspect missing data, outliers, winsorization, or nonlinearities. Reported returns are gross of transaction costs, borrow costs, and shorting constraints, which would reduce net performance. Multiple testing across nine features means the leverage signal is marginal after strict correction. Finally, the study does not control for size, value, industry, momentum, or other known risk exposures.
