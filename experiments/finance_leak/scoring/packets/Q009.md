# Robust Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a Point-in-Time Monthly Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns using a point-in-time panel of 500 index stocks per month over 120 months. Rank IC tests and quintile long-short portfolios identify four statistically reliable signals: standardized unexpected earnings and gross profitability predict returns positively, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility predict returns negatively. A composite long-short strategy that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of an IC-weighted signal has an estimated annualized return of approximately 40% and an annualized Sharpe ratio near 8. The remaining six characteristics do not have statistically significant predictive power.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations, with 500 stocks per month over 120 months. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. For each of the ten features, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` each month and average it over all months. We use a threshold of |t-statistic| > 2.0 to identify statistically reliable signals. We also construct equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile each month.

To combine the valid signals, we cross-sectionally z-score each feature each month, sign it according to its observed direction, and weight it by its average rank IC. The composite score is:
```
score = 0.0952*sue + 0.0378*gross_prof - 0.0303*asset_growth - 0.0261*idio_vol
```
A trading strategy is formed by buying the top quintile of this composite score and selling the bottom quintile, equal-weighted and rebalanced monthly.

## Results
Four features exhibit robust predictive power:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `sue` | 0.0952 | 26.55 | 2.96% | 7.65 |
| `gross_prof` | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

`asset_growth` and `idio_vol` are negative predictors: high asset growth and high idiosyncratic volatility are associated with lower next-month returns. After reversing their sign, their long-short strategies have positive expected returns of 0.99% and 0.74% per month, respectively.

The remaining features are not statistically significant. Their average rank ICs are close to zero, with |t-statistics| below 2.0: `book_to_market` (t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` (t = 1.26), `accruals` (t = 0.36), `leverage` (t = -0.30), `turnover` (t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` (t = -0.10).

The composite strategy based on the four valid signals is estimated to have an annualized return of approximately 40% and an annualized Sharpe ratio near 8.

## Discussion
The results are broadly consistent with known equity anomalies. The strongest signal, `sue`, captures post-earnings-announcement drift: firms with positive earnings surprises continue to outperform. `gross_prof` is a quality/profitability signal, with more profitable firms earning higher subsequent returns. `asset_growth` captures the investment effect: firms with high asset growth tend to underperform. `idio_vol` captures the low-volatility anomaly: high idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns.

Notably, `book_to_market` does not show a significant value effect in this sample. Size (`log_mktcap`) is also not a reliable predictor after controlling for the other characteristics. The composite signal is economically intuitive because it combines earnings surprise, profitability, investment, and volatility information into one cross-sectional rank.

## Limitations
The performance estimates are in-sample and may overstate out-of-sample live results. The backtest ignores transaction costs, market impact, and short-selling constraints. The sample is limited to 500 index stocks per month, so small-cap and non-index effects are not captured. The SUE signal uses consensus forecast data that may not be fully point-in-time for all stocks.
