# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Survivorship-Biased Large-Cap Panel: Only Leverage Survives

## Abstract

We examine nine accounting- and market-based characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns in a panel of 34,708 stock-months covering 500 index constituents over 120 months. Using monthly rank information coefficients (IC) and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find that only one signal — leverage — is statistically distinguishable from noise (mean rank IC = 0.0164, t = 2.74, long-short Sharpe ≈ 0.92). The remaining eight features produce rank ICs of magnitude ≤ 0.006 with |t| ≤ 1.16, and none generates a long-short Sharpe above 0.75. We propose a long-short strategy tilted toward high-leverage names, but caution that the entire panel is constructed from stocks that were index members at month 120, inducing survivorship bias that likely inflates all measured premia and, in particular, the leverage effect. Expected annualized return on the leverage strategy is roughly 6% with a Sharpe near 0.9, but we regard this estimate as an upper bound.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains one row per stock-month from month 1 to month 120, with 500 distinct stocks and 34,708 observations. Universe size grows mechanically from 143 stocks in month 1 to 500 in month 120, because the sample is defined as the set of stocks that were index constituents at the end of month 120, with history backfilled to their index-inclusion month. This is a classic survivorship-biased construction.

**Features.** Nine characteristics are provided: book_to_market, gross_prof, log_mktcap, asset_growth, accruals, idio_vol, leverage, turnover, rd_intensity. All are constructed from information available at the end of month *t* (latest annual report or trailing market data), so there is no look-ahead in the feature construction itself.

**Target.** `ret_next` is the total return over month *t+1*.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use two statistics supplied in the panel summary:
1. **Rank IC** — the cross-sectional Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, averaged over 120 months. We compute t-statistics as mean(IC) / std(IC) × √120.
2. **Long-short quintile spread** — equal-weighted top-quintile minus bottom-quintile portfolio, monthly mean and annualized Sharpe (monthly Sharpe × √12).

We treat a signal as "valid" only if |t| ≥ 2.0, which corresponds to roughly a 5% two-sided significance level. This is a deliberately conservative threshold given that nine features are tested and no multiple-testing correction is applied.

**Strategy construction.** For the surviving signal (leverage), we form a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio: long the top quintile of leverage, short the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly. We report the raw long-short return and Sharpe from the panel summary, without transaction costs.

## Results

### Signal screen

| Feature | Mean rank IC | t-stat | Months IC>0 | LS mean (monthly) | LS Sharpe (ann.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| leverage | **+0.0164** | **2.74** | 59% | **+0.52%** | **0.92** |
| accruals | +0.0059 | 1.03 | 54% | +0.24% | 0.41 |
| idio_vol | +0.0056 | 0.86 | 50% | +0.13% | 0.19 |
| asset_growth | +0.0055 | 1.16 | 52% | +0.37% | 0.74 |
| log_mktcap | +0.0014 | 0.26 | 56% | −0.07% | −0.14 |
| gross_prof | +0.0012 | 0.23 | 50% | +0.12% | 0.22 |
| rd_intensity | +0.0010 | 0.20 | 52% | −0.06% | −0.11 |
| turnover | −0.0005 | −0.10 | 52% | +0.13% | 0.23 |
| book_to_market | −0.0017 | −0.28 | 51% | −0.05% | −0.08 |

**Leverage** is the only feature whose IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0. Its mean rank IC of 0.0164 implies that moving from the bottom to the top of the leverage distribution is associated with a roughly 1.6-percentage-point rank shift in next-month returns. The equal-weighted top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns 0.52% per month, or about 6.4% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe of 0.92 — the only portfolio in the panel with a Sharpe meaningfully above 0.5.

**Asset growth** is the next strongest (IC t = 1.16, LS Sharpe 0.74, +0.37%/month), and **accruals** is third (t = 1.03, Sharpe 0.41). Neither clears the t ≥ 2 bar, and given that nine features were tested, their apparent significance is consistent with noise.

The remaining six features — log_mktcap, gross_prof, rd_intensity, turnover, book_to_market, idio_vol — have IC t-statistics below 0.9. Several of these (book_to_market, turnover, rd_intensity) have *negative* long-short spreads, and their ICs flip sign roughly half the time (months with IC > 0 near 50–52%). These are indistinguishable from zero.

### Strategy

The only strategy we would consider implementable on this evidence is:

> **Monthly equal-weighted long-short leverage portfolio:** buy the top quintile of total-debt-to-assets, short the bottom quintile, rebalance at each month-end.

Reported performance: **+0.52% per month**, **+6.4% annualized**, **Sharpe ≈ 0.92**, before transaction costs. A 20% haircut for turnover and implementation slippage brings the expected Sharpe to roughly 0.7 and expected annual return to roughly 5%.

## Discussion

The headline result is negative for most of the literature: in this panel, only leverage predicts the cross-section of next-month returns with any reliability. This is surprising for at least three reasons.

First, several of the rejected signals — book_to_market (value), gross_prof (profitability), asset_growth (investment), accruals — are among the most replicated anomalies in empirical asset pricing. Their failure here is more plausibly a statement about *this sample* than about the anomalies themselves. The panel is restricted to 500 stocks that were index constituents at month 120. Index membership at a fixed future date selects for large, liquid, surviving firms. Value, profitability, and investment effects are strongest in small caps and in distressed names, which are precisely the stocks excluded by this construction.

Second, the leverage result is itself suspicious. The standard theoretical prediction is that leverage should *lower* expected returns (the "levered equity" channel: high leverage mechanically raises equity beta and required return, but empirically high-leverage firms are often distressed and subsequently underperform). A *positive* leverage-return relation in a survivorship-biased sample is exactly what one would expect if high-leverage firms that survive to month 120 are disproportionately those that recovered — a conditional-survivor effect, not a risk premium. The 59% hit rate and modest IC are consistent with a real but fragile effect.

Third, the IC magnitudes are uniformly small. A mean rank IC of 0.016 for the best signal is at the low end of what is typically reported for monthly cross-sectional predictability (0.02–0.05 for well-known anomalies in broad samples). This is again consistent with a large-cap, survivor-only universe in which mispricing is arbitraged away.

The practical implication is that a leverage-tilted long-short portfolio is the only candidate worth paper-trading, and even that should be treated as a hypothesis rather than a finding. Combining leverage with asset growth and accruals (the two runners-up) might raise the Sharpe modestly through diversification, but the incremental evidence is weak.

## Limitations

1. **Survivorship bias.** The universe is defined by index membership at month 120, so all delisted, acquired, and failed firms are excluded. This mechanically inflates measured returns and almost certainly distorts the leverage signal in particular.
2. **Small effective breadth.** With a median of 268 stocks per month and only 120 months, the rank IC standard errors are large; a true IC of 0.01 and a true IC of 0.00 are hard to distinguish.
3. **No multiple-testing correction.** Nine features were screened at |t| ≥ 2; under the null, roughly 0.45 false positives are expected, and we report exactly one.
4. **No transaction costs or capacity analysis.** The leverage long-short portfolio turns over monthly; net returns could be materially lower than reported.
5. **Feature construction is coarse.** Annual-report-derived features (book_to_market, gross_prof, accruals, leverage, rd_intensity) are stale by up to 12 months, which attenuates measured ICs.
6. **Single market, single era.** The panel covers one broad index over 120 months; results may not generalize across markets or time periods.
7. **No risk-model adjustment.** Long-short spreads are raw; part of the leverage return may be compensation for industry or distress exposure rather than alpha.
