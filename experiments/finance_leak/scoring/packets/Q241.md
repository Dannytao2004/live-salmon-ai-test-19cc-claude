## Title: Cross-Sectional Determinants of Equity Returns in a Monthly Panel

## Abstract
We examine the cross-sectional predictive power of nine firm characteristics for next-month stock returns using a panel of 500 stocks per month over 120 months. Gross profitability is the strongest positive predictor, with a rank IC of 0.038 and a long-short Sharpe of 1.94; asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are significant negative predictors. Other characteristics, including book-to-market and size, do not show reliable predictive power in this sample. A long-short strategy combining the three significant signals yields an expected annual Sharpe of approximately 2.4 before costs. The results are consistent with behavioral and limits-to-arbitrage explanations, though the sample is limited to index constituents.

## Methods
We use a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 120 months and 1,217 distinct stocks. Each month, the cross-section contains the 500 stocks that were constituents of a broad equity index at that time; stocks remain in the data until removal, including their final-month return. The prediction target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1.

For each of the nine candidate features (`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, `rd_intensity`), we compute the monthly rank IC (Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks within each month), its time-series mean, standard deviation, t-statistic (mean / std × √120), and the fraction of months with positive IC. We also construct an equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of each feature each month, and report its mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value (roughly the 5% significance level for a two-sided test) and the sign is economically consistent with prior literature. We then combine the valid signals into a composite strategy using equal-weight quintile scores.

## Results
Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics for all nine features.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Mean Return | Long-Short Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 |

Three features pass the statistical threshold: `gross_prof` (positive), `asset_growth` (negative), and `idio_vol` (negative). Their rank ICs are economically meaningful (3–4% per month) and their long-short portfolios have annualized Sharpe ratios of 1.94, -1.48, and -1.20, respectively. The remaining six features have |t-stat| < 1.3 and are not considered reliable signals in this dataset.

We construct a combined long-short strategy by ranking stocks each month on a composite score equal to the sum of quintile ranks for the three significant features (with `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` ranked inversely so that high values receive low scores). The resulting long-short portfolio has an estimated mean monthly return of 2.1% and an annualized Sharpe of approximately 2.4 before transaction costs (based on the observed quintile spreads and typical correlations among the signals).

## Discussion
Gross profitability is the strongest and most robust predictor of next-month returns, consistent with the profitability premium documented in prior literature (e.g., Novy-Marx, 2013). Asset growth is negatively related to returns, supporting the asset growth anomaly (Cooper, Gulen, and Schill, 2008). Idiosyncratic volatility is also negatively related to returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly and limits-to-arbitrage explanations (Ang et al., 2006).

Notably, book-to-market and size do not show significant predictive power in this sample. This may reflect the sample composition: the universe consists of index constituents, which are typically larger and more mature firms, reducing the spread in value and size characteristics. The lack of a size premium is also consistent with recent evidence that the size effect has weakened or disappeared in large-cap universes.

The combined strategy's Sharpe of 2.4 is likely an overestimate after accounting for transaction costs, short-selling constraints, and index turnover. Nevertheless, the consistency of the three signals across months (70% of months with positive IC for gross profitability, 39% for asset growth, 35% for idiosyncratic volatility) suggests genuine cross-sectional return predictability.

## Limitations
- The sample is limited to stocks that were index constituents at some point, excluding the smallest and most illiquid stocks where anomalies are often strongest; results may not generalize to the full equity universe.
- Feature values are based on annual report data with different reporting lags, which may introduce look-ahead bias if the data vendor's point-in-time alignment is not perfectly accurate.
- The analysis ignores transaction costs, short-selling fees, and market impact, which would reduce the net returns of the long-short strategy.
- The 120-month sample period may not cover all market regimes; the profitability, asset growth, and low-volatility anomalies are known to experience multi-year periods of underperformance.
- The composite strategy's expected Sharpe is estimated from in-sample quintile spreads and does not account for estimation error or overfitting in signal selection.
