# Cross-Sectional Predictors of Next-Month Returns: A Point-in-Time Evaluation

## Abstract
We evaluate ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns in a point-in-time panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Analyst consensus revision shows an extremely strong univariate association with returns, but its data definition indicates it is measured during the same month as the target return and is therefore not an ex ante predictor. All remaining characteristics have economically small rank ICs and t-statistics below conventional significance thresholds. We conclude that the panel contains no robust valid cross-sectional return signal and propose a passive equal-weight benchmark.

## Methods
The dataset consists of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 500 stocks per month over 120 months. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month, average these rank ICs across the 120 months, and compute a t-statistic as `mean(rank IC) / std(rank IC) * sqrt(120)`. We use a conventional absolute t-statistic threshold of 2.0 for statistical significance. We also examine equal-weighted top-minus-bottom quintile long-short portfolios for each feature. A valid predictive signal must be known at the end of month t, before the month t+1 return is realized.

## Results
The table below summarizes the univariate signal statistics.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | -0.27 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| analyst_rev | 0.2958 | 89.59 | 8.66% | 26.38 |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | -0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 |

`analyst_rev` has an extraordinary rank IC of 0.296, a t-statistic of 89.6, and a top-minus-bottom quintile monthly return of 8.66%. However, the data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, recorded at the end of month t+1. Since `ret_next` is also realized over month t+1, this variable is contemporaneous with the return and is not available at the time a trading decision would be made at the end of month t. It is therefore not a valid predictive signal despite its strong in-sample association.

None of the other nine features has an absolute t-statistic above 2.0. The largest among them are `asset_growth` at 1.64 and `accruals` at 1.34, but these are well below conventional significance levels and would not survive even a modest multiple-testing adjustment. Their long-short returns are small, and their signs are not consistently aligned with established anomaly literature.

## Discussion
The only economically large return predictor in the sample is `analyst_rev`, and that signal is invalid because of look-ahead timing. The remaining characteristics show no robust cross-sectional predictive power. This is consistent with the interpretation that the dataset is an efficient large-cap index universe in which simple annual-report and market-based characteristics do not generate statistically detectable return spreads over this sample period.

Given the absence of valid signals, the most defensible strategy is a passive equal-weight portfolio of the 500 index constituents, rebalanced monthly. The sample average monthly return of `ret_next` is 0.936%, implying an expected annual return of approximately 11.2%. Using the individual-stock return volatility from the sample as a conservative benchmark, the annualized Sharpe ratio is approximately 0.29; the actual equal-weight portfolio Sharpe ratio would likely be higher because of diversification.

## Limitations
The sample is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents over 120 months, so results may not generalize to small caps, other markets, or different periods. The analysis is univariate and does not test multivariate signals, interactions, or nonlinear transformations. Transaction costs, short-sale constraints, and implementation costs are not considered. The equal-weight benchmark Sharpe ratio is approximated because pairwise return correlations are not available in the provided summary. The rejection of `analyst_rev` depends critically on the stated data timing; a properly lagged analyst revision measure could be a meaningful signal.
