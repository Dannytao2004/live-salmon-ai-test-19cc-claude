# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a 500-Stock Index Universe: Evidence from Ten Firm Characteristics

## Abstract

We examine ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-months). Only one feature—analyst consensus revision (`analyst_rev`)—shows an economically and statistically meaningful relation to `ret_next`, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.296 (t = 89.6) and a long-short quintile spread of 8.66% per month (annualized Sharpe ≈ 26). All nine other characteristics display rank ICs within ±0.006 and t-statistics below 1.7, indistinguishable from noise. Critically, `analyst_rev` is recorded at the end of month t+1, the same month over which `ret_next` is measured, so its predictive power reflects a look-ahead/contemporaneous-information artifact rather than a tradable signal. We conclude that, absent the contaminated feature, no reliable cross-sectional predictor is identifiable in this panel, and we caution against deploying `analyst_rev` in a live strategy.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows: 500 index constituents per month for 120 months, with 1,213 distinct stocks. Stocks remain in the sample through their final month (including the return over that month), so the panel is survivorship-aware with respect to index deletions.

**Features.** Ten characteristics are provided: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `analyst_rev`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`. All are point-in-time as of the end of month t, with one exception: `analyst_rev` is explicitly documented as "change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, scaled by share price. Recorded at the end of month t+1." This places it *after* the start of the return window, making it unavailable at the portfolio formation date (end of month t).

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the reported mean monthly rank IC (Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks within each month, averaged across 120 months), its t-statistic (mean IC / IC std × √120), the fraction of months with positive IC, and the equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile long-short return and annualized Sharpe. We treat |t| > 2 as the threshold for statistical relevance and require an economically meaningful long-short spread.

**Strategy construction.** For each valid (available-at-t) signal we form equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios, rebalanced monthly, buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile. Because only `analyst_rev` clears the statistical bar and it is not tradable at t, we also assess a conservative fallback strategy.

## Results

**The one "strong" signal is contaminated.** `analyst_rev` has a mean monthly rank IC of 0.2958 (t = 89.6), positive in 100% of months, and a long-short spread of 8.66% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 26.4. An IC this large and this consistent is implausible for a genuine ex-ante predictor of monthly returns. The data dictionary resolves the puzzle: the revision is measured *during* month t+1 and recorded at the end of t+1, i.e., it overlaps the return window. The signal therefore embeds contemporaneous information—analysts revise upward as prices rise—rather than forecasting future returns. It is a look-ahead artifact, not an implementable alpha.

**All remaining signals are noise.** The nine other features produce rank ICs between −0.0043 and +0.0061, with t-statistics ranging from −1.00 to +1.64, none significant at conventional levels:

- `book_to_market`: IC = −0.0030 (t = −0.72), long-short −0.11%/month.
- `gross_prof`: IC = 0.0039 (t = 0.93), long-short +0.10%/month.
- `log_mktcap`: IC = 0.0001 (t = 0.02), long-short +0.03%/month.
- `asset_growth`: IC = 0.0061 (t = 1.64), long-short +0.28%/month.
- `accruals`: IC = 0.0055 (t = 1.34), long-short +0.29%/month (note the *positive* sign, opposite the accrual anomaly).
- `idio_vol`: IC = 0.0043 (t = 1.14), long-short +0.03%/month.
- `leverage`: IC = −0.0043 (t = −1.00), long-short −0.02%/month.
- `turnover`: IC = 0.0001 (t = 0.02), long-short +0.01%/month.
- `rd_intensity`: IC = −0.0002 (t = −0.05), long-short +0.05%/month.

The best of these, `asset_growth` and `accruals`, reach t-statistics of only 1.64 and 1.34, and their long-short Sharpes (0.72 and 0.70) are far below what would survive transaction costs and multiple-testing correction across ten candidate signals.

**Strategy performance.** Because no tradable signal is identified, the honest expected performance of a characteristic-based long-short strategy in this panel is approximately zero. A portfolio built on `analyst_rev` would appear to earn ~100%+ annualized, but this is unattainable: the signal is not known at formation. We therefore report an expected annual return near zero with a Sharpe near zero for any implementable strategy.

## Discussion

The results illustrate a classic pitfall in empirical asset pricing: a feature whose measurement window overlaps the return window will exhibit spectacular in-sample predictive power that is entirely spurious for trading purposes. The 100% monthly hit rate and Sharpe of 26 for `analyst_rev` are diagnostic of look-ahead bias, not of a genuine anomaly. Genuine monthly cross-sectional signals in liquid index constituents typically exhibit rank ICs of 0.02–0.05 and long-short Sharpes of 0.5–1.5; values an order of magnitude larger should trigger immediate suspicion about timing.

Among the genuinely point-in-time features, we find no reliable cross-sectional predictability. This is consistent with the view that large-cap index constituents are efficiently priced with respect to commonly available accounting and market characteristics, at least at the monthly horizon and in this sample. The weak, statistically insignificant tilt toward `asset_growth` and `accruals` (with an anomalous positive accrual sign) is best interpreted as noise rather than a replicable effect. The absence of the value, profitability, low-volatility, and momentum-like patterns that appear in broader universes likely reflects both the large-cap, index-constrained sample and the short 120-month window.

## Limitations

- We cannot inspect the raw panel or recompute statistics; all inferences rely on the provided summary tables.
- The 120-month sample with 500 stocks/month is modest for detecting small ICs, so weak signals may be underpowered rather than truly absent.
- The universe is restricted to index constituents, which excludes small caps where many anomalies are strongest.
- We cannot verify the exact timing convention of `analyst_rev` beyond the data dictionary, though the dictionary is explicit that it is recorded at t+1.
- Multiple-testing across ten features is not formally corrected; the single significant result is attributed to timing bias rather than treated as a discovery.
- Transaction costs, borrow costs, and capacity are not modeled; the near-zero expected strategy return should be read as gross.
