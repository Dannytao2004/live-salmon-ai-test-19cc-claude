# Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility Predict Cross-Sectional Equity Returns

## Abstract
We evaluate nine stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a point-in-time monthly panel of 500 equity index constituents over 120 months. Using monthly rank ICs, we find robust evidence that gross profitability predicts returns positively, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility predict returns negatively; all three have absolute t-statistics above 4. The remaining six characteristics—book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—are not statistically reliable in this sample. A composite long-short strategy based on the three significant signals has an estimated annual return of about 12% and an estimated annual Sharpe ratio of about 1.6.

## Methods
The dataset is a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months, with 60,000 stock-month observations. The universe is point-in-time index membership: stocks are included up to removal, including their final return. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. All characteristics are observable at month-end t: accounting variables use the latest annual report available by then, and trailing variables use only prior daily or monthly data, so there is no look-ahead bias.

For each characteristic, I compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank IC with `ret_next` each month, then average the monthly ICs. The t-statistic is the mean IC divided by its standard deviation and multiplied by sqrt(120). I also form equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios: top quintile minus bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly. A feature is treated as a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic has absolute value above 2.0 and its long-short Sharpe is economically consistent with that IC.

## Results
Three features clear the significance threshold.

`gross_prof` has a mean monthly rank IC of 0.0378, an IC t-statistic of 6.07, positive ICs in 70% of months, a long-short return of 1.16% per month, and an annualized long-short Sharpe of 1.94. Higher gross profitability predicts higher next-month returns.

`asset_growth` has a mean rank IC of -0.0303, an IC t-statistic of -4.29, negative ICs in 61% of months, a long-short return of -0.99% per month, and an annualized long-short Sharpe of -1.48. Higher asset growth predicts lower next-month returns.

`idio_vol` has a mean rank IC of -0.0261, an IC t-statistic of -4.19, negative ICs in 65% of months, a long-short return of -0.74% per month, and an annualized long-short Sharpe of -1.20. Higher idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower next-month returns.

The other six features are not statistically reliable. `book_to_market` has an IC t-statistic of 0.68, `log_mktcap` 1.26, `accruals` 0.36, `leverage` -0.30, `turnover` 0.14, and `rd_intensity` -0.10. Their long-short Sharpe ratios are also close to zero. I therefore reject them as tradable signals in this sample.

A composite strategy ranks stocks monthly on the average of cross-sectional z-scores of `gross_prof`, `-asset_growth`, and `-idio_vol`; it buys the top composite quintile and sells the bottom composite quintile. Using the historical long-short legs as a guide, the expected gross annual return is approximately 12%, with an expected annual Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.6.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with several well-documented anomalies. Gross profitability supports the quality/profitability premium. Asset growth is an investment factor: high asset growth often signals overinvestment and lower subsequent returns. Idiosyncratic volatility is a low-volatility effect: high-volatility stocks tend to underperform low-volatility stocks on a risk-adjusted basis.

The absence of a significant book-to-market effect in this sample is notable. It may reflect the index-constituent universe, sample period, or the specific book-to-market construction. The composite strategy combines three relatively distinct signals and is expected to improve diversification relative to any single signal.

## Limitations
- All returns are gross of transaction costs, financing costs, and short-sale constraints.
- The sample covers 120 months and may not generalize to other periods or markets.
- The composite strategy is calibrated on the same sample used to test the signals, so its estimated performance may be optimistic.
- The universe is limited to index constituents, so results may not apply to small-cap or non-index stocks.
- Accounting variables are updated only when annual reports become available, which may make some signals stale.
