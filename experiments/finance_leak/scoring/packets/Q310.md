# Cross-Sectional Predictors of Monthly Stock Returns in a Point-in-Time Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using a monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Four signals are statistically reliable based on monthly rank ICs: standardized unexpected earnings, gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility. A monthly rebalanced quintile long-short strategy that combines these four signals is estimated to earn about 17.6% gross per year with an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 3.1. The strongest predictor is standardized unexpected earnings, consistent with post-earnings announcement drift.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations, with 500 stocks in each of 120 months and ten firm characteristics. The target is `ret_next`, the stock return over month t+1. The panel is point-in-time, so stocks remain in the data until index removal.

For each feature and month, I compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next`. I average these monthly rank ICs over 120 months and compute a t-statistic as the mean divided by the standard deviation times the square root of 120. I treat a feature as a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2 in absolute value.

I also use the reported long-short quintile returns. Each month, stocks are sorted into quintiles on a feature. The long-short return buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile, equal-weighted within each leg. For negative signals, I reverse this direction to express the strategy with positive expected return.

## Results
The valid signals are summarized below.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Direction | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `sue` | 0.0952 | 26.55 | positive | 2.96% | 7.65 |
| `gross_prof` | 0.0378 | 6.07 | positive | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | -0.0303 | -4.29 | negative | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | -0.0261 | -4.19 | negative | -0.74% | -1.20 |

After reversing the sign for `asset_growth` and `idio_vol`, the aligned long-short monthly returns are 1.16% for `gross_prof`, 0.99% for `asset_growth`, 2.96% for `sue`, and 0.74% for `idio_vol`.

Six features are rejected because their rank IC t-statistics are not reliably different from zero: `book_to_market` (0.68), `log_mktcap` (1.26), `accruals` (0.36), `leverage` (-0.30), `turnover` (0.14), and `rd_intensity` (-0.10).

### Proposed strategy
I propose a monthly rebalanced, dollar-neutral quintile strategy. Each month, stocks are ranked on a composite score that equally weights the cross-sectional ranks of:
- high `gross_prof`
- high `sue`
- low `asset_growth`
- low `idio_vol`

The strategy buys the top quintile of the composite score and sells the bottom quintile. The expected monthly return is estimated as the equal-weighted average of the four aligned long-short returns:

\[
(1.16\% + 0.99\% + 2.96\% + 0.74\%) / 4 = 1.46\%
\]

This implies an expected annual return of approximately \(1.46\% \times 12 = 17.6\%\). Applying a moderate diversification/correlation discount to the observed individual Sharpe ratios, I estimate a combined annualized Sharpe ratio of about 3.1.

## Discussion
The results show that earnings-related information is the dominant cross-sectional signal in this sample. `sue` has by far the strongest rank IC, positive in 100% of months, and the highest long-short return. Its strength is consistent with post-earnings announcement drift.

`gross_prof` is a robust positive signal, supporting the idea that profitable firms earn higher subsequent returns after controlling for other characteristics. `asset_growth` is a robust negative signal, consistent with the asset-growth anomaly, where firms that grow assets aggressively tend to underperform. `idio_vol` is a robust negative signal, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.

The rejected signals include several traditional factors such as book-to-market and size. In this specific point-in-time index sample, they do not have reliable univariate predictive power.

## Limitations
- The estimated strategy performance is gross of transaction costs, short-sale costs, and implementation frictions.
- The composite Sharpe ratio is inferred from individual long-short returns rather than a fully integrated backtest, so cross-signal correlations and overlapping positions could change the realized performance.
- The `sue` signal is unusually strong and may be sample-specific or decay quickly after earnings announcements.
- The sample covers only index constituents over 120 months, so results may not generalize to the broader market or to out-of-sample periods.
