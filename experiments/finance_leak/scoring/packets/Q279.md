# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Monthly Equity Panel: Evidence from Profitability, Investment, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
Using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month, I evaluate nine accounting and market-based characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns. Three signals have statistically reliable rank ICs and economically meaningful long-short spreads: gross profitability is positively predictive, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negatively predictive. A composite long-short strategy that buys high-profitability, low-asset-growth, low-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks and sells the opposite is estimated to earn about 12% annualized before costs with an annualized Sharpe near 2.2. The remaining six characteristics—book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show weak or insignificant predictive power in this sample.

## Methods
### Data and universe
The panel contains 60,000 stock-month observations from month 1 to 120. Each month has exactly 500 stocks, with 1,217 distinct stocks over the sample. The universe is point-in-time: stocks remain in the panel until index removal, including the final-month return, which reduces obvious survivorship bias relative to a fixed current-constituent sample.

The prediction target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month \(t+1\). Features are measured at the end of month \(t\): `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

### Signal evaluation
For each feature and month, I compute the Spearman rank IC between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks. I average these monthly ICs over 120 months. The reported t-statistic is:

\[
t = \frac{\overline{IC}}{\sigma(IC)} \sqrt{120}
\]

I also examine equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile of each feature. For negatively predictive features, the profitable strategy is the reverse: buy the bottom quintile and sell the top quintile.

I classify a feature as a valid signal if it has \(|t| > 2\), a consistent directional sign across months, and a long-short return that is economically meaningful after considering turnover and implementation frictions.

## Results
### Valid signals
Three features satisfy the validity criteria.

| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short mean monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | Positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | Negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | Negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

`gross_prof` is the strongest single signal. Its mean rank IC is 0.0378, its t-statistic is 6.07, and it is positive in 70% of months. The top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns 1.16% per month, or roughly 13.9% annualized before costs, with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94.

`asset_growth` is reliably negative. Its mean rank IC is -0.0303 with a t-statistic of -4.29. The top-minus-bottom quintile return is -0.99% per month, meaning a strategy that buys low-asset-growth stocks and sells high-asset-growth stocks earns about 0.99% per month, or 11.9% annualized, with a Sharpe of 1.48 in the profitable direction.

`idio_vol` is also reliably negative. Its mean rank IC is -0.0261 with a t-statistic of -4.19. The top-minus-bottom quintile return is -0.74% per month, so buying low-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks and selling high-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks earns about 0.74% per month, or 8.9% annualized, with a Sharpe of 1.20 in the profitable direction.

### Rejected signals
The remaining six features are not reliable predictors in this sample.

- `book_to_market`: Mean rank IC is 0.0027 with t-stat 0.68; the long-short Sharpe is only 0.23.
- `log_mktcap`: Mean rank IC is 0.0055 with t-stat 1.26; the size effect is weak and statistically insignificant.
- `accruals`: Mean rank IC is 0.0015 with t-stat 0.36; the long-short return is near zero.
- `leverage`: Mean rank IC is -0.0011 with t-stat -0.30; the long-short Sharpe is 0.05.
- `turnover`: Mean rank IC is 0.0006 with t-stat 0.14; the long-short return is negligible.
- `rd_intensity`: Mean rank IC is -0.0004 with t-stat -0.10; the long-short Sharpe is 0.32.

These features may be useful in other samples, regions, or specifications, but they do not provide robust cross-sectional return prediction here.

## Strategy and Expected Performance
I propose a monthly equal-weighted long-short quintile strategy using a composite score that combines the three valid signals in their profitable directions:

\[
\text{Score} = z(\text{gross\_prof}) - z(\text{asset\_growth}) - z(\text{idio\_vol})
\]

where \(z(\cdot)\) denotes cross-sectional standardization each month. Each month, the strategy goes long the top quintile of the composite score and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, and rebalances monthly.

Because the three individual long-short portfolios each earn roughly 0.7%–1.2% per month and have modest positive Sharpe ratios, a diversified composite should improve risk-adjusted returns. A conservative estimate is an expected gross annualized return of about 12% and an annualized Sharpe of about 2.2 before transaction costs, shorting costs, and slippage.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with several well-documented cross-sectional regularities. Gross profitability behaves like a quality/profitability signal: more profitable firms outperform less profitable firms. Asset growth is a proxy for investment, and the negative relation is consistent with the investment anomaly—firms that expand assets aggressively tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility is a proxy for firm-specific risk, and its negative relation is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly, where high-volatility stocks underperform on a risk-adjusted basis.

The failure of `book_to_market` and `log_mktcap` suggests that traditional value and size effects are not strong in this particular index-constituent sample over these 120 months. The other rejected features—`accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—show near-zero rank ICs and low long-short Sharpe ratios, so there is no reliable basis for including them in the strategy.

## Limitations
- The sample covers only 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to small-cap, illiquid, or non-index stocks.
- Reported returns are gross of transaction costs, shorting costs, bid-ask spreads, and market impact, which can materially reduce implementable long-short performance.
- The composite strategy assumes equal weighting and linear combination of signals; correlations among signals, nonlinear interactions, and regime changes are not modeled.
- The analysis is in-sample with respect to the 120-month period and does not include a true out-of-sample or post-sample validation.
- No sector, industry, beta, or risk-factor neutralization is applied, so the long-short portfolio may carry unintended exposures.
- Rank ICs are small and noisy even for the valid signals, so monthly realizations can deviate substantially from the estimated averages.
