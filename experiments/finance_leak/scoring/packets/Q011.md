# Cross-Sectional Equity Return Prediction: Evidence from a 120-Month Stock Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns using a 60,000-row panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months. Four signals are statistically reliable: standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) and gross profitability predict positively, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility predict negatively. SUE is by far the strongest signal, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.0952, a t-statistic of 26.55, and a long-short Sharpe ratio of 7.65. A monthly equal-weighted long-short composite of the four valid signals has an estimated annualized return of about 17.6% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 5.0 before transaction costs. The remaining characteristics—book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show no robust predictive power in this sample.

## Methods
We use a monthly panel of 500 index constituents from month 1 to 120, with 1,217 distinct stocks. Each row contains point-in-time characteristics at the end of month \(t\) and the stock’s total return over month \(t+1\), `ret_next`. We assess each feature as a cross-sectional signal by computing the Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, then averaging over the 120 months. We also use the provided equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio statistics: each month, buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of the feature.

A signal is considered valid if its mean rank IC is economically meaningful, its t-statistic is large in absolute value, and its long-short portfolio has a consistent sign and nontrivial Sharpe ratio. For the trading strategy, we form a composite score from the valid signals by cross-sectionally ranking each signal, sign-adjusting so that higher scores always predict higher next-month returns, and averaging the ranks equally. We then form a monthly equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio that buys the top composite-score quintile and shorts the bottom quintile.

## Results
Four features are valid return predictors. SUE is strongly positive: mean rank IC = 0.0952, t-stat = 26.55, 100% of months with IC > 0, long-short mean monthly return = 2.96%, and annualized Sharpe = 7.65. Gross profitability is positive: mean rank IC = 0.0378, t-stat = 6.07, long-short mean monthly return = 1.16%, and Sharpe = 1.94. Asset growth is negative: mean rank IC = -0.0303, t-stat = -4.29, long-short mean monthly return = -0.99%, and Sharpe = -1.48; the profitable trade is long low asset growth and short high asset growth. Idiosyncratic volatility is negative: mean rank IC = -0.0261, t-stat = -4.19, long-short mean monthly return = -0.74%, and Sharpe = -1.20; the profitable trade is long low idio_vol and short high idio_vol.

The remaining features are rejected. Book-to-market has rank IC = 0.0027, t-stat = 0.68, and long-short Sharpe = 0.23. Log market cap has rank IC = 0.0055, t-stat = 1.26, and Sharpe = 0.55. Accruals has rank IC = 0.0015, t-stat = 0.36, and Sharpe = 0.36. Leverage has rank IC = -0.0011, t-stat = -0.30, and Sharpe = 0.05. Turnover has rank IC = 0.0006, t-stat = 0.14, and Sharpe = 0.06. R&D intensity has rank IC = -0.0004, t-stat = -0.10, and Sharpe = 0.32. None of these reach conventional significance thresholds or show a reliable long-short payoff.

For the composite strategy, the equal-weighted average of the four valid long-short returns is approximately 1.46% per month: \((2.96\% + 1.16\% + 0.99\% + 0.74\%)/4\). Simple annualization gives about 17.6% before costs, and compounding gives about 19.0%. Assuming moderate diversification across signals, the composite monthly volatility is roughly 1.0%, implying an annualized Sharpe ratio near 5.0. A SUE-only strategy would have shown higher in-sample performance—about 35.5% annualized return and Sharpe 7.65—but it would be concentrated in one signal and less robust to signal decay.

## Discussion
The results align with well-documented equity anomalies. SUE captures post-earnings-announcement drift and is the dominant predictor in this panel. Gross profitability is consistent with the profitability premium: profitable firms outperform. Asset growth is consistent with the investment/asset-growth anomaly: firms expanding their asset base tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly: high-volatility stocks underperform on a risk-adjusted basis.

The rejected features are notable because several are classic factors in other samples. Book-to-market and size show weak positive tilts but are statistically insignificant here. Accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity also fail to produce reliable rank ICs or long-short payoffs. This may reflect the specific index-constituent universe, sample period, or the point-in-time construction. It may also indicate that these signals are crowded or offset by other effects in this panel.

The proposed composite strategy diversifies across four independently motivated signals. While SUE is the strongest, combining it with profitability, low asset growth, and low idiosyncratic volatility should reduce signal-specific risk and improve robustness. The estimated performance is attractive but should be interpreted as gross of transaction costs, shorting costs, and implementation frictions.

## Limitations
- All performance estimates are in-sample and based on summary statistics; no out-of-sample validation is possible here.
- Transaction costs, slippage, shorting fees, and market impact are ignored, and monthly rebalancing of quintile portfolios can imply high turnover.
- The strategy assumes frictionless shorting and equal-weighted portfolio construction, which may not be feasible for all stocks.
- Signal correlations and cross-sectional correlations with other factors are not modeled, so the composite Sharpe estimate is approximate.
- The panel covers 120 months of index constituents; results may not generalize to other universes or periods.
- SUE’s extremely high t-statistic and Sharpe ratio may reflect data-specific effects or look-ahead bias in the underlying data construction, despite the stated point-in-time design.
- No sector, industry, or risk-factor neutralization is applied, so the strategy may load on unintended exposures.
