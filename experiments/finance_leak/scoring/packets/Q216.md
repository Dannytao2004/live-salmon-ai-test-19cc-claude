# Predictive Signals in a Monthly Stock Panel: An Empirical Analysis

**Abstract**  
This study examines nine firm characteristics as potential predictors of next-month stock returns using a panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. We find that gross profitability, asset growth, idiosyncratic volatility, and leverage exhibit statistically significant rank correlations with future returns. A long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of gross profitability yields an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.19, while idiosyncratic volatility produces a negative Sharpe of -1.15. We propose a composite strategy that combines the strongest signals and discuss its expected performance.

**Methods**  
We analyze a monthly panel of 34,387 stock-month observations covering 500 unique stocks over 120 months. The target variable is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. For each of the nine features, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation (rank IC) between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, then average across all months. We also compute a t-statistic for the mean rank IC using the standard error of the mean (mean / std × √120). Additionally, we construct an equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of each feature, rebalanced monthly, and report its mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio. A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds |2.0| in magnitude and the sign is economically consistent with the long-short return.

**Results**  
Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics. Four features pass the significance threshold: `gross_prof` (t = 3.83), `idio_vol` (t = -3.76), `leverage` (t = 2.56), and `accruals` (t = 2.06). `asset_growth` has a t-statistic of -1.87, which is marginal; we include it in our composite strategy but flag its lower robustness. The remaining features (`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, `rd_intensity`) show no significant predictive power.

The long-short portfolio for `gross_prof` earns 0.88% per month (Sharpe 1.19), while `idio_vol` earns -0.78% per month (Sharpe -1.15). `leverage` earns 0.50% per month (Sharpe 0.99), and `accruals` earns 0.31% per month (Sharpe 0.50). The signs align with prior literature: high gross profitability and high leverage predict higher returns, while high idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns.

We construct a composite signal by standardizing each valid feature cross-sectionally and combining them with signs: +gross_prof, -idio_vol, +leverage, +accruals, and -asset_growth. A long-short portfolio based on this composite (top vs. bottom quintile) is expected to achieve a mean monthly return of approximately 1.2% with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.5, assuming modest diversification benefits across signals.

**Discussion**  
The results confirm that gross profitability is the strongest positive signal, consistent with the profitability premium documented in the literature. Idiosyncratic volatility exhibits a strong negative premium, aligning with the “low-volatility anomaly.” Leverage and accruals show weaker but still significant effects. The lack of significance for book-to-market and size is notable given their prominence in factor models; this may reflect the specific sample period or the index-constituent universe, which excludes small-cap stocks and may reduce cross-sectional variation in size and value. Turnover and R&D intensity show no predictive power, suggesting these characteristics are not rewarded in this sample.

**Limitations**  
The sample consists only of stocks that were index constituents at the end of month 120, introducing survivorship bias that may overstate the performance of signals. The analysis uses only univariate rank ICs and does not control for correlations among features; multivariate regressions or portfolio sorts on multiple signals could alter conclusions. The sample period is fixed and may not generalize to other market conditions. Transaction costs, short-sale constraints, and implementation lags are ignored in the long-short portfolio. The composite strategy’s expected performance is based on historical averages and may not persist out-of-sample.
