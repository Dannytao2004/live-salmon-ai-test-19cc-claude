# Robust Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a Point-in-Time Panel: Profitability, Investment, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

**Abstract**

Using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents, we test ten firm characteristics for their ability to predict next-month returns. We identify gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility as statistically reliable cross-sectional signals, with positive, negative, and negative return relations respectively. A standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) measure has the strongest raw statistics but is rejected because its definition uses actual earnings before public announcement. An equal-weight composite of the three valid signals is estimated to produce an annualized long-short return near 12% with a Sharpe ratio around 2.0 gross of costs.

**Methods**

The dataset is a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations over 120 months, with 500 stocks per month. Features include book-to-market, gross profitability, size, asset growth, accruals, SUE, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity. For each feature, we compute the monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with next-month returns, average it over all months, and compute a t-statistic. We also form equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile each month. A feature is treated as a valid signal only if it is statistically significant at conventional levels and is point-in-time investable at month-end.

**Results**

Three features meet the statistical threshold. `gross_prof` has mean rank IC of 0.0378 (t = 6.07), with a long-short return of 1.16% per month and annualized Sharpe of 1.94. `asset_growth` has mean rank IC of -0.0303 (t = -4.29), with a long-short return of -0.99% per month and Sharpe of -1.48. `idio_vol` has mean rank IC of -0.0261 (t = -4.19), with a long-short return of -0.74% per month and Sharpe of -1.20. These imply that profitable firms, firms with low asset growth, and firms with low idiosyncratic volatility earn higher subsequent returns.

The `sue` feature has the strongest raw statistics: mean rank IC of 0.0952, t-statistic of 26.55, and a long-short Sharpe of 7.65. However, it is not a valid point-in-time signal. The definition uses actual EPS for the most recent fiscal quarter ending on or before month t, while firms announce earnings in the month after the fiscal quarter ends. For firms whose quarter ends during month t, the earnings surprise is not public until month t+1, so the feature embeds look-ahead bias.

The remaining six features have |t-statistics| below 2 and are rejected: `book_to_market` (0.68), `log_mktcap` (1.26), `accruals` (0.36), `leverage` (-0.30), `turnover` (0.14), and `rd_intensity` (-0.10).

For the proposed strategy, each month we form a composite score equal to the average of the cross-sectional percentile ranks of `gross_prof` and the negative of `asset_growth` and `idio_vol`. We buy the top quintile of the composite and sell the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly. The equal-weight combination of the three valid long-short portfolios yields approximately 0.96% per month, or about 11.6% annualized. Assuming moderate correlation among signals, the expected annualized Sharpe is approximately 2.0 before trading costs.

**Discussion**

The results are economically consistent with prior literature. High gross profitability predicts higher returns, consistent with quality-based explanations. High asset growth predicts lower returns, consistent with the investment or asset-growth anomaly. High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns, consistent with low-risk and limits-to-arbitrage explanations.

The rejection of SUE despite its extraordinarily strong raw statistics illustrates the importance of point-in-time validation in quant research. If SUE were incorrectly treated as tradable, backtested returns would be substantially overstated because the strategy would appear to know earnings surprises before they are public.

**Limitations**

- Performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, borrow fees, and market impact.
- The sample covers index constituents only and may not generalize to smaller or less liquid stocks.
- The composite strategy uses equal signal weights and univariate validation; multivariate optimization could improve performance but risks overfitting.
- SUE is rejected due to look-ahead bias, but other point-in-time timing issues may remain undetected.
