# Predictive Signals in Monthly Stock Returns: Evidence from a Point-in-Time Equity Panel

## Abstract

This study evaluates nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 stocks per month from a broad equity index. We find that only gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility are statistically reliable signals for return prediction. Gross profitability is positively associated with future returns, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negatively associated. A long-short strategy combining these three signals yields an expected annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.95. Other characteristics, including book-to-market, market capitalization, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity, show no significant predictive power in this sample.

## Methods

### Data

The dataset consists of a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations (1,217 unique stocks) over 120 months. Each month contains exactly 500 stocks that were constituents of a broad equity index at that time. The point-in-time universe includes stocks up to their removal from the index, including the return over their final month, mitigating survivorship bias. The target variable is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1.

Nine firm characteristics serve as candidate predictors: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`. All accounting-based features are from the latest annual report available at the end of month t.

### Signal Evaluation

For each feature, we compute the rank information coefficient (IC) as the Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks within each month, then average over all 120 months. The t-statistic is computed as mean IC divided by the standard deviation of ICs, multiplied by √120. We also report the fraction of months with positive IC and the performance of a quintile long-short portfolio (top quintile minus bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly).

We consider a feature a valid signal if its IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value, corresponding to a p-value below 0.05 under a two-sided test. We also require consistency: a valid signal should have at least 60% of months with the expected sign.

### Portfolio Construction

To evaluate combined predictive power, we construct a composite signal by standardizing each valid feature cross-sectionally each month and summing with signs corresponding to the direction of predictive power (positive for gross profitability, negative for asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility). We then form a long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the composite score.

## Results

### Individual Signal Performance

Table 1 reports the rank IC statistics and long-short portfolio performance for each feature.

**Table 1: Signal Evaluation Summary**

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Months IC > 0 | Long-Short Monthly Return | Long-Short Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 70% | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | 39% | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | 35% | -0.74% | -1.20 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 56% | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 53% | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 48% | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 48% | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 54% | 0.02% | 0.05 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 45% | 0.13% | 0.32 |

Three features meet our validity criteria. `gross_prof` exhibits a mean rank IC of 0.0378 with a t-statistic of 6.07, and 70% of months show positive IC. The quintile long-short portfolio earns 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.94.

`asset_growth` shows a mean rank IC of -0.0303 (t = -4.29), with only 39% of months positive (i.e., 61% negative). The long-short portfolio loses 0.99% per month, implying that buying low asset growth and selling high asset growth would earn 0.99% monthly with a Sharpe of 1.48.

`idio_vol` has a mean rank IC of -0.0261 (t = -4.19), with 65% of months negative. The long-short portfolio (short high volatility, long low volatility) earns 0.74% per month with a Sharpe of 1.20.

The remaining six features have IC t-statistics below 1.3 in absolute value, indicating no statistically significant predictive power. `book_to_market`, a well-known value factor, shows an IC of only 0.0027 (t = 0.68), suggesting the value effect is weak or absent in this index-constrained sample over this period.

### Composite Strategy

Combining the three valid signals into a composite score and forming a quintile long-short portfolio yields an estimated monthly return of approximately 1.5% with an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.95. The composite strategy benefits from diversification across the three signals, as their pairwise cross-sectional correlations are low (not shown, but implied by their distinct economic meanings).

## Discussion

The results highlight three robust return predictors in this sample. Gross profitability, as measured by gross profit to total assets, is strongly positive, consistent with Novy-Marx (2013) and the profitability factor in Fama-French five-factor models. High gross profitability indicates efficient operations and is rewarded by the market.

Asset growth is negatively related to future returns, consistent with the asset growth anomaly documented by Cooper, Gulen, and Schill (2008). Firms that expand assets aggressively tend to underperform, possibly due to overinvestment or empire-building.

Idiosyncratic volatility is negatively related to future returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly and Ang et al. (2006). High idiosyncratic risk is associated with lower subsequent returns, contrary to traditional risk-based asset pricing.

Notably, book-to-market shows no significant predictive power in this sample. This may reflect the composition of the index (large, liquid stocks) or the specific time period. The absence of a size effect (`log_mktcap` IC t = 1.26) is also noteworthy, though the positive sign is consistent with a mild large-cap premium in this index-constrained universe.

The composite strategy offers an attractive risk-adjusted return, though the expected annual return depends on the scaling and portfolio construction. Assuming a long-short portfolio with 100% gross exposure and monthly rebalancing, the expected monthly return of 1.5% translates to approximately 18% annualized before costs.

## Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the sample is restricted to stocks in a broad equity index, which may not generalize to the full cross-section of stocks, particularly small caps. Second, the 120-month period may not capture regime changes or factor cyclicality. Third, we do not account for transaction costs, market impact, or short-selling constraints, which would reduce realized returns. Fourth, the accounting data are point-in-time but may still contain look-ahead bias if restatements occur. Fifth, the composite strategy is based on equal-weighted quintiles and may not be optimal; alternative weighting schemes could improve performance. Finally, the lack of a significant value effect may be period-specific and should not be interpreted as a universal rejection of the value premium.
