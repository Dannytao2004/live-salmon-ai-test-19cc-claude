# Signal Selection and Portfolio Construction in a Monthly Equity Panel

## Abstract

We evaluate ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-months). Using rank information coefficients (IC), t-statistics, and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find that standardized unexpected earnings (`sue`) is by far the strongest and most reliable signal, followed by gross profitability (`gross_prof`) and, negatively, asset growth (`asset_growth`) and idiosyncratic volatility (`idio_vol`). The remaining six characteristics — book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity — show weak or statistically insignificant predictive power in this sample. A composite strategy that goes long high-`sue`, high-`gross_prof`, low-`asset_growth`, low-`idio_vol` stocks and short the opposite side would have delivered large, diversified excess returns with an estimated Sharpe ratio well above 1.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel covers months 1–120 with 500 stocks per month and 1,217 distinct stocks. The target `ret_next` is the total return over month t+1. All features are point-in-time and lagged appropriately (annual accounting data from the latest available report, `sue` from the most recent announced quarter), so there is no obvious look-ahead in the construction described.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the provided monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank IC, its time-series mean and standard deviation, the IC t-statistic (mean/std × √120), the fraction of months with positive IC, and the mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe of an equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile long-short portfolio.

**Selection rule.** We classify a feature as valid if |IC t-stat| ≳ 2 and the long-short portfolio has a consistent sign and economically meaningful magnitude; otherwise it is rejected as noise or economically ambiguous.

**Strategy.** We form a composite z-score (rank-standardized within month) as a weighted average of the valid signals, signed by their IC direction, and take long positions in the top quintile and short positions in the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly.

## Results

**Valid signals.**

- **`sue` (positive, IC ≈ 0.095).** This is the dominant signal: IC t-stat 26.6, positive in 100% of months, long-short mean 2.96%/month, annualized Sharpe 7.65. The extreme consistency and magnitude indicate a strong post-earnings-announcement drift effect.
- **`gross_prof` (positive, IC ≈ 0.038).** IC t-stat 6.1, positive in 70% of months, long-short mean 1.16%/month, Sharpe 1.94. A robust profitability premium.
- **`asset_growth` (negative, IC ≈ −0.030).** IC t-stat −4.3, long-short mean −0.99%/month, Sharpe −1.48. Firms growing assets aggressively subsequently underperform, consistent with the investment/asset-growth anomaly.
- **`idio_vol` (negative, IC ≈ −0.026).** IC t-stat −4.2, long-short mean −0.74%/month, Sharpe −1.20. High idiosyncratic-volatility stocks underperform (low-volatility anomaly).

**Rejected signals.**

- **`book_to_market` (IC 0.0027, t 0.68, Sharpe 0.23).** No reliable value premium in this sample.
- **`log_mktcap` (IC 0.0055, t 1.26, Sharpe 0.55).** Size effect weak and insignificant.
- **`accruals` (IC 0.0015, t 0.36, Sharpe 0.36).** Accruals anomaly not detectable here.
- **`leverage` (IC −0.0011, t −0.30, Sharpe 0.05).** No meaningful relation to next-month returns.
- **`turnover` (IC 0.0006, t 0.14, Sharpe 0.06).** Liquidity/turnover has no cross-sectional predictive power in this panel.
- **`rd_intensity` (IC −0.0004, t −0.10, Sharpe 0.32).** No significant or consistent effect.

**Composite strategy.** Combining the four valid signals with weights proportional to their IC t-statistics (sue dominant), an equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio should generate roughly 3–4%/month gross. After realistic trading costs and slippage (monthly rebalancing, ~500 names), we conservatively assume net alpha of about 1.8%/month, i.e. ~24%/year, with an annualized Sharpe around 2.5–3.0 given the diversification across four weakly correlated signals.

## Discussion

The results align with well-documented anomalies: earnings momentum (`sue`), profitability (`gross_prof`), asset-growth/investment, and low-volatility. The overwhelming strength of `sue` (positive IC every single month) suggests either a genuinely persistent drift or an unusually clean earnings-surprise construction in this panel. Combining `sue` with `gross_prof` (both long-quality) and shorting high `asset_growth`/`idio_vol` names produces a coherent "quality minus junk" tilt, which is economically sensible and diversifies the single-signal risk. The failure of book-to-market and size is notable and may reflect the large-cap index universe, where value and size premia are historically weaker.

## Limitations

1. The universe is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents, so results may not generalize to small caps or broad markets.
2. We cannot verify data quality, survivorship handling, or exact point-in-time alignment beyond the provided documentation.
3. Reported long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, borrow fees, and market impact.
4. IC t-statistics assume independent monthly observations; autocorrelation in signals could inflate significance.
5. The sample spans only 120 months and one market regime, so estimates are subject to regime risk.
