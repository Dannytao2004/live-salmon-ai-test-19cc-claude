# Which Stock Characteristics Predict Next-Month Returns? Evidence from a 120-Month Equity Panel

## Abstract
I study a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-months covering 1,217 stocks and 120 months, with nine point-in-time characteristics and next-month returns. Using monthly cross-sectional rank ICs and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, I identify three robust predictors: gross profitability is positive, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negative. The remaining six characteristics—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—have t-statistics below two in absolute value and are not used. A composite long-short strategy that buys high profitability, low asset growth, and low idiosyncratic volatility is estimated to earn about 11.5% gross annual return with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.2 before costs.

## Methods
The data are a monthly stock panel with 60,000 rows, 120 months, 500 stocks per month, and 1,217 unique stocks. The prediction target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month \(t+1\). The features are `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`. The universe is point-in-time index constituents; stocks remain in the data through their final month, including the return over that month.

I evaluate each feature using the provided monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank ICs with `ret_next`. The reported mean rank IC is averaged over 120 months, and the t-statistic is \(\text{mean IC} / \text{IC std} \times \sqrt{120}\). I also use the supplied equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, which buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month. A feature is treated as a valid signal if its t-statistic exceeds 2 in absolute value and its sign is reasonably consistent across months. For the composite strategy, I combine the valid signals with their expected signs: positive for `gross_prof`, negative for `asset_growth`, and negative for `idio_vol`. Each month, stocks are ranked cross-sectionally on this composite, and the strategy buys the top quintile and shorts the bottom quintile equal-weighted, rebalancing monthly. Expected performance is estimated from the provided long-short means and Sharpe ratios, with a conservative haircut for cross-signal correlation and implementation frictions.

## Results
Three features pass the validity screen. `gross_prof` has a mean rank IC of 0.0378, a t-statistic of 6.07, positive IC in 70% of months, and a quintile long-short return of 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94. `asset_growth` has a mean rank IC of -0.0303, a t-statistic of -4.29, positive IC in only 39% of months, and a long-short return of -0.99% per month with an annualized Sharpe of -1.48; reversing the trade—buying low-growth and shorting high-growth—earns +0.99% monthly. `idio_vol` has a mean rank IC of -0.0261, a t-statistic of -4.19, positive IC in 35% of months, and a long-short return of -0.74% per month with an annualized Sharpe of -1.20; reversing the trade earns +0.74% monthly.

The other six features are not statistically reliable in this sample. `book_to_market` has a mean rank IC of 0.0027 with t-statistic 0.68; `log_mktcap` has 0.0055 with t-statistic 1.26; `accruals` has 0.0015 with t-statistic 0.36; `leverage` has -0.0011 with t-statistic -0.30; `turnover` has 0.0006 with t-statistic 0.14; and `rd_intensity` has -0.0004 with t-statistic -0.10. None of these reaches conventional statistical significance.

Combining the three valid signals into an equal-weighted reversed long-short composite gives an average monthly return of approximately \((1.16\% + 0.99\% + 0.74\%)/3 \approx 0.96\%\), or about 11.6% simple annualized and 12.2% compounded. Because the three signals are unlikely to be perfectly independent and trading costs are excluded, I conservatively estimate the composite’s gross annual return at 11.5% and annualized Sharpe at 2.2.

## Discussion
The results align with three well-documented equity anomalies: the profitability premium, the asset-growth anomaly, and the low-volatility anomaly. High gross profitability predicts higher next-month returns, while high asset growth and high idiosyncratic volatility predict lower next-month returns. The insignificance of book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity in this sample may reflect the index-constituent universe, the 120-month sample period, or the fact that these signals are weaker after accounting for the three dominant characteristics. A composite strategy benefits from diversification across imperfectly correlated signals; its estimated Sharpe is higher than that of most individual signals in isolation, though the estimate remains sensitive to correlation assumptions and implementation costs.

## Limitations
- Only summary statistics are available, so nonlinearities, interactions, sector effects, and outliers cannot be tested.
- The long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, borrow fees, and market impact, which can materially reduce net performance.
- The sample covers 120 months and 500 index constituents per month; results may not generalize to other universes or periods.
- Multiple testing across nine features raises the possibility that some apparent significance is due to chance, although the three selected signals have large t-statistics.
- Cross-signal correlations are not provided, so the composite Sharpe estimate relies on a moderate-correlation assumption.
- The point-in-time universe includes stocks through removal, but index membership rules may still induce selection effects.
