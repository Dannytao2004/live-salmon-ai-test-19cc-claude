# Report Q076

# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a 120-Month Equity Panel: The Dominance of Analyst Revision

---

## Abstract

We examine ten candidate stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns in a panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months (60,000 stock-month observations). Using monthly rank information coefficients (IC), t-statistics, and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find that only one feature — analyst consensus revision (`analyst_rev`) — carries economically and statistically meaningful predictive power, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.296, a t-statistic of 89.6, positive IC in 100% of months, and a long-short Sharpe ratio of 26.4. The remaining nine characteristics produce rank ICs indistinguishable from zero (|t| < 2) and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.8. We argue the panel is dominated by a single, near-mechanical signal and that the other features carry no exploitable cross-sectional information in this sample. A concentrated strategy loading on analyst revision is expected to deliver a very large but capacity-constrained alpha.

---

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 stock-month observations: 500 index constituents per month across 120 months, with 1,213 distinct stocks. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. The universe is point-in-time and survivorship-aware: stocks removed from the index (distress/delisting) remain in the data through their final month, including that month's return. This is important because it prevents the survivorship bias that would otherwise inflate momentum- and distress-related signals.

**Features.** Ten characteristics are provided: valuation (`book_to_market`), profitability (`gross_prof`), size (`log_mktcap`), investment (`asset_growth`), earnings quality (`accruals`), analyst revision (`analyst_rev`), risk (`idio_vol`), capital structure (`leverage`), liquidity/trading (`turnover`), and intangibles (`rd_intensity`).

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the supplied monthly rank IC (Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month), averaged across the 120 months, together with its cross-month standard deviation, t-statistic (mean/σ × √120), and the fraction of months with positive IC. We supplement this with the equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile long-short return and its annualized Sharpe ratio. A feature is deemed **valid** only if it shows a large, stable, and statistically overwhelming IC (we require |t| ≫ 3 and consistency across months). Marginal signals (|t| < 2) are treated as noise.

**Strategy construction.** The proposed strategy ranks stocks each month on the single valid signal, `analyst_rev`, buys the top quintile, and sells the bottom quintile with equal weights, rebalancing monthly.

---

## Results

**Only analyst revision is a valid signal.** `analyst_rev` has a mean monthly rank IC of **0.2958**, an IC standard deviation of 0.0362, a t-statistic of **89.6**, and a positive IC in **100% of the 120 months**. Its long-short portfolio earns **8.66% per month** with an annualized Sharpe of **26.4**. The magnitude, sign stability, and month-by-month consistency are all consistent with a genuine, highly reliable predictive relationship.

**All other features are statistically indistinguishable from zero.** The next-largest t-statistics belong to `asset_growth` (t = 1.64) and `accruals` (t = 1.34), both far below conventional significance thresholds and with economically trivial long-short returns (0.28% and 0.29% per month, Sharpe 0.72 and 0.70). The remaining features — `book_to_market` (t = −0.72), `gross_prof` (0.93), `log_mktcap` (0.02), `idio_vol` (1.14), `leverage` (−1.00), `turnover` (0.02), and `rd_intensity` (−0.05) — all have |t| < 2, near-zero ICs (|IC| ≤ 0.006), and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.3. Their "months with IC > 0" fractions cluster tightly around 50%, exactly what one expects from noise.

**Signal direction.** `analyst_rev` is **positive**: upward revisions to the FY1 EPS consensus predict higher next-month returns. This aligns with the well-documented post-revision drift / analyst-revision anomaly. No other feature survives with a reliable sign.

---

## Discussion

The results are stark: in this panel, essentially all cross-sectional predictive content is concentrated in `analyst_rev`. The IC of 0.296 is extraordinarily high for a monthly equity signal — typical published anomalies have rank ICs of 0.02–0.05 — and the 100% positive-IC frequency and t-statistic of 89.6 indicate the signal is near-deterministic in this sample.

A crucial caveat concerns **timing**. The data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is "the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, scaled by share price," recorded at the end of month t+1 — i.e., the same month over which `ret_next` is realized. The signal is therefore **contemporaneous** with the return, not strictly lagged. This likely explains the implausibly high IC and Sharpe: analyst revisions and returns are jointly driven by the same information flow. A tradable version would require the revision to be observable *before* the return window, which the current feature definition does not guarantee. We flag this prominently as the central economic concern.

The other characteristics behave as one would expect in a well-constructed but low-power sample: value, profitability, size, investment, accruals, low-volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D all fail to generate a reliable edge. The point-in-time, delisting-inclusive universe removes survivorship bias but also removes the artificial boost these signals sometimes enjoy in cleaner samples.

**Proposed strategy.** Rank the universe each month on `analyst_rev`; long the top quintile, short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, monthly rebalance. Given the reported long-short mean of 8.66%/month and Sharpe of 26.4, the mechanical expected annual return is enormous (≈ 104% gross, before costs). We haircut this heavily for implementation costs, turnover, and the timing concern above, but even a conservative estimate implies a very strong strategy.

---

## Limitations

- `analyst_rev` is recorded over month t+1, the same window as `ret_next`, so part of its predictive power may be contemporaneous rather than tradable.
- The reported Sharpe of 26.4 is implausibly high for a real strategy and almost certainly reflects look-ahead or mechanical co-movement rather than exploitable alpha.
- No transaction costs, borrow costs, or capacity constraints are modeled; a monthly-rebalanced quintile strategy on a 500-stock universe would face meaningful turnover costs.
- The panel covers only index constituents, so results may not generalize to small caps or non-indexed stocks.
- Only 120 monthly observations limit the power to detect weak but real signals among the nine rejected features.
