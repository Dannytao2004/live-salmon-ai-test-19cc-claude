# Are There Tradable Stock Characteristics in This Monthly Equity Panel? Evidence from Rank IC and Quintile Portfolios

## Abstract

We evaluate ten stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns using a 60,000-row monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Rank IC and equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios show only one economically large relation: `analyst_rev`. However, `analyst_rev` is recorded at the end of month t+1, contemporaneous with the prediction target `ret_next`, so its strong performance is a look-ahead artefact rather than a tradable signal. The remaining nine characteristics have economically small and statistically insignificant rank ICs, with absolute t-statistics below 1.7 and long-short annualized Sharpes below 0.75. We therefore reject all ten features as valid tradable signals and recommend no active characteristic-based allocation.

## Methods

The dataset contains one row per stock per month: 120 months, 500 stocks per month, and 60,000 total observations. The target is `ret_next`, the stock return over month t+1. The candidate features are `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `analyst_rev`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

We assess predictive power using monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank ICs. For each month, we rank each feature across stocks and correlate it with ranked `ret_next`; the reported mean rank IC is the average over 120 months. The t-statistic is computed as `mean IC / std(IC) * sqrt(120)`. We also use equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature. A signal is considered valid only if it is both point-in-time available at month t and statistically reliable, ideally with |t-stat| > 2 and a plausible economic mechanism.

A critical point-in-time check is applied to `analyst_rev`. Its data dictionary states it is the change in mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, recorded at the end of month t+1. Because `ret_next` is also realized over month t+1, `analyst_rev` is not known at the formation date and cannot be used to predict `ret_next` in real time.

## Results

The signal statistics are summarized below.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Long-Short Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `book_to_market` | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | -0.27 |
| `gross_prof` | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| `log_mktcap` | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| `asset_growth` | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| `accruals` | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| `analyst_rev` | 0.2958 | 89.59 | 8.66% | 26.38 |
| `idio_vol` | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| `leverage` | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | -0.05 |
| `turnover` | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| `rd_intensity` | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 |

Only `analyst_rev` appears strong. Its mean rank IC is 0.2958, it is positive in 100% of months, and its long-short portfolio earns 8.66% per month with a Sharpe of 26.38. This magnitude is implausible for a real-time equity signal. The reason is mechanical: `analyst_rev` is recorded at the end of month t+1, so it overlaps the return being predicted. It is not available at month t and therefore is not a valid predictor of `ret_next`.

The other nine features are weak. Their mean rank ICs range from -0.0043 to 0.0061. The largest absolute t-statistic among them is `asset_growth` at 1.64, followed by `accruals` at 1.34; neither reaches conventional two-sided significance. Their long-short portfolios produce monthly returns between -0.11% and 0.29%, with annualized Sharpes between -0.27 and 0.72. Some signs also contradict well-known anomaly directions: `asset_growth` has a positive IC rather than the expected negative one, and `accruals` has a positive IC rather than the expected negative one.

## Discussion

The main finding is cautionary. The only feature with a dramatic statistical relation, `analyst_rev`, is contaminated by look-ahead bias. Because it is measured during month t+1 and `ret_next` is the return over month t+1, the feature and target share the same information window. A real-time investor could not know `analyst_rev` at the end of month t when forming positions for month t+1. The enormous IC t-statistic of 89.59 and 100% positive-month rate are symptoms of this leakage rather than evidence of alpha.

After excluding `analyst_rev`, the remaining characteristics provide no reliable evidence of cross-sectional return predictability in this panel. Their rank ICs are small relative to their monthly standard deviations, and their long-short portfolio Sharpes are low. With ten features tested, some t-statistics near 1.6 could easily arise by chance. There is also no consistent alignment with established economic priors: value, profitability, asset growth, accruals, low volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity do not show robust or correctly signed effects here.

Given these results, an active characteristic-based long-short strategy is not supported. A conservative implementation would be to hold no active signal-based position, or if benchmark exposure is required, to hold a passive equal-weighted universe. The passive equal-weighted universe has an average monthly `ret_next` of 0.936%, implying roughly 11.8% annualized return and annualized volatility of about 39.2%, for a Sharpe ratio near 0.30. That return is not alpha from the features; it is simply the unconditional return of the index constituents.

## Limitations

This analysis relies on summary statistics and cannot inspect outliers, nonlinearities, sector effects, or interactions among features. The sample covers only 120 months and one broad index universe, so results may not generalize across regimes or markets. The `analyst_rev` leakage prevents a real-time test of that characteristic from this panel. Multiple testing across ten features means weak t-statistics should be interpreted cautiously. Transaction costs, borrow costs, liquidity, and capacity are ignored in the long-short statistics. Finally, the universe consists of index constituents, which may introduce survivorship or constituent-selection biases.
