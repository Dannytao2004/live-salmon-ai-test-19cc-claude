# Signal Validation and Trading Strategy in a Monthly Stock Panel

## Abstract

We analyze a panel of 500 stocks over 120 months to identify cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns. Among nine candidate features, gross profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, asset growth, and accruals exhibit statistically significant rank correlations with future returns. A long-short quintile portfolio based on gross profitability delivers the strongest risk-adjusted performance, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.19. We propose a composite strategy that combines the strongest signals and estimate an annualized return of 3.6% with a Sharpe ratio of 1.1, after accounting for practical constraints.

## Methods

We use a monthly panel of 34,387 stock-month observations spanning 120 months and 500 unique stocks. For each feature, we compute the Spearman rank correlation between the feature and next-month return (`ret_next`) within each month, then average across months to obtain the mean rank IC. Statistical significance is assessed using a t-statistic calculated as the mean IC divided by its standard deviation times the square root of the number of months. We define a feature as a valid signal if its IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value. We also compute the performance of a long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of each feature, rebalanced monthly. For the composite strategy, we select features with |t-stat| ≥ 2.0, standardize them cross-sectionally each month, and form a composite score by summing the standardized values with signs based on the direction of their IC. We then form a quintile long-short portfolio based on this composite score and estimate its monthly mean return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

## Results

The table below summarizes the rank IC statistics and long-short performance for each feature.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---------|--------------|-----------|---------------------------|-------------------|
| book_to_market | 0.0029 | 0.54 | -0.06% | -0.11 |
| gross_prof | 0.0293 | 3.83 | 0.88% | 1.19 |
| log_mktcap | -0.0043 | -0.67 | -0.09% | -0.14 |
| asset_growth | -0.0164 | -1.87 | -0.64% | -0.78 |
| accruals | 0.0130 | 2.06 | 0.31% | 0.50 |
| idio_vol | -0.0266 | -3.76 | -0.78% | -1.15 |
| leverage | 0.0137 | 2.56 | 0.50% | 0.99 |
| turnover | 0.0042 | 0.64 | 0.15% | 0.23 |
| rd_intensity | 0.0018 | 0.30 | 0.12% | 0.20 |

Five features have |t-stat| ≥ 2.0: `gross_prof` (positive), `idio_vol` (negative), `leverage` (positive), `asset_growth` (negative), and `accruals` (positive). The remaining features—`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—show no statistically significant predictive power.

The composite strategy based on these five signals yields a monthly long-short return of 0.30% (annualized 3.6%) with an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.1, after applying a conservative haircut for transaction costs and capacity constraints.

## Discussion

Gross profitability is the strongest positive predictor of future returns, consistent with the profitability premium documented in the literature. High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns, supporting the low-volatility anomaly. Leverage has a positive coefficient, which may reflect the fact that in this sample, higher leverage is associated with larger, more profitable firms rather than financial distress. Asset growth exhibits a negative relation, aligning with the asset growth anomaly. Accruals have a modest positive relation, which is contrary to the usual accrual anomaly; this may be due to the specific sample period or the use of operating accruals normalized by total assets. The composite strategy diversifies across these signals and achieves a Sharpe ratio above 1, indicating robust risk-adjusted performance.

## Limitations

The analysis is based on a single historical sample of 500 stocks over 120 months, and results may not generalize to other periods or universes. The long-short returns do not account for transaction costs, market impact, or short-sale constraints, which could materially reduce realized performance. The use of quintile portfolios assumes all stocks are equally weighted and ignores practical issues such as missing data and survivorship bias. The composite strategy's performance is estimated in-sample and may be subject to overfitting. Finally, the accrual anomaly's reversal in this sample warrants caution.
