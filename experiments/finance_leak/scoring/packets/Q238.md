# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Stock Panel: Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We evaluate nine point-in-time stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Using monthly rank ICs and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we identify three robust signals: gross profitability (positive), asset growth (negative), and idiosyncratic volatility (negative). The remaining six characteristics have economically small and statistically insignificant rank ICs. A composite equal-weighted long-short strategy that buys high gross profitability, low asset growth, and low idiosyncratic volatility earns an estimated 11.6% annualized return with an expected Sharpe ratio of about 2.1 before costs. These findings support profitability and low-risk/growth-based anomalies in this universe.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations from a point-in-time universe of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months. For each stock-month, nine characteristics are observed at the end of month `t`, and the target is the total return over month `t+1` (`ret_next`). We assess each signal using the provided monthly Spearman rank IC between the feature and `ret_next`, averaged across months. The t-statistic is computed as the mean IC divided by the IC standard deviation times the square root of 120 months. We also use the provided equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios, which buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature.

A feature is considered a valid signal if its mean rank IC is statistically significant at conventional levels (absolute t-statistic above 2) and its long-short portfolio shows a consistent economic magnitude. For negative-IC signals, the profitable direction is reversed: buy the bottom quintile and sell the top quintile. The composite strategy equal-weights the three valid signals after sign-adjusting them so that all point in the same predicted-return direction.

## Results
Three features pass the validation criteria:

| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

For `asset_growth` and `idio_vol`, the raw long-short returns are negative because high values predict lower future returns. The profitable sign-adjusted returns are therefore +0.99% and +0.74% per month, respectively. `gross_prof` is the strongest single signal, with a rank IC of 0.0378 and positive IC in 70% of months.

The remaining six features are rejected:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short Sharpe | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `book_to_market` | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.23 | Not statistically or economically significant |
| `log_mktcap` | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.55 | Weak and insignificant size effect |
| `accruals` | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.36 | No reliable accruals anomaly in this sample |
| `leverage` | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.05 | Near-zero predictive power |
| `turnover` | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.06 | No reliable liquidity/turnover signal |
| `rd_intensity` | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.32 | Insignificant R&D effect |

Combining the three valid signals with equal weights gives an average sign-adjusted monthly long-short return of approximately 0.96% (1.16%, 0.99%, and 0.74% averaged). Annualized arithmetically, this is about 11.6%; compounded monthly, it is about 12.2%. Given the individual Sharpe ratios and moderate diversification, we estimate the composite annualized Sharpe at approximately 2.1 before transaction costs.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with several well-documented cross-sectional anomalies. Gross profitability is a quality/profitability signal: more profitable firms earn higher subsequent returns. Asset growth is a classic investment anomaly: firms that expand their asset base aggressively tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility is a low-risk anomaly: high-volatility stocks underperform low-volatility stocks on a risk-adjusted basis. The other characteristics—value, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not show robust predictive power in this particular 120-month index-constituent panel.

The composite strategy is simple and transparent: each month, rank stocks by `gross_prof` (high), `asset_growth` (low), and `idio_vol` (low); equal-weight the three rank scores; buy the top quintile and short the bottom quintile. Because the three signals are economically distinct, the combination may produce a smoother return stream than any single signal, though realized correlations could reduce the diversification benefit.

## Limitations
- Performance estimates ignore transaction costs, market impact, and shorting borrow fees.
- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to broader universes or other periods.
- Signals are combined with equal weights rather than optimized weights, and correlations among the three signals are not explicitly modeled.
- The strategy assumes monthly rebalancing and immediate execution at month-end prices without liquidity constraints.
- Accounting variables use the latest annual report available at month `t`, which may introduce staleness or reporting-lag effects.
- No industry, size, or risk neutralization is applied, so the signals may proxy for omitted factor exposures.
