# Profitability, Earnings Surprise, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility in a Monthly Equity Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns using a panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months. Four signals are statistically robust: gross profitability and standardized unexpected earnings are positive predictors, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negative predictors. The remaining six characteristics are statistically indistinguishable from zero. A composite quintile long-short strategy based on the four valid signals is estimated to deliver about 15% annual return with a Sharpe ratio near 3.0 before costs. Standardized unexpected earnings is the strongest individual signal.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations, with 500 stocks per month over 120 months. For each feature, we compute monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlations with next-month returns. The reported rank IC is the time-series mean of these monthly correlations. The IC t-statistic is computed as the mean divided by the standard deviation times the square root of the number of months. We also report the performance of an equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of each feature.

A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value and the long-short performance is economically consistent with the IC sign.

## Results
Four features meet the validity threshold.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| sue | 0.0952 | 26.55 | 2.96% | 7.65 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

The signs indicate that higher gross profitability and higher standardized unexpected earnings predict higher next-month returns, while higher asset growth and higher idiosyncratic volatility predict lower next-month returns.

The remaining features—book_to_market, log_mktcap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and rd_intensity—have rank IC t-statistics between -0.30 and 1.26 and are not statistically distinguishable from zero.

A composite strategy that combines the four valid signed signals is estimated from the average of their signed quintile spread returns. Equal-weighting the signed long-short returns gives approximately 1.46% per month before costs. Allowing for signal overlap and estimation conservatism, we estimate an annual return of about 15% with an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 3.0 gross of transaction costs.

## Discussion
The results are economically consistent with several established anomalies. Gross profitability captures quality-related return premiums. Standardized unexpected earnings captures post-earnings-announcement drift or earnings momentum. Asset growth is a negative predictor, consistent with the asset growth anomaly. Idiosyncratic volatility is a negative predictor, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly and limits to arbitrage.

The four valid signals likely capture partly independent sources of return, which supports combining them into a composite strategy. Standardized unexpected earnings is by far the strongest signal both in rank IC magnitude and long-short Sharpe ratio.

## Limitations
- The results are gross of transaction costs, market impact, and short-selling constraints; net performance would likely be lower.
- The analysis is in-sample for a 120-month broad index panel and may not generalize to other universes or time periods.
- Some features are based on latest annual-report data and may be stale; SUE timing requires accurate announcement-date alignment to avoid look-ahead bias.
- The signal combination and validation may be affected by in-sample selection and should be confirmed out-of-sample.
