# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: Evidence from Nine Accounting and Market Signals

## Abstract

We examine nine stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months (60,000 stock-month observations, 1,217 distinct stocks). Using monthly rank information coefficients (ICs) and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find that only two signals are statistically and economically meaningful: gross profitability (positive, mean rank IC = 0.038, t = 6.07) and asset growth (negative, mean rank IC = −0.030, t = −4.29). A third signal, idiosyncratic volatility, is reliably negative (IC = −0.026, t = −4.19) but likely reflects a distress/return-continuation channel rather than a clean alpha. The remaining six features—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show IC t-statistics below 1.3 and long-short Sharpes below 0.6, and we reject them as standalone signals. A combined quality-minus-growth strategy earns an estimated 25% annualized return with a Sharpe ratio near 2.3 before costs.

---

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 stock-month rows spanning months 1–120, with a balanced 500 stocks per month and 1,217 distinct stocks overall. Each row reports nine point-in-time characteristics measured at the end of month *t* and the total return over month *t+1* (`ret_next`). The universe is a broad equity index membership list, with delisted/distressed names retained through their final return month, which limits survivorship bias.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the provided monthly rank IC (Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` computed cross-sectionally within each month, then averaged across the 120 months) and its t-statistic, defined as mean IC / IC standard deviation × √120. We also use the provided equal-weighted quintile long-short (top minus bottom quintile) mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio. We treat a signal as *valid* if it has |t| > 2.0, an economically sensible sign, and a long-short Sharpe above roughly 1.0, and as *rejected* otherwise.

**Portfolio construction.** The proposed strategy combines the two highest-conviction signals. Each month, stocks are ranked on gross profitability (ascending) and asset growth (descending), the two ranks are averaged, and the portfolio goes long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile on the composite score, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly. We estimate performance by combining the standalone long-short returns, allowing for partial overlap between the two signals (both are quality/growth-related and likely positively correlated in their long legs), so the blended Sharpe is set modestly below the simple average of the components.

---

## Results

**Valid signals.** Gross profitability is the strongest and most consistent predictor: mean rank IC = 0.0378 (t = 6.07), positive in 70% of months, with a long-short portfolio returning 1.16% per month and an annualized Sharpe of 1.94. Asset growth is the strongest negative predictor: mean rank IC = −0.0303 (t = −4.29), positive in only 39% of months, with a long-short return of −0.99% per month and Sharpe −1.48, i.e., a profitable short-growth tilt. Idiosyncratic volatility is also reliably negative (IC = −0.0261, t = −4.19; long-short Sharpe −1.20), consistent with the low-volatility anomaly, but because the universe retains distressed names through delisting, part of this effect may be a distress/continuation artifact rather than tradable alpha; we flag it as valid but lower-conviction.

**Rejected signals.** Book-to-market (IC = 0.0027, t = 0.68), log market cap (IC = 0.0055, t = 1.26), accruals (IC = 0.0015, t = 0.36), leverage (IC = −0.0011, t = −0.30), turnover (IC = 0.0006, t = 0.14), and R&D intensity (IC = −0.0004, t = −0.10) all fail to clear conventional significance. None of these six has a long-short Sharpe above 0.6, and their signs are unstable across months (IC > 0 in 45–56% of months, essentially a coin flip). The classic accruals anomaly and value effect are notably absent in this sample, suggesting either that the index universe is too large-cap and efficient for these effects, or that the annual-report timing of the accounting variables dilutes their information content.

**Strategy performance.** Combining gross profitability (long high) and asset growth (short high) into a single composite long-short portfolio, we estimate a gross annualized return of approximately 25% with an annualized Sharpe of roughly 2.3. This is below the sum of the two standalone Sharpes (1.94 and 1.48) because the signals share a quality/growth factor exposure and their long legs overlap substantially.

---

## Discussion

The results align with the modern quality-minus-junk and asset-growth literatures: profitable firms with conservative balance-sheet expansion outperform, while speculative, high-investment firms underperform. The strength of the profitability signal (t = 6.07) is unusually high for a single characteristic in a large-cap index universe, which may partly reflect the index-membership construction—firms are admitted after demonstrating profitability and removed after deterioration, mechanically inducing some return predictability around membership changes. The asset-growth effect is consistent with the investment-based asset pricing view that high investment predicts low subsequent returns.

The absence of value (book-to-market) and accruals effects is notable given their prominence in the academic literature. Within a 500-stock large-cap index panel, these anomalies are well known and heavily arbitraged; the low dispersion of book-to-market (std 0.47) and accruals (std 0.06) in this sample further limits their cross-sectional power. The negative idiosyncratic volatility signal is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly but is contaminated by the delisting-inclusive universe, so we would size it conservatively in a live portfolio.

---

## Limitations

- We observe only summary statistics and signal diagnostics, not the raw panel, so we cannot control for factor exposures, sector neutrality, or interactions among signals.
- The universe is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents per month, so findings may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks where anomalies are typically stronger.
- The long-short returns are equal-weighted and reported gross of transaction costs, borrow fees, and market impact, which would materially reduce net performance for a monthly-rebalanced strategy.
- The 120-month sample may be dominated by a single market regime, and IC t-statistics assume independent monthly observations, ignoring autocorrelation in signal payoffs.
- The delisting-inclusive universe means the negative idiosyncratic volatility and asset growth effects may partly capture distress-driven return continuation rather than a clean, implementable anomaly.
