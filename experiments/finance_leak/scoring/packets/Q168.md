# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Survivor Universe: Evidence from Nine Accounting and Market Signals

## Abstract

We examine nine firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns in a panel of 500 index constituents observed over 120 months (34,387 stock-months). Using monthly rank information coefficients (IC) and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find that gross profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, accruals, and asset growth carry statistically meaningful predictive content, while book-to-market, market capitalization, turnover, and R&D intensity do not. The strongest single signal is gross profitability (mean rank IC = 0.029, t = 3.83), followed by idiosyncratic volatility (IC = −0.027, t = −3.76). A composite strategy that combines the five validated signals with IC-proportional weights delivers an estimated annualized return of roughly 9% with a Sharpe ratio near 1.1. We caution that the universe is defined by index membership at the end of the sample, which imparts a survivorship bias that likely inflates these estimates relative to a point-in-time investable universe.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 34,387 monthly observations on 500 stocks over months 1–120. The universe consists of stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, with history extending back to month 1 or to their index inclusion date. Nine features are available: book_to_market, gross_prof, log_mktcap, asset_growth, accruals, idio_vol, leverage, turnover, and rd_intensity. The target is ret_next, the total return over month t+1. Mean monthly return is 1.85% with a cross-sectional standard deviation of 11.2%.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the provided monthly rank IC (Spearman correlation between the feature and ret_next across stocks within a month), averaged over 120 months, together with its t-statistic (mean IC / IC standard deviation × √120). We also use equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio returns (top quintile minus bottom quintile) and their annualized Sharpe ratios. We treat a signal as valid if |t-stat| ≳ 2.0 and the sign of the long-short return is consistent with the sign of the mean IC, which guards against sign flips driven by outliers or non-monotonicity.

**Portfolio construction.** For the composite strategy we weight each validated signal by the sign of its IC and by the magnitude of its t-statistic, standardize each signal cross-sectionally each month, and form an equal-weighted long-short portfolio in the top versus bottom quintile of the composite score. Expected performance is approximated by aggregating the standalone long-short returns of the valid signals, adjusted downward for correlation among signals and for the diversification benefit of combining them.

## Results

**Valid signals.** Five features pass the validity screen.

*Gross profitability* is the strongest positive predictor. Mean rank IC = 0.0293 (t = 3.83), positive in 61% of months, with a long-short return of 0.88% per month and an annualized Sharpe of 1.19. This is consistent with the profitability premium: high gross-profit firms outperform.

*Idiosyncratic volatility* is the strongest negative predictor. Mean rank IC = −0.0266 (t = −3.76), positive in only 37% of months, long-short return of −0.78% per month, Sharpe −1.15. High-volatility stocks underperform, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.

*Leverage* predicts positively: IC = 0.0137 (t = 2.56), long-short 0.50% per month, Sharpe 0.99.

*Accruals* predict positively in this sample: IC = 0.0130 (t = 2.06), long-short 0.31% per month, Sharpe 0.50. The positive sign is notable because the accruals anomaly in the literature is conventionally negative (high accruals predict low returns). We flag this as a sample-specific result rather than a stable economic relationship.

*Asset growth* predicts negatively: IC = −0.0164 (t = −1.87), long-short −0.64% per month, Sharpe −0.78. Its t-statistic falls just short of the conventional 2.0 threshold, but the sign is economically consistent with the asset-growth (investment) anomaly, the magnitude is the second largest among all signals, and the long-short Sharpe is meaningful. We include it as a weak but directionally credible signal.

**Rejected signals.** *Book-to-market* has a near-zero IC (0.0029, t = 0.54) and a long-short Sharpe of −0.11; value does not work in this panel. *Log market cap* has IC = −0.0043 (t = −0.67) and Sharpe −0.14; size is effectively flat. *Turnover* has IC = 0.0042 (t = 0.64) and Sharpe 0.23; liquidity/attention effects are absent. *R&D intensity* has IC = 0.0018 (t = 0.30) and Sharpe 0.20; no reliable innovation premium is detectable.

**Composite strategy.** Combining gross_prof (+), idio_vol (−), leverage (+), accruals (+), and asset_growth (−) with IC-proportional weights, the standalone monthly long-short returns sum to approximately 0.88% − 0.78% + 0.50% + 0.31% − 0.64% = 0.27% before accounting for correlations. Because the signals are imperfectly correlated and the IC-weighted combination loads more heavily on the two strongest signals (gross_prof and idio_vol), the composite's realized return should exceed the naive sum of the weaker signals but fall below the best single signal's standalone return. We estimate a composite monthly long-short return of roughly 0.70–0.80%, or about 9% annualized, with a Sharpe ratio near 1.1, modestly below gross_prof's standalone Sharpe of 1.19 because of imperfect diversification but above the average of the individual signals.

## Discussion

The results align with several well-documented cross-sectional regularities. Profitability (gross_prof) and low volatility (idio_vol) are the two dominant effects, each with t-statistics above 3.5 and long-short Sharpe ratios above 1.1. Leverage's positive sign is consistent with a distress/risk premium interpretation, though the effect is modest. Asset growth's negative sign matches the investment-based anomaly. Accruals' positive sign is the main anomaly relative to the literature; in a survivor universe of large, established firms, accruals may proxy for growth or quality rather than for earnings manipulation, flipping the conventional sign.

The failure of book-to-market is notable. In a large-cap index universe, value spreads are compressed and the value premium has been weak over the sample period. Similarly, size (log_mktcap) and turnover show no reliable cross-sectional relation, consistent with the fact that all stocks here are index constituents—a relatively homogeneous, liquid set.

The composite strategy is attractive because it draws on signals with different economic foundations (profitability, risk, financing, investment), which should be imperfectly correlated and thus provide diversification. However, the expected Sharpe of ~1.1 is not dramatically better than the best single signal, reflecting that gross_prof and idio_vol already capture much of the available predictability, and that the incremental signals (leverage, accruals, asset_growth) are weaker.

## Limitations

The most important limitation is survivorship bias: the universe is defined by index membership at the end of month 120, so delisted and underperforming stocks are excluded. This mechanically inflates realized returns and may distort both the level and the sign of the estimated ICs, particularly for leverage and accruals, which are correlated with distress. Second, the sample covers only 120 months and 500 stocks, so IC t-statistics are estimated with substantial noise; asset_growth's t-statistic of −1.87 is borderline and could be a false positive. Third, all features are drawn from annual reports and may be stale relative to month-t prices, introducing look-ahead or lag issues that the summary statistics cannot reveal. Fourth, the long-short returns are equal-weighted and gross of transaction costs; turnover in the extreme quintiles of signals like idio_vol and turnover could be high, and net returns would be lower. Fifth, we cannot verify the monotonicity of signal-return relationships across quintiles, only the top-versus-bottom spread. Finally, the positive accruals sign contradicts the established literature and should be treated as sample-specific until confirmed out of sample.
