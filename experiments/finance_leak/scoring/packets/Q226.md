# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a 500-Stock Monthly Panel: The Dominance of Analyst Revision

## Abstract
We examine ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month cross-sectional stock returns in a panel of 500 index constituents observed over 120 months. Using rank information coefficients (IC) and quintile long-short portfolios, we find that `analyst_rev` is by far the strongest and most reliable signal, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.296 (t = 89.6) and a long-short portfolio delivering 8.66% per month (Sharpe ≈ 26). All other nine features produce rank ICs within ±0.007 of zero with |t| < 1.7, consistent with no exploitable cross-sectional signal at this horizon. A simple, low-turnover strategy concentrated in the analyst-revision signal would have generated extreme risk-adjusted returns in-sample, though we caution that the effect size is implausibly large and likely reflects look-ahead contamination in the feature's construction.

## Methods
**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 stock-month observations: 500 index constituents per month across 120 months. Each row provides ten point-in-time features and the subsequent month's total return (`ret_next`). The dataset is described as clean, so no additional filtering was applied.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the provided monthly Spearman rank IC (correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks within a month), averaged over 120 months. We assess statistical significance via the reported t-statistic (mean IC ÷ IC standard deviation × √120). We also use the reported equal-weighted quintile long-short (Q5 − Q1) mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

**Signal selection rule.** We treat a feature as a *valid* signal only if it is both economically interpretable and statistically credible, which we operationalize as |t| ≳ 2 and a consistent sign of the IC across months. Marginal features (|t| between roughly 1.3 and 2) are noted but not traded.

**Feature-construction audit.** Because the target is month t+1 return, any feature whose measurement window overlaps t+1 is mechanically forward-looking. We inspect the data dictionary for such overlaps.

**Strategy.** A long-short portfolio that each month buys the top quintile and shorts the bottom quintile of the selected valid signal(s), equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly.

## Results
**Signal statistics.** The table below reproduces the key evidence (rank IC, t-stat, long-short return, Sharpe):

| Feature | Mean rank IC | t-stat | LS monthly | LS Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| analyst_rev | **0.2958** | **89.59** | **8.66%** | **26.38** |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 |
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | -0.27 |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | -0.05 |

**Only one feature qualifies.** `analyst_rev` has a mean rank IC of 0.296 and is positive in 100% of the 120 months — a level of consistency essentially never observed for a genuine return predictor. Its quintile long-short spread of 8.66%/month (≈ 170% annualized) with a Sharpe near 26 is far outside the range documented for any real anomaly.

**Everything else is noise.** The remaining nine features have ICs within ±0.007 and |t| ≤ 1.64. `asset_growth` (IC = +0.0061, t = 1.64) and `accruals` (IC = +0.0055, t = 1.34) are the only near-marginal cases; both are economically small and would not survive a multiple-testing adjustment across ten features. Several signs are also economically counterintuitive given the literature (e.g., accruals typically predicts *negatively*, and book-to-market typically predicts *positively*), reinforcing that these are sampling artifacts.

**Feature audit.** The data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is "the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, scaled by share price. **Recorded at the end of month t+1**." Since `ret_next` is also realized over month t+1, the feature and target share the same measurement window. The near-perfect IC is therefore mechanical: the analyst revision *incorporates* the month-t+1 return information rather than predicting it. This is a look-ahead / point-in-time violation, not a tradable edge.

**Proposed strategy (with the caveat above).** If one naively took the statistics at face value, the optimal strategy is a pure `analyst_rev` long-short: buy the top quintile, short the bottom quintile, equal weight, monthly rebalance. Reported in-sample: ≈ 8.66%/month ≈ 104% annualized, Sharpe ≈ 26. Adding any other feature would only dilute the signal. Because the signal is not implementable in real time (it requires month-t+1 information), the honest expected live performance of this strategy is approximately zero; we report the in-sample figures only to characterize the data.

## Discussion
The central finding is a cautionary one. A panel with ten plausible anomalies yields exactly one "significant" signal, and that signal is significant for the wrong reason: its construction window overlaps the prediction target. The remaining nine features — including several (book-to-market, gross profitability, accruals, momentum-like turnover) that have well-documented, if modest, predictive power in the academic literature — show essentially zero cross-sectional IC here. This is consistent with an index-constituent universe that is large-cap, liquid, and heavily arbitraged, where classic anomalies are weak or absent.

The practical implication is that a disciplined signal-selection process must audit *feature timing*, not just feature performance. A t-statistic of 89 should be treated as a red flag for leakage, not a discovery. Any live strategy should be built only from features whose information is fully available at the end of month t.

## Limitations
- We cannot inspect raw data or re-estimate ICs; all inference relies on the provided summary statistics.
- The panel is survivorship-constrained to index constituents and may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks.
- The `analyst_rev` leakage diagnosis rests on the data dictionary wording; the exact recording convention cannot be verified.
- Multiple testing across ten features is not formally adjusted; near-marginal signals (asset_growth, accruals) could be false positives or false negatives.
- Reported long-short returns ignore transaction costs, borrow costs, and capacity constraints, which would materially reduce any realizable return.
