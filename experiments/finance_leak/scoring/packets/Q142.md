# Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a 120-Month Panel: Profitability, Investment, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We examine ten stock characteristics in a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months to identify cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns. Gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility are robust return signals after accounting for statistical significance and data timing. Analyst forecast revision is exceptionally strong but is recorded contemporaneously with the target return and is therefore not a usable predictive signal. A long-short strategy that combines the three valid signals has an expected return of approximately 11.6% per year and an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.0 before costs.

## Methods
We use a point-in-time panel of 500 stocks per month for 120 months. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. For each feature, we compute the monthly cross-sectional rank IC between the feature and `ret_next`, average the rank IC over months, and compute a t-statistic as the monthly IC mean divided by its standard deviation times the square root of the number of months. We also form equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios each month; these buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of the feature.

A feature is considered a valid signal if its IC t-statistic is reliably different from zero and if the feature is available for portfolio formation at the end of month t. We reject features with weak IC t-statistics and features with timing or look-ahead problems. Because ten features are tested, we interpret marginal t-statistics cautiously, but the main conclusions are unchanged under standard multiple-testing thresholds.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics.

| feature | mean rank IC | IC t-stat | long-short monthly return | annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| analyst_rev | 0.2963 | 80.29 | 8.73% | 24.23 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 |

Three features have strong and statistically reliable rank ICs:

- **gross_prof**: positive signal; high gross profitability predicts higher next-month returns.
- **asset_growth**: negative signal; high asset growth predicts lower next-month returns.
- **idio_vol**: negative signal; high idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower next-month returns.

The `analyst_rev` feature has extremely strong univariate statistics: mean rank IC of 0.2963, t-stat of 80.29, positive IC in 100% of months, and a long-short monthly return of 8.73%. However, the data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is the forecast revision during month t+1 and is recorded at the end of month t+1. Since `ret_next` is also the return over month t+1, this feature uses information that is not available at the end of month t. Therefore, despite its strong in-sample association, it is not a valid predictive signal and is excluded from the strategy.

The remaining features—`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—have weak or statistically insignificant rank ICs and are not considered reliable signals in this sample.

## Discussion
The valid signals are economically consistent with prior research. High gross profitability is associated with higher future returns, consistent with profitability-based explanations of the cross-section of expected returns. High asset growth predicts lower returns, consistent with the investment effect: firms that grow assets aggressively tend to underperform. High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.

We propose a monthly rebalanced long-short strategy that equally combines three quintile-spread legs:

1. Long high `gross_prof`, short low `gross_prof`.
2. Long low `asset_growth`, short high `asset_growth`.
3. Long low `idio_vol`, short high `idio_vol`.

The average monthly long-short return of the three legs is approximately 0.96% per month, implying an expected annualized return of roughly 11.6% before costs. Assuming moderate positive correlation across the three long-short returns, the combined strategy is expected to deliver an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 2.0 before transaction costs. If the legs were uncorrelated, the Sharpe ratio would be higher; positive correlation among anomalies typically reduces diversification benefits.

## Limitations
- The performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, short-selling costs, and market impact.
- The sample contains 500 stocks per month, likely representing large and liquid index constituents, so results may not generalize to small-cap or illiquid stocks.
- The sample period is 120 months, which may not cover multiple full economic cycles.
- The strategy assumes the ability to form quintile portfolios at month-end and to short the bottom quintile without constraints.
- The strong `analyst_rev` result cannot be used in its current form because of look-ahead timing; a properly lagged analyst revision variable would be needed to test its predictive value.
- The expected Sharpe ratio of the combined strategy is approximate and depends on the correlation structure of the individual long-short returns.
