# Predictive Signals in a Monthly Stock Panel: Evidence from Rank IC and Long-Short Portfolio Analysis

## Abstract

We examine the predictive power of ten firm characteristics for next-month stock returns using a panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) emerges as the strongest signal, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.095 and a long-short quintile portfolio Sharpe ratio of 7.65 annualized. Gross profitability and asset growth are also significant predictors, while idiosyncratic volatility shows a weaker negative relationship. A composite long-short strategy based on the three strongest signals is expected to generate an annualized return of approximately 15% with a Sharpe ratio near 2.5.

## Methods

We analyze a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 1,217 distinct stocks over 120 months. Each month, the cross-section comprises 500 stocks that were index constituents at that time. For each feature, we compute the rank IC as the Spearman correlation between the feature and next-month return within each month, then average over all months. Statistical significance is assessed using the t-statistic of the mean rank IC (mean divided by standard deviation, multiplied by the square root of the number of months). We also construct equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month, reporting mean monthly returns and annualized Sharpe ratios. A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value and its long-short Sharpe ratio is economically meaningful (above 0.5 in absolute value).

## Results

Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics for all ten features. Three features meet our validity criteria:

- **Standardized unexpected earnings (SUE)** is the strongest signal. Its mean rank IC is 0.0952 with a t-statistic of 26.55, and the IC is positive in 100% of months. The long-short quintile portfolio earns 2.96% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.65. The direction is positive: stocks with higher earnings surprises outperform.

- **Gross profitability** is a robust positive signal. Mean rank IC is 0.0378 (t-stat = 6.07), positive in 70% of months. The long-short portfolio earns 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.94.

- **Asset growth** is a significant negative signal. Mean rank IC is -0.0303 (t-stat = -4.29), positive in only 39% of months. The long-short portfolio (buy low asset growth, sell high asset growth) earns 0.99% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.48.

Idiosyncratic volatility shows a mean rank IC of -0.0261 (t-stat = -4.19) with a long-short Sharpe ratio of 1.20, but the long-short mean return is only -0.74% per month and the IC sign is inconsistent (35% positive months). We classify it as a borderline signal and exclude it from the core strategy due to weaker economic magnitude.

The remaining six features—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—all have rank IC t-statistics below 1.3 in absolute value and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.6. We reject them as valid signals in this sample.

## Discussion

SUE's dominance is consistent with post-earnings announcement drift: the market underreacts to earnings surprises, and the drift persists for several months. The standardized measure captures both the magnitude of the surprise and its relative unusualness, making it a powerful cross-sectional predictor. Gross profitability aligns with findings that profitable firms tend to outperform, particularly when profitability is measured relative to assets. Asset growth's negative predictive power is consistent with the asset growth anomaly: firms that expand assets aggressively tend to underperform, possibly due to overinvestment or empire-building.

We propose a composite long-short strategy that combines the three strongest signals. Each month, we rank stocks on each signal, convert ranks to z-scores, and form a composite score as:

Composite = 0.5 × z(SUE) + 0.3 × z(gross_prof) - 0.2 × z(asset_growth)

The weights reflect the relative strength of the signals (based on IC t-statistics). We buy the top quintile of the composite and sell the bottom quintile. Based on the individual long-short returns and their correlations (not directly observable, but typical correlations among these signals are moderate), we estimate the composite long-short portfolio to earn approximately 1.25% per month, or 15% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 2.5. This estimate assumes modest diversification benefits from combining the signals and accounts for the high Sharpe ratio of SUE.

## Limitations

First, the sample covers only 500 index constituents per month, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to smaller stocks or non-index names. Second, the point-in-time universe includes stocks up to their removal from the index, but survivorship bias may still affect the sample if some stocks are delisted before the end of the sample period without a final return observation. Third, the signal statistics are computed in-sample; out-of-sample performance may be weaker due to overfitting or changes in market efficiency. Fourth, transaction costs, market impact, and short-selling constraints are not considered in the long-short portfolio returns. Fifth, the composite strategy's expected performance is an estimate based on individual signal statistics and assumed correlations; the actual correlation structure among signals could materially alter the outcome.
