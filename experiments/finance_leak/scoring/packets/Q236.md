# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a 500-Stock Index Panel: The Dominance of Analyst Revision and the Nullity of Firm Fundamentals

## Abstract

We examine ten firm characteristics for predicting next-month stock returns in a 120-month panel of 500 large-cap index constituents (60,000 stock-months). Only one feature, analyst consensus revision (`analyst_rev`), exhibits economically and statistically significant predictive power, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.296 (t = 89.6) and a long-short quintile spread of 8.66% per month (Sharpe ≈ 26). The nine accounting- and market-based features (book-to-market, gross profitability, size, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, R&D intensity) produce rank ICs within ±0.006 of zero and long-short spreads near zero, consistent with chance. We caution that `analyst_rev` is recorded contemporaneously with the return month (t+1), which raises a serious look-ahead concern that dominates the interpretation of our results. We conclude that within this universe and specification, fundamentals are essentially unpriced at the one-month horizon, and any apparent alpha is concentrated in a feature whose timing must be verified before it is tradable.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows — 500 index constituents per month for 120 months — with ten features and a one-month-ahead return target (`ret_next`). All features are stated to be point-in-time as of the end of month *t*, with one exception: `analyst_rev` is described as "the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month *t+1*," recorded at the end of month *t+1*. This timing is central to our analysis.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the supplied monthly rank IC (Spearman correlation between feature and `ret_next` across stocks within a month), averaged over 120 months, with the reported t-statistic = mean(IC)/sd(IC) × √120. We also use the reported equal-weighted long-short quintile spread and its annualized Sharpe. We adopt a threshold of |t| > 2 as the minimum bar for statistical significance, and require a consistent sign of IC across months (share of months with IC > 0 materially different from 50%) as a robustness check.

**Strategy construction.** For the single feature passing our screen, we form a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio buying the top quintile and shorting the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly. Expected performance is taken from the reported long-short statistics, with a haircut discussed below.

We deliberately avoid fitting new models on the 10 displayed sample rows, which are insufficient for estimation; we rely on the reported panel-wide signal statistics.

## Results

**Fundamental and market features are indistinguishable from noise.** The nine non-analyst features cluster tightly around zero:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | t-stat | L/S monthly | L/S Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| book_to_market | −0.0030 | −0.72 | −0.11% | −0.27 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| leverage | −0.0043 | −1.00 | −0.02% | −0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | −0.0002 | −0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 |

No feature exceeds |t| = 1.7. The best of these, `asset_growth` (t = 1.64, negative direction expected from the asset-growth anomaly but here *positive*), and `accruals` (t = 1.34, also opposite the expected negative sign), are not significant at conventional levels. The classic anomalies — value (book-to-market), profitability, size, accruals, low-volatility, leverage — are absent. The signs where nominally nonzero are frequently opposite to the well-documented literature, reinforcing that these are sampling fluctuations.

**Analyst revision dominates.** `analyst_rev` shows a mean rank IC of 0.2958 with an IC standard deviation of only 0.0362, giving t = 89.6. The IC is positive in **100% of the 120 months**. The long-short quintile portfolio returns 8.66% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 26.4. This is not a marginal signal; it is an order of magnitude larger than anything else in the panel and larger than typical published anomalies by a wide margin.

## Discussion

The results admit two readings, and the correct one hinges on data timing.

**Reading 1 — a genuine, exploitable signal.** If `analyst_rev` were known at the end of month *t* and predicted month *t+1* returns, it would represent one of the strongest cross-sectional signals ever documented, with an implausibly high Sharpe. A Sharpe of 26 is roughly 20–40× typical long-short equity strategies and is not consistent with an efficient or even semi-efficient market. This alone should trigger skepticism.

**Reading 2 — a timing artifact (most likely).** The data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is "the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month *t+1*, recorded at the end of month *t+1*." If the forecast revision occurs *during* the same month over which `ret_next` is measured, the feature and the target overlap in time. Analysts revise forecasts in response to news, and returns move with news. The near-perfect IC (100% positive months, t = 90) is exactly the signature of a contemporaneous, mechanically related variable, not a forecast. Under this reading, `analyst_rev` is **not tradable** as specified — an investor at the end of month *t* cannot observe a revision that happens in month *t+1*.

We therefore treat `analyst_rev` as a valid *statistical* predictor of the return it is aligned with, but flag that its tradability is unproven and probably nil under the stated timing. We report it as a valid signal in the JSON because it is the only feature with real predictive association, while making the caveat explicit in the strategy description and limitations.

**Economic interpretation of the null results.** For a large-cap index universe, the absence of value, size, profitability, and accrual effects at the one-month horizon is not entirely surprising: these anomalies are weaker in mega-caps, are better measured at annual horizons, and are arbitraged more aggressively in index constituents. The near-zero ICs also suggest the panel may be a relatively efficient, survivor-biased sample — though the dictionary explicitly retains delisted names through their removal month, mitigating survivorship bias.

## Limitations

- **Look-ahead contamination of the headline signal.** `analyst_rev` is recorded in month *t+1*, contemporaneous with the target return; its apparent alpha is very likely a timing artifact and not investable as specified.
- **No transaction costs or capacity analysis.** The long-short spreads are gross; turnover, borrow costs, and market impact are unmodeled, and would materially reduce net returns, especially for a high-turnover monthly strategy.
- **Single specification.** We rely on the supplied rank ICs and quintile portfolios; no multivariate regression, neutralization (e.g., against size or sector), or interaction analysis was possible from the summary alone.
- **Short sample and single universe.** 120 months and 500 large-cap names per month limit generalization to small-caps, other markets, or other periods; anomaly strength is known to vary over time.
- **No multiple-testing adjustment reported.** With ten features tested, some apparent significance could arise by chance, though our threshold of |t| > 2 and the extreme t = 90 for `analyst_rev` make this a minor concern for the conclusions.
- **Sign reversals vs. literature.** Several features (asset growth, accruals) show signs opposite to published anomalies, which we attribute to noise but cannot fully diagnose without the raw data.
