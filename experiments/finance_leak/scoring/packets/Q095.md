# Cross-Sectional Equity Return Signals in a Survivorship-Biased Index Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using a panel of up to 500 index stocks over 120 months. Rank IC tests identify gross profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, and accruals as statistically significant signals. A quintile long-short composite that buys high gross profitability, low idiosyncratic volatility, high leverage, and high accruals is estimated to earn about 7.4% annually before costs with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.4. The remaining five characteristics do not clear the significance threshold. Results are likely inflated by survivorship bias and exclude transaction costs.

## Methods
The dataset contains 34,387 stock-month observations from months 1 to 120, with up to 500 distinct stocks. For each month, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between each feature and next-month return, then average these rank ICs over the 120 months. The t-statistic is calculated as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation and multiplied by the square root of the number of months. We pre-specify a significance threshold of |t| > 2.0.

We also construct equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios. Each month, stocks are sorted into quintiles on each feature; the long-short return buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile. For the composite strategy, each of the four valid signals is converted to a cross-sectional percentile rank, signed so that higher values indicate higher expected returns, and averaged. The strategy then buys the top composite quintile and sells the bottom composite quintile, rebalanced monthly.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Valid Signal | Direction |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| book_to_market | 0.0029 | 0.54 | No | — |
| gross_prof | 0.0293 | 3.83 | Yes | Positive |
| log_mktcap | -0.0043 | -0.67 | No | — |
| asset_growth | -0.0164 | -1.87 | No | — |
| accruals | 0.0130 | 2.06 | Yes | Positive |
| idio_vol | -0.0266 | -3.76 | Yes | Negative |
| leverage | 0.0137 | 2.56 | Yes | Positive |
| turnover | 0.0042 | 0.64 | No | — |
| rd_intensity | 0.0018 | 0.30 | No | — |

Four features are statistically significant. Gross profitability has the strongest positive rank IC (0.0293, t = 3.83), followed by idiosyncratic volatility with a negative rank IC (-0.0266, t = -3.76). Leverage (0.0137, t = 2.56) and accruals (0.0130, t = 2.06) are also positive and significant. The long-short quintile portfolios for these four signals earn monthly returns of 0.88%, -0.78%, 0.50%, and 0.31%, respectively, before costs. A composite rank strategy using these four signals is estimated to earn approximately 0.62% per month, or 7.4% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 1.4.

## Discussion
The positive gross profitability signal is consistent with a quality-based return premium: firms with higher gross profit relative to assets tend to deliver higher subsequent returns. The negative idiosyncratic volatility signal is consistent with the low-risk anomaly, where high idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower future returns. The positive leverage signal suggests that, in this sample and period, higher leverage is rewarded, possibly reflecting a distress risk premium or a sample-specific effect. The positive accruals signal is unusual relative to the classic accrual anomaly, but we follow the data as provided and do not impose an external sign prior.

A composite strategy combining these four signals is more diversified than any single signal and is therefore likely to have a higher Sharpe ratio than the individual long-short portfolios, assuming signal returns are not perfectly correlated.

## Limitations
The sample includes only stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, creating survivorship bias that likely inflates performance. All performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, short-selling costs, and capacity constraints. The signal selection and composite weights are estimated in-sample, so out-of-sample performance is likely to be lower. The panel is unbalanced, with only 139 stocks in the first month, which may make early-month rank estimates noisier. Finally, financial statement variables are based on the latest annual report available and may be stale or revised after the fact.
