# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: Evidence from Earnings Surprise, Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We analyze a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 1,217 index constituents over 120 months. Using monthly Spearman rank information coefficients and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we identify four statistically robust return signals: standardized unexpected earnings (SUE), gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility. SUE is the dominant signal, with a mean rank IC of 0.0952, a t-statistic of 26.55, and positive IC in 100% of months. We propose an equal-weighted composite long-short strategy that buys high-SUE, high-gross-profitability, low-asset-growth, and low-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks and sells the opposite. The strategy has an estimated gross monthly return of 1.46%, or about 19% annualized, with an expected Sharpe ratio near 4.0 before transaction costs.

## Methods
The dataset contains one row per stock per month, with 500 stocks in each of 120 months. The target is `ret_next`, the stock return over month \(t+1\). I evaluate each feature using its average monthly Spearman rank IC with `ret_next`, its IC t-statistic, and the mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio of an equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio. The long-short portfolio buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of each feature; negative ICs imply that the profitable aligned trade is long the bottom quintile and short the top quintile.

I classify a signal as valid if its IC t-statistic is economically and statistically significant, using a rough threshold of \(|t| > 2\), and if the sign is interpretable. I then form a composite signal by ranking each valid feature cross-sectionally each month, sign-aligning them so that higher composite scores predict higher returns, averaging the ranks, and forming top-quintile long / bottom-quintile short portfolios. Expected performance is estimated from the average of the individual aligned long-short mean returns. Because the full covariance matrix is not provided, the composite Sharpe ratio is approximated assuming modest average pairwise signal correlation around 0.2. All estimates are gross of transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact.

## Results
The individual signal results are summarized below. Four features pass the validity screen.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short mean monthly return | Annualized Sharpe | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `sue` | 0.0952 | 26.55 | 2.96% | 7.65 | Valid, positive |
| `gross_prof` | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 | Valid, positive |
| `asset_growth` | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 | Valid, negative |
| `idio_vol` | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 | Valid, negative |
| `book_to_market` | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 | Rejected |
| `log_mktcap` | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 | Rejected |
| `accruals` | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 | Rejected |
| `leverage` | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 | Rejected |
| `turnover` | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 | Rejected |
| `rd_intensity` | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 | Rejected |

SUE is by far the strongest signal: its IC is positive in every month, and its long-short portfolio earns 2.96% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.65. Gross profitability is also robust and positive. Asset growth has a negative relation, meaning high-growth firms underperform; the aligned trade is long low-asset-growth stocks and short high-asset-growth stocks. Idiosyncratic volatility is similarly negative, so low-volatility stocks outperform high-volatility stocks.

For the aligned composite, the average of the four valid long-short mean returns is:
\[
(2.96\% + 1.16\% + 0.99\% + 0.74\%) / 4 = 1.46\% \text{ per month}.
\]
This corresponds to about 17.6% simple annualized return, or approximately 19.0% compounded annualized return. Assuming modest positive correlation among the signals, the composite annualized Sharpe ratio is approximately 4.0.

## Discussion
The results support a multi-signal long-short strategy. SUE captures post-earnings-announcement drift or a related earnings-surprise effect and is the clear primary alpha source. Gross profitability captures a quality/profitability premium. Low asset growth reflects the investment anomaly: firms that expand assets aggressively tend to earn lower subsequent returns. Low idiosyncratic volatility reflects the well-documented low-volatility anomaly. The remaining features—book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not show reliable cross-sectional predictive power in this panel.

A practical strategy is to rank stocks each month on the four valid dimensions, align the signs, average the ranks, and trade the top versus bottom quintile. The strategy would be dollar-neutral, equal-weighted within each leg, and rebalanced monthly. The expected gross return is about 19% annualized, with an expected Sharpe ratio near 4.0. A SUE-only strategy would have a higher estimated Sharpe ratio, but the composite spreads risk across several economically distinct effects and reduces reliance on a single signal.

## Limitations
- The analysis is based on summary statistics and does not incorporate transaction costs, borrow fees, slippage, or market impact.
- The universe is limited to 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to microcaps or non-index stocks.
- The composite assumes equal weights and an approximate correlation structure; the true covariance matrix of the signals is not available.
- Monthly rebalancing may generate high turnover, especially for the SUE signal, which could materially reduce net performance.
- The 120-month sample may be subject to regime dependence and data-mining bias, and the SUE effect may be sensitive to exact earnings-announcement timing.
