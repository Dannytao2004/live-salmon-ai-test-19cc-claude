# Cross-Sectional Equity Return Prediction in a Monthly Panel of 500 Stocks

## Abstract
We evaluate nine stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using a panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Four signals are statistically robust at the conventional single-test threshold: gross profitability and leverage are positive predictors, idiosyncratic volatility is a negative predictor, and accruals is a weaker positive predictor. A composite top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile strategy based on these signals is estimated to produce a gross annual return of about 7% with an annualized Sharpe ratio around 1.2. The remaining five characteristics do not pass the significance threshold.

## Methods
The dataset contains 34,387 stock-month observations. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month, then average over the 120 months. The reported t-statistic is the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation and multiplied by sqrt(120). A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic has absolute value above 2.0. We also inspect equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature.

## Results
Four features pass the |t|>2 threshold:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short mean monthly return | Direction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `gross_prof` | 0.0293 | 3.83 | 0.88% | Positive |
| `idio_vol` | -0.0266 | -3.76 | -0.78% | Negative |
| `leverage` | 0.0137 | 2.56 | 0.50% | Positive |
| `accruals` | 0.0130 | 2.06 | 0.31% | Positive |

The rejected features are `book_to_market` (t=0.54), `log_mktcap` (t=-0.67), `asset_growth` (t=-1.87), `turnover` (t=0.64), and `rd_intensity` (t=0.30). `asset_growth` is borderline but does not meet the pre-specified significance threshold.

## Discussion
The positive signal for `gross_prof` is consistent with the profitability literature. The negative signal for `idio_vol` is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly. The positive relation for `leverage` may reflect a risk premium in this sample. The positive `accruals` coefficient is weak and directionally opposite to the traditional accruals anomaly, suggesting caution. Combining the four valid signals into a monthly composite score—long high gross profitability, high accruals, low idiosyncratic volatility, and high leverage—yields an estimated gross long-short return near 7% annualized, with an estimated Sharpe ratio of about 1.2 assuming modest signal correlations.

## Limitations
The sample covers only 500 index constituents with possible survivorship and backfill bias. The analysis does not incorporate transaction costs, short-sale constraints, or liquidity filters. Signal correlations are not provided, so the composite Sharpe estimate is approximate. The accruals direction is inconsistent with prior literature and may be sample-specific.
