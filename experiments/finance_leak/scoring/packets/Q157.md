# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a 500-Stock Index Panel: Evidence from Ten Firm Characteristics

## Abstract

We evaluate ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents (60,000 stock-months). Only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) exhibits a robust predictive relationship, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.0998 (t = 23.25) and a long-short quintile spread of 3.04% per month (annualized Sharpe 7.3). No other characteristic produces an economically or statistically meaningful signal; the remaining nine features have |IC t-stats| below 1.7 and long-short spreads under 0.3% per month. We conclude that the panel is dominated by a single earnings-momentum effect, and propose a long-short strategy concentrated in SUE.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 500 index constituents per month for 120 months (60,000 rows, 1,213 unique stocks). Returns include the final month before index removal, so the sample embeds delisting/distress returns and is free of survivorship selection after removal. Features are point-in-time: accounting variables come from the latest annual report available at month-end t, SUE from the most recent announced quarter (announced in the month after quarter-end), and market variables (log_mktcap, idio_vol, turnover) are measured at month-end t. The target is the total return over month t+1.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the supplied within-month Spearman rank IC averaged across 120 months, its t-statistic (mean/σ × √120), the sign-consistency rate (months with IC > 0), and the equal-weighted top-minus-bottom quintile long-short return and annualized Sharpe. We treat a signal as valid only if it shows both statistical reliability (|t| ≳ 3) and economic magnitude after the near-zero cost assumption implied by the summary statistics.

**Strategy construction.** For the surviving signal we form a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio: long the top quintile, short the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly at month-end using only information available at t.

SUE is the only feature with a decisive signal. Its IC is positive in 99% of months, implying near-monotonic cross-sectional ordering, and the 3.04% monthly quintile spread compounds to roughly 43% annually before costs. The other nine features cluster around zero: none reaches |t| = 2, and most long-short spreads are within a few basis points of zero. Notably, the classic value (book_to_market) and profitability (gross_prof) signals are weak or slightly negative here, and investment (asset_growth) and accruals show only marginal, statistically insignificant tilts in their conventional directions. The magnitude gap between SUE and everything else (t = 23 vs. next-best t = 1.6) suggests the panel was constructed to isolate an earnings-surprise effect.

## Discussion

The results are consistent with a strong post-earnings-announcement-drift (PEAD) mechanism: SUE's IC is stable across essentially every month, which is unusual for a real-world signal and indicates that the drift is not arbitraged away in this universe. Because SUE is announced before month t+1 begins and the universe is liquid index constituents, the signal is implementable without look-ahead bias. The near-zero ICs elsewhere are plausibly explained by the index-constituent universe: large, liquid names have already had well-documented anomalies (value, profitability, low volatility) arbitraged or neutralized, whereas earnings surprise retains its information content because it reflects genuine fundamental news. We therefore recommend a concentrated SUE-based long-short strategy and reject the remaining nine features as standalone signals.

## Limitations

- The 7.3 Sharpe and 99% IC sign-consistency are implausibly high for a real market and suggest the panel may be synthetic or that SUE was engineered to be the dominant signal.
- Rank IC and long-short returns are reported gross of transaction costs, borrow fees, and slippage, which would materially reduce a monthly-rebalanced strategy.
- The universe is restricted to 500 index constituents, so findings may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks.
- We cannot inspect factor correlations, sector exposures, or time variation in signal strength from the summary statistics alone.
- Delisting returns are included only through the final index month; returns after removal are unobserved, which may bias extreme losers.
