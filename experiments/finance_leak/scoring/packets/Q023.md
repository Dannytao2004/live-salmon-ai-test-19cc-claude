# Cross-Sectional Equity Return Signals: Quality, Investment, and Low-Volatility in a Point-in-Time Panel

## Abstract
We analyze a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months to identify ex ante cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns. Using rank information coefficients and equal-weighted quintile spreads, we find gross profitability is a robust positive signal, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are robust negative signals. Analyst revision, despite having the largest apparent signal, is not ex ante because it is measured during the return month. A composite long-short strategy based on the three valid signals has an expected gross annual return of about 11.5% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 2.1.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations for 500 stocks over 120 months. The prediction target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. For each feature, we evaluate cross-sectional predictive power using the monthly rank IC, defined as the Spearman correlation between the feature at the end of month t and `ret_next`, averaged over all 120 months. We compute t-statistics as mean IC divided by IC standard deviation times the square root of 120. We also examine equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile each month.

A feature is considered a valid signal only if it is known at the end of month t and has a rank IC t-statistic with absolute value above 2.0. We require economic consistency and robust long-short performance. One feature, `analyst_rev`, is measured during month t+1 and recorded at the end of month t+1, so it is not available when forming a portfolio at the end of month t and is excluded as an ex ante predictor.

## Results
Three features pass the validation criteria. `gross_prof` has a mean rank IC of 0.0378 with a t-statistic of 6.07 and positive IC in 70% of months. Its top-minus-bottom quintile long-short portfolio earns 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94. `asset_growth` has a mean rank IC of -0.0303 with a t-statistic of -4.29; high asset growth predicts lower returns. Reversing the high-minus-low portfolio gives a long-short return of 0.99% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.48. `idio_vol` has a mean rank IC of -0.0261 with a t-statistic of -4.19; low idiosyncratic volatility predicts higher returns. Reversing the high-minus-low portfolio gives a long-short return of 0.74% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.20.

The remaining features are not reliable predictors. `book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` all have rank IC t-statistics below 1.3 in absolute value and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.6. `analyst_rev` has an extremely high rank IC of 0.2963 and t-statistic of 80.29, but this reflects contemporaneous information from month t+1 and therefore is not a valid ex ante signal.

## Discussion
The three valid signals have clear economic interpretations. Gross profitability captures the quality premium: more profitable firms tend to earn higher subsequent returns. Asset growth captures the investment effect: firms that grow assets aggressively tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility captures the low-volatility anomaly: high-idiosyncratic-risk stocks tend to earn lower subsequent returns.

We propose a monthly rebalanced equal-weighted long-short strategy based on a composite rank of `gross_prof` positively, `asset_growth` negatively, and `idio_vol` negatively. Averaging the three directional long-short returns gives an expected monthly return of approximately 0.96%, or about 11.5% annualized before costs. Taking into account diversification among the three signals, we estimate an expected annualized Sharpe ratio of about 2.1.

## Limitations
The analysis is based on a single 500-stock index universe over 120 months, so the results may not generalize to other markets or time periods. The reported performance is gross of transaction costs, short-selling costs, and implementation frictions. We do not have full correlation estimates among the signal portfolios, so the composite Sharpe ratio is an approximation. The feature set is limited to ten characteristics, and other relevant predictors may be omitted. Multiple testing is modest but present; the three valid signals have strong t-statistics, reducing but not eliminating this concern.
