# Predictive Signals in a Monthly Cross-Section of Stocks: Evidence from a 120-Month Panel

## Abstract

We examine the cross-sectional predictive power of ten firm characteristics for next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 stocks per month. Among the characteristics, standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) exhibits a remarkably strong and consistent positive rank correlation with future returns (mean rank IC 0.0998, t-stat 23.25), while all other features show economically negligible and statistically insignificant associations. A long-short quintile portfolio based on SUE generates an average monthly return of 3.04% with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. We conclude that SUE is the only robust return predictor in this dataset, and discuss implications for post-earnings announcement drift and the construction of quantitative equity strategies.

## Methods

We analyze a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations spanning 120 months, with exactly 500 stocks per month drawn from a broad equity index. The dataset includes ten firm characteristics and the one-month-ahead total return (`ret_next`). All features are point-in-time, using the latest available annual or quarterly data as of month-end.

For each feature, we compute the rank information coefficient (rank IC) as the Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks within each month, then average over all 120 months. We report the mean rank IC, its standard deviation, a t-statistic computed as mean / (std / sqrt(120)), the percentage of months with positive IC, and the performance of an equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the feature each month.

To assess economic significance, we use conventional thresholds: a feature is considered a valid signal if its mean rank IC has an absolute t-statistic above 2.0 (approximately the 95% confidence level) and its long-short portfolio has an annualized Sharpe ratio above 0.5. Features failing these thresholds are rejected.

## Results

Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics. Among the ten features, only `sue` (standardized unexpected earnings) demonstrates a statistically and economically significant relationship with future returns. Its mean rank IC is 0.0998 with a t-statistic of 23.25, and the long-short quintile portfolio returns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. The IC is positive in 99% of months, indicating remarkable consistency.

All other features have mean rank ICs near zero, with absolute t-statistics below 2.0 and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.75. Specifically:

- `book_to_market` has a slightly negative IC (-0.0030, t = -0.72), inconsistent with the classic value premium.
- `gross_prof` shows a weak positive IC (0.0039, t = 0.93).
- `log_mktcap` is essentially unrelated to returns (IC = 0.0001, t = 0.02).
- `asset_growth` has a modest positive IC (0.0061, t = 1.64) and a long-short Sharpe of 0.72.
- `accruals` has a positive IC (0.0055, t = 1.34) and Sharpe of 0.70, but the economic magnitude is small and the t-statistic is below conventional significance.
- `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` all show negligible ICs and Sharpe ratios.

Given the strong performance of SUE, we construct a simple trading strategy: each month, rank stocks by `sue`, go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly. Based on the observed long-short mean monthly return of 3.04% and assuming no transaction costs, the strategy would generate an annualized return of approximately 36.5% (3.04% × 12) before costs. The annualized Sharpe ratio is 7.31, though this is likely inflated by the absence of transaction costs and the equal-weighting scheme.

## Discussion

The dominance of SUE as a return predictor is consistent with the well-documented post-earnings announcement drift (PEAD) anomaly. Stocks with positive earnings surprises tend to continue outperforming, while those with negative surprises continue underperforming, as the market slowly incorporates earnings information into prices. The magnitude of the rank IC (0.0998) is exceptionally high relative to typical cross-sectional signals, which usually exhibit monthly rank ICs of 0.02–0.05. This suggests either a particularly strong PEAD effect in this sample or a data construction that emphasizes earnings surprise information.

The absence of predictive power for other well-known anomalies (value, profitability, size, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, R&D intensity) is notable. This could reflect the specific sample period, the index membership constraint, or the use of point-in-time annual data that may not align perfectly with the return month. For example, `book_to_market` uses annual book equity, which may be stale for some firms, and the index universe may exclude the small-cap stocks where many anomalies are strongest.

The strategy based solely on SUE suggests a potentially profitable trading rule, but the high Sharpe ratio of 7.31 is almost certainly overstated due to the absence of transaction costs, bid-ask spreads, and capacity constraints. In practice, trading the top and bottom quintiles of 500 stocks monthly would incur significant turnover and market impact, especially in the most extreme earnings-surprise stocks, which are often small and illiquid.

## Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the dataset covers a single 120-month period and a specific index universe, limiting the generalizability of the findings. Second, we do not account for transaction costs, short-selling constraints, or market impact, which would substantially reduce the net returns of the SUE long-short strategy. Third, the sample is limited to 500 stocks per month, which may exclude smaller stocks where anomalies are typically stronger. Fourth, the rank IC and Sharpe ratio for SUE are unusually high, raising the possibility of look-ahead bias or data snooping in the construction of the SUE variable, despite the stated point-in-time methodology. Fifth, we do not examine interactions among signals or control for risk factors beyond a simple long-short portfolio.
