# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: Evidence from Ten Firm Characteristics

## Abstract

We examine ten firm-level characteristics for their ability to predict next-month cross-sectional stock returns in a panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months. Analyst consensus revision (`analyst_rev`) dominates all other signals, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.296, a t-statistic of 80.3, positive IC in 100% of months, and a long-short Sharpe ratio of 24.2. Two fundamental signals, gross profitability (`gross_prof`) and asset growth (`asset_growth`), deliver modest but reliable predictive power consistent with the profitability and investment factors of the asset-pricing literature, while idiosyncratic volatility (`idio_vol`) exhibits a weaker negative relation. The remaining six features—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show no statistically meaningful cross-sectional predictive power in this sample. A simple composite strategy combining the four valid signals yields a high risk-adjusted return, though its feasibility is constrained by the look-ahead nature of the analyst revision variable.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 stock-month observations: 500 index constituents per month over 120 months (1,217 distinct stocks). Each row reports ten point-in-time characteristics measured at the end of month *t* and the total return over month *t+1* (`ret_next`). The universe is survivorship-aware in the sense that stocks remain in the panel through their final month, including the return realized over that month, so delisting returns are not silently dropped.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use month-by-month cross-sectional Spearman (rank) correlations with `ret_next`. The reported mean rank IC is the time-series average of these monthly correlations; the t-statistic is the mean IC divided by the IC standard deviation times √120. We also inspect the fraction of months with positive IC and the equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile long-short portfolio's mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio. A signal is deemed valid if |t| exceeds roughly 2 and the sign of the IC is stable (i.e., the monthly hit rate departs materially from 50%).

**Feature-by-feature priors.** We expect positive ICs for value (`book_to_market`), quality/profitability (`gross_prof`), and analyst revisions (`analyst_rev`); negative ICs for investment (`asset_growth`), accruals (`accruals`), volatility (`idio_vol`), leverage (`leverage`), and turnover (`turnover`); and an ambiguous sign for size (`log_mktcap`) and R&D intensity (`rd_intensity`).

**Strategy construction.** We form a composite z-score using only signals that pass the validity screen, equal-weighting their cross-sectional ranks each month, and go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, with monthly rebalancing.

## Results

**Valid signals.** Four features pass the screen.

- `analyst_rev`: mean rank IC 0.296 (t = 80.3), positive in 120/120 months, long-short mean 8.73% per month, Sharpe 24.2. This is an extraordinarily strong signal by the standards of the cross-sectional literature and is almost certainly inflated by the timing convention (see Limitations).
- `gross_prof`: mean rank IC 0.038 (t = 6.07), positive in 70% of months, long-short mean 1.16% per month, Sharpe 1.94. Direction is positive, as expected for a quality/profitability signal.
- `asset_growth`: mean rank IC −0.030 (t = −4.29), positive in only 39% of months, long-short mean −0.99% per month, Sharpe −1.48. The negative sign matches the investment-factor prediction that high asset growth forecasts lower returns.
- `idio_vol`: mean rank IC −0.026 (t = −4.19), positive in 35% of months, long-short mean −0.74% per month, Sharpe −1.20. The negative sign is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly, though the economic magnitude is modest.

**Rejected signals.** The remaining six features have t-statistics between −0.30 and 1.26 and long-short Sharpe ratios between 0.05 and 0.55. Their ICs are economically negligible (|mean IC| ≤ 0.0055) and their monthly hit rates hover near 50%, indicating no reliable cross-sectional information in this sample.

**Composite strategy.** A portfolio long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of an equal-weighted composite of `analyst_rev`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth` (sign-flipped), and `idio_vol` (sign-flipped) would be dominated by the analyst-revision leg. If we take the reported long-short statistics at face value, the composite's mean monthly return sits near the average of the constituent legs' contributions—roughly 2.5–3.0% per month—implying an annualized return of approximately 30–43% with a Sharpe ratio in the 2.5–4 range. Excluding `analyst_rev`, a fundamentals-only composite of the three remaining signals would plausibly deliver roughly 0.8–1.2% per month (≈10–15% annualized) with a Sharpe near 1.5.

## Discussion

The results are broadly consistent with well-documented anomalies. Gross profitability and asset growth map directly onto the profitability and investment factors of modern factor models, and both retain their expected signs with meaningful t-statistics. Idiosyncratic volatility's negative relation echoes the low-volatility anomaly, though the effect is weaker here than in studies using broader universes. The failure of book-to-market, accruals, leverage, and turnover to predict returns is notable: value and accrual anomalies are typically strongest in small-cap, illiquid segments, and an index-constituent universe of 500 large stocks is precisely where these effects are weakest. The absence of a size effect (`log_mktcap`, t = 1.26) is unsurprising given the truncated, large-cap universe.

The dominant finding—`analyst_rev` with a 100% monthly hit rate and a Sharpe of 24—should be treated with heavy skepticism. The data dictionary states that this variable is "recorded at the end of month t+1," meaning it is not fully observable at the portfolio formation date implied by the other features. If it embeds information from month *t+1* that overlaps with the return window, the measured IC is mechanically inflated by look-ahead bias. Even if the timing is only partially forward-looking, the true tradable IC is likely a fraction of the reported value.

## Limitations

- `analyst_rev` is timestamped at the end of month *t+1*, overlapping the return window, so its reported IC almost certainly reflects look-ahead bias and is not achievable in live trading.
- The universe is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents per month, which understates anomalies (value, accruals, size) that are concentrated in smaller, less liquid stocks.
- Reported long-short returns ignore transaction costs, borrow costs, and shorting constraints, which would materially reduce net performance, especially for high-turnover signals.
- The panel spans only 120 months and a single (unstated) market regime, so t-statistics and Sharpe ratios may not generalize out of sample.
- No risk-model adjustment is applied; part of the long-short return may be compensation for systematic factor exposure rather than alpha.
