# Cross-Sectional Equity Return Predictability in a Monthly Stock Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate nine firm characteristics for predicting next-month stock returns using a monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Monthly rank ICs and equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios show that gross profitability, accruals, and leverage positively predict returns, while idiosyncratic volatility negatively predicts returns. A composite long-short strategy that equal-weights these four signals is expected to earn roughly 7.5% annually with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.9. The remaining five characteristics—book-to-market, log market cap, asset growth, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not provide statistically reliable return prediction in this sample. The positive accruals sign is contrary to the standard accrual anomaly and warrants caution.

## Methods
The panel contains 34,387 stock-month observations for 500 stocks over 120 months. For each stock-month, nine characteristics are observed at month-end \(t\), and the target is the total return over month \(t+1\). We assess predictive power using the average monthly Spearman rank correlation between each characteristic and next-month returns (rank IC), the associated t-statistic across 120 months, and an equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of each characteristic each month.

A signal is considered valid if its mean rank IC is statistically distinguishable from zero at conventional levels (absolute t-statistic above approximately 2) and if its long-short portfolio delivers an economically meaningful return. For the trading strategy, we form a composite score by equal-weighting the standardized cross-sectional ranks of the valid signals, reversing the sign of signals with negative ICs. The composite portfolio is long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly.

## Results
Four signals are statistically reliable.

| Signal | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gross_prof | positive | 0.0293 | 3.83 | 0.88% | 1.19 |
| accruals | positive | 0.0130 | 2.06 | 0.31% | 0.50 |
| idio_vol | negative | -0.0266 | -3.76 | -0.78% | -1.15 |
| leverage | positive | 0.0137 | 2.56 | 0.50% | 0.99 |

Gross profitability is the strongest single signal, with a mean rank IC of 0.0293 and a long-short Sharpe of 1.19. Idiosyncratic volatility is the strongest negative signal: high-volatility stocks underperform low-volatility stocks, and the reversed long-short strategy earns 0.78% monthly with a Sharpe of 1.15. Leverage and accruals are weaker but statistically positive.

Five signals are rejected. Book-to-market has a near-zero rank IC (0.0029, t = 0.54) and a slightly negative long-short return. Log market cap has a rank IC of -0.0043 (t = -0.67). Asset growth is marginally negative (rank IC = -0.0164, t = -1.87) but falls short of conventional significance. Turnover (rank IC = 0.0042, t = 0.64) and R&D intensity (rank IC = 0.0018, t = 0.30) show no reliable predictive power.

A composite strategy using the four valid signals equal-weighted would have an estimated monthly long-short return of about 0.62%, or roughly 7.5% annualized. Assuming moderate diversification across signals, the expected annualized Sharpe ratio is approximately 1.9.

## Discussion
The results are broadly consistent with quality and low-risk anomalies: profitable firms outperform, and low-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks outperform high-volatility stocks. Leverage positively predicts returns, which may reflect a risk premium or distress-related compensation rather than a pure quality effect. Accruals positively predict returns in this sample, opposite to the usual accrual anomaly; this could be sample-specific, related to the index-constituent universe, or driven by the particular 120-month period. The rejected signals do not show sufficient consistency to justify inclusion in a systematic strategy.

## Limitations
The universe consists of current index constituents, creating survivorship bias and potentially overstating performance. The analysis is in-sample and does not account for transaction costs, shorting costs, or capacity constraints. The 120-month sample is relatively short for validating monthly signals. Signal correlations are not modeled, so the composite Sharpe estimate may be optimistic if the signals are positively correlated. Annual accounting variables may be stale or subject to reporting lags, and the positive accruals sign is contrary to prior literature, suggesting the need for out-of-sample confirmation.
