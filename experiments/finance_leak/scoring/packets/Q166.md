# Cross-Sectional Equity Return Predictors in a 120-Month Index Panel

## Abstract
We analyze a monthly panel of 34,387 stock-month observations covering 500 stocks over 120 months to identify cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns. Four of nine characteristics—gross profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, and accruals—have statistically significant rank ICs. A composite long-short strategy that buys high gross profitability, high leverage, high accruals, and low idiosyncratic volatility, and sells the opposite, is estimated to earn about 7.4% annually with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.4 before transaction costs. The remaining characteristics, including book-to-market, size, asset growth, turnover, and R&D intensity, are not reliable signals in this sample.

## Methods
The dataset consists of one row per stock per month from month 1 to month 120. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. Nine features are considered: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

For each feature, we evaluate predictive power using the monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next`, averaged across all 120 months. The t-statistic is computed as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation and multiplied by sqrt(120). We also compute an equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the feature each month. A feature is treated as a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic is at least 2.0 in absolute value and its long-short return has the same sign as the rank IC.

To form the composite strategy, each month we standardize the four valid signals cross-sectionally and compute:
\[
\text{score} = z(\text{gross\_prof}) + z(\text{leverage}) + z(\text{accruals}) - z(\text{idio\_vol})
\]
Stocks are sorted on this composite score. The strategy buys the top quintile, sells the bottom quintile, and rebalances monthly.

## Results
The table below summarizes the valid signals.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | 0.0293 | 3.83 | 0.88% | 1.19 |
| `idio_vol` | -0.0266 | -3.76 | -0.78% (top-minus-bottom) | -1.15 |
| `leverage` | 0.0137 | 2.56 | 0.50% | 0.99 |
| `accruals` | 0.0130 | 2.06 | 0.31% | 0.50 |

For `idio_vol`, the sign is negative: high idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower next-month returns. The profitable low-minus-high quintile spread is therefore +0.78% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.15.

The remaining five features are rejected. `book_to_market` has a rank IC of 0.0029 with a t-statistic of 0.54. `log_mktcap` has a rank IC of -0.0043 with a t-statistic of -0.67. `asset_growth` has a negative rank IC of -0.0164 but a t-statistic of only -1.87, below our significance threshold. `turnover` and `rd_intensity` have rank ICs near zero with t-statistics of 0.64 and 0.30, respectively.

Combining the four valid signals into an equal-weighted composite long-short portfolio gives an estimated monthly spread of approximately 0.62%, or about 7.4% annualized. Under an assumed average pairwise correlation of 0.3 among the four characteristic portfolios, the composite annualized Sharpe ratio is approximately 1.4.

## Discussion
Gross profitability is the strongest positive signal, consistent with the quality-profitability literature. Idiosyncratic volatility is the strongest negative signal, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly. Leverage is a positive predictor in this sample, which is less standard and may reflect a distress-risk or value-related premium. Accruals is positive but only marginally significant and contrary to the traditional accruals anomaly; this may be due to the specific accruals definition or sample period.

The composite strategy diversifies across four relatively distinct signals, producing a higher expected Sharpe ratio than any single signal in isolation. However, these performance estimates are in-sample and gross of transaction costs.

## Limitations
- The universe is constructed from stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, which may introduce survivorship and backfill biases.
- Performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, and quintile rebalancing may generate meaningful turnover.
- Multiple testing is not fully corrected; the accruals signal in particular is only marginally significant.
- The analysis assumes linear monotonic relationships captured by rank IC and quintile sorts, and may miss nonlinear effects.
