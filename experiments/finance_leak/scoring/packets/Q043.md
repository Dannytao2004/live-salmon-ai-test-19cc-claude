# Report Q043

# Signal Evaluation in a Monthly Equity Panel: The Dominance of Analyst Revisions and the Importance of Point-in-Time Alignment

---

## Abstract

We evaluate ten candidate stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns in a 60,000-row panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months. Only one feature—analyst consensus revision (`analyst_rev`)—exhibits a strong, reliable cross-sectional relation with `ret_next`, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.296, a t-statistic of 89.6, and positive IC in 100% of months. All nine other features have rank ICs below 0.007 in absolute value and t-statistics under 1.7, consistent with noise. Critically, `analyst_rev` is recorded at the end of month t+1, meaning it is contemporaneous with the return it appears to predict; this is a point-in-time leakage that invalidates it as a tradeable ex-ante signal. An equal-weighted long-short strategy on `analyst_rev` shows a large in-sample spread (8.66%/month, annualized Sharpe 26.4), but this is not implementable. We conclude that, under strict point-in-time discipline, no feature in this panel is a credible standalone predictor, and we flag the leakage as the central methodological finding.

---

## 1. Methods

### 1.1 Data

The panel contains 60,000 stock-month observations: 500 index constituents per month across 120 months, with 1,213 distinct stocks. Each row reports ten characteristics measured at or before month-end *t*, and the target `ret_next` is the total return over month *t+1*. The universe is point-in-time: stocks removed from the index remain in the data through their removal month, including the return over their final month, which mitigates survivorship bias but introduces delisting-return effects.

### 1.2 Signal validity criteria

We classify a feature as a **valid signal** only if it satisfies all of the following:

1. **Timing/availability**: the feature must be observable at the end of month *t*, before `ret_next` is realized. A feature recorded during or after month *t+1* cannot be used to trade month *t+1* returns.
2. **Economic plausibility**: a documented risk premium or behavioral mechanism should support the sign of the relation.
3. **Statistical reliability**: |mean rank IC| should be meaningfully positive with a t-statistic large enough to reject zero (we use a rough threshold of |t| ≥ 2), and the sign should be reasonably stable across months.

### 1.3 Evaluation metrics

We use the reported per-month Spearman rank IC (averaged over 120 months), its t-statistic, the fraction of months with positive IC, and the equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile (long-short) mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

### 1.4 Strategy construction

For any feature passing the validity screen, we form a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio: long the top quintile, short the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly. Expected annual return is approximated as 12 × mean monthly long-short return; expected Sharpe uses the reported annualized long-short Sharpe. We do not assume transaction costs in the headline numbers but discuss them in Limitations.

### 2.2 The analyst revision anomaly

`analyst_rev` stands apart: its rank IC (0.296) is roughly 50 times larger than the next-best feature, and its t-statistic of 89.6 is implausible for a genuine return predictor. A long-short portfolio earns 8.66% per month with a Sharpe of 26.4—far beyond what is achievable in liquid equity markets. This pattern is the signature of **look-ahead bias**: the data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is "change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, recorded at the end of month t+1." It is thus contemporaneous with `ret_next`, not predictive of it. The near-perfect IC and 100% positive-month rate confirm the feature is essentially a proxy for the realized return itself.

### 2.3 The remaining features

The other nine features produce rank ICs in the range −0.004 to +0.006, with t-statistics between −1.0 and +1.6. None clears a conventional significance bar. The two largest in magnitude—`asset_growth` (+0.006, t = 1.64) and `accruals` (+0.0055, t = 1.34)—are directionally consistent with the asset-growth and accruals anomalies (low growth and low accruals predict higher returns), but here the signs are *positive* for both, i.e., the opposite of the classic anomalies, and the magnitudes are economically trivial. `book_to_market` is slightly negative (−0.003), inconsistent with the value premium. `log_mktcap` and `turnover` are effectively zero. `idio_vol`, `leverage`, and `rd_intensity` are indistinguishable from noise.

### 2.4 Strategy

Because no feature survives the point-in-time screen, the only "strategy" with material in-sample performance—long-short on `analyst_rev`—is not implementable. We therefore report it as a diagnostic rather than a recommendation.

---

## 3. Discussion

The central finding is methodological: **the strongest apparent signal in this panel is an artifact of point-in-time misalignment.** The data dictionary is explicit that `analyst_rev` is measured over month *t+1*, yet it is presented alongside features measured at month *t*. Any backtest that treats it as known at month-end *t* will produce spectacular but fictitious results. This is a textbook example of why feature availability dates must be audited before signal evaluation, and why implausibly high ICs and Sharpes should be treated as red flags rather than discoveries.

Among the genuinely point-in-time features, none is a reliable standalone predictor. This is not surprising: the panel contains only 500 large-cap index constituents per month, a segment where many classic anomalies are weakest. The value, profitability, and investment signals that work in broad cross-sections of small and mid-cap stocks are attenuated or absent in a large-cap universe. The signs of `asset_growth` and `accruals` are also opposite to the canonical anomalies, which could reflect the large-cap sample, the short 120-month window, or noise.

A practical implication is that single-feature long-short portfolios on these characteristics would not be viable after costs. The long-short spreads for the non-leaking features are 0.01%–0.29% per month—well below plausible round-trip transaction costs for monthly rebalancing. Any realistic strategy would need to combine multiple weakly informative features and control turnover.

---

## 4. Limitations

- We cannot inspect the raw data or re-estimate ICs; all conclusions rely on the provided summary statistics.
- The `analyst_rev` leakage is inferred from the data dictionary and the implausible IC magnitude; we cannot rule out that a subset of the feature is genuinely lagged.
- The 120-month sample and 500-stock large-cap universe limit generalizability to broader or longer samples.
- Transaction costs, borrow costs, and capacity are not modeled; reported long-short returns are gross.
- Multiple-testing concerns apply: with ten features, some marginal t-statistics (e.g., `asset_growth` at 1.64) could be spurious.
- The panel's point-in-time construction retains delisting returns, which may affect the tail behavior of `ret_next` but is not separately analyzed here.
