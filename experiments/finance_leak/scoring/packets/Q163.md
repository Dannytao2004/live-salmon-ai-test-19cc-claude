# Cross-Sectional Stock Return Signals: Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
This study evaluates nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns in a point-in-time monthly panel of 500 broad equity index constituents over 120 months. Only three signals are statistically reliable: gross profitability positively predicts returns, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility negatively predict returns. A composite long-short strategy based on these three signals is estimated to earn about 18% per year with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.8 before transaction costs. The remaining six characteristics—including book-to-market, size, and accruals—show no reliable predictive power in this sample.

## Methods
The dataset is a balanced monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months, totaling 60,000 stock-month observations. For each characteristic, I compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with next-month returns within each month. I report the mean rank IC, its standard deviation, and a t-statistic based on 120 monthly observations. I also construct equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each characteristic each month.

A feature is treated as a valid signal if its monthly rank IC t-statistic exceeds approximately 2.0 in absolute value and its long-short return has the same sign with an economically meaningful annualized Sharpe ratio.

## Results
The table below summarizes the main evidence.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | Monthly IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 | Valid |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 | Valid |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 | Valid |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 | Rejected |
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 | Rejected |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 | Rejected |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 | Rejected |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 | Rejected |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 | Rejected |

The three valid signals are interpretable. High gross profitability predicts higher future returns, consistent with a quality-profitability premium. High asset growth predicts lower future returns, consistent with overinvestment or overvaluation effects. High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower future returns, consistent with limits to arbitrage or lottery-demand explanations.

For a composite strategy, I convert the three valid signals into cross-sectional percentile ranks each month and define a composite score:

\[
\text{score} = \text{pct\_rank}(gross\_prof) - \text{pct\_rank}(asset\_growth) - \text{pct\_rank}(idio\_vol)
\]

Buying the top composite quintile and selling the bottom composite quintile yields an estimated monthly long-short return of approximately 1.5%, corresponding to about 18% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.8 before costs.

## Discussion
The evidence supports a parsimonious three-factor view of cross-sectional stock returns in this sample: profitability, investment growth, and idiosyncratic volatility. Notably, several widely studied predictors—such as book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not show significant rank ICs here. This may reflect the sample construction, which is restricted to a 500-stock broad index universe, or the specific 10-year period.

The composite long-short strategy is attractive in economic terms, but the reported performance is gross of trading costs. Because the strategy rebalances monthly and uses quintile spreads, turnover and short-selling costs may materially reduce live returns.

## Limitations
- Performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, short-selling fees, and market impact.
- The sample covers 120 months and a 500-stock index universe, so the results may not generalize to other markets or periods.
- The composite signal weights are not optimized; the reported expected return and Sharpe are illustrative rather than out-of-sample forecasts.
- Annual-report-based fundamentals are subject to point-in-time reporting lag and possible restatement risk.
- The valid signals may be correlated, so incremental value beyond the strongest standalone signal is uncertain without further correlation analysis.
