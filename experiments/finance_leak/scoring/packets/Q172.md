# Predictive Signals in a Monthly Equity Panel: A Rank IC Analysis

**Abstract**

This study investigates the cross-sectional predictive power of nine firm characteristics for next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 stocks. Rank IC analysis identifies leverage as the only statistically significant signal (mean rank IC = 0.0164, t-stat = 2.74, p < 0.01), with high-leverage stocks outperforming low-leverage stocks. Asset growth and accruals show economically meaningful but statistically marginal positive rank ICs (0.0055 and 0.0059, respectively), while the remaining features exhibit no reliable predictive content. A long-short quintile portfolio based on leverage delivers an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92 and a monthly return of 0.52%. We conclude that leverage is a robust positive predictor of returns in this sample, though the economic significance is moderate and warrants further validation.

## Methods

We analyze a monthly stock panel containing 34,708 stock-month observations across 120 months and 500 distinct stocks. The target variable is next-month total return (`ret_next`). For each of nine features, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month and average it over all months to obtain the mean rank IC. The IC t-statistic is computed as mean / standard deviation × √(number of months). We also compute the equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio return and its annualized Sharpe ratio for each feature. A feature is considered a valid signal if its IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 (approximately p < 0.05) and its long-short portfolio exhibits a positive Sharpe ratio.

## Results

Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics. Leverage is the only feature with a statistically significant mean rank IC (0.0164, t = 2.74, p < 0.01), indicating a positive cross-sectional relationship: stocks with higher leverage tend to have higher next-month returns. The long-short quintile portfolio sorted on leverage earns 0.52% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. Asset growth (IC = 0.0055, t = 1.16) and accruals (IC = 0.0059, t = 1.03) show positive but statistically insignificant ICs; their long-short portfolios earn 0.37% and 0.24% per month, respectively. All other features—book-to-market, gross profitability, log market cap, idiosyncratic volatility, turnover, and R&D intensity—have ICs near zero (absolute t-statistics < 0.3) and negligible long-short returns, indicating no reliable predictive power in this sample.

## Discussion

The positive and significant rank IC for leverage is consistent with the interpretation that leverage captures exposure to systematic risk or distress risk that is rewarded in the cross-section. High-leverage firms may be more sensitive to economic conditions, and investors may demand higher expected returns for bearing that risk. The magnitude of the leverage long-short return (0.52% monthly, 0.92 Sharpe) is economically meaningful but modest compared to well-documented anomalies such as value or momentum. The lack of significance for book-to-market and gross profitability is notable, as these are often strong predictors in the broader literature; this may reflect the specific index-constituent universe, the sample period, or the use of rank IC rather than portfolio sorts. The weak positive ICs for asset growth and accruals are directionally consistent with prior findings (asset growth negatively predicts returns in some studies, but here the sign is positive), suggesting that these effects may be sample-specific or confounded by other characteristics.

## Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the sample consists only of stocks that are constituents of a broad equity index at the end of month 120, introducing survivorship bias; stocks that were delisted or removed from the index before month 120 are excluded, which may distort signal estimates. Second, the 120-month period is relatively short, and the results may be driven by specific market regimes. Third, we evaluate each feature in isolation; multivariate analysis is needed to determine whether leverage's predictive power is incremental to other characteristics. Fourth, the use of rank IC and quintile portfolios does not account for transaction costs, which may erode the profitability of a leverage-based strategy. Finally, the data are provided as summary statistics and sample rows only; we cannot verify data quality or handle outliers in a granular way.
