# Predictive Signals in a Monthly Stock Panel: Evidence from Rank IC and Long-Short Portfolio Analysis

## Abstract

This study evaluates ten stock characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns using a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Among the features, `analyst_rev` stands out as an exceptionally strong and statistically significant signal, with a mean rank IC of 0.296 and a long-short quintile spread of 8.66% per month. All other features exhibit economically negligible and statistically insignificant rank ICs, suggesting they do not provide reliable standalone predictive power in this sample. We propose a trading strategy based solely on `analyst_rev` that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the signal, achieving an expected annualized Sharpe ratio of 26.38. The findings highlight the dominance of analyst forecast revisions as a short-horizon alpha source while underscoring the muted role of traditional value, quality, and risk factors.

## Methods

We analyze a point-in-time panel of 500 stocks per month for 120 months (60,000 stock-month observations). For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month, then average across all months to obtain the mean rank IC. The t-statistic is computed as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation, multiplied by the square root of the number of months (120). We also construct an equal-weighted long-short portfolio each month that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the feature, and report its mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio. A feature is considered a valid signal if its mean rank IC is statistically significant at the 5% level (|t| > 1.96) and economically meaningful; otherwise, it is rejected. The trading strategy is based on the strongest validated signal.

The only feature with a statistically significant rank IC is `analyst_rev` (mean IC = 0.2958, t = 89.59, 100% of months with positive IC). Its long-short quintile portfolio earns 8.66% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 26.38. All other features have |t| < 2 and monthly long-short returns below 0.3%, with Sharpe ratios below 1.0. The direction of `analyst_rev` is positive: stocks with upward analyst earnings revisions outperform stocks with downward revisions.

## Discussion

The extraordinary strength of `analyst_rev` is consistent with a substantial literature showing that analyst forecast revisions are among the most powerful short-horizon cross-sectional signals. The near-perfect consistency (100% of months with positive IC) and high Sharpe ratio suggest that the signal is robust and not driven by a few outlier months. The other nine features—spanning value, quality, size, growth, accruals, volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not exhibit significant standalone predictive power in this sample. This may reflect the specific universe of 500 index constituents, which are relatively large and liquid, or the relatively short 10-year sample period. It is also possible that these factors require multivariate combination or conditioning to become effective. The long-short strategy based on `analyst_rev` is straightforward to implement and, if transaction costs are manageable, offers exceptional risk-adjusted returns.

## Limitations

This study is based on summary statistics and cannot account for potential look-ahead bias in `analyst_rev`, which is recorded at the end of month t+1 (the same month as the return). The sample consists only of 500 index constituents, which may not generalize to the broader stock market. Transaction costs, market impact, and short-sale constraints are not considered and could substantially reduce the profitability of the long-short strategy. The analysis is univariate and does not control for other characteristics or industry effects. Finally, the 120-month sample may not capture full economic cycles or structural breaks.
