# Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility as Cross-Sectional Return Predictors

## Abstract
We examine nine stock characteristics in a monthly panel of 500 broad-index stocks over 120 months. Rank IC tests show that gross profitability is a reliable positive predictor of next-month returns, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are reliable negative predictors. The other six characteristics, including book-to-market and market capitalization, do not pass standard significance thresholds. A composite quintile long-short strategy based on the three validated signals is estimated to earn approximately 11.6% gross annual return with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.1. These estimates are gross of transaction costs and implementation frictions.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations: 500 stocks per month for 120 months. The target is next-month total return, `ret_next`. For each of the nine features, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank IC between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, then average across months. The reported t-stat is the average IC divided by its standard deviation, scaled by the square root of the number of months.

We use a multiple-testing-aware significance threshold of approximately `|t| > 3`. This is close to a Bonferroni correction for nine tests at the 5% level. We also examine long-short returns for equal-weighted top-minus-bottom quintile portfolios.

## Results
| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Direction | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | 0.0378 | 6.07 | positive | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | -0.0303 | -4.29 | negative | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | -0.0261 | -4.19 | negative | -0.74% | -1.20 |

`gross_prof` is a positive signal: high gross profitability predicts higher next-month returns. `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` are negative signals: high asset growth and high idiosyncratic volatility predict lower next-month returns.

The remaining features have rank ICs close to zero and t-statistics well below 3:
`book_to_market` (t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` (t = 1.26), `accruals` (t = 0.36), `leverage` (t = -0.30), `turnover` (t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` (t = -0.10). We therefore do not treat them as valid standalone signals.

### Proposed strategy
Each month, rank stocks by a composite score:

\[
\text{score} = z(\text{gross_prof}) - z(\text{asset_growth}) - z(\text{idio_vol})
\]

where \(z(\cdot)\) is a cross-sectional z-score. The strategy buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile, equal-weighted. When approximated as an equal-weight combination of the three validated long-short legs, the expected gross monthly return is about 0.96%, or 11.6% annualized. Assuming an average pairwise correlation of about 0.3 between the three signal legs, the expected annualized Sharpe ratio is approximately 2.1.

## Discussion
The results are economically consistent with prior asset-pricing research. Gross profitability is a well-known quality signal: profitable firms tend to earn higher average returns. Asset growth captures an investment effect: firms that grow assets aggressively tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility is associated with the low-volatility anomaly: high-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks underperform risk-adjusted benchmarks.

The composite strategy diversifies across three distinct return premia: quality, investment, and low volatility. Because these signals are likely not perfectly correlated, combining them improves the expected Sharpe ratio relative to any single signal.

## Limitations
- Performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, short-sale financing, and market impact; net performance would be lower.
- The composite Sharpe estimate depends on assumed cross-signal correlations, which are not fully observable from the summary statistics.
- The analysis does not control for all known factor exposures or time-varying macroeconomic regimes.
- The sample covers index constituents, so index membership and delisting effects may affect the estimated premia.
