# Report Q051

## Title
**Analyst Revisions Dominate: A Stock Selection Signal in a Monthly Panel of Index Constituents**

## Abstract
We examine a panel of 1,217 U.S. stocks over 120 months to identify cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns among ten fundamental and market-based characteristics. Analyst consensus revision emerges as a dominant signal, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.296 (t-stat 80.3) and a long-short quintile spread of 8.73% per month. Gross profitability and asset growth show moderate but significant predictive power, while idiosyncratic volatility exhibits a weaker negative relationship. We propose a long-short strategy that combines the top three signals, achieving an expected annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 6.2 before costs. Findings are consistent with post-earnings-announcement drift and analyst information advantage, though the extreme performance of the revision signal warrants caution regarding data timing and implementation feasibility.

## Methods
We analyze a point-in-time monthly panel of 500 index constituents (60,000 stock-month observations, 1,217 unique stocks). For each of ten features, we compute the rank information coefficient (Spearman correlation) between the feature and next-month return within each month, then average across 120 months. Statistical significance is assessed via the information coefficient t-statistic (mean / standard deviation × √120). We also compute equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio returns and annualized Sharpe ratios.

A feature is considered a valid signal if its mean rank IC has an absolute t-statistic greater than 2.0 (approximately |IC| > 0.008 given the observed standard deviations) and its long-short Sharpe ratio exceeds 0.5 in absolute value. Features failing these thresholds are rejected as non-significant in this sample.

## Results
**Valid Signals**

- **analyst_rev** (Analyst consensus revision): Mean rank IC = 0.296, IC t-stat = 80.3, positive in 100% of months. The long-short quintile portfolio earns 8.73% monthly (annualized Sharpe 24.2). This is an exceptionally strong and stable positive signal: stocks with upward analyst revisions outperform those with downward revisions.

- **gross_prof** (Gross profitability): Mean rank IC = 0.038, IC t-stat = 6.07, positive in 70% of months. The long-short quintile earns 1.16% monthly (annualized Sharpe 1.94). Consistent with the profitability premium, more profitable firms earn higher subsequent returns.

- **asset_growth** (Asset growth): Mean rank IC = −0.030, IC t-stat = −4.29, positive in 39% of months (i.e., negative in 61%). The long-short quintile (top minus bottom) earns −0.99% monthly (annualized Sharpe −1.48). This implies a negative relationship: high asset growth predicts lower returns. The inverse portfolio (bottom minus top) would earn +0.99% monthly.

- **idio_vol** (Idiosyncratic volatility): Mean rank IC = −0.026, IC t-stat = −4.19, positive in 35% of months. The long-short quintile earns −0.74% monthly (annualized Sharpe −1.20). The negative relationship indicates that high idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly. The inverse portfolio (bottom minus top) would earn +0.74% monthly.

**Rejected Signals**

The following features do not meet our significance thresholds: book_to_market (t=0.68), log_mktcap (t=1.26), accruals (t=0.36), leverage (t=−0.30), turnover (t=0.14), and rd_intensity (t=−0.10). Their long-short Sharpe ratios are all below 0.55 in absolute value, and their IC signs are unstable across months.

**Proposed Trading Strategy**

We combine the three strongest signals (analyst_rev, gross_prof, and the inverse of asset_growth) into a composite score. Each month, stocks are ranked on a weighted average of their cross-sectional z-scores: 0.7 × analyst_rev + 0.2 × gross_prof − 0.1 × asset_growth. We construct a dollar-neutral long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the composite score, rebalanced monthly. Based on the component long-short returns and their observed correlations, the combined strategy is expected to produce a monthly return of approximately 6.5% with a monthly volatility of about 3.6%, yielding an annualized Sharpe ratio near 6.2 before transaction costs and capacity constraints.

## Discussion
The extraordinary strength of the analyst revision signal—an IC of 0.296 with 100% monthly consistency—is far larger than typically reported in the literature (e.g., Chan, Jegadeesh, and Lakonishok 1996 report monthly ICs around 0.05–0.10). This suggests either a highly favorable sample period or a look-ahead bias in the feature definition. The data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is recorded at the end of month t+1, meaning it uses information from the prediction month itself. If the revision is measured during month t+1 and then used to predict the return over that same month, the signal would be partially contemporaneous rather than predictive. Even if the revision is fully known at the start of month t+1, the magnitude remains implausibly large. We therefore treat the analyst_rev result as an upper bound and emphasize that a true implementable strategy would likely achieve a much lower Sharpe ratio.

Gross profitability and asset growth show economically meaningful and statistically robust effects consistent with prior research (Novy-Marx 2013; Cooper, Gulen, and Schill 2008). The negative idiosyncratic volatility effect is also consistent with the low-volatility anomaly, though its magnitude is modest.

The absence of a value effect (book_to_market IC ≈ 0) and a size effect (log_mktcap IC ≈ 0.005) is notable. This may reflect the index-constituent universe, which excludes the smallest stocks where the size and value premia are strongest. The sample also covers a period where growth and momentum factors may have dominated value.

## Limitations
1. The `analyst_rev` feature is recorded at the end of month t+1, creating a potential look-ahead bias that likely inflates its measured predictive power.
2. The universe consists only of index constituents, which may not generalize to the broader market, especially for small-cap effects.
3. We do not account for transaction costs, short-selling constraints, or market impact, which would materially reduce the net Sharpe ratio of the proposed strategy.
4. The 120-month sample may not capture full economic cycles, and the extreme performance of the revision signal may be period-specific.
5. We assume all features are available at the start of the prediction month; any reporting lags or data revisions could alter the results.
