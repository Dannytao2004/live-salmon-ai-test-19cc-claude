# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Monthly Index Panel: Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We evaluate nine stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Monthly rank ICs and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios identify three statistically reliable signals: gross profitability is positively related to future returns, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negatively related. A composite long-short strategy that buys profitable, low-growth, low-volatility stocks and shorts their opposites is estimated to earn roughly 11.5% annualized gross return with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.1. The remaining six characteristics show weak, unstable, and statistically insignificant evidence.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations: 120 months, 500 stocks per month, and 1,217 distinct stocks. The universe is point-in-time, with stocks remaining in the panel through their removal from the index, including their final-month return. The target is `ret_next`, the stock’s total return over month t+1.

For each feature, I use the reported monthly Spearman rank IC between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks. The mean IC is averaged over 120 months, and the t-statistic is `mean IC / std IC × sqrt(120)`. I also use the reported equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, which buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of the feature each month. For features with negative ICs, the economically profitable direction is reversed: long the bottom quintile and short the top quintile.

I classify a feature as a valid signal if it has a large absolute mean rank IC, a high absolute t-statistic, and consistent long-short performance. A composite strategy is formed by ranking stocks each month on gross profitability positively, asset growth negatively, and idiosyncratic volatility negatively. The strategy buys the top quintile of the composite score and shorts the bottom quintile, equal-weighting within each leg and rebalancing monthly.

## Results
Three features are valid signals.

**Gross profitability** is the strongest positive signal. Its mean rank IC is 0.0378 with t-statistic 6.07, and it is positive in 70% of months. The top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.94. This supports the profitability premium: more profitable firms outperform less profitable firms.

**Asset growth** is a strong negative signal. Its mean rank IC is -0.0303 with t-statistic -4.29, and the feature has a positive IC in only 39% of months, meaning high asset growth underperforms in most months. The top-minus-bottom quintile return is -0.99% per month, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of -1.48. The profitable direction is therefore long low-asset-growth stocks and short high-asset-growth stocks, yielding roughly +0.99% per month and Sharpe +1.48.

**Idiosyncratic volatility** is also a strong negative signal. Its mean rank IC is -0.0261 with t-statistic -4.19, and it has a positive IC in only 35% of months. The top-minus-bottom quintile return is -0.74% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of -1.20. The profitable direction is long low-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks and short high-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks, yielding roughly +0.74% per month and Sharpe +1.20.

The remaining features are rejected. `book_to_market` has mean IC 0.0027 (t=0.68) and long-short Sharpe 0.23. `log_mktcap` has mean IC 0.0055 (t=1.26) and Sharpe 0.55. `accruals` has mean IC 0.0015 (t=0.36) and Sharpe 0.36. `leverage` has mean IC -0.0011 (t=-0.30) and Sharpe 0.05. `turnover` has mean IC 0.0006 (t=0.14) and Sharpe 0.06. `rd_intensity` has mean IC -0.0004 (t=-0.10) and Sharpe 0.32. None reaches conventional statistical significance, and their economic magnitudes are small.

For the composite strategy, the three directional component long-short returns average approximately 0.96% per month, or 11.6% annualized gross. The individual annualized Sharpe ratios are 1.94, 1.48, and 1.20. If the component returns were independent, the equal-weight composite Sharpe could be about 2.6; with moderate positive correlation among the signals, a more conservative estimate is around 2.1. Thus I estimate the composite strategy at roughly 11.5% annualized gross return and annualized Sharpe 2.1.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with well-known cross-sectional regularities. Gross profitability captures a quality/profitability premium. Asset growth is consistent with an investment or overvaluation effect: firms expanding their asset bases tend to earn lower subsequent returns. Idiosyncratic volatility captures a low-volatility or lottery-demand effect: high-volatility stocks underperform. Together, these three signals provide complementary information and support a diversified long-short composite.

The rejected signals may still matter in other samples, universes, or specifications. For example, book-to-market is a classic value proxy, but in this index-constituent panel over 120 months it does not predict returns reliably. Accruals show a positive but insignificant IC, which is contrary to the usual accrual-anomaly direction and may reflect the specific sample or accounting timing. Turnover and leverage are essentially flat. R&D intensity and market cap are also weak in this summary.

The composite strategy should be interpreted as a gross, simplified estimate. It does not account for transaction costs, shorting constraints, sector neutrality, or capacity. The estimates also rely on the provided summary statistics rather than direct portfolio construction on the raw panel.

## Limitations
The sample covers only 120 months and index constituents, so results may not generalize to broader universes or other periods. The analysis is gross of transaction costs, borrow fees, and market impact, which could materially reduce net performance. The signals are not sector- or risk-neutralized, so industry, size, or other common-factor exposures may explain part of the return. Annual accounting variables may be stale and affected by reporting lags or restatements not visible in the summary. The t-statistics assume independently distributed monthly ICs and do not adjust for multiple testing across nine features. Correlations among the three valid signals are not directly observable, so the composite Sharpe estimate carries uncertainty.
