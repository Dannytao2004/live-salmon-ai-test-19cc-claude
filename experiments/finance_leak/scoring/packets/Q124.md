# Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a 120-Month Index Panel: Profitability, Investment, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We examine nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Three signals are statistically significant: gross profitability is positively related to future returns, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negatively related. A composite quintile long-short strategy based on these three signals is expected to earn about 11.6% annually with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.0 before transaction costs. The remaining six features, including book-to-market and size, do not show reliable predictive power. The results are consistent with profitability, investment, and low-risk anomalies.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations for 1,217 distinct stocks, with exactly 500 index constituents in each of 120 months. For each feature, we compute the monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with next-month returns (`ret_next`) and average it over the 120 months. The t-statistic is computed as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation and multiplied by the square root of 120.

We also form equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month by sorting stocks on each feature. The long-short return is the top quintile return minus the bottom quintile return. Annualized Sharpe ratios are computed as the square root of 12 times the monthly mean long-short return divided by the monthly standard deviation.

For the proposed strategy, we combine the three significant signals by averaging each stock’s cross-sectional ranks on `gross_prof`, `-asset_growth`, and `-idio_vol`. We then buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile. Expected performance is estimated from the individual long-short portfolios, assuming moderate correlation among the three signal portfolios.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | Valid, positive |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | Valid, negative |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | Valid, negative |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | Rejected |
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | Rejected |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | Rejected |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | Rejected |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | Rejected |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | Rejected |

The three valid signals are economically meaningful in quintile spreads. `gross_prof` has a top-minus-bottom monthly return of 1.16% with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94. `asset_growth` has a top-minus-bottom monthly return of -0.99%, implying a low-minus-high return of 0.99% with an annualized Sharpe of 1.48. `idio_vol` has a top-minus-bottom monthly return of -0.74%, implying a low-minus-high return of 0.74% with an annualized Sharpe of 1.20.

Combining these three signals equally gives an expected monthly long-short return of approximately (1.16% + 0.99% + 0.74%) / 3 = 0.963%, or 11.6% annualized. Assuming an average pairwise correlation of about 0.3 among the signal long-short portfolios, the expected annualized Sharpe ratio is approximately 2.0.

## Discussion
The positive premium for gross profitability is consistent with the profitability anomaly. The negative premium for asset growth is consistent with the investment effect, where high asset growth firms tend to underperform. The negative premium for idiosyncratic volatility supports the low-risk anomaly. These three signals appear to capture complementary dimensions of expected returns.

Notably, book-to-market and size do not show significant univariate predictive power in this index-constituent sample. This may reflect the sample’s focus on relatively large index members, the limited 10-year window, or crowding among value and size factors in this universe.

The proposed composite strategy is implementable with monthly rebalancing. It combines profitability, investment, and low-volatility tilts into a single long-short portfolio.

## Limitations
- The estimated returns are before transaction costs, market impact, and short-sale constraints.
- The sample contains only 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to smaller stocks or the full equity universe.
- The composite Sharpe estimate assumes moderate correlation among the three signals and is not an out-of-sample test.
- Financial statement features may be stale relative to fiscal period ends, even though they are point-in-time at month end.
