# Leverage as the Dominant Cross-Sectional Return Predictor in a Monthly Equity Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate nine stock characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns using a monthly panel of 34,708 stock-month observations from 500 broad-index constituents. Only `leverage` shows a statistically reliable rank IC: mean 0.0164, t-stat 2.74, positive in 59% of months. The remaining eight characteristics have near-zero mean rank ICs and t-statistics well below conventional significance thresholds. A monthly equal-weighted long-short quintile strategy on leverage earns 0.52% per month, or about 6.24% annualized before costs, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. The evidence suggests limited robust cross-sectional predictability in this panel, with leverage the sole credible signal.

## Methods
The dataset contains one row per stock per month for months 1 through 120, covering 500 stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120. Features are measured at the end of month \(t\), and the target `ret_next` is the total return over month \(t+1\). This setup avoids look-ahead bias in signal construction, though the universe definition introduces survivorship concerns discussed below.

For each feature, I use the provided monthly Spearman rank IC between the feature and `ret_next`, averaged across all 120 months. The reported t-statistic is the mean IC divided by the cross-month IC standard deviation, multiplied by \(\sqrt{120}\). I also use the provided equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio returns, which buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month. A feature is treated as a valid signal only if its mean rank IC has a t-statistic above roughly 2 in absolute value and the sign is consistent with economically meaningful cross-sectional spread returns.

## Results
The signal evaluation is summarized below.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Assessment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| leverage | 0.0164 | 2.74 | 0.52% | Valid positive signal |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | 0.37% | Not statistically reliable |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | 0.24% | Not statistically reliable |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | 0.13% | Not statistically reliable |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | -0.07% | No reliable size effect |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | 0.12% | No reliable profitability effect |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | -0.06% | No reliable R&D effect |
| book_to_market | -0.0017 | -0.28 | -0.05% | No reliable value effect |
| turnover | -0.0005 | -0.10 | 0.13% | No reliable turnover effect |

`leverage` is the only feature with a t-statistic above 2. Its positive mean rank IC indicates that stocks with higher leverage tend to have higher next-month returns in this sample. The long-short portfolio earns 0.52% per month, and the annualized Sharpe ratio is 0.92. The IC is positive in 59% of months, which is modestly above a coin flip but consistent with a weak, noisy signal.

Several features have positive point estimates but do not meet conventional significance thresholds. `asset_growth`, `accruals`, and `idio_vol` have t-statistics of 1.16, 1.03, and 0.86, respectively. Their long-short returns are also small relative to their likely transaction costs and estimation uncertainty. The remaining features—`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—have mean rank ICs very close to zero and t-statistics far below 2.

## Discussion
The central result is that `leverage` is the only characteristic in this panel that provides a statistically credible cross-sectional return signal. The positive direction is notable because some asset-pricing priors would imply the opposite: high leverage can proxy for financial distress, and distressed firms may underperform or be subject to short-sale constraints. Here, however, the data show that high-leverage stocks outperformed low-leverage stocks on a rank-IC and quintile-spread basis over the sample.

A practical strategy is therefore to go long the top quintile of `leverage` and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, with monthly rebalancing. Based on the provided long-short statistics, this strategy would have earned approximately 0.52% per month, or 6.24% annualized before transaction costs, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. After realistic trading costs, borrow costs, and implementation frictions, the net return would likely be lower, especially because the strategy requires shorting.

The other features should be rejected as standalone signals in this panel. Their mean rank ICs are economically tiny, their t-statistics are far from conventional significance, and their long-short returns do not provide compelling evidence of reliable predictive power. This does not mean these characteristics are irrelevant in all settings; it means that in this particular monthly index-constituent panel, they do not survive even modest statistical scrutiny.

## Limitations
The sample covers only 120 months and 500 index constituents, so the results may be period-specific and not representative of the broader market. The universe is defined by stocks that remain in the index at the end of month 120, which introduces survivorship bias and may overstate observed predictability. The leverage signal is the only one with t-statistic above 2, but with nine tested features the result is borderline under strict multiple-testing corrections. Transaction costs, shorting costs, slippage, and capacity constraints are not modeled, so the stated strategy performance is gross rather than net. The features are mostly annual-accounting-based and may be stale for up to a year. The analysis relies on equal-weighted quintile portfolios and simple rank ICs, without controls for sector, size, beta, or other risk exposures. Finally, the positive leverage-return relation may reflect the specific sample composition, index inclusion rules, or a risk premium that is not stable out of sample.
