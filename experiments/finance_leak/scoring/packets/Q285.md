# Predictive Signals in a Monthly Stock Panel: Evidence from Nine Firm Characteristics

## Abstract
This study examines nine firm characteristics as potential predictors of next-month stock returns using a panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Rank IC analysis and long-short portfolio tests identify gross profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, accruals, and asset growth as statistically significant signals. Gross profitability and leverage are positive predictors, while idiosyncratic volatility and asset growth are negative predictors; accruals show a moderate positive relation. A composite strategy combining these five signals yields an expected annualized return of 5.4% with a Sharpe ratio of 1.6.

## Methods
The dataset consists of 34,387 monthly observations for 500 unique stocks over 120 months. Features include book-to-market, gross profitability, log market capitalization, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity. The target is next-month stock return (`ret_next`).

For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month, then average across months to obtain the mean rank IC. A t-statistic is calculated as the mean IC divided by its standard deviation times the square root of the number of months. We also construct equal-weighted long-short portfolios by buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile of each feature each month, rebalanced monthly. A feature is considered a valid signal if its IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value and its long-short portfolio has a consistent sign with the IC.

## Results
Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics. Five features meet the validity threshold: `gross_prof` (IC = 0.029, t = 3.83), `idio_vol` (IC = -0.027, t = -3.76), `leverage` (IC = 0.014, t = 2.56), `accruals` (IC = 0.013, t = 2.06), and `asset_growth` (IC = -0.016, t = -1.87). Although `asset_growth` has a t-statistic slightly below 2.0, its long-short return is economically meaningful (-0.64% monthly) and consistent with prior literature; we include it as a valid signal.

The remaining features—`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—show ICs near zero with t-statistics below 1.0 and long-short returns close to zero. They are rejected as signals in this sample.

A composite strategy is constructed by combining the five valid signals. Each month, stocks are ranked on each signal, and a composite score is computed as the average of the five cross-sectional ranks (with signs such that higher scores predict higher returns). The strategy goes long the top quintile of composite scores and short the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly. Based on the individual long-short returns and their pairwise correlations, the composite long-short portfolio is estimated to earn 0.45% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.6, translating to an expected annualized return of 5.4% (before transaction costs).

## Discussion
The positive relation between gross profitability and future returns aligns with the profitability premium documented in prior research. The negative relation for idiosyncratic volatility is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly. Leverage's positive relation may reflect lower distress risk or higher asset efficiency in this sample. Accruals' positive sign is contrary to the classic accrual anomaly, possibly due to the sample period or index membership effects. Asset growth's negative relation supports the asset growth anomaly.

The rejected signals—book-to-market, size, turnover, and R&D intensity—show no reliable predictive power in this sample, which may be due to the index-constituent universe, the relatively short sample period, or changing market dynamics.

## Limitations
This study is limited by the absence of transaction costs and liquidity constraints in the backtest. The sample consists solely of index constituents at the end of the sample period, introducing survivorship bias. The 120-month period may not capture full economic cycles. Feature definitions rely on annual accounting data with varying reporting lags. The composite strategy's performance is estimated from summary statistics rather than full portfolio backtests, and may overstate true performance due to correlation simplifications.
