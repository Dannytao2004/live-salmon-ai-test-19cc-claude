# Report Q272

# Title
Point-in-Time Predictive Signals in a Monthly Equity Panel: A Cautionary Note on Analyst Revisions

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. Analyst consensus revision exhibits an implausibly strong rank IC of 0.296 and a long-short Sharpe ratio of 26.4, but it is recorded at the end of month t+1 and therefore uses future information relative to the portfolio formation date. After excluding this look-ahead feature, only asset growth and accruals show marginal positive predictive power. A monthly long-short composite of these two signals yields an estimated gross annual return of about 3.4% and an annual Sharpe ratio of about 0.8. Most other characteristics are statistically indistinguishable from zero in this sample.

## Methods
We use a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 1,213 stocks and 120 months, with 500 stocks per month. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank IC with `ret_next` within each month and average across the 120 months. The reported t-statistic is the mean IC divided by the IC standard deviation times the square root of 120. Long-short portfolios are equal-weighted top-quintile minus bottom-quintile portfolios formed each month.

A critical step is checking point-in-time availability. All features except `analyst_rev` are known at the end of month t or are based on the latest annual report available at the end of month t. `analyst_rev` is recorded at the end of month t+1, which is contemporaneous with the target return and therefore cannot be used to predict `ret_next` at month t. We define valid signals as those that are point-in-time available and have at least marginal statistical and economic support (absolute t-stat above roughly 1.3 and a non-negligible long-short return).

## Results
The signal statistics are summarized below.

| feature | mean rank IC | IC t-stat | long-short monthly return | annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | -0.27 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| analyst_rev | 0.2958 | 89.59 | 8.66% | 26.38 |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | -0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 |

`analyst_rev` is a textbook look-ahead artifact. Its rank IC is 0.296 with a t-statistic of 89.6, and the long-short portfolio earns 8.66% per month with a Sharpe ratio of 26.4. Such performance is not plausible for a real-time equity signal and is explained by the fact that the feature is measured during month t+1, exactly the period over which `ret_next` is computed.

Excluding `analyst_rev`, the only features with even marginal support are `asset_growth` and `accruals`. `asset_growth` has a mean rank IC of 0.0061 (t = 1.64), a long-short return of 0.28% per month, and an annualized Sharpe of 0.72. `accruals` has a mean rank IC of 0.0055 (t = 1.34), a long-short return of 0.29% per month, and an annualized Sharpe of 0.70. Both are positive in this sample, although the canonical anomalies would usually suggest negative signs for asset growth and accruals. This sign reversal, combined with the modest t-statistics, suggests these are weak and possibly sample-specific signals.

The remaining features — `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` — have near-zero or statistically insignificant ICs and long-short returns. They are rejected as reliable predictors in this panel.

## Discussion
The central finding is not that analyst revisions predict returns, but that the data dictionary exposes a timing problem. A real-time investor at the end of month t cannot observe `analyst_rev`, because it is recorded at the end of month t+1. Using it in a backtest would produce spectacular but unattainable performance. This illustrates why point-in-time availability must be checked before interpreting signal statistics.

After removing the look-ahead feature, the remaining opportunity set is weak. The best available signals are `asset_growth` and `accruals`, which are only marginally significant. A simple strategy is to form a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio on the average cross-sectional rank of these two features: buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile. Based on the underlying long-short returns, the composite would have an expected gross annual return of roughly 3.4% and an annual Sharpe ratio of about 0.8 before transaction costs. After realistic trading costs, capacity constraints, and slippage, the net expected return is likely much lower and may not be economically meaningful.

The results also highlight the danger of multiple testing. With ten features and 120 months, some characteristics will appear mildly predictive by chance. The absence of strong, theory-consistent signs for the non-look-ahead features suggests caution. `analyst_rev` should be excluded from any live strategy unless it is properly lagged to a point-in-time safe version.

## Limitations
- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 large-cap index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to broader or smaller-cap universes.
- Transaction costs, market impact, borrow fees, and shorting constraints are not modeled, and these would significantly reduce the net performance of the proposed strategy.
- The two retained signals have modest t-statistics (1.64 and 1.34) and signs opposite to well-known anomalies, raising concerns about data snooping or sample-specific noise.
- No sector neutrality, risk-factor neutrality, or multivariate controls are applied, so the weak signals may proxy for unmodeled exposures.
- The analyst revision signal is rejected solely on availability grounds; a properly lagged version might have different, likely much weaker, predictive power.
