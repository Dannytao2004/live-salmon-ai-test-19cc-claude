# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Stock Panel: Only Leverage Survives

## Abstract
We evaluate nine stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using 34,708 monthly observations on up to 500 broad-index constituents. We compute monthly rank information coefficients (ICs), t-statistics, and equal-weighted top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio returns. Only leverage has a statistically significant positive rank IC of 0.0164 (t = 2.74), while the other eight features have t-statistics below 1.2 in absolute value. A monthly leverage-based long-short quintile strategy earns 0.52% per month gross, or approximately 6.24% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. These findings are sample-specific and subject to survivorship, data-availability, and transaction-cost caveats.

## Methods
We use a monthly panel of 500 stocks that are constituents of a broad equity index at the end of month 120, with history going back to month 1 or the month they joined the index. The panel has 34,708 rows and 120 months; stocks per month range from 143 to 500. The target, `ret_next`, is the total stock return over month t+1. All predictors are observed at or before the end of month t, so they are available for forming portfolios for month t+1.

Data availability is important. Market-based variables (`log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, `turnover`) and the market-equity component of `book_to_market` use end-of-month t data. Accounting variables (`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `rd_intensity`) use the latest annual report available at the end of month t, as stated in the data dictionary. We assume this is point-in-time and avoids look-ahead bias, although annual-report data may be stale.

For each feature, we compute the monthly Spearman rank IC between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks, then average across the 120 months. The t-statistic is the mean IC divided by the IC standard deviation times √120. We also form equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month, buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile of the feature. We treat a feature as a valid signal if its mean rank IC has |t| ≥ 2.0 and its long-short performance is directionally consistent.

## Results
The signal statistics are summarized below.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| book_to_market | -0.0017 | -0.28 | -0.05% | -0.08 | Rejected |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | 0.12% | 0.22 | Rejected |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | -0.07% | -0.14 | Rejected |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | 0.37% | 0.74 | Rejected |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | 0.24% | 0.41 | Rejected |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | 0.13% | 0.19 | Rejected |
| leverage | 0.0164 | 2.74 | 0.52% | 0.92 | Valid |
| turnover | -0.0005 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.23 | Rejected |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | -0.06% | -0.11 | Rejected |

Only `leverage` meets our validity criterion. Its mean rank IC is positive at 0.0164, with a t-statistic of 2.74, and it is positive in 59% of months. The equal-weighted top-minus-bottom leverage quintile portfolio earns 0.52% per month, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. This implies an annualized gross return of roughly 6.24% if returns are treated arithmetic, or about 6.43% compounded.

The other features are not statistically reliable in this sample. `asset_growth` and `accruals` have positive ICs and long-short returns, but their t-statistics are only 1.16 and 1.03, respectively. `idio_vol` is weaker still. `book_to_market`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` show near-zero ICs and inconsistent long-short returns. `log_mktcap` has a slightly positive rank IC but a slightly negative long-short return, suggesting non-monotonicity or noise rather than a robust size effect. `gross_prof` is also economically and statistically insignificant.

## Discussion
The main finding is that leverage is the only characteristic in this panel that reliably predicts next-month returns. The direction is positive: higher-leverage stocks outperform lower-leverage stocks within this index-constituent universe. This could reflect compensation for distress risk, cyclical exposure, or a sample-specific value/leverage tilt. However, the economic magnitude is modest: 0.52% per month gross before costs, and the Sharpe ratio of 0.92 is moderate.

The weakness of the other signals is notable. Several well-known anomalies, such as book-to-market, gross profitability, accruals, and size, do not appear strongly in this panel. This may be because the universe is limited to stocks that remain index constituents through month 120, which is a survivor sample and may exclude the kinds of firms where these anomalies are strongest. Annual-report-based fundamentals may also be stale, attenuating their predictive power. Market-based signals such as turnover and idiosyncratic volatility show no reliable cross-sectional return relation here.

A practical strategy based on these results would be a monthly equal-weighted long-short leverage portfolio: long the top quintile of leverage and short the bottom quintile, rebalanced at each month end. Based on the reported long-short mean monthly return of 0.52%, the gross expected annual return is about 6.24%, with an expected annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. This is a simple, transparent strategy, but it should be viewed as gross of transaction costs and shorting frictions.

## Limitations
- The universe is composed of stocks that survive as index constituents through month 120, introducing survivorship and look-ahead selection bias.
- The panel is unbalanced, with only 143 stocks in month 1 and 500 by month 120, so early cross-sections are thin.
- Accounting variables come from annual reports and may be stale or subject to point-in-time availability errors.
- The results are based on 120 months and a single sample, so out-of-sample generalization is uncertain.
- Multiple testing across nine features means the leverage signal’s significance is marginal after adjusting for the number of tests.
- The backtest ignores transaction costs, shorting fees, and market impact, which could substantially reduce net returns.
- Equal-weighted quintile portfolios may load on small-cap or illiquid stocks within the index universe.
