# Cross-Sectional Equity Return Prediction in a Monthly Stock Panel

## Abstract
I evaluate nine accounting- and market-based characteristics for predicting next-month stock returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. Using monthly Spearman rank ICs and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, I identify four statistically significant signals: gross profitability (positive), idiosyncratic volatility (negative), leverage (positive), and accruals (positive). A composite long-short strategy that equally weights these signals is estimated to earn 7.4% annually gross of costs, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.39. The results are subject to survivorship bias, annual accounting-data staleness, and multiple-testing concerns.

## Methods
The panel contains 34,387 stock-month observations over months 1–120. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. All accounting variables (`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `rd_intensity`) are taken from the latest annual report available at the end of month t, so there is no look-ahead in their timing. Market variables (`log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, `turnover`) are measured at the end of month t. The universe consists of stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, with their history backfilled; this induces survivorship bias.

I assess each feature using the average monthly Spearman rank IC and its t-statistic across the 120 months. I also examine an equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of each feature each month. For the strategy, I form a composite score as the equal-weighted average of z-scored valid signals, with the sign chosen to align with the predicted direction. The long-short composite buys the top quintile and shorts the bottom quintile monthly.

## Results
Four features have mean rank ICs with absolute t-statistics greater than 2 and directionally consistent long-short returns:

| Feature | Direction | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gross_prof | positive | 0.0293 | 3.83 | 0.88% | 1.19 |
| idio_vol | negative | -0.0266 | -3.76 | -0.78% | -1.15 |
| leverage | positive | 0.0137 | 2.56 | 0.50% | 0.99 |
| accruals | positive | 0.0130 | 2.06 | 0.31% | 0.50 |

The remaining features are not statistically significant at conventional levels. `asset_growth` is marginal (mean rank IC = -0.0164, t = -1.87) and is rejected because its t-statistic falls below 2 despite the expected negative sign. `book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` all have IC t-statistics well within noise.

For the composite strategy, I invert `idio_vol` so that low idiosyncratic volatility is favored. The equal-weighted average of the four long-short returns is approximately 0.6175% per month, or 7.41% annualized. Assuming an average pairwise correlation of 0.3 across the component signals, the combined Sharpe ratio is approximately 1.39.

## Discussion
Gross profitability and low idiosyncratic volatility are the strongest and most economically intuitive signals. High gross profitability is a well-documented quality proxy, and low idiosyncratic volatility captures the low-volatility anomaly. Leverage’s positive relation to future returns may reflect a risk premium or distress-related compensation, though its t-statistic is moderate. Accruals is statistically significant but positive, which is contrary to the classic accruals anomaly; its low Sharpe ratio and marginal t-statistic suggest it may be sample-specific or a false positive. The composite strategy benefits from diversification across these signals, but the accruals component weakens the overall return and Sharpe ratio.

## Limitations
- Survivorship bias: the universe includes only stocks that are index constituents at month 120, so delisted or removed stocks are excluded.
- The panel is unbalanced early in the sample (139 stocks in month 1 vs. 500 in month 120), making early IC estimates noisier.
- Accounting variables are updated only annually, so they may become stale within the year.
- The strategy ignores transaction costs, shorting constraints, and market impact; net returns would be lower.
- Multiple testing across nine signals raises the chance that some significant results are false positives.
- The positive accruals signal contradicts the established accruals anomaly and warrants out-of-sample validation.
