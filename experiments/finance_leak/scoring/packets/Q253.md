# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Monthly Stock Panel: Only Leverage Survives

## Abstract
We evaluate nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns in a panel of up to 500 stocks over 120 months. Using monthly cross-sectional rank ICs and quintile long-short portfolios, only leverage shows a statistically reliable positive signal, with a mean rank IC of 0.0164 (t-stat = 2.74) and a top-minus-bottom quintile return of 0.52% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 0.92. The remaining eight signals do not pass a t-stat threshold of 2.0 and are rejected as unreliable predictors in this sample. A monthly-rebalanced long-short leverage strategy is estimated to earn about 6.2% gross annual return. Results are gross of transaction costs and subject to survivorship and multiple-testing concerns.

## Methods
The dataset is a monthly panel of 34,708 stock-month observations covering 500 distinct stocks and 120 months. The target variable is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. For each of the nine features, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month, then average these rank ICs across all 120 months. The IC t-stat is computed as the mean rank IC divided by its monthly standard deviation and multiplied by sqrt(120). We also form equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month, buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile of each feature. A feature is classified as a valid signal if its mean rank IC t-stat exceeds 2.0 in absolute value and its economic direction is consistent with the quintile spread.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| leverage | 0.0164 | 2.74 | 0.52% | 0.92 |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | 0.37% | 0.74 |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | 0.24% | 0.41 |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | 0.13% | 0.19 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | -0.07% | -0.14 |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | 0.12% | 0.22 |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | -0.06% | -0.11 |
| turnover | -0.0005 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.23 |
| book_to_market | -0.0017 | -0.28 | -0.05% | -0.08 |

Only `leverage` passes the t-stat threshold of 2.0. Its positive mean rank IC of 0.0164 indicates that stocks with higher total debt relative to assets tend to have higher next-month returns. The long-short quintile portfolio earns 0.52% per month on average, with an annualized Sharpe of 0.92. While `asset_growth` and `accruals` have positive mean rank ICs and positive long-short returns, their t-stats are well below 2.0, so we do not classify them as statistically reliable signals in this sample.

## Discussion
The positive relation between leverage and next-month returns is consistent with a distress-risk or credit-risk premium: firms with higher debt relative to assets are riskier and may earn higher expected returns as compensation. The economic magnitude is meaningful, with a top-minus-bottom quintile spread of 0.52% per month before costs. However, the signal is relatively weak in IC terms; a monthly rank IC of 0.0164 is modest, though its consistency across months gives it a t-stat of 2.74. The other tested characteristics, including value, size, profitability, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, turnover, and R&D intensity, do not display reliable univariate predictive power in this specific sample.

## Limitations
This study has several limitations. First, the universe is constructed from stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, which may introduce survivorship or backfill bias. Second, the leverage signal is not robust to strict multiple-testing adjustment across the nine features. Third, all performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, short-selling costs, and market impact. Fourth, the sample is relatively short at 120 months, so the leverage premium may be period-specific. Fifth, the analysis is univariate and does not control for other risk factors or interactions among characteristics.
