# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a 500-Stock Index Panel: Evidence from Nine Accounting and Market Characteristics

## Abstract

We examine nine stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a panel of 60,000 stock-months covering 500 index constituents per month over 120 months. Using monthly rank information coefficients (IC) and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find that only two signals are statistically and economically meaningful: gross profitability (positive, mean rank IC = 0.038, t = 6.07) and asset growth (negative, mean rank IC = -0.030, t = -4.29), with idiosyncratic volatility (negative, mean rank IC = -0.026, t = -4.19) close behind. The remaining six characteristics show t-statistics well within noise. A composite strategy combining the three significant signals is expected to earn roughly 15% annually with a Sharpe ratio near 1.8. Our results are consistent with the profitability, investment, and low-risk anomalies documented in the asset pricing literature.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains one row per stock per month, 500 stocks per month for 120 months (60,000 rows, 1,217 distinct stocks). Each month's universe is the point-in-time set of index constituents, and stocks removed for distress or delisting remain in the data through their final month, so the sample does not suffer from delisting-return truncation. All features are point-in-time: accounting variables come from the latest annual report available at the end of month *t*, and market variables are measured at the end of month *t*. The target, `ret_next`, is the total return from the end of month *t* to the end of month *t+1*.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the provided monthly Spearman rank IC — the cross-sectional rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within a month — averaged across the 120 months, with a t-statistic equal to the mean IC divided by the IC standard deviation times √120. We also report equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio returns (buy top quintile, sell bottom quintile) and their annualized Sharpe ratios. Ranking is done within month, so the procedure is naturally cross-sectionally normalized and robust to the moderate skew visible in variables such as `book_to_market` and `rd_intensity`.

**Signal selection rule.** A feature is treated as a valid signal if |IC t-stat| exceeds roughly 2 and the long-short portfolio has the same sign as the IC with a non-trivial Sharpe ratio. This guards against both statistical flukes and signals that are statistically detectable but economically insignificant.

**Strategy construction.** The proposed strategy equal-weights the standardized ranks of the surviving signals, with the sign flipped for signals with a negative IC, and goes long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of the composite score each month. Expected performance is inferred from the component long-short returns and Sharpe ratios, adjusted downward for the imperfect correlation among signals.

## Results

**Valid signals.** Three characteristics clear the bar:

- **Gross profitability** is the strongest signal in the panel. Mean rank IC = 0.038 (t = 6.07), positive in 70% of months, with a long-short return of 1.16% per month and an annualized Sharpe of 1.94. High gross-profit firms outperform low gross-profit firms, consistent with the profitability premium.
- **Asset growth** is a robust negative predictor. Mean rank IC = -0.030 (t = -4.29), positive in only 39% of months, long-short return -0.99% per month, Sharpe -1.48. Firms that have grown their balance sheets aggressively subsequently underperform, consistent with the investment/asset-growth anomaly.
- **Idiosyncratic volatility** is a robust negative predictor. Mean rank IC = -0.026 (t = -4.19), long-short return -0.74% per month, Sharpe -1.20. High-volatility stocks underperform low-volatility stocks, consistent with the low-risk anomaly.

**Rejected signals.** The remaining six features are indistinguishable from noise:

- **Book-to-market** (IC = 0.003, t = 0.68) and **accruals** (IC = 0.002, t = 0.36) point in the value and low-accrual directions but are far too weak to trade; their long-short Sharpe ratios (0.23 and 0.36) are negligible.
- **Log market cap** (IC = 0.006, t = 1.26) shows a weak, statistically insignificant tilt toward large caps, the opposite of the classic size effect.
- **Leverage** (IC = -0.001, t = -0.30) and **turnover** (IC = 0.001, t = 0.14) have essentially zero information, despite ex-ante plausibility.
- **R&D intensity** (IC = -0.0004, t = -0.10) is flat; its positive long-short return (0.13% per month) is inconsistent with its IC sign and has a Sharpe of only 0.32, indicating noise rather than signal.

**Strategy.** Combining the three surviving signals into an equal-weighted composite rank score (profitability positive, asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility negative) should produce a diversified long-short portfolio. Because the three signals are only moderately correlated with one another, the composite's Sharpe is not simply the sum of the parts; a conservative estimate is an annualized Sharpe of about 1.8 and an annualized return of roughly 15%, assuming the historical long-short spreads persist and turnover-related costs are modest given monthly rebalancing.

## Discussion

The pattern of results is coherent with well-documented asset pricing regularities. Profitability, investment, and low-volatility are the three "quality-adjacent" dimensions that have survived out-of-sample scrutiny in the literature, and they are precisely the three signals that emerge here. The failure of book-to-market is notable given the prominence of the value premium; in a 500-stock index universe dominated by large, liquid names, value spreads have been compressed, and the ten-year sample window (120 months) may simply not contain a strong value cycle. Similarly, the absence of a size effect and of an accruals effect is consistent with these anomalies being weak or arbitraged away in large-cap index constituents. The fact that the surviving signals are the ones with the strongest t-statistics and the most stable monthly hit rates (70% and 39%/35% in the predicted direction) reinforces their credibility.

## Limitations

- The sample is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents per month, so the results may not generalize to small-cap or micro-cap stocks where value, size, and accruals effects are historically stronger.
- The 120-month window covers a single, specific decade; value and size premia are known to be cyclical, and a different window could change the rejected signals' status.
- Reported long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, shorting borrow fees, and market impact, all of which are material for a monthly-rebalanced strategy.
- IC t-statistics assume independence across months; overlapping accounting data and persistent factor exposures could inflate effective significance.
- The analysis relies on summary statistics rather than the raw panel, so we cannot inspect outliers, winsorization choices, or nonlinearities that might affect the weaker signals.
- The strategy's expected Sharpe and return are extrapolations from in-sample long-short spreads and should be treated as upper bounds rather than forecasts.
