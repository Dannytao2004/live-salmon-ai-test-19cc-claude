# The Predictive Power of Standardized Unexpected Earnings in a Monthly Equity Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using 120 months of a 500-stock monthly panel. Only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) exhibits a robust positive relation: mean rank IC 0.0998 (t-stat 23.25), positive in 99% of months, and a top-minus-bottom quintile long-short portfolio earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 7.31. The remaining nine features have rank ICs near zero and t-statistics below conventional significance thresholds. A monthly SUE-based long-short strategy would have generated large gross returns, but these are likely overstated by transaction costs, capacity limits, and data-mining concerns.

## Methods
We use a monthly panel of 60,000 observations covering 120 months and 1,213 distinct stocks, with 500 index-constituent stocks per month. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. The ten candidate features are `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `sue`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

For each feature, we evaluate its cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month, then average across months to obtain the mean rank IC. The IC t-statistic is computed as mean IC divided by the standard deviation of monthly ICs, multiplied by the square root of 120. We also examine an equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of each feature each month. We consider a signal valid if it has a large, statistically reliable IC and an economically meaningful long-short return. Given ten features, we apply a conservative interpretation of significance rather than relying on marginal t-statistics.

## Results
The only feature with a strong and statistically robust relation to `ret_next` is `sue`. Its mean rank IC is 0.0998 with an IC standard deviation of 0.0470, giving a t-statistic of 23.25. The IC is positive in 99% of months. The long-short portfolio that buys high-SUE stocks and sells low-SUE stocks earns an average 3.04% per month, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. This implies an arithmetic annualized gross return of approximately 36.5% (0.0304 × 12), or about 43.2% compounded.

The other nine features show weak and unreliable relations. Their mean rank ICs range from -0.0043 to 0.0061, and none reaches conventional significance. For example, `asset_growth` has mean IC 0.0061 (t = 1.64), `accruals` has 0.0055 (t = 1.34), and `idio_vol` has 0.0043 (t = 1.14). `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` are all statistically indistinguishable from zero, with |t| ≤ 1.00. Several signs also contradict well-known anomalies from the broader literature, such as positive rather than negative accruals and positive rather than negative asset growth.

## Discussion
The results identify SUE as a powerful positive predictor of next-month returns. High standardized unexpected earnings relative to consensus forecasts predict high subsequent returns, consistent with post-earnings announcement drift: investors underreact to earnings news, and prices continue to drift in the direction of the surprise. The effect is exceptionally strong in this panel, with a mean rank IC of about 0.10 and a long-short return of 3.04% per month. However, the implied Sharpe ratio of 7.31 is unusually high for a liquid equity strategy and suggests either a very strong sample-specific anomaly, an implementation artifact, or a data limitation.

The other features do not survive scrutiny. Their rank ICs are economically tiny—often less than 0.01—and their t-statistics are below 2. Even `asset_growth` and `accruals`, which have the next-largest ICs, are not significant after accounting for multiple testing. Some signs also run opposite to established anomalies, which reduces confidence that they are stable signals in this sample. A practical strategy would therefore focus on SUE: each month, rank all stocks by SUE, buy the top quintile, short the bottom quintile, equal-weight within each leg, and rebalance monthly. Based on the summary statistics, this strategy would have delivered a gross arithmetic annualized return of about 36.5% with an annualized Sharpe of 7.31, before transaction costs, borrow fees, and market impact.

## Limitations
- The panel covers only 120 months and 500 index-constituent stocks per month, so results may not generalize to broader or smaller-cap universes.
- The SUE signal may embed look-ahead bias if the consensus forecast or earnings announcement timing is not perfectly point-in-time.
- Reported long-short returns are gross and ignore transaction costs, shorting costs, taxes, and market impact, which could substantially reduce net performance.
- The extremely high Sharpe ratio of 7.31 is implausible for a scalable liquid strategy and raises concerns about data errors, overfitting, or outlier effects.
- Equal-weighted quintile portfolios can be sensitive to small, illiquid, or distressed stocks, even within an index universe.
- Multiple-testing and specification-search concerns mean the single surviving signal should be validated out-of-sample before live trading.
