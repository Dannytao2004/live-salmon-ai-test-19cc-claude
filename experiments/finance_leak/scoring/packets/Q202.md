# Cross-Sectional Equity Return Predictors in a Monthly Panel: Evidence from Fundamental, Earnings, and Risk Characteristics

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month equity returns using a point-in-time panel of 500 index stocks per month over 120 months. Four characteristics are statistically robust predictors: standardized unexpected earnings and gross profitability predict returns positively, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility predict returns negatively. A quintile long-short portfolio combining these four signals is estimated to earn about 17.5% annually with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 3.7 before transaction costs. The remaining six characteristics—book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not show reliable predictive power in this sample.

## Methods
The dataset consists of 60,000 stock-month observations: 1,217 distinct stocks, with 500 index constituents each month for 120 months. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. All characteristics are measured at or before the end of month t.

For each characteristic, I compute monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlations with `ret_next`, averaged over the 120 months. The associated t-statistic is computed as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation times the square root of the number of months. I also form equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios: each month, stocks are sorted into quintiles on the characteristic, and the portfolio buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile. A characteristic is considered a valid signal if its rank IC has |t-statistic| > 2.0 and its long-short return is economically consistent with the IC sign.

To build a composite strategy, I average cross-sectional percentile ranks of the valid positive signals and subtract the average percentile ranks of the valid negative signals. The strategy buys the top quintile of the composite score and sells the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly. Expected composite performance is estimated from the univariate long-short return means, volatilities, and an assumed average pairwise correlation of 0.30 among the component long-short returns.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics and screening decisions.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | L/S mean monthly | L/S annualized Sharpe | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 | Reject |
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 | Valid, positive |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 | Reject |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 | Valid, negative |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 | Reject |
| sue | 0.0952 | 26.55 | 2.96% | 7.65 | Valid, positive |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 | Valid, negative |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 | Reject |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 | Reject |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 | Reject |

Four signals pass the statistical and economic screens. Standardized unexpected earnings is the strongest predictor: its mean rank IC is 0.0952 with a t-statistic of 26.55, and its top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns 2.96% per month on average. Gross profitability also predicts positively, with a rank IC of 0.0378 and a monthly long-short return of 1.16%. Asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility predict negatively, with rank ICs of -0.0303 and -0.0261, respectively. The inverse long-short versions of these two signals earn 0.99% and 0.74% per month, respectively.

The equal-weight composite of the four valid signals has an expected monthly long-short return of approximately:
(1.16% + 0.99% + 0.74% + 2.96%) / 4 = 1.46%, which annualizes to about 17.5%. Using the implied univariate monthly volatilities and an assumed average pairwise correlation of 0.30, the expected monthly composite volatility is about 1.36%, implying an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 3.7 before costs.

## Discussion
The results are broadly consistent with several known cross-sectional anomalies. The positive signal from standardized unexpected earnings supports a post-earnings-announcement drift interpretation: stocks with positive earnings surprises continue to outperform in the following month. Gross profitability aligns with the quality or profitability premium. The negative predictive power of asset growth is consistent with the asset-growth anomaly, while the negative relation between idiosyncratic volatility and future returns is consistent with the idiosyncratic-volatility puzzle.

The composite strategy combines four signals that are individually significant and, based on the available evidence, likely to provide diversification benefits because they capture different aspects of firm fundamentals, earnings news, and risk. The estimated gross Sharpe ratio is high, but it does not account for implementation costs, short-sale constraints, or capacity limits.

## Limitations
The performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, borrowing fees, and market impact. The sample consists only of index constituents, so the findings may not generalize to smaller, less liquid, or non-index stocks. In addition, the extremely high rank IC and consistency of standardized unexpected earnings should be interpreted cautiously, as such strong empirical performance may reflect data construction choices or in-sample overfitting. Finally, the composite performance calculation relies on an assumed correlation structure rather than directly observed composite portfolio returns.
