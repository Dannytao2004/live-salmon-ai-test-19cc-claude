# Predictive Signals in a Monthly Stock Panel: Evidence from Rank IC and Long-Short Portfolios

## Abstract

This paper evaluates ten firm characteristics as potential predictors of next-month stock returns using a panel of 500 stocks per month over 120 months. Among the candidate signals, only analyst consensus revision (`analyst_rev`) exhibits a robust and economically meaningful relationship with future returns, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.296 (t-stat = 89.59) and a long-short quintile spread of 8.66% per month. All other features show rank ICs near zero with t-statistics below conventional significance thresholds, indicating no reliable predictive power in this sample. A trading strategy based solely on analyst revisions would be expected to generate substantial returns, though practical implementation would face capacity and data-timing constraints.

## Methods

**Data.** The analysis uses a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 1,213 distinct stocks over 120 months. Each month contains exactly 500 stocks that were constituents of a broad equity index at that time. The target variable is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. Ten candidate features are evaluated: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `analyst_rev`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature, I compute the monthly rank IC—the Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks within each month—and average it over all 120 months. Statistical significance is assessed using the t-statistic: mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation, multiplied by the square root of the number of months. A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value and its sign is economically consistent with prior literature or a plausible mechanism.

**Long-short portfolios.** For each feature, I also construct an equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the feature each month. The mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio of this portfolio provide an economic magnitude estimate.

## Results

Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics for all ten features.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Long-Short Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | -0.27 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| **analyst_rev** | **0.2958** | **89.59** | **8.66%** | **26.38** |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | -0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 |

**Analyst revisions.** The standout result is `analyst_rev`, with a mean rank IC of 0.2958 and a t-statistic of 89.59. The IC is positive in 100% of the 120 months, indicating an extraordinarily consistent relationship: stocks with upward analyst earnings revisions outperform those with downward revisions. The long-short quintile portfolio earns 8.66% per month (annualized Sharpe 26.38), which is economically very large.

**Other features.** None of the remaining nine features approaches statistical significance. The largest absolute t-statistic among them is 1.64 for `asset_growth`, well below the 2.0 threshold. The sign of the rank IC for `book_to_market` is negative (-0.0030), contrary to the typical value premium, but its t-statistic is only -0.72. Similarly, `accruals` shows a positive IC (0.0055), opposite to the usual accrual anomaly sign, but is not significant. All other features have t-statistics below 1.34 in absolute value, and their long-short portfolio returns are economically negligible (≤ 0.29% per month).

## Discussion

The results indicate that, in this particular sample, only analyst consensus revision carries meaningful predictive information for next-month returns. The magnitude of the rank IC (0.296) is far larger than what is typically observed for monthly cross-sectional equity signals, where a rank IC of 0.05 is already considered strong. The 100% consistency across months further underscores the robustness of this relationship.

The absence of predictive power for well-known factors such as book-to-market, gross profitability, asset growth, and accruals is notable. This may reflect the specific sample composition—stocks in a broad equity index with point-in-time membership—where index constituents are relatively large, liquid, and heavily covered by analysts. Under such conditions, many traditional anomalies may be arbitraged away or may not be present in the first place. The negative (though insignificant) sign on book-to-market is consistent with some recent evidence that the value premium has weakened or inverted in certain periods and universes.

The strong performance of analyst revisions is consistent with the post-earnings-announcement drift and analyst forecast revision literature: analyst revisions aggregate information about future earnings that is not immediately impounded into prices. The fact that `analyst_rev` is measured at the end of month t+1 (i.e., during the return month) raises a timing concern, however. If the revision data are only available at the end of the return month, the signal would not be tradable in real time without look-ahead bias. This is discussed further in Limitations.

## Limitations

Several limitations warrant caution. First, the `analyst_rev` variable is recorded at the end of month t+1, meaning it may incorporate information that is not available at the time the return is realized. If revisions during month t+1 are correlated with returns in that same month, the measured IC would be inflated by look-ahead bias. Second, the sample is restricted to index constituents, which limits generalizability to the broader cross-section of stocks, especially small caps. Third, the long-short return of 8.66% per month is gross of transaction costs and assumes frictionless monthly rebalancing; in practice, turnover and market impact would erode returns substantially. Fourth, the analysis does not control for risk factors or other covariates, so the univariate results may not reflect marginal predictive power in a multivariate setting. Finally, the 120-month sample may not cover a full economic cycle, and the consistency of the analyst revision signal may be period-specific.
