# Cross-Sectional Equity Return Predictors in a 500-Stock Monthly Panel: Evidence from Rank IC and Long-Short Portfolios

## Abstract
This study evaluates ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Signals are assessed with average monthly rank ICs, t-statistics, and equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios. Four characteristics are statistically reliable: `sue` and `gross_prof` positively predict returns, while `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` negatively predict returns. A composite long-short strategy combining these four signals is expected to earn roughly 19% annualized with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.5, before transaction costs. The remaining six characteristics show no reliable cross-sectional predictive power in this sample.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations from 120 months, with 500 stocks per month. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month \(t+1\). Point-in-time availability is respected: annual-report variables (`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `rd_intensity`) use the latest annual report available at the end of month \(t\). `sue` is based on the most recent announced quarterly earnings surprise; because firms announce earnings in the month after the fiscal quarter ends, the signal is assumed to reflect only information available by the end of month \(t\). Market-based variables (`log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, `turnover`) use data through the end of month \(t\).

I evaluate each feature using within-month Spearman rank IC, averaged over 120 months. I compute the IC t-statistic as mean IC divided by IC standard deviation times \(\sqrt{120}\). I also examine equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature. A signal is considered valid if its average rank IC is economically meaningful and its t-statistic exceeds conventional significance thresholds (roughly \(|t| \ge 2\)).

## Results
Four features are valid return predictors. `sue` is the strongest: mean rank IC = 0.0952, t = 26.55, with a positive long-short return of 2.96% per month and an annualized Sharpe of 7.65. `gross_prof` is also positive and significant: mean IC = 0.0378, t = 6.07, long-short return 1.16% per month, Sharpe 1.94. `asset_growth` has a negative relation: mean IC = -0.0303, t = -4.29, long-short return -0.99% per month, implying that buying low-growth stocks and shorting high-growth stocks earns about 0.99% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.48. `idio_vol` is similarly negative: mean IC = -0.0261, t = -4.19, long-short return -0.74% per month, reversed Sharpe 1.20.

The other six features are rejected as standalone signals. `book_to_market` has mean IC = 0.0027 (t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` has mean IC = 0.0055 (t = 1.26), `accruals` has mean IC = 0.0015 (t = 0.36), `leverage` has mean IC = -0.0011 (t = -0.30), `turnover` has mean IC = 0.0006 (t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` has mean IC = -0.0004 (t = -0.10). None show reliable cross-sectional predictive power.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with several well-documented anomalies. `sue` captures post-earnings-announcement drift, and its extreme t-statistic suggests strong persistence in this panel. `gross_prof` captures a quality/profitability premium: firms with higher gross profits relative to assets tend to outperform. `asset_growth` reflects the investment factor: firms expanding their asset base subsequently underperform. `idio_vol` reflects the low-volatility anomaly: high idiosyncratic volatility stocks underperform on a risk-adjusted basis. The weak performance of `book_to_market`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` may reflect the specific index-constituent universe, the 120-month sample, or the fact that these signals are weaker or already arbitraged away in this setting.

A practical strategy is to combine the four valid signals into a composite score. Cross-sectionally standardize each feature, reverse the signs of `asset_growth` and `idio_vol`, and equal-weight the standardized values. Each month, buy the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, rebalancing monthly. Using the average of the four individual long-short returns as a rough guide, the composite would have earned about 1.46% per month, or roughly 19% annualized when compounded. Given moderate signal correlations and the very strong `sue` component, a conservative expected annualized Sharpe is around 2.5. This estimate is gross of transaction costs, borrowing costs, and slippage.

## Limitations
The sample covers only 120 months and 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to broader or non-index universes. The `sue` signal has an exceptionally high Sharpe ratio, which may reflect the synthetic or in-sample nature of the data rather than a tradable edge. The strategy ignores transaction costs, shorting constraints, capacity limits, and market impact. No sector, size, or risk-factor neutralization is applied, so part of the return may be unintentional factor exposure. The composite uses equal weights and in-sample signal estimates, with no out-of-sample validation. Signal correlations are not provided, so the combined Sharpe estimate is approximate. Finally, point-in-time annual-report variables depend on the exact availability lag, and any misalignment would introduce lookahead bias.
