## Title: Standardized Unexpected Earnings as a Robust Cross-Sectional Return Predictor

## Abstract
We examine the cross-sectional predictive power of ten firm characteristics for one-month-ahead stock returns in a monthly panel of 1,213 stocks over 120 months. Among the considered features, only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) exhibits a statistically and economically significant rank correlation with future returns, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.0998 (t = 23.25) and a quintile long-short return of 3.04% per month (annualized Sharpe 7.31). All other features have rank ICs indistinguishable from zero after accounting for sampling variability. A monthly-rebalanced strategy that buys the top SUE quintile and sells the bottom quintile yields an expected annualized return of approximately 36.5% before transaction costs. These results underscore the persistence of post-earnings-announcement drift in a broad equity index universe.

## Methods
We use a monthly stock panel with 60,000 observations (1,213 unique stocks over 120 months, 500 stocks per month). The prediction target is the total stock return over the following month (`ret_next`). For each of the ten features, we compute the monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation (rank IC) with `ret_next`, average it over the 120 months, and calculate a t-statistic as the mean divided by the standard error (standard deviation / √120). We also construct an equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio each month (top minus bottom quintile of the feature) and report its mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio (monthly mean / monthly std × √12). A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value and its long-short portfolio has an economically meaningful Sharpe ratio.

## Results
Table 1 summarizes the rank IC and long-short performance for all features. SUE stands out with a rank IC of 0.0998 (t = 23.25), positive in 99% of months, and a quintile long-short monthly return of 3.04% (annualized Sharpe 7.31). All other features have rank ICs with absolute t-statistics below 1.7 and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.75. In particular, asset_growth (IC t = 1.64, Sharpe 0.72) and accruals (IC t = 1.34, Sharpe 0.70) show weak positive associations that do not reach conventional significance. book_to_market, leverage, and rd_intensity show negative but insignificant rank ICs. log_mktcap, turnover, idio_vol, and gross_prof have near-zero rank ICs. Based on the SUE signal, a long-short quintile strategy would have generated an average monthly return of 3.04% with a monthly standard deviation of approximately 1.44% (implied by the Sharpe ratio), yielding an annualized return of about 36.5% (0.0304 × 12) before costs.

## Discussion
The dominance of SUE is consistent with the post-earnings-announcement drift anomaly: stocks with positive earnings surprises continue to outperform, while negative surprises underperform. The effect is economically large and statistically robust, with a rank IC that is positive in 99% of months. The absence of significant predictive power for other well-known anomalies (value, size, profitability, asset growth, accruals, volatility, leverage, turnover, R&D intensity) may reflect the specific universe (broad index constituents) or the relatively short sample period. The weak positive ICs for asset_growth and accruals are directionally consistent with prior literature (asset growth negatively predicts returns, accruals negatively predict returns), but their signs here are opposite to the classical anomalies—suggesting either sample-specific noise or a different underlying mechanism. The long-short SUE strategy’s high Sharpe ratio implies that even after accounting for realistic transaction costs and short-sale constraints, the signal is likely to remain profitable, though capacity may be limited by the 500-stock universe.

## Limitations
This analysis is based solely on summary statistics and does not account for transaction costs, market impact, or short-sale fees. The sample covers only 120 months and 1,213 stocks, all of which are broad index constituents, limiting generalizability to small caps or non-index stocks. The rank IC and long-short returns are computed in-sample; no out-of-sample validation is performed. The SUE signal may be subject to data-mining or survivorship bias in the underlying analyst forecast data. Finally, the long-short portfolio assumes equal weighting and monthly rebalancing without considering the implementation lag of SUE (earnings announcements occur in the month after quarter-end), which could reduce realized returns.
