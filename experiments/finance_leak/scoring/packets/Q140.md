# Title
Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Monthly Equity Panel: Only Leverage Passes Conventional Significance

## Abstract
We evaluate nine firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. Only `leverage` has a statistically significant average monthly rank IC of 0.0164 (t = 2.74), implying that higher-leverage stocks tended to outperform lower-leverage stocks in this sample. A monthly equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio on leverage earns 0.52% per month, or approximately 6.24% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. The remaining eight characteristics—book-to-market, gross profitability, log market cap, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not show reliable predictive power. The evidence is weak in economic terms and may be sensitive to multiple testing, survivorship, and implementation costs.

## Methods
The dataset contains 34,708 stock-month observations from months 1 to 120, covering up to 500 stocks. The target is `ret_next`, the stock’s total return in month t+1. Predictors are firm characteristics measured at the end of month t: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

I assessed predictive power using monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlations between each feature and `ret_next`. For each feature, I averaged the rank IC across all 120 months and computed a t-statistic as:

\[
t = \frac{\text{mean IC}}{\text{std IC}} \times \sqrt{120}
\]

I also considered the reported equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios, which buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month. A feature is treated as a valid signal if its average rank IC is statistically distinguishable from zero at the 5% level, corresponding approximately to \(|t| > 1.96\). Because nine features are tested, this threshold is liberal; I therefore interpret marginal results cautiously.

## Results
The signal statistics are summarized below.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Annualized Sharpe | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| leverage | 0.0164 | 2.74 | 0.52% | 0.92 | Valid |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | 0.37% | 0.74 | Rejected |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | 0.24% | 0.41 | Rejected |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | 0.13% | 0.19 | Rejected |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | -0.07% | -0.14 | Rejected |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | 0.12% | 0.22 | Rejected |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | -0.06% | -0.11 | Rejected |
| turnover | -0.0005 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.23 | Rejected |
| book_to_market | -0.0017 | -0.28 | -0.05% | -0.08 | Rejected |

Only `leverage` clears the conventional significance threshold. Its mean rank IC is positive, meaning higher leverage predicts higher next-month returns in this panel. The IC is positive in 59% of months, and the equal-weighted long-short portfolio earns 0.52% per month gross of costs. Annualized arithmetically, this is approximately 6.24%, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92.

The other features are not reliably predictive. `asset_growth` and `accruals` have positive ICs and modest long-short returns, but their t-statistics are only 1.16 and 1.03, respectively. `idio_vol` is similarly insignificant at t = 0.86. `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` have t-statistics close to zero and no consistent directional relationship with `ret_next`.

## Discussion
The central finding is that leverage is the only characteristic in this panel with a statistically significant rank IC. The direction is positive: firms with higher total debt / total assets tended to have higher next-month returns. This is economically notable because standard risk-based arguments often predict the opposite—high leverage may signal financial distress and therefore higher required returns or greater crash risk. In this sample, the positive relationship could reflect a risk premium, a distress anomaly, or a sample-specific artifact.

The economic magnitude is modest. A rank IC of 0.0164 indicates very weak cross-sectional ranking ability. The long-short portfolio return of 0.52% per month is meaningful only before transaction costs, shorting constraints, borrow fees, and market impact. The Sharpe ratio of 0.92 is attractive in isolation, but investors should be skeptical of a single-factor strategy identified after testing nine signals.

A practical strategy would be a monthly equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio: long stocks in the highest leverage quintile and short stocks in the lowest leverage quintile. Given the weak evidence, this should be viewed as a small satellite signal rather than a standalone alpha strategy. It would be prudent to combine it with risk controls, sector neutrality, and out-of-sample validation.

## Limitations
The universe consists of stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, with history backfilled to earlier months. This introduces survivorship and look-ahead membership bias. The analysis ignores transaction costs, shorting constraints, borrow fees, and market impact, all of which would reduce realized performance. Accounting variables are taken from annual reports and may be stale or measured with error. With 120 months and nine tested signals, the leverage result may be a false positive due to multiple testing. The strategy is not neutralized to sector, size, or other risk factors, so returns may reflect unintended exposures. Finally, the rank IC is small, implying that the signal explains only a tiny fraction of cross-sectional return variation.
