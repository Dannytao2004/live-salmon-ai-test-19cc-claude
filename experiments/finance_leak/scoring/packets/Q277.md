# Predicting Stock Returns with Fundamental and Market-Based Signals: Evidence from a 10-Year Panel

## Abstract
This study examines the predictive power of nine firm characteristics for monthly stock returns using a panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Rank IC analysis reveals that gross profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, and leverage are the most robust signals, with t-statistics exceeding 2.5 in absolute value. A long-short quintile strategy based on a composite signal of these three features yields an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.8. The results suggest that combining profitability, risk, and capital structure information can generate economically meaningful return spreads.

## Methods
We analyze a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months (34,387 stock-month observations). For each feature, we compute the rank IC (Spearman correlation between the feature and next-month return) within each month, then average across months. Statistical significance is assessed using the t-statistic of the mean rank IC. We also construct equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature. To evaluate combined strategies, we standardize the three most significant features (gross_prof, idio_vol, leverage) by their monthly cross-sectional rank, form a composite score, and construct a long-short portfolio based on quintiles of the composite.

## Results
The rank IC analysis (Table 1) identifies three features with statistically significant predictive power at the 5% level: gross_prof (mean IC = 0.0293, t = 3.83), idio_vol (mean IC = -0.0266, t = -3.76), and leverage (mean IC = 0.0137, t = 2.56). Accruals shows a marginal positive IC (t = 2.06), while asset_growth is weakly negative (t = -1.87). The remaining features (book_to_market, log_mktcap, turnover, rd_intensity) do not show significant univariate predictive power.

Long-short quintile portfolios confirm these findings. The gross_prof long-short portfolio earns 0.88% monthly with an annualized Sharpe of 1.19; the idio_vol long-short (buying low idio_vol, selling high idio_vol) earns 0.78% monthly with a Sharpe of 1.15; and the leverage long-short earns 0.50% monthly with a Sharpe of 0.99. A composite strategy combining standardized ranks of gross_prof (positive), idio_vol (negative), and leverage (positive) produces a monthly long-short return of approximately 1.2% with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.8.

## Discussion
Gross profitability is a strong positive predictor, consistent with the profitability premium documented in the literature. High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns, supporting the low-volatility anomaly. Leverage shows a positive relation with returns, suggesting that within this sample, higher leverage is associated with higher expected returns, possibly reflecting a distress risk premium. The composite strategy benefits from diversification across these three signals, as their pairwise rank IC correlations are modest (approximately 0.1–0.3 in absolute value).

## Limitations
The sample consists of stocks that are index constituents at month 120, introducing survivorship bias that may overstate return predictability. The analysis uses point-in-time annual fundamental data but does not account for reporting lag beyond the end of the fiscal year. Transaction costs, short-sale constraints, and market impact are not considered. The composite strategy is constructed in-sample, and its Sharpe ratio may be inflated relative to out-of-sample performance. Finally, the relatively short sample (10 years) limits the robustness of the findings across different market regimes.
