# Title
Leverage as the Only Robust Cross-Sectional Return Signal in a 120-Month Equity Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate nine stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns in an unbalanced panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Using monthly Spearman rank ICs and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, only leverage has a statistically reliable positive relation with future returns. Leverage earns a mean rank IC of 0.0164 (t = 2.74) and a top-minus-bottom quintile return of 0.52% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. The remaining characteristics have low and statistically insignificant ICs, with no long-short Sharpe above 0.74. A monthly long high-leverage/short low-leverage strategy would have delivered approximately 6.2% annualized before costs in this sample.

## Methods
The dataset is a monthly stock panel with 34,708 rows, 500 distinct stocks, and months 1 through 120. The target is `ret_next`, the stock return over month t+1. The predictor set contains `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

For each month, I compute the Spearman rank correlation between each feature and `ret_next` across all stocks with available data. The monthly rank ICs are averaged over the 120 months. The reported t-statistic is the mean IC divided by the IC standard deviation, multiplied by the square root of the number of months. I also evaluate equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month. A feature is treated as a valid signal if it has a statistically reliable rank IC, a consistent long-short return direction, and an economically interpretable magnitude. Returns are expressed as decimals.

## Results
Only `leverage` qualifies as a valid signal. Its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0164 with an IC standard deviation of 0.0654 and a t-statistic of 2.74. The IC is positive in 59% of months. The equal-weighted top-minus-bottom leverage quintile portfolio earns 0.52% per month, corresponding to about 6.24% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92.

The other features are weak and statistically insignificant. `asset_growth` has the next strongest t-statistic at 1.16, with a mean rank IC of 0.0055 and a long-short return of 0.37% per month. `accruals` has a t-statistic of 1.03, a mean rank IC of 0.0059, and a long-short return of 0.24% per month. `idio_vol` has a t-statistic of 0.86, a mean rank IC of 0.0056, and a long-short return of 0.13% per month. The remaining signals are close to zero: `gross_prof` has a t-statistic of 0.23, `log_mktcap` 0.26, `book_to_market` -0.28, `turnover` -0.10, and `rd_intensity` 0.20. Their long-short Sharpe ratios are all below 0.75, and most are below 0.25.

## Discussion
The main finding is that leverage is the only characteristic in this panel with a reliable positive relation to next-month returns. High-leverage stocks outperform low-leverage stocks by a modest but statistically meaningful margin. This could reflect compensation for financial distress risk, but it may also reflect industry composition, value exposure, or omitted risk factors. Notably, `book_to_market` is not predictive here, so the leverage result is not simply a value effect.

The economic magnitude is modest. A gross long-short return of 0.52% per month, or about 6.2% annualized, with a Sharpe ratio of 0.92, is attractive in isolation but likely sensitive to transaction costs, borrow costs, and risk controls. The other features, including profitability, investment, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, turnover, and R&D intensity, do not show reliable cross-sectional predictive power in this sample.

## Limitations
The universe consists of stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, which introduces survivorship bias. The sample covers only 120 months and becomes more balanced over time as more current constituents enter the history. The analysis does not account for transaction costs, shorting costs, or market impact. No sector, size, or risk-factor neutralization is applied. Testing nine signals increases the risk of false discovery, although leverage is the only feature with |t| > 2. Equal-weighted quintile portfolios may be sensitive to small, illiquid, or extreme stocks. Finally, accounting features are based on stale annual reports, and daily-return-based features such as `idio_vol` may contain noise.
