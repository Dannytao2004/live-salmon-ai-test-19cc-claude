# Valid Cross-Sectional Signals in a Monthly Large-Cap Equity Panel

## Abstract
I evaluate nine stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns using 34,387 monthly observations on 500 index constituents over 120 months. Signals are assessed with average monthly rank ICs, IC t-statistics, and equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios. Gross profitability, accruals, leverage, and idiosyncratic volatility show statistically reliable predictive power, while book-to-market, size, asset growth, turnover, and R&D intensity do not meet conventional significance thresholds. A combined long-short strategy using the four validated signals is estimated to return about 7.4% annually with an expected Sharpe ratio near 1.2, before costs.

## Methods
The dataset contains one row per stock per month for 500 stocks and 120 months. For each feature, I use the provided monthly Spearman rank IC relative to `ret_next`, averaged across all months, and its t-statistic computed as mean IC / standard deviation of IC × √120. I also use long-short quintile portfolio returns: each month, stocks are ranked by the feature, and an equal-weighted portfolio buys the top quintile and shorts the bottom quintile. I treat a signal as valid if its IC t-statistic is at least 1.96 in absolute value and its long-short return has a consistent sign and economically meaningful magnitude. I then form a combined strategy by equal-weighting the valid long-short portfolios, reversing the sign for signals with negative expected direction.

## Results
Four features are valid signals:

- `gross_prof`: positive. Mean rank IC = 0.0293, t = 3.83. Long-short mean monthly return = 0.88%, annualized Sharpe = 1.19.
- `accruals`: positive. Mean rank IC = 0.0130, t = 2.06. Long-short mean monthly return = 0.31%, annualized Sharpe = 0.50.
- `idio_vol`: negative. Mean rank IC = -0.0266, t = -3.76. High-minus-low long-short mean monthly return = -0.78%, so the valid long-low/short-high direction earns +0.78% monthly with annualized Sharpe = 1.15.
- `leverage`: positive. Mean rank IC = 0.0137, t = 2.56. Long-short mean monthly return = 0.50%, annualized Sharpe = 0.99.

Five features are rejected:

- `book_to_market`: mean rank IC = 0.0029, t = 0.54, long-short Sharpe = -0.11.
- `log_mktcap`: mean rank IC = -0.0043, t = -0.67, long-short Sharpe = -0.14.
- `asset_growth`: mean rank IC = -0.0164, t = -1.87; economically plausible but below the 5% significance threshold.
- `turnover`: mean rank IC = 0.0042, t = 0.64, long-short Sharpe = 0.23.
- `rd_intensity`: mean rank IC = 0.0018, t = 0.30, long-short Sharpe = 0.20.

The proposed strategy equal-weights the four validated long-short portfolios, using the correct sign for `idio_vol`. Its expected monthly return is the average of the four component returns in the valid direction: (0.88% + 0.31% + 0.78% + 0.50%) / 4 = 0.62% monthly, or about 7.4% annually. Given the component Sharpe ratios and conservative allowance for cross-signal correlation, I estimate the combined annualized Sharpe at approximately 1.2.

## Discussion
The strongest signal is gross profitability, consistent with a quality/profitability premium: more profitable firms earn higher subsequent returns. Idiosyncratic volatility is strongly negative, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly: high-volatility stocks underperform. Accruals and leverage are positive in this panel, which is notable because some prior literature suggests the opposite for accruals and distress-related leverage. These directions should therefore be treated as sample-specific until confirmed out-of-sample. The rejected signals are weak in both rank IC and long-short performance, so including them would add noise rather than expected return. A diversified combination of the four valid signals improves risk-adjusted performance because their individual Sharpe ratios vary and their return sources are not perfectly aligned.

## Limitations
The universe is limited to 500 current index constituents, which can induce survivorship and backfill bias. Transaction costs, slippage, and borrowing costs are not deducted from the estimated returns. The combined Sharpe estimate relies on approximate diversification and assumes reasonably stable correlations among signals. Monthly rank ICs can vary across regimes, and past predictive relationships may not persist. Some detected directions, especially positive accruals and leverage, differ from prior literature and may be sample-specific.
