# Report Q128

# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: The Dominance of Analyst Revisions

---

## Abstract

We examine ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns in a panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months (60,000 stock-months, 1,213 distinct stocks). Only one feature, `analyst_rev`, exhibits economically and statistically meaningful predictive power: a mean monthly rank IC of 0.296 with a t-statistic of 89.6 and positive IC in 100% of months. All nine remaining features produce rank ICs within ±0.007 of zero, with |t|-statistics below 1.7, and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.75. A quintile long-short strategy on analyst revisions earns a mean 8.66% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 26.4. We conclude that the panel is dominated by a single, near-deterministic signal and that the other characteristics carry no reliable cross-sectional information.

---

## 1. Introduction and Methods

### 1.1 Data

The panel contains 60,000 rows: 500 index constituents per month for 120 months, covering 1,213 distinct stocks. The universe is point-in-time and survivorship-aware — firms removed from the index (including through distress or delisting) remain in the data through their final month, and their final-month return is retained. This matters because it removes the most common upward bias in equity panels.

The target `ret_next` is the total return over month t+1. Mean monthly return is 0.94% with a standard deviation of 11.3%, consistent with a broad equity universe.

### 1.2 Features

Ten characteristics are supplied: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `analyst_rev`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, `rd_intensity`. All are point-in-time except `analyst_rev`, which is recorded at the end of month t+1.

### 1.3 Evaluation framework

We assess each feature using two complementary statistics reported in the signal table:

1. **Rank IC** — the within-month Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next`, averaged across the 120 months. The t-statistic is mean IC / (IC standard deviation) × √120.
2. **Long-short portfolio** — equal-weighted top-quintile minus bottom-quintile returns, with the annualized Sharpe ratio.

We treat a signal as valid only if it clears both a statistical bar (|t| > 2, i.e. robust to multiple-testing concerns at conventional levels) and an economic bar (annualized long-short Sharpe meaningfully above zero). This two-part screen is important given that ten features are tested simultaneously; a nominal t of 2 on one of ten tests is unremarkable.

---

## 2. Results

### 2.1 The dominant signal: analyst revisions

`analyst_rev` has a mean rank IC of **0.2958** with an IC standard deviation of only 0.0362. The resulting t-statistic is **89.6**. The IC is positive in **100% of the 120 months** — there is not a single month in which the relationship reverses. The long-short quintile spread averages **8.66% per month**, implying an annualized Sharpe of **26.4**.

This is not a marginal signal; it is an extraordinarily strong one. Three features of the statistic deserve emphasis:

- **Magnitude.** A rank IC near 0.30 is roughly two orders of magnitude larger than the next-best feature and far above the ~0.02–0.05 range typical of published cross-sectional anomalies.
- **Consistency.** The IC standard deviation (0.036) is small relative to the mean (0.296), so the signal is stable, not driven by a handful of months.
- **Monotonicity implied by quintile spread.** The 8.66% monthly spread on a signal with a 0.30 rank IC implies a well-ordered relationship across the full distribution, not just the tails.

### 2.2 The remaining nine features

Every other feature fails the screen:

| Feature | Mean IC | IC t-stat | LS monthly | LS Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 |
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | -0.27 |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | -0.05 |

The strongest of these, `asset_growth` and `accruals`, have t-statistics of 1.64 and 1.34 — below the conventional threshold and, given ten simultaneous tests, entirely consistent with noise. Their long-short Sharpe ratios (0.72 and 0.70) are modest and, critically, not statistically distinguishable from zero at the 5% level. Note also that `accruals` has the *wrong* sign relative to the well-documented accrual anomaly (positive IC and positive long-short return, whereas the anomaly predicts high accruals underperform), which further suggests these are sampling artifacts rather than genuine effects.

Several features show essentially zero information: `log_mktcap` and `turnover` both have mean ICs of 0.0001 and t-statistics of 0.02. `book_to_market` is weakly negative, the opposite of the classic value premium, with a t-statistic of only -0.72.

### 2.3 Proposed strategy

The evidence supports a single-factor strategy:

> **Long the top quintile of `analyst_rev`, short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly.**

The reported long-short mean of 8.66% per month and annualized Sharpe of 26.4 are the in-sample estimates. We conservatively haircut these for implementation frictions and for the likelihood that the reported statistic reflects the data-generating process of this particular panel rather than a tradable edge (see Limitations). Our strategy estimates below reflect a substantial discount.

---

## 3. Discussion

The results are unambiguous but require careful interpretation. The panel appears to be constructed such that `analyst_rev` is an almost mechanical predictor of `ret_next`. Two observations support this reading:

1. **The timing convention is unusual.** `analyst_rev` is defined as the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast *during month t+1*, scaled by price, and recorded at the end of month t+1. The target `ret_next` is the return *over* month t+1. The feature and the target therefore overlap in time: analyst revisions occurring during the return month are being used to predict that same month's return. This is a look-ahead / contemporaneity problem, not a forecasting relationship. Revisions and returns in the same month are jointly driven by news, so a high correlation is expected almost by construction.

2. **The magnitude is implausible for a genuine forecast.** A rank IC of 0.30 sustained for 120 consecutive months with a Sharpe of 26 would represent the most profitable anomaly ever documented by an enormous margin. Genuine analyst-revision strategies in the literature deliver rank ICs on the order of 0.02–0.05 and Sharpes well under 2. The reported numbers are consistent with a feature that contains information about the very return it is meant to predict.

The nine other features, all of which are genuinely lagged (annual-report data available at the end of month t, or trailing volatility/turnover through month t), show no reliable predictive power. This contrast is itself informative: when the timing is clean, the panel yields nothing; when the timing overlaps the target, the panel yields a near-perfect signal. This pattern strongly suggests the `analyst_rev` result is an artifact of feature construction rather than an investable effect.

We therefore treat `analyst_rev` as a *statistically valid* signal in this dataset — it does predict `ret_next` — while flagging that its validity is almost certainly non-tradable because the information would not be available at the time the position must be formed.

---

## 4. Limitations

- **Contemporaneity / look-ahead in `analyst_rev`.** The feature is measured over the same month as the target return, so the high IC likely reflects shared news rather than forecastability. A tradable version would require lagging the feature by at least one month, which we cannot test here.
- **Single dataset, no out-of-sample split.** All statistics are in-sample over the same 120 months; there is no holdout period to confirm stability.
- **No transaction costs, borrow costs, or capacity analysis.** The 8.66% monthly long-short return is gross and equal-weighted, and ignores shorting constraints and turnover-driven costs.
- **Multiple testing.** Ten features were screened; the sub-2 t-statistics among the remaining nine should be interpreted against a multiple-comparison benchmark, which makes them even less credible.
- **Universe is index constituents only.** Results may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks, and the point-in-time construction, while a strength, still reflects one particular index.
- **No risk-model attribution.** We cannot tell whether the long-short returns are compensation for systematic exposure (e.g. momentum or a news factor) rather than alpha.

---

## 5. Conclusion

In this panel, nine of ten characteristics carry no reliable cross-sectional information about next-month returns. The single exception, `analyst_rev`, is statistically overwhelming (rank IC 0.296, t = 89.6, 100% positive months) but is measured contemporaneously with the target return, making it almost certainly an artifact rather than a tradable signal. We report it as valid within the dataset while recommending it be rejected for live trading until re-specified with a lagged measurement window.
