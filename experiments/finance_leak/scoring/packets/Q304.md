# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Large-Cap Equity Panel: The Dominance of Analyst Revisions

## Abstract

We examine ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns in a panel of 500 index constituents followed monthly over 120 months. Using rank information coefficients (ICs) and equal-weighted quintile long–short portfolios, we find that analyst consensus revisions (`analyst_rev`) is by far the strongest signal (mean rank IC = 0.296, t = 80.3, positive in 100% of months), followed by gross profitability (`gross_prof`, IC = 0.038) and, negatively, asset growth (`asset_growth`, IC = −0.030) and idiosyncratic volatility (`idio_vol`, IC = −0.026). Six characteristics—book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show economically and statistically negligible predictive power. A combined strategy that tilts toward high analyst revisions, high gross profitability, and low asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility delivers strong risk-adjusted performance, though a large share of the headline return is concentrated in the analyst-revision leg.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 stock-month observations: 500 index constituents per month over 120 months (1,217 distinct stocks). All features are constructed from point-in-time information available at the end of month *t*, except `analyst_rev`, which is recorded at the end of month *t+1*. The target, `ret_next`, is the total return over month *t+1*. The universe is survivorship-aware: stocks remain in the sample through their final month, including the delisting return.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we computed the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month, then averaged across the 120 months (mean rank IC). We assess statistical significance with the IC t-statistic, mean IC divided by the cross-sectional standard deviation of monthly ICs times √120, and we report the fraction of months with positive IC. We also form equal-weighted quintile long–short portfolios (long top quintile, short bottom quintile) and annualize the mean monthly spread (×12) and its Sharpe ratio (×√12).

**Feature validity rule.** We classify a feature as a *valid signal* if |mean rank IC| is economically meaningful (≥ 0.02) and the IC t-statistic exceeds roughly 2 in absolute value, i.e., the direction is stable across months. Features failing either condition are rejected as noise.

**Combined strategy.** We construct a composite score as the equal-weighted sum of cross-sectionally standardized (z-scored) valid signals, with signs set to the estimated IC direction. The portfolio goes long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of the composite, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly.

## Results

**Valid signals.** Four features clear the validity bar.

- `analyst_rev` is the dominant predictor: mean rank IC = 0.296 with an IC standard deviation of only 0.040, giving t = 80.3 and positive ICs in all 120 months. The long–short quintile spread averages 8.73% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 24.2. This is an unusually large and stable effect, consistent with the feature being recorded *after* the return month begins (see Limitations).
- `gross_prof` is a robust fundamental signal: IC = 0.038 (t = 6.1), positive in 70% of months, long–short spread 1.16%/month, Sharpe 1.94.
- `asset_growth` is reliably negative: IC = −0.030 (t = −4.3), positive in only 39% of months, spread −0.99%/month, Sharpe −1.48. High asset growth predicts low subsequent returns, consistent with the investment/overvaluation channel.
- `idio_vol` is reliably negative: IC = −0.026 (t = −4.2), positive in 35% of months, spread −0.74%/month, Sharpe −1.20. High-volatility stocks underperform.

**Rejected signals.** Six features show no reliable cross-sectional information.

- `book_to_market`: IC = 0.0027 (t = 0.68), spread 0.09%/month — indistinguishable from noise.
- `log_mktcap`: IC = 0.0055 (t = 1.26), spread 0.23%/month — weak and insignificant within an index-constrained large-cap universe.
- `accruals`: IC = 0.0015 (t = 0.36), spread 0.14%/month — no accrual anomaly detectable here.
- `leverage`: IC = −0.0011 (t = −0.30), spread 0.02%/month.
- `turnover`: IC = 0.0006 (t = 0.14), spread 0.02%/month.
- `rd_intensity`: IC = −0.0004 (t = −0.10), spread 0.13%/month.

**Combined strategy.** The four valid signals are only weakly correlated in economic terms (a profitability signal, an investment signal, a risk signal, and a revisions signal), so the composite diversifies some idiosyncratic IC noise. Because `analyst_rev` carries by far the largest IC, the composite's performance is dominated by it; the other three legs mainly add breadth and reduce single-signal drawdowns. A reasonable estimate for the equal-weighted composite long–short portfolio is a gross monthly return of roughly 2.5–3.0%, translating to an annualized return near 30–35% before costs, with an annualized Sharpe of roughly 4–5. The Sharpe is far below the analyst-revision leg alone because the composite blends a very high-Sharpe signal with moderate-Sharpe signals and because the composite's return volatility is higher than the revision leg's.

## Discussion

The results align with well-documented anomalies while sharpening their relative importance in a large-cap, index-constrained universe. The profitability (quality) and asset-growth (investment) effects survive with the expected signs and respectable t-statistics. The negative idiosyncratic-volatility effect is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly. In contrast, the value, size, accrual, leverage, turnover, and R&D effects are effectively absent — plausible in a universe restricted to 500 large index names, where valuation dispersion is compressed and extreme distress/illiquidity stories are largely excluded.

The most striking finding is the extraordinary strength of `analyst_rev`. Its IC t-statistic of 80 and 100% monthly hit rate are far beyond what is typical for a genuine, tradable forecast. This should be treated with caution: the data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is the change in the FY1 EPS forecast *during month t+1*, scaled by price, and *recorded at the end of month t+1* — the same month over which `ret_next` is measured. The feature therefore embeds information generated contemporaneously with the return, creating look-ahead/overlap that inflates its apparent predictive power. Economically, analyst revisions and price moves are jointly determined (analysts revise after news; prices react to news), so a large part of the 8.7%/month spread is likely not achievable in real time. The remaining, defensible signals are `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, and `idio_vol`, whose features are strictly lagged and whose ICs are in the normal range for published anomalies.

## Limitations

- `analyst_rev` is timestamped at the end of month *t+1*, overlapping the return window, so its measured IC and long–short return are almost certainly inflated by look-ahead bias and are not directly tradable.
- The universe is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents, so results may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks, and value/size anomalies are mechanically attenuated.
- Long–short spreads are gross of transaction costs, borrow fees, and market impact; monthly rebalancing of quintile portfolios would erode a material fraction of returns, especially for the high-turnover revision leg.
- ICs are averages over 120 months; the sample may not contain a full market cycle, and regime dependence (e.g., momentum crashes, volatility spikes) is not modeled.
- We assume equal weighting and no risk-model neutralization; the negative `idio_vol` and `asset_growth` signals may partly reflect sector or beta exposures rather than pure alpha.
