# Cross-Sectional Predictors of Monthly Equity Returns: Evidence from a 500-Stock Panel

**Abstract**  
We evaluate nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a monthly panel of 500 index stocks over 120 months. Using cross-sectional rank ICs, we find gross profitability to be a positive predictor, idiosyncratic volatility a negative predictor, and leverage and accruals to be weaker positive predictors. A composite long-short quintile strategy based on these four signals is estimated to deliver an annualized return of about 8% with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.3 before transaction costs. The results are broadly consistent with quality and low-risk anomalies, though the positive accruals signal departs from the traditional accruals anomaly.

## Methods

The dataset contains 34,387 stock-month observations for 500 stocks over months 1 to 120. The target is `ret_next`, the stock return over month \(t+1\). For each feature in each month, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next`, then average these monthly rank ICs across 120 months. The reported t-statistic is

\[
t = \frac{\text{mean rank IC}}{\text{std rank IC}} \times \sqrt{120}.
\]

We also report equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature. A feature is treated as valid if its IC t-statistic exceeds approximately 2 in absolute value. Accounting variables are from the latest annual report available at the end of month \(t\), and market-based variables use trailing data ending at month \(t\), so no future information is used.

## Results

Four features pass the validation threshold. The table below reports their direction, average rank IC, t-statistic, and long-short performance.

| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short mean monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | positive | 0.0293 | 3.83 | 0.88% | 1.19 |
| `idio_vol` | negative | -0.0266 | -3.76 | -0.78% | -1.15 |
| `leverage` | positive | 0.0137 | 2.56 | 0.50% | 0.99 |
| `accruals` | positive | 0.0130 | 2.06 | 0.31% | 0.50 |

The remaining five features are rejected because their ICs are statistically indistinguishable from zero at conventional significance levels:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `asset_growth` | -0.0164 | -1.87 | Weak negative signal, but not significant at the 5% level |
| `log_mktcap` | -0.0043 | -0.67 | Near-zero IC and low t-statistic |
| `book_to_market` | 0.0029 | 0.54 | Near-zero IC and low t-statistic |
| `turnover` | 0.0042 | 0.64 | Near-zero IC and low t-statistic |
| `rd_intensity` | 0.0018 | 0.30 | Near-zero IC and low t-statistic |

For a composite strategy, each month we cross-sectionally standardize the four valid signals, average them with signs matching their return directions, rank the composite score, and form an equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile. Based on the individual long-short returns and assuming moderate positive correlation among signals, we estimate a gross annualized return of approximately 8% with an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 1.3.

## Discussion

Gross profitability is the strongest positive signal, suggesting that profitable firms earn higher subsequent returns in this sample. Idiosyncratic volatility is strongly negative, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly: high-idiosyncratic-risk stocks underperform low-risk stocks. Leverage is positively related to returns, potentially reflecting a distress- or leverage-related risk premium. Accruals are positively related to returns, which contrasts with the usual accruals anomaly; this may reflect sample composition, the specific accruals definition, or limited power. Asset growth is weakly negative, consistent with the investment anomaly, but it does not meet the significance threshold. Value, size, turnover, and R&D intensity do not appear to be reliable signals in this index stock panel.

## Limitations

- The sample consists of index constituents as of month 120, creating survivorship bias because stocks removed from the index before month 120 are excluded.
- Performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, short-selling costs, and implementation frictions.
- Leverage and accruals are only marginally significant and may not survive strict multiple-testing adjustments.
- The sample period is limited to 120 months, and early months contain relatively few stocks.
- Accounting data may be subject to restatements or reporting lags despite using latest-available annual reports.
