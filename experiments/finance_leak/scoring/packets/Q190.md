# Earnings Surprise Drift as the Only Robust Cross-Sectional Predictor in a Monthly Index-Stock Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months. Standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) is the only statistically significant signal, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.0998 (t = 23.25) and a top-minus-bottom quintile return of 3.04% per month (annualized Sharpe 7.31). The other nine characteristics, including value, profitability, size, asset growth, accruals, volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity, do not show reliable predictive power. A monthly-rebalanced long-short SUE strategy has an expected annualized return of about 36.5% before costs. The result is economically large and should be interpreted cautiously given the in-sample nature of the evidence and possible earnings-timing look-ahead.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations: 500 index stocks per month for 120 months. For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with next-month returns within each month, then average these rank ICs over the 120 months. The t-statistic is computed as mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation, multiplied by sqrt(120). We also form equal-weighted quintile portfolios: the long-short return buys the top quintile of each feature and sells the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly.

We define a feature as a valid signal if its rank IC is statistically significant at conventional levels after accounting for the multiple signals tested. Under this criterion, only SUE is valid.

## Results
SUE is a strong positive predictor of next-month returns. Its mean rank IC is 0.0998 with a t-statistic of 23.25, and its IC is positive in 99% of months. The equal-weighted top-minus-bottom SUE quintile portfolio earns 3.04% per month, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31.

The remaining features are not statistically reliable. Book-to-market has a rank IC of -0.0030 (t = -0.72), gross profitability 0.0039 (t = 0.93), log market cap 0.0001 (t = 0.02), asset growth 0.0061 (t = 1.64), accruals 0.0055 (t = 1.34), idiosyncratic volatility 0.0043 (t = 1.14), leverage -0.0043 (t = -1.00), turnover 0.0001 (t = 0.02), and R&D intensity -0.0002 (t = -0.05). Asset growth and accruals show weak positive long-short returns, but their rank ICs are not statistically significant after accounting for multiple testing.

## Discussion
The SUE result is consistent with post-earnings-announcement drift: positive earnings surprises tend to be followed by continued positive returns, and negative surprises by continued negative returns. However, the observed magnitude is unusually high. The data dictionary states that firms announce quarterly earnings in the month after the fiscal quarter ends, so as constructed, SUE may incorporate earnings information before it is publicly available for fiscal quarters ending in month t. A tradable implementation would need to lag SUE so that only announced earnings are used.

The failure of the other characteristics may reflect the specific index universe, the sample period, or the definitions used. Several well-known factors, such as value and profitability, show no significant spread in this panel.

## Limitations
- Results are in-sample and do not account for transaction costs, market impact, or short-selling constraints.
- SUE timing may be affected by earnings announcement lag; using actual EPS for quarters ending near month-end could introduce look-ahead bias unless the signal is properly lagged.
- The panel contains only 500 index constituents per month, so findings may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks.
- The weak but positive long-short returns for asset growth and accruals may merit further study, but they are not statistically reliable here.
