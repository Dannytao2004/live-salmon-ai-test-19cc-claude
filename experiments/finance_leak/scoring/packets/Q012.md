# Report Q012

# No Robust Cross-Sectional Equity Signals After Excluding Look-Ahead Bias

## Abstract
We examine ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. One characteristic, analyst consensus revision, exhibits a mean rank IC of 0.296 and a long-short annualized Sharpe of 26.4, but it is recorded at the end of the return month and therefore suffers from look-ahead bias. Excluding that feature, none of the remaining nine characteristics has a rank IC t-statistic above 1.64 in absolute value. We conclude that the panel contains no valid tradable cross-sectional signal and propose a passive equal-weight index strategy with an expected annual return of about 11.2% and an expected Sharpe of about 0.70.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations: 500 index constituents per month for 120 months. For each characteristic and each month, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between the characteristic and next-month returns, `ret_next`. We average these rank ICs over the 120 months and compute a t-statistic as the mean divided by the standard deviation, multiplied by the square root of 120. A feature is considered a valid signal only if its absolute rank IC t-statistic is at least 2.0 and the feature is available at portfolio formation, i.e., at the end of month `t`. We also form equal-weighted top-minus-bottom quintile portfolios to assess economic magnitude and annualized Sharpe ratios.

## Results
The table below summarizes the univariate signal statistics and decisions.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | L/S annualized Sharpe | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.27 | Reject |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.26 | Reject |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.08 | Reject |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.72 | Reject |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.70 | Reject |
| analyst_rev | 0.2958 | 89.59 | 26.38 | Reject: look-ahead |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.08 | Reject |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.05 | Reject |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.02 | Reject |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.13 | Reject |

The only feature with economically and statistically strong univariate performance is `analyst_rev`. However, the data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month `t+1`, recorded at the end of month `t+1`. Since the target return is also over month `t+1`, this feature is not known at the end of month `t` and cannot be used in a tradable monthly strategy. Its extreme Sharpe ratio is a classic symptom of look-ahead bias.

Among the remaining nine point-in-time features, the strongest is `asset_growth`, with a rank IC t-statistic of 1.64. This does not meet the conventional threshold of 2.0, and no other feature approaches statistical significance. We therefore reject all nine as reliable signals.

## Discussion
The failure of standard fundamental and risk characteristics in this panel is notable. Variables such as book-to-market, gross profitability, asset growth, and accruals have historically shown predictive power in some markets, but here their rank ICs are economically small and statistically indistinguishable from zero. This may reflect the specific universe of large index constituents, the sample period, or point-in-time data cleaning that removes some biases.

The `analyst_rev` result illustrates how easy it is to generate apparently strong backtest performance when feature timing is misaligned with the return window. Once the look-ahead feature is removed, there is no basis for active cross-sectional positioning. The proposed strategy is therefore a passive equal-weight long-only portfolio of the 500 index constituents, rebalanced monthly. Using the sample mean next-month return of 0.936%, the expected annual return is approximately 11.2%. Assuming a diversified equal-weight index annualized volatility of about 16%, the expected annual Sharpe ratio is approximately 0.70.

## Limitations
- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 index constituents, limiting statistical power.
- No transaction costs, short-selling costs, or market impact are modeled.
- The passive strategy Sharpe ratio is estimated using an assumed annualized volatility of about 16%, not an observed index return series.
- Weak signals could still be real but are statistically indistinguishable from zero in this panel.
