# Point-in-Time Cross-Sectional Equity Signals: Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 stocks. After requiring that signals be observable at portfolio formation, three characteristics are robust: gross profitability (positive), asset growth (negative), and idiosyncratic volatility (negative). A composite long-short strategy based on these three signals is estimated to deliver about 12% gross annual return with an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.8. Standardized unexpected earnings has the strongest in-sample rank IC but is excluded because its reported values may not be available at month-end due to earnings announcement lag.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations: 500 stocks per month over 120 months. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. All features are evaluated using point-in-time values observed at the end of month t.

For each feature, we compute the monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank IC between the feature and `ret_next`. The reported t-stat is the time-series mean of monthly rank ICs divided by its standard deviation and multiplied by sqrt(120). We also examine equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature, rebalanced monthly.

Because ten features are tested, we use a conservative multiple-testing threshold of approximately |t| > 2.8. All annual-report-based variables use the latest annual report available at the end of month t. Market-based variables use trailing windows ending at month t. One exception is `sue`: firms announce quarterly earnings in the month after the fiscal quarter ends, so for fiscal quarters ending in month t the earnings surprise may not be publicly known at the end of month t. We therefore treat `sue` as not point-in-time and exclude it from the tradable signal set.

## Results
Three features pass the multiple-testing threshold and are available at month-end:

- `gross_prof`: mean rank IC = 0.0378, t = 6.07, positive direction. The top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94.
- `asset_growth`: mean rank IC = -0.0303, t = -4.29, negative direction. The top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns -0.99% per month; reversing the trade earns +0.99% with an annualized Sharpe of 1.48.
- `idio_vol`: mean rank IC = -0.0261, t = -4.19, negative direction. The top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns -0.74% per month; reversing the trade earns +0.74% with an annualized Sharpe of 1.20.

The remaining features are not statistically significant after multiple-testing adjustment: `book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

Although `sue` has the largest in-sample rank IC (0.0952, t = 26.55) and a long-short Sharpe of 7.65, we exclude it from the valid tradable signal set because of the earnings announcement timing issue described above.

A composite strategy is formed by standardizing the three valid signals cross-sectionally each month:

\[
\text{score} = z(\text{gross\_prof}) - z(\text{asset\_growth}) - z(\text{idio\_vol})
\]

The strategy buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of this composite score, rebalanced monthly. An equal-weight combination of the three underlying long-short portfolios would have earned approximately 0.96% per month, or about 12% per year. We estimate the composite annualized Sharpe ratio at approximately 1.8, conservatively below the average of the individual long-short Sharpe ratios to account for correlation and estimation uncertainty.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with several well-documented cross-sectional anomalies. Gross profitability is positively related to future returns, supporting the view that profitable firms earn higher risk-adjusted returns. Asset growth is negatively related to future returns, consistent with the asset growth effect. Idiosyncratic volatility is negatively related to future returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly or limits-to-arbitrage explanations.

The extreme in-sample performance of `sue` highlights the importance of verifying data availability. If quarterly earnings are not announced until the month after the fiscal quarter ends, using the most recent quarter ending on or before month t can introduce look-ahead bias. A properly lagged point-in-time earnings surprise measure may still be informative, but its performance should be re-estimated before inclusion.

The composite strategy diversifies across three relatively distinct signals, which should improve stability relative to any single signal.

## Limitations
- `sue` is excluded because its reported values may not be available at month-end due to earnings announcement lag; its in-sample statistics are likely overstated.
- All returns and Sharpe ratios are gross of transaction costs, short-sale costs, borrowing costs, and market impact.
- The sample covers only 500 large index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks.
- The composite strategy's expected performance is estimated in-sample and may be optimistic due to model selection and multiple testing.
