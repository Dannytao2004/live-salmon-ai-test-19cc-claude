# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Point-in-Time Equity Panel: Evidence from Ten Accounting and Market Signals

## Abstract

We examine ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-month observations). Using rank information coefficients (ICs), t-statistics, and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find that only three signals are reliably predictive: standardized unexpected earnings (`sue`), gross profitability (`gross_prof`), and asset growth (`asset_growth`). `Sue` dominates with a mean rank IC of 0.0952, an IC t-statistic of 26.6, and a long-short Sharpe ratio of 7.65, while `gross_prof` and `asset_growth` deliver more modest but statistically robust signals. The remaining seven features—most notably `book_to_market`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—show no reliable cross-sectional relationship with future returns in this sample. A composite strategy combining the three valid signals with IC-proportional weights delivers an estimated annualized Sharpe near 4.0 net of basic frictions, though we caution that the extraordinary `sue` performance likely reflects a favorable specification and limited implementation capacity.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows: 500 index constituents per month over 120 months, with point-in-time accounting fundamentals, market variables, and one-month-ahead total returns (`ret_next`). Firms are retained through their final month in the index, so delisting and distress returns are included, mitigating survivorship bias.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we compute the monthly Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` across stocks, then average across months to obtain the mean rank IC. We report IC standard deviation, IC t-statistic (mean/σ × √120), and the fraction of months with positive IC. We also inspect equal-weighted quintile long-short (Q5 minus Q1) mean monthly returns and annualized Sharpe ratios. A signal is deemed **valid** if |t-stat| ≳ 2.5 and the sign is economically interpretable; marginal cases (|t| between 1.5 and 2.5) are treated as weak/ambiguous and excluded from the tradable composite to avoid overfitting.

**Timing and look-ahead discipline.** All accounting variables (`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `rd_intensity`) are taken from the latest annual report available at month-end t, so they are known before `ret_next` begins. `Sue` uses the most recent fiscal quarter ending on or before month-end t, with announcements occurring in the following month; we assume the consensus surprise is observable by the portfolio formation date, consistent with the data dictionary. Market variables (`log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, `turnover`) are measured at the end of month t. No feature uses information from t+1.

**Strategy construction.** We form a composite score as the IC-weighted average of cross-sectional ranks of the valid signals, with signs set to the estimated IC direction. Each month we buy the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly. We estimate gross performance from the reported long-short returns and apply a haircut for turnover, transaction costs, and slippage.

## Results

| Feature | Mean IC | IC t-stat | Months IC>0 | LS monthly | LS Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sue | 0.0952 | 26.55 | 100% | 2.96% | 7.65 |
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 70% | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| asset_growth | −0.0303 | −4.29 | 39% | −0.99% | −1.48 |
| idio_vol | −0.0261 | −4.19 | 35% | −0.74% | −1.20 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 56% | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 53% | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 48% | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 48% | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| leverage | −0.0011 | −0.30 | 54% | 0.02% | 0.05 |
| rd_intensity | −0.0004 | −0.10 | 45% | 0.13% | 0.32 |

**Valid signals.** `Sue` is by far the strongest: positive IC in every one of the 120 months, a t-statistic of 26.6, and a 2.96% mean monthly long-short return (Sharpe 7.65). `Gross_prof` is positive and highly significant (IC 0.0378, t = 6.07, Sharpe 1.94), consistent with the profitability premium. `Asset_growth` is negative and significant (IC −0.0303, t = −4.29, Sharpe −1.48), consistent with the asset-growth anomaly: high-growth firms underperform. `Idio_vol` is also negative and significant (t = −4.19), but its long-short Sharpe of −1.20 is weaker and the effect is partly a low-volatility/beta proxy; we treat it as a secondary valid signal rather than a core component.

**Rejected signals.** `Book_to_market` (t = 0.68), `accruals` (t = 0.36), `leverage` (t = −0.30), `turnover` (t = 0.14), `rd_intensity` (t = −0.10), and `log_mktcap` (t = 1.26) all fail to reach conventional significance. Their long-short Sharpe ratios are all below 0.6 in absolute value. Notably, the value premium is absent here, likely because the sample is restricted to large-cap index constituents where book-to-market dispersion is compressed.

**Composite strategy.** Combining `sue`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, and `idio_vol` with IC-proportional weights and the signs above, the gross composite long-short return is approximately 3.5–4.0% per month in-sample, but this is dominated by `sue`. After a conservative 50% haircut for turnover, transaction costs, and capacity constraints—and recognizing that `sue`'s 100% hit rate is implausibly clean—we estimate a net annualized return of roughly 12–15% with an annualized Sharpe of about 3.5–4.0. A more conservative, capacity-aware estimate (capping `sue` weight, assuming 30% implementation shortfall) gives ~8–10% annual return and Sharpe ~2.5.

## Discussion

The results align with well-documented anomalies: earnings momentum (`sue`), profitability (`gross_prof`), and asset-growth (investment) effects are all present and directionally consistent with the literature. The absence of a value premium and accrual effect is notable but plausible given the large-cap, index-constituent universe, where these effects are known to be weaker. The extreme `sue` statistics—100% positive months and a Sharpe of 7.65—are almost certainly optimistic: they suggest either a favorable data-generating process, limited arbitrage in the sample, or that the consensus-surprise construction embeds information not fully available at formation. A realistic implementation would face capacity limits, borrow costs on the short side, and crowding, all of which would compress realized performance. The strategy is therefore best viewed as a proof-of-concept composite rather than a deployable alpha.

## Limitations

- The panel is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to small-caps where value, accrual, and liquidity effects are typically stronger.
- `Sue`'s near-perfect IC (100% positive months) is implausibly strong and likely reflects a favorable specification or look-ahead in the consensus-surprise timing, so its Sharpe should be treated as an upper bound.
- Transaction costs, borrow fees, market impact, and capacity constraints are estimated rather than modeled from actual execution data.
- Only 120 months of data are available, limiting the power to detect regime changes and making out-of-sample validation impossible within this panel.
- Equal-weighted quintile portfolios ignore liquidity and may overweight illiquid names within the index.
