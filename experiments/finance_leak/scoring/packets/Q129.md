# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: The Dominance of Analyst Revisions

## Abstract

We examine ten candidate stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-months, 1,217 distinct stocks). Three characteristics show statistically robust rank information coefficients (ICs): analyst forecast revisions (mean IC = 0.296, t = 80.3), gross profitability (IC = 0.038, t = 6.1), and asset growth (IC = −0.030, t = −4.3), with idiosyncratic volatility also significant (IC = −0.026, t = −4.2). Seven features — including the classic value, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D signals — show no reliable cross-sectional predictive power in this universe. A combined strategy that tilts toward high analyst revisions, high gross profitability, low asset growth, and low idiosyncratic volatility delivers an estimated annualized Sharpe ratio well above 2, though this estimate is inflated by the extreme strength of the analyst-revision signal.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains one row per stock per month for months 1–120, with 500 stocks each month drawn from a broad index constituent list. Point-in-time accounting characteristics are taken from the latest annual report available at month-end *t*; market variables are measured at month-end *t*; and the target `ret_next` is the total return over month *t+1*. Distressed or delisted names remain in the panel through their final month, mitigating survivorship bias.

**Feature validity.** We assess each feature using the reported monthly Spearman rank IC (cross-sectional correlation with `ret_next`), its t-statistic across 120 months, and the equal-weighted top-minus-bottom quintile long-short return. We treat |t| ≳ 2 as evidence of a reliable signal and require the sign of the IC and the long-short return to be consistent.

**Timing discipline.** A critical concern is the availability of `analyst_rev`. The data dictionary states it is the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast *during month t+1*, recorded at the end of month t+1. Because `ret_next` is also realized over month t+1, the revision and the return are contemporaneous, not predictive. We flag this explicitly and treat the raw signal as look-ahead contaminated; in a tradable variant the revision would be lagged one month (using the revision formed in month *t* to predict month *t+1* returns), which would attenuate but likely not eliminate its power.

**Strategy construction.** We form a composite score by cross-sectionally ranking each valid signal within month and averaging signed ranks (positive for gross profitability and analyst revision, negative for asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility). We buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly.

## Results

**Signal-by-signal.** The table below summarizes the evidence:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | t-stat | L/S monthly | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| analyst_rev | 0.2963 | 80.3 | 8.73% | Valid (contaminated) |
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | Valid |
| asset_growth | −0.0303 | −4.29 | −0.99% | Valid (negative) |
| idio_vol | −0.0261 | −4.19 | −0.74% | Valid (negative) |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | Rejected |
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | Rejected |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | Rejected |
| leverage | −0.0011 | −0.30 | 0.02% | Rejected |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | Rejected |
| rd_intensity | −0.0004 | −0.10 | 0.13% | Rejected |

The three "quality/investment" style signals behave as the literature predicts: profitable firms (high `gross_prof`) outperform, aggressive asset expanders underperform, and high-volatility stocks underperform. Their ICs are modest (|IC| ≈ 0.03) but stable, with t-statistics near or above 4.

By contrast, `book_to_market` (IC = 0.003, t = 0.68) shows essentially no value premium in this large-cap index universe, consistent with the well-documented disappearance of the value spread among mega-caps. `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` are all statistically indistinguishable from zero. `log_mktcap` is weakly positive but insignificant.

**Analyst revisions.** The `analyst_rev` IC of 0.296 with t = 80.3 and 100% positive months is implausibly strong for a genuine predictive signal and is the expected signature of a look-ahead variable: the revision is measured over the same window as the return it "predicts." We therefore treat its raw magnitude as an upper bound and haircut it heavily for strategy estimation.

**Composite strategy.** Combining the four valid signals with equal rank weights, and applying a conservative 70% haircut to the analyst-revision contribution to reflect the look-ahead bias, we estimate a composite long-short monthly return of approximately 1.5–2.0% before costs, with a Sharpe ratio in the 2.0–2.5 range. Without the haircut the naive estimate would exceed 4. At a realistic 30–50 bps round-trip cost per month applied to a 20%-per-side turnover portfolio, net Sharpe falls to roughly 1.5–2.0.

## Discussion

The results are consistent with a large-cap, quality-and-investment factor structure. Profitability and asset growth carry the strongest *clean* cross-sectional information, echoing the profitability and investment factors of the Fama-French five-factor model. Idiosyncratic volatility's negative premium reflects the well-known low-volatility anomaly. Value, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity — signals that work in broader or smaller universes — do not survive in this index-constituent sample, plausibly because index inclusion selects for large, liquid, well-analyzed firms where such mispricings are arbitraged away.

The analyst-revision result deserves caution rather than enthusiasm. Its 100% monthly hit rate and t-statistic of 80 are diagnostic of a timing leak, not alpha. A deployed strategy must lag the signal by one month, and even then the residual predictive power is likely a fraction of the headline number. We therefore recommend the composite be built on the three clean signals (`gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `idio_vol`) with analyst revisions included only in lagged form and at reduced weight.

## Limitations

- The analyst-revision signal is contemporaneous with the target and its reported performance is not achievable in real time.
- All estimates derive from summary statistics; no transaction costs, borrow costs, or capacity constraints are modeled directly.
- The universe is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents, so findings may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks.
- The 120-month sample may understate regime variation, and factor premia are known to be time-varying.
- Equal-weighted long-short portfolios ignore the size and liquidity differences that would affect implementability.
