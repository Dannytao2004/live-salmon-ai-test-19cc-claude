# Title: Identifying Robust Cross-Sectional Equity Return Signals in a Monthly Panel

## Abstract
We examine nine candidate stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Using rank IC and long-short portfolio tests, we find that gross profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, and accruals exhibit statistically significant predictive power. A composite strategy based on these signals yields an expected annual return of 7.2% with a Sharpe ratio of 1.35. The remaining five signals—book-to-market, log market cap, asset growth, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not demonstrate reliable predictive ability in this sample.

## Methods
We analyze a monthly stock panel of 34,387 observations spanning 120 months and 500 unique stocks. For each month, we compute the Spearman rank correlation (rank IC) between each of nine candidate features and next-month returns across all stocks present in that month. We report the mean rank IC, its t-statistic, the proportion of months with positive IC, and the performance of an equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of each feature. We define a signal as valid if its rank IC has an absolute t-statistic exceeding 2.0 and its long-short portfolio has a consistent direction aligned with the sign of the mean IC. To construct a composite strategy, we combine the valid signals into a single composite score by averaging the cross-sectional percentile ranks of each valid signal, with signs adjusted so that higher composite scores predict higher returns. We then form a long-short portfolio based on this composite score and estimate its expected performance using historical statistics.

## Results
Table 1 summarizes the predictive performance of each feature. Four features meet our validity threshold:

- **Gross profitability** (`gross_prof`): mean rank IC = 0.0293, t-stat = 3.83, long-short monthly return = 0.88%, annualized Sharpe = 1.19. Direction: positive.
- **Idiosyncratic volatility** (`idio_vol`): mean rank IC = −0.0266, t-stat = −3.76, long-short monthly return = −0.78%, annualized Sharpe = −1.15. Direction: negative.
- **Leverage** (`leverage`): mean rank IC = 0.0137, t-stat = 2.56, long-short monthly return = 0.50%, annualized Sharpe = 0.99. Direction: positive.
- **Accruals** (`accruals`): mean rank IC = 0.0130, t-stat = 2.06, long-short monthly return = 0.31%, annualized Sharpe = 0.50. Direction: positive.

The remaining five features—`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—have rank IC t-statistics with absolute values below 2.0 and long-short Sharpe ratios close to zero or negative. We therefore classify them as rejected signals.

Using the four valid signals, we construct a composite score. The equal-weighted long-short portfolio based on this composite score has an estimated monthly return of 0.60% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.35. Assuming monthly rebalancing and no transaction costs, this corresponds to an expected annual return of approximately 7.2%.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with prior literature. Gross profitability is a well-documented positive predictor of returns, reflecting the quality of a firm's operations. Idiosyncratic volatility exhibits a strong negative relationship with future returns, consistent with the idiosyncratic volatility puzzle. Leverage shows a positive predictive relationship, possibly capturing distress risk or financing effects. Accruals show a positive relationship, which is somewhat unusual as the accrual anomaly typically predicts a negative relationship; however, in this sample, the sign is positive and statistically significant, suggesting the accrual measure may be capturing different underlying economics or that the anomaly has weakened or reversed in recent periods.

The rejected signals, such as book-to-market and asset growth, may have been stronger in earlier decades but appear weak in this sample, possibly due to changes in market efficiency or the specific index membership of the stocks. The composite strategy benefits from diversification across four independent signals, yielding a higher Sharpe ratio than any individual signal.

## Limitations
This study has several limitations. First, the sample consists only of stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, introducing survivorship bias that may overstate the performance of the signals. Second, we do not account for transaction costs, which can significantly reduce the net returns of a monthly rebalanced long-short strategy. Third, the accruals signal has an unexpected positive sign, which may be sample-specific and not robust out-of-sample. Fourth, the composite strategy assumes equal weighting of signals without optimizing weights, which may not be optimal. Fifth, the short sample period (120 months) may not capture full market cycles or regime changes.
