# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Index-Stock Panel: Only Leverage Is Robust

## Abstract

I evaluate nine stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. Using monthly rank ICs and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, only `leverage` is statistically reliable. Leverage has a positive mean rank IC of 0.0164 (t = 2.74) and a long-short portfolio with 0.52% monthly gross return and 0.92 annualized Sharpe. Other common signals are weak or insignificant in this sample. A simple monthly long-top-quintile-leverage / short-bottom-quintile strategy earns about 6.2% annualized gross with a Sharpe near 0.9, but the result is subject to survivorship and multiple-testing caveats.

## Methods

The dataset contains 34,708 stock-month observations from 500 stocks over 120 months. The universe consists of stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, with history back to month 1 or to their index inclusion month. The number of stocks expands from 143 in month 1 to 500 in month 120.

The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. The nine features are `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`. All accounting variables are taken from the latest annual report available at the end of month t; market-based variables are measured at the end of month t. Thus the features are aligned with information available before the prediction month, and there is no intentional look-ahead bias.

I assess each signal using two complementary statistics. First, I compute the Spearman rank IC between the feature and `ret_next` within each month and average it across all 120 months. The reported t-stat is mean IC divided by the standard deviation of monthly ICs, multiplied by √120. Second, I form equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature, rebalanced monthly. I treat a signal as statistically valid if the absolute t-stat exceeds roughly 2.0.

## Results

Only `leverage` clears the statistical reliability threshold. Its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0164 with a t-stat of 2.74, and the IC is positive in 59% of months. The equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys high-leverage stocks and shorts low-leverage stocks earns 0.52% per month, implying approximately 6.2% simple annualized gross return, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92.

The other eight features show little reliable predictive power:

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| book_to_market | -0.0017 | -0.28 | -0.05% | -0.08 |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | 0.12% | 0.22 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | -0.07% | -0.14 |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | 0.37% | 0.74 |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | 0.24% | 0.41 |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | 0.13% | 0.19 |
| leverage | 0.0164 | 2.74 | 0.52% | 0.92 |
| turnover | -0.0005 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.23 |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | -0.06% | -0.11 |

Some signals have the expected sign of well-known anomalies but not enough strength: `asset_growth` and `accruals` are weakly positive, contrary to the usual negative growth/accruals anomalies, and their t-stats are only 1.16 and 1.03. `idio_vol` is also weakly positive rather than negative. `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` are essentially flat.

## Discussion

The main finding is that higher leverage predicts higher next-month returns in this sample. This is directionally consistent with some classical evidence that leverage is positively related to expected stock returns, potentially as compensation for financial distress or risk. However, the effect is modest: the rank IC is only about 1.6%, and the long-short spread is 0.52% per month before costs.

The weak performance of the other signals likely reflects several features of the dataset. The universe is limited to 500 stocks that survived to the end of month 120, which introduces survivorship bias. Known anomalies such as value, profitability, low volatility, and asset growth often rely on broader cross-sections, including smaller and distressed firms that may be missing here. The sample is also only 120 months, and annual accounting data are stale by construction. Finally, with nine signals tested, finding one t-stat near 2.74 is only borderline after multiple-testing adjustment.

A practical strategy would be a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio on `leverage`: long the top quintile, short the bottom quintile. Its gross expected annual return is about 6.2% with an annualized Sharpe of 0.92. Net of transaction costs, short-borrow costs, and capacity constraints, the realized performance would likely be lower.

## Limitations

- The universe includes only stocks that survived as index constituents through month 120, creating survivorship bias.
- The sample has only 120 months, limiting statistical power and increasing sensitivity to regime shifts.
- With nine features tested, the leverage result is borderline after multiple-testing corrections.
- Equal-weighted quintile portfolios ignore transaction costs, short-borrow costs, market impact, and capacity.
- Accounting variables come from annual reports and may be stale or inconsistent across firms.
- The panel excludes delisting returns and may miss distressed or failing firms that are central to leverage effects.
- The analysis is univariate, so leverage may proxy for omitted risk or style exposures.
