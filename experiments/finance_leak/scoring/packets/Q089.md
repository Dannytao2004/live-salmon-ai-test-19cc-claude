# Look-Ahead Bias in SUE and the Absence of Robust Point-in-Time Signals

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock-level characteristics for predicting next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index stocks. Although SUE displays a very large rank IC of 0.0998 with a t-statistic of 23.25, its construction uses earnings that are not necessarily announced by the end of month t, so it is not a point-in-time signal and likely captures earnings announcement returns. After excluding this look-ahead feature, no remaining characteristic has a rank IC t-statistic above 1.64 in absolute value. We conclude that the dataset contains no tradable point-in-time signal and recommend no active long-short strategy.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations over 120 months, with 500 index constituents per month. The prediction target is `ret_next`, the stock return over month t+1. For each feature, we use the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month, averaged over all months. The reported t-statistic is `mean rank IC / (IC std / sqrt(months))`. We also consider quintile long-short returns.

We require that a valid signal be known at the close of month t. Annual-report-based features and market-based features satisfy this condition. However, `sue` is defined as using the most recent fiscal quarter ending on or before the end of month t, while firms announce earnings in the month after the fiscal quarter ends. Thus, for fiscal quarter-end months, actual EPS is not yet known at the end of month t. This creates look-ahead bias. We therefore reject `sue` as supplied and use a conventional significance threshold of |t-stat| > 2 for the remaining features.

## Results
The strongest reported signal is `sue`, with mean rank IC 0.0998, IC t-stat 23.25, and a top-minus-bottom quintile return of 3.04% per month. However, because of the announcement-lag issue, this result is not implementable point-in-time.

Among the other nine features, none reaches statistical significance:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat |
|---|---:|---:|
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 |
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 |

The strongest non-SUE long-short returns are for `asset_growth` and `accruals`, but their rank IC t-statistics are below the significance threshold. Therefore, no feature is a valid point-in-time signal in this panel.

## Discussion
The dramatic SUE result is a clear illustration of look-ahead bias. If actual earnings for a fiscal quarter are known before the announcement date, the signal can mechanically predict returns in the announcement month. A properly lagged SUE would be required to test post-earnings-announcement drift, but that variable is not available in the supplied data.

The weak positive ICs for `asset_growth` and `accruals` are not robust enough to support a strategy. Their long-short Sharpe ratios are modest and likely consistent with noise given the number of features examined.

## Limitations
- The analysis relies on summary statistics and cannot construct a correctly lagged SUE or estimate risk-adjusted alphas.
- Transaction costs, short-sale constraints, and index reconstitution are not incorporated.
- Multiple testing is not formally controlled; weak signals may be false positives.
- The sample covers only 500 index constituents and may not generalize to the broader equity market.
