# Predicting Monthly Stock Returns with Fundamental and Market Signals: A Cross-Sectional Analysis

## Abstract
We evaluate ten firm characteristics for predicting next-month stock returns using a monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Using cross-sectional rank information coefficients (ICs) and equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios, we identify four statistically robust signals: standardized unexpected earnings (SUE, positive), gross profitability (positive), asset growth (negative), and idiosyncratic volatility (negative). A composite strategy that equal-weights these four long-short portfolios is expected to earn about 17.6% annualized gross return with an estimated Sharpe ratio of 3.3. The remaining six characteristics—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not show reliable predictive power in this sample. These results align with earnings drift, profitability, investment, and low-volatility anomalies.

## Methods
We analyze 60,000 stock-month observations from 1,217 stocks, with 500 stocks per month over 120 months. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month \(t+1\). Features are point-in-time characteristics as described in the data dictionary.

For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month and average it across months (rank IC). The reported t-statistic is the mean IC divided by the time-series standard deviation multiplied by \(\sqrt{120}\). We also form equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios each month: long the top quintile of the feature and short the bottom quintile, with signs adjusted for negative-direction signals. A signal is considered valid if \(|t\text{-stat}| > 2\), the sign is economically interpretable, and the long-short portfolio has a consistent sign.

The proposed composite strategy equal-weights the four validated long-short portfolios. Each month, 25% is allocated to each signal’s long-short quintile portfolio. Expected return is the average of the four directional monthly long-short returns. Sharpe is estimated from individual portfolio volatilities and an assumed moderate average pairwise correlation of 0.4–0.5.

## Results
### Valid signals
| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Directional LS monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `sue` | Positive | 0.0952 | 26.55 | 2.96% | 7.65 |
| `gross_prof` | Positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | Negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | 0.99% (long low, short high) | 1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | Negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | 0.74% (long low, short high) | 1.20 |

`SUE` is by far the strongest signal, with a positive rank IC in every month and a t-statistic of 26.55. `gross_prof` is also strongly positive. `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` are reliably negative: high asset growth and high idiosyncratic volatility predict lower next-month returns.

### Rejected signals
The following features have weak and statistically insignificant rank ICs: `book_to_market` (IC 0.0027, t=0.68), `log_mktcap` (IC 0.0055, t=1.26), `accruals` (IC 0.0015, t=0.36), `leverage` (IC -0.0011, t=-0.30), `turnover` (IC 0.0006, t=0.14), and `rd_intensity` (IC -0.0004, t=-0.10). None exceed the conventional \(|t|>2\) threshold, and their long-short returns are economically small.

### Composite strategy
Equal-weighting the four valid long-short portfolios gives an average monthly return of:
\[
(2.96\% + 1.16\% + 0.99\% + 0.74\%) / 4 = 1.46\%.
\]
Annualized, this is approximately 17.55% gross. Assuming moderate cross-signal correlation, the estimated Sharpe ratio is about 3.3. A `SUE`-only strategy would have a much higher expected annual return of 35.5% and Sharpe of 7.65, but the composite is more diversified and less dependent on a single signal.

## Discussion
The strongest result is the `SUE` signal, consistent with post-earnings announcement drift. Its extreme t-statistic and 100% positive-month rate suggest either a very strong anomaly in this sample or possible timing/look-ahead effects in the data construction. `gross_prof` captures the profitability/quality premium. `asset_growth` negatively predicts returns, consistent with the investment anomaly: firms that expand assets aggressively tend to underperform. `idio_vol` negatively predicts returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.

The rejected characteristics do not show reliable cross-sectional predictive power in this universe. This does not mean they are economically irrelevant; they may already be priced, noisy, or better suited to different horizons or universes. The composite strategy benefits from diversification across signals, though correlations among the four signals would reduce the effective Sharpe relative to a naive independent-signal calculation. Transaction costs, shorting constraints, and monthly turnover would further lower net performance.

## Limitations
- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to other periods or universes.
- The `SUE` signal’s extraordinary strength may reflect sample-specific factors, announcement timing, or look-ahead bias.
- Cross-signal correlations are not directly observed; the composite Sharpe is an approximation.
- The analysis ignores transaction costs, shorting costs, capacity limits, taxes, and market impact.
- No formal out-of-sample or cross-validation testing was performed.
