# Title
Leverage as a Cross-Sectional Predictor of Monthly Stock Returns: Evidence from a 500-Stock Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate nine firm characteristics as monthly cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns in a 34,708-row panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Using average monthly Spearman rank ICs and equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios, only `leverage` displays statistically reliable predictive power. Its mean rank IC is 0.0164 (t-stat = 2.74), and a portfolio long high-leverage stocks and short low-leverage stocks earns 0.52% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. Other characteristics, including book-to-market, profitability, size, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, turnover, and R&D intensity, do not show reliable return predictability in this sample. We propose a monthly long-short leverage strategy, while cautioning that the result is modest, potentially fragile, and subject to survivorship and implementation caveats.

## Methods
The dataset contains one row per stock per month for 500 stocks over 120 months, with an unbalanced panel of 34,708 observations. Each month, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between each feature and `ret_next`, then average across the 120 months. The t-statistic is the mean rank IC divided by the time-series standard deviation of rank ICs, multiplied by the square root of 120. We also examine equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios that buy the top quintile of each feature and sell the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly. A feature is considered a valid standalone signal if its mean rank IC has the expected sign and a t-statistic above the conventional threshold of approximately 2.0. We do not apply transaction costs, shorting constraints, or risk-model neutralization.

## Results
Only `leverage` passes the significance threshold. It has a positive mean rank IC of 0.0164, a t-statistic of 2.74, and positive ICs in 59% of months. Its long-short quintile portfolio earns 0.52% per month, or approximately 6.24% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. The remaining features are statistically indistinguishable from zero. `asset_growth` and `accruals` are the strongest of the rejected signals, with mean rank ICs of 0.0055 and 0.0059 and t-statistics of 1.16 and 1.03, respectively, but neither is reliably significant. `idio_vol` has a positive but insignificant IC of 0.0056 (t = 0.86). `book_to_market` and `turnover` have near-zero negative ICs, while `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, and `rd_intensity` have near-zero positive ICs. Long-short returns for rejected signals are small and inconsistent with their rank ICs, with annualized Sharpe ratios ranging from -0.14 to 0.74.

## Discussion
The positive leverage-return relation is the central finding. This direction is notable because leverage is often associated with distress risk and lower future returns in some equity universes; here, high-leverage stocks outperform low-leverage stocks among index constituents. The effect is economically modest but statistically meaningful in this sample. The marginal performance of `asset_growth` and `accruals` suggests they might be useful in a multifactor combination, but as standalone signals they lack sufficient statistical support. A practical strategy would therefore focus on leverage: long the top leverage quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted and monthly rebalanced. Because the signal is not risk-neutralized, it may partly reflect sector, size, or distress-risk exposures rather than a pure alpha.

## Limitations
- The universe consists only of stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, inducing survivorship and index-membership bias.
- The backtest ignores transaction costs, shorting fees, borrow constraints, and market impact, which would reduce net returns.
- With only 120 monthly observations and nine tested features, the leverage result may be affected by multiple-testing and false-discovery concerns.
- Annual-report-based features such as leverage may be stale or measured with error by the time they enter the monthly panel.
- The long-short portfolio is not neutralized for industry, size, momentum, or other common risk factors, so the return may reflect omitted exposures.
- No out-of-sample test or parameter sensitivity analysis is performed, leaving the robustness of the leverage signal uncertain.
