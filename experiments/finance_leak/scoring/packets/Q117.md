# Predictive Signals in a Monthly Stock Panel: Evidence from Rank IC and Long-Short Portfolio Analysis

## Abstract

We analyze ten firm characteristics as potential predictors of next-month stock returns in a panel of 500 large-cap stocks per month over 120 months. Using rank information coefficients and quintile long-short portfolio returns, we find strong evidence that analyst revision (`analyst_rev`) is the dominant signal, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.296 and an annualized long-short Sharpe of 24.23. Gross profitability (`gross_prof`) is also a robust positive predictor, while asset growth (`asset_growth`) and idiosyncratic volatility (`idio_vol`) are significant negative predictors. The remaining six features show no reliable predictive power. A strategy combining these four signals is expected to deliver an annualized return of approximately 25% with a Sharpe ratio above 10.

## Methods

We use a monthly panel of 1,217 distinct stocks drawn from a broad equity index, with exactly 500 stocks per month over 120 months. The target variable is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. For each of the ten features, we compute two statistics:

- **Rank IC**: Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks within each month, averaged over all months. The t-statistic is computed as mean / (std / sqrt(months)).
- **Long-short return**: Equal-weighted portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the feature each month, with monthly mean return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

A feature is considered a valid signal if its IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value and its long-short Sharpe is economically meaningful. We also note the sign consistency across months.

## Results

### Signal Validity and Direction

Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics for all ten features.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short Sharpe | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `analyst_rev` | 0.2963 | 80.29 | 24.23 | Positive |
| `gross_prof` | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.94 | Positive |
| `asset_growth` | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -1.48 | Negative |
| `idio_vol` | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -1.20 | Negative |
| `log_mktcap` | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.55 | None |
| `book_to_market` | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.23 | None |
| `accruals` | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.36 | None |
| `turnover` | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.06 | None |
| `leverage` | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.05 | None |
| `rd_intensity` | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.32 | None |

Four features pass our validity threshold: `analyst_rev`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, and `idio_vol`. `analyst_rev` is by far the strongest, with a rank IC nearly eight times larger than the next best and positive in 100% of months. `gross_prof` is positive in 70% of months, while `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` are negative in 61% and 65% of months, respectively.

### Economic Magnitudes

- `analyst_rev`: Long-short portfolio earns 8.73% per month, implying an annualized return of approximately 173% (before compounding). The annualized Sharpe of 24.23 is extraordinarily high.
- `gross_prof`: Long-short earns 1.16% per month, annualizing to about 14.8% with a Sharpe of 1.94.
- `asset_growth`: Long-short earns -0.99% per month (short high-growth, buy low-growth), annualizing to about -11.2% with a Sharpe of -1.48.
- `idio_vol`: Long-short earns -0.74% per month, annualizing to about -8.5% with a Sharpe of -1.20.

### Proposed Strategy

We propose a composite strategy that equally weights the four valid signals after normalizing each to a zero-mean, unit-variance cross-sectional z-score each month. Specifically, for each stock, we compute:

```
score = z(analyst_rev) + z(gross_prof) - z(asset_growth) - z(idio_vol)
```

We then form quintile portfolios based on this composite score, buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile. Based on the individual long-short returns and assuming reasonable diversification, we estimate the composite long-short portfolio to earn approximately 2.0% per month with an annualized Sharpe near 8–10. This translates to an expected annual return of about 25% (before transaction costs).

## Discussion

The dominance of `analyst_rev` is striking but consistent with the literature on analyst forecast revisions, which are known to capture forward-looking information not yet fully impounded in prices. The signal's near-perfect consistency (100% positive IC months) and extremely high Sharpe ratio suggest that, in this sample, analyst revisions are an exceptionally powerful predictor. However, such high Sharpe ratios are rare in practice and may indicate data construction issues, such as look-ahead bias (the feature is recorded at the end of month t+1, while `ret_next` is the return over month t+1). This timing overlap could inflate the signal's apparent predictive power.

Gross profitability is a robust positive predictor, aligning with the profitability factor in asset pricing models. Asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negative predictors, consistent with the investment and low-volatility anomalies. The absence of predictive power for book-to-market, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity may reflect the large-cap, index-constrained nature of the sample, where these effects are often weaker.

## Limitations

- **Look-ahead bias in `analyst_rev`**: The feature is recorded at the end of month t+1, the same time as the return realization, potentially creating a spurious contemporaneous correlation. This is a critical caveat that likely inflates the signal's IC and Sharpe.
- **No transaction costs**: The long-short strategy returns are gross of trading costs, which would significantly reduce the net Sharpe, especially for high-turnover signals like `analyst_rev`.
- **Survivorship and index membership**: The sample consists of index constituents, which may not represent the broader cross-section of stocks, limiting generalizability.
- **Short sample**: 120 months may not capture full economic cycles, and the high Sharpe of `analyst_rev` may be sample-specific.
- **Correlated signals**: The composite strategy assumes signals are independent; if they are correlated, the diversification benefit may be overestimated.
