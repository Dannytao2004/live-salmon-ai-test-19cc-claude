# Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a Broad Index Universe: Evidence from a 120-Month Panel

## Abstract

We examine the predictive power of nine firm characteristics for next-month stock returns in a panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Using monthly rank ICs and quintile long-short portfolio returns, we find that only leverage exhibits a statistically significant positive relationship with future returns (rank IC = 0.0164, t-stat = 2.74). All other eight features show economically negligible and statistically insignificant rank ICs. We propose a monthly-rebalanced quintile long-short strategy based on leverage, which achieves an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. Our results suggest that, in this universe, leverage is the sole robust cross-sectional signal among the tested characteristics.

## Methods

We analyze a monthly panel of 34,708 stock-month observations covering 500 unique stocks over 120 months. The universe is the set of stocks that are constituents of a broad equity index at the end of month 120, with their available history back to month 1 or the month they joined the index. For each stock-month, we observe nine characteristics: book-to-market, gross profitability, log market cap, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity. The target variable is the total stock return over the following month (`ret_next`).

For each feature, we compute the monthly rank IC as the Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks within a month, then average over all 120 months. We report the mean rank IC, its standard deviation, and the t-statistic computed as mean / standard deviation × √120. We also construct equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios: each month, stocks are sorted into quintiles based on a feature; the portfolio buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile. We report the mean monthly long-short return and its annualized Sharpe ratio (assuming monthly returns are i.i.d. and scaling by √12).

A feature is considered a valid signal if its mean rank IC has an absolute t-statistic exceeding 2.0 (approximately the 5% two-sided significance threshold) and its long-short portfolio has a positive mean return consistent with the IC direction. Features failing this threshold are rejected as not providing reliable predictive power in this dataset.

## Results

Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics for all nine features.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short Monthly Return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| leverage | 0.0164 | 2.74 | 0.52% | 0.92 |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | 0.24% | 0.41 |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | 0.37% | 0.74 |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | 0.13% | 0.19 |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | 0.12% | 0.22 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | -0.07% | -0.14 |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | -0.06% | -0.11 |
| book_to_market | -0.0017 | -0.28 | -0.05% | -0.08 |
| turnover | -0.0005 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.23 |

The only feature with a statistically significant rank IC is **leverage**, with a mean rank IC of 0.0164 (t-stat = 2.74). The positive IC indicates that stocks with higher leverage tend to earn higher next-month returns. The long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile of leverage and sells the bottom quintile yields an average monthly return of 0.52% with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. The direction is positive: high-leverage stocks outperform low-leverage stocks.

All other features have rank ICs near zero with t-statistics well below 2.0. While asset_growth and accruals show somewhat positive long-short returns (0.37% and 0.24% monthly, respectively), their rank ICs are not statistically significant (t-stats of 1.16 and 1.03). We therefore reject them as reliable signals in this sample.

## Discussion

The positive relation between leverage and future returns is consistent with the interpretation that leverage captures exposure to systematic risk factors, such as financial distress or credit risk, which are compensated with higher expected returns. In a broad index universe, firms with higher debt-to-assets ratios may be more sensitive to economic conditions, and investors demand a premium for bearing that risk.

Notably, several well-known cross-sectional predictors—book-to-market, gross profitability, asset growth, accruals, and idiosyncratic volatility—fail to show significant predictive power in this dataset. This may reflect the specific universe construction: the sample consists of stocks that are index constituents at the end of the sample period, which introduces survivorship bias. Many distressed or delisted stocks may be excluded, weakening the value and quality anomalies that often rely on the extremes of the distribution. Additionally, the relatively short sample (120 months) and the varying number of stocks per month may reduce statistical power.

The leverage long-short strategy, while having a Sharpe ratio of 0.92, is based on a single characteristic and may be subject to regime dependence. The positive return spread appears economically meaningful but should be interpreted with caution given the modest t-statistic of the rank IC.

## Limitations

1. The universe is survivorship-biased because it includes only stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, which may exclude delisted or bankrupt firms and thus understate or distort certain anomalies.
2. The sample period is limited to 120 months, and the number of stocks per month grows from 143 to 500, which may affect the stability of rank IC estimates.
3. The analysis does not control for transaction costs, turnover, or portfolio rebalancing frictions.
4. The long-short portfolios are equal-weighted and quintile-based; results may differ with value-weighting or alternative portfolio construction.
5. The features are based on annual accounting data with varying lag structures, which may introduce look-ahead bias if not properly aligned with information availability dates.
6. The leverage signal's statistical significance is marginal (t-stat = 2.74); further validation on out-of-sample data or in different universes is warranted.
7. The paper does not examine multivariate interactions or control for common risk factors; the leverage premium may be subsumed by a standard factor model.
