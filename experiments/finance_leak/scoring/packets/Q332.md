# Cross-Sectional Predictors of Next-Month Returns: Evidence from a Monthly Index Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate nine stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Rank information-coefficient tests identify gross profitability as a positive signal and asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility as negative signals, all with absolute t-statistics above 4. The remaining six characteristics—including book-to-market, size, and accruals—do not show statistically reliable predictive power. A monthly-rebalanced long-short composite of the three significant signals is estimated to earn about 11.6% annually with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.9 before transaction costs.

## Methods
The dataset consists of 60,000 stock-month observations from a point-in-time equity index universe. For each feature, we compute the monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next`; the reported rank IC is the average across 120 months. The IC t-statistic is computed as mean IC divided by IC standard deviation, multiplied by sqrt(120). A feature is treated as a valid signal if the absolute IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0.

We also examine equal-weighted long-short portfolios formed by buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile of each feature. To form a composite strategy, each valid feature is z-scored each month, signs are set so that higher values predict higher returns, and the z-scores are averaged. The portfolio buys the top composite quintile and sells the bottom composite quintile each month.

## Results
Three of the nine features have statistically significant rank ICs.

| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

The remaining six features are not statistically reliable predictors. `book_to_market` has a mean rank IC of only 0.0027 (t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` has a mean rank IC of 0.0055 (t = 1.26), and `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` all have t-statistics below 0.5 in absolute value.

A composite strategy using the sign-corrected signals—long high `gross_prof`, long low `asset_growth`, and long low `idio_vol`—would have an expected monthly long-short return of approximately (1.16% + 0.99% + 0.74%) / 3 = 0.96%, or about 11.6% annually. Assuming moderate correlation among the three signals, the expected annualized Sharpe ratio is approximately 1.9 before costs.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with several well-known anomalies. Gross profitability positively predicts returns, consistent with the quality dimension. Asset growth negatively predicts returns, consistent with the investment effect. Idiosyncratic volatility negatively predicts returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly. The absence of a reliable book-to-market effect may reflect the specific universe, sample period, or index-constituent composition.

## Limitations
- The analysis uses summary statistics rather than raw data and does not estimate multivariate models, so the signals may overlap with each other or with omitted characteristics.
- Transaction costs, short-sale constraints, turnover, and market impact are not modeled and would reduce net performance.
- The composite Sharpe ratio is an estimate because pairwise correlations among the valid signals are not provided.
- Results apply to this 500-stock index universe and 120-month sample and may not generalize to other markets or periods.
