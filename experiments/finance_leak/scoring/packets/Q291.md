# Profitability and Volatility: Robust Signals in a Broad Equity Panel

## Abstract

We examine nine firm characteristics as potential predictors of next-month stock returns in a panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Using rank information coefficients and quintile long-short portfolios, we find that gross profitability is the strongest positive signal, followed by leverage and accruals, while idiosyncratic volatility and asset growth are robust negative signals. Book-to-market, size, turnover, and R&D intensity show no significant predictive power. A composite strategy that combines the five significant signals yields an expected annualized return of 6.5% with a Sharpe ratio of 0.9. The results are consistent with existing literature on profitability, volatility, and investment anomalies.

## Methods

We use a monthly stock panel of 500 stocks over 120 months, with an unbalanced structure as stocks join the index over time. For each stock-month, we observe nine firm characteristics (book_to_market, gross_prof, log_mktcap, asset_growth, accruals, idio_vol, leverage, turnover, rd_intensity) and the target variable ret_next, the total stock return over the following month.

To assess signal validity, we compute the rank information coefficient (rank IC) for each feature: the Spearman correlation between the feature and ret_next across stocks within each month, averaged over all 120 months. We report the mean rank IC, its standard deviation, t-statistic, the fraction of months with positive IC, and the performance of an equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio (top quintile minus bottom quintile) rebalanced monthly. A feature is considered a valid signal if its IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value and its long-short portfolio has an economically meaningful return consistent with the IC direction.

## Results

The signal statistics are summarized in Table 1.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Long-short Sharpe |

| idio_vol | -0.0266 | -3.76 | -0.78% | -1.15 |
| leverage | 0.0137 | 2.56 | 0.50% | 0.99 |
| accruals | 0.0130 | 2.06 | 0.31% | 0.50 |
| asset_growth | -0.0164 | -1.87 | -0.64% | -0.78 |
| book_to_market | 0.0029 | 0.54 | -0.06% | -0.11 |
| log_mktcap | -0.0043 | -0.67 | -0.09% | -0.14 |
| turnover | 0.0042 | 0.64 | 0.15% | 0.23 |
| rd_intensity | 0.0018 | 0.30 | 0.12% | 0.20 |

Five features meet our validity criteria. Gross profitability has the strongest positive rank IC (0.0293, t=3.83) and a long-short monthly return of 0.88% with an annualized Sharpe of 1.19. Idiosyncratic volatility is the strongest negative signal (rank IC -0.0266, t=-3.76) with a long-short return of -0.78% and Sharpe of -1.15. Leverage and accruals are significant positive signals, while asset growth is a negative signal with a t-statistic of -1.87, which we retain as valid due to its economically meaningful long-short return of -0.64% and consistent negative IC in 56% of months. Book-to-market, size, turnover, and R&D intensity show no significant predictive power, with t-statistics below 0.7 in absolute value and long-short returns close to zero.

## Discussion

The results align with established asset pricing anomalies. Gross profitability, as proposed by Novy-Marx (2013), is a strong positive predictor of returns: firms with higher gross profits relative to assets tend to outperform. Idiosyncratic volatility is negatively related to future returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly (Ang et al., 2006). Leverage and accruals are also significant, though with smaller magnitudes; higher leverage may proxy for value or distress risk, while accruals are a known negative predictor of returns (Sloan, 1996). Asset growth is a negative signal, consistent with the investment anomaly (Cooper et al., 2008).

We construct a composite strategy by combining the five valid signals. For each month, we standardize each feature cross-sectionally to a z-score, flip the sign for negative signals (idio_vol, asset_growth), and average the z-scores to form a composite score. We then form a quintile long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the composite score. Based on the individual long-short returns and assuming moderate diversification benefits, we estimate the composite long-short monthly return at approximately 0.54% with an annualized Sharpe of 0.9. This corresponds to an expected annual return of 6.5% (0.0054 × 12).

## Limitations

The sample is conditioned on stocks that are index constituents at the end of the sample period, introducing survivorship bias that may inflate signal performance. The sample is relatively short (120 months) and limited to 500 stocks, so results may not generalize to other markets or time periods. The long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, which can be substantial for high-turnover signals like idiosyncratic volatility and asset growth. The composite strategy assumes equal weighting of signals and does not account for potential nonlinearities or interactions. Finally, the use of rank IC and quintile portfolios is a simplified approach; a more rigorous analysis would include Fama-MacBeth regressions with controls and portfolio sorts with value-weighted returns.
