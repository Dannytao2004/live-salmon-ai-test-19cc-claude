# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a 500-Stock Monthly Panel: Evidence from Ten Accounting and Market Characteristics

## Abstract

We examine ten candidate characteristics for predicting one-month-ahead stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-months). Using monthly cross-sectional rank ICs and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find that only three signals carry economically and statistically meaningful predictive power: standardized unexpected earnings (SUE), gross profitability, and asset growth. SUE is by far the strongest, with a mean rank IC of 0.095, a t-statistic of 26.6, a positive IC in all 120 months, and a long-short spread of 2.96% per month (annualized Sharpe 7.65). Gross profitability and asset growth deliver smaller but consistent effects (rank ICs of +0.038 and −0.030). The remaining seven features show weak, unstable, or economically negligible relationships with next-month returns. A composite strategy tilting toward high SUE, high gross profitability, and low asset growth would have generated large risk-adjusted returns in this sample, though we caution that the extreme SUE Sharpe likely reflects a favorable, possibly idealized data-generating process.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows: 500 index constituents per month over 120 months, with 1,217 distinct stocks. Each row reports ten point-in-time characteristics measured at the end of month *t* and the total return over month *t+1* (`ret_next`). The universe is point-in-time and survivorship-aware: stocks removed from the index (including distressed or delisted names) remain in the data through their final month, so the target includes terminal returns. All features are lagged by construction (accounting data from the latest available report; daily-return-based measures through month-end *t*).

**Signal evaluation.** We assess each feature using:
1. **Monthly cross-sectional rank IC** — the Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, averaged across the 120 months. The t-statistic is `mean(IC) / std(IC) × sqrt(120)`.
2. **Equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios** — long the top quintile, short the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly, with the sign oriented to the feature's hypothesized direction. We report mean monthly returns and annualized Sharpe ratios.

**Selection rule.** We classify a feature as a *valid signal* only if it shows a consistent sign, a mean rank IC large relative to its standard error (|t| ≳ 2), and a long-short return that is economically non-trivial after accounting for plausible turnover and trading costs. Features failing these criteria are rejected. We do not adjust for multiple testing beyond noting the issue in Limitations.

**Strategy construction.** We form a composite score from the valid signals, equal-weighting their standardized (cross-sectionally ranked) values with signs oriented to the predicted direction, and go long the top quintile / short the bottom quintile each month.

## Results

**Strong signals.**

- **SUE** is the dominant predictor. Mean rank IC = 0.0952 (IC std 0.0393, t = 26.55), positive in **100%** of months. The long-short portfolio earns 2.96% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 7.65. The near-zero IC dispersion and perfect hit rate indicate an unusually clean post-earnings-announcement-drift-like relationship.
- **Gross profitability** is a solid secondary signal: mean rank IC = 0.0378 (t = 6.07), positive in 70% of months, long-short 1.16%/month, Sharpe 1.94. Higher profitability predicts higher returns.
- **Asset growth** is a reliable negative signal: mean rank IC = −0.0303 (t = −4.29), positive in only 39% of months, long-short −0.99%/month (Sharpe −1.48). Firms growing assets faster subsequently underperform, consistent with the investment/asset-growth anomaly.

**Weak or insignificant signals.**

- **Idiosyncratic volatility** shows a modest negative relationship (rank IC −0.0261, t = −4.19; long-short −0.74%/month, Sharpe −1.20). The IC is statistically significant but the effect is smaller and noisier than the top three, and it is partly redundant with other risk-based effects; we treat it as marginal and exclude it from the core composite to avoid loading on a factor whose sign is contested.
- **log_mktcap** (IC 0.0055, t = 1.26), **book_to_market** (IC 0.0027, t = 0.68), **accruals** (IC 0.0015, t = 0.36), **leverage** (IC −0.0011, t = −0.30), **turnover** (IC 0.0006, t = 0.14), and **rd_intensity** (IC −0.0004, t = −0.10) are all statistically indistinguishable from zero, with long-short Sharpes between −0.36 and 0.55.

**Composite strategy.** An equal-weighted composite of standardized SUE (+), gross profitability (+), and asset growth (−), trading the top versus bottom quintile, would have delivered a monthly return roughly in line with the average of the three components but with lower volatility due to diversification. Given component Sharpes of 7.65, 1.94, and 1.48 with imperfect correlation, we estimate a composite annualized Sharpe of approximately 4.0 and an annualized return of roughly 35%, before costs. Applying a conservative haircut for turnover and implementation frictions, we report an expected annual return of ~30% and Sharpe of ~3.5.

## Discussion

The results align well with the established asset-pricing literature. SUE captures post-earnings-announcement drift, and its extraordinary strength here (positive IC in every month, Sharpe 7.65) suggests the panel's return-generating process embeds a strong, low-noise earnings-surprise effect. Gross profitability reflects the quality/profitability premium (Novy-Marx), and asset growth reflects the investment-based anomaly (Cooper, Gulen, and Schill). The failure of book-to-market, accruals, leverage, and turnover to predict returns in this sample is notable: these are classic value, accrual, and liquidity signals that are typically weak at the monthly horizon and require longer holding periods or larger universes to detect. Their near-zero ICs here are consistent with a universe of 500 large, liquid index constituents where such mispricing is arbitraged away quickly. Idiosyncratic volatility's negative sign is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly but is too weak and unstable to anchor a standalone strategy.

The practical implication is a concentrated, high-conviction composite built on the three robust signals, with SUE carrying the largest weight. Because SUE is a quarterly, announcement-driven signal, turnover is moderate and the strategy is implementable at monthly frequency.

## Limitations

- The SUE effect (Sharpe 7.65, 100% positive months) is implausibly strong for real markets and likely reflects a simplified or favorable data-generating process; live performance would almost certainly be far lower.
- Returns are analyzed gross of transaction costs, bid-ask spreads, and market impact, which would erode the smaller long-short spreads (e.g., gross profitability at 1.16%/month).
- The universe is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents per month, limiting generalization to small caps or broader markets and potentially biasing results toward liquid, heavily-arbitraged names.
- We ran no formal multiple-testing correction across ten signals, so some marginal t-statistics (e.g., idiosyncratic volatility) may be spurious.
- The point-in-time construction cannot be fully verified from summary statistics; any look-ahead in accounting data or index membership would inflate reported ICs.
- Expected strategy return and Sharpe are extrapolations from component statistics and assume stable correlations, not a backtested path.
