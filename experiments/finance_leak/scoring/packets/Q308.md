## Title: Evaluating Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a Monthly Panel

## Abstract
This paper evaluates nine fundamental and market-based characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns using a panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. We compute monthly rank correlations between each feature and future returns, along with long-short quintile portfolio performance. Only leverage exhibits a statistically significant and economically meaningful positive relationship with future returns (mean rank IC 0.0164, t-stat 2.74). All other features show weak or inconsistent signals, with absolute rank ICs below 0.006 and t-statistics below 1.2. A long-short strategy based on leverage yields an annualized Sharpe of 0.92, suggesting a modest but reliable premium for high-leverage stocks in this sample. We conclude that leverage is the only robust signal among those tested, while the remaining features should be rejected as return predictors in this dataset.

## Methods
We analyze a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months (34,708 stock-month observations). For each month, we compute the Spearman rank correlation between each of nine features and next-month returns (`ret_next`), then average these correlations across months to obtain the mean rank IC. The significance of each mean rank IC is assessed using a t-statistic computed as the mean divided by the standard deviation, multiplied by the square root of the number of months. We also construct equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month, and report the mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio. A feature is considered a valid signal if its mean rank IC has an absolute t-statistic exceeding 2.0 and its long-short portfolio exhibits a consistent positive return with a Sharpe ratio above 0.5.

## Results
Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics for all nine features. Leverage stands out as the only feature with a statistically significant mean rank IC of 0.0164 (t-stat = 2.74), indicating a positive relationship between leverage and future returns. Its long-short portfolio earns 0.52% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 0.92. Asset growth and accruals show marginally positive ICs (0.0055 and 0.0059) but with t-statistics of 1.16 and 1.03, respectively, failing to reach conventional significance thresholds. Their long-short portfolios earn 0.37% and 0.24% per month with Sharpe ratios of 0.74 and 0.41. All other features—book-to-market, gross profitability, log market cap, idiosyncratic volatility, turnover, and R&D intensity—exhibit mean rank ICs near zero (absolute values ≤ 0.0056) with t-statistics below 1.0 and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.25. Notably, book-to-market shows a slightly negative IC (-0.0017), contrary to the traditional value premium, though the effect is negligible. The proportion of months with positive ICs is close to 50% for all features except leverage (59%), reinforcing the lack of consistent predictive power among the rejected signals.

## Discussion
The positive and significant rank IC for leverage suggests that, in this sample, stocks with higher debt-to-assets ratios tend to earn higher next-month returns. This finding is consistent with leverage being a proxy for financial distress risk, which may command a risk premium in equity markets. However, the economic magnitude is modest: a long-short quintile spread of 0.52% per month translates to roughly 6.4% annually before costs, with a Sharpe ratio just below 1. The absence of significant signals for other well-known factors—such as value (book-to-market), profitability (gross profit), size (log market cap), investment (asset growth), and accruals—may reflect the specific sample construction: the universe consists of stocks that survived to be index constituents at month 120, introducing survivorship bias that can attenuate or distort factor premia. For example, the negative book-to-market IC contradicts the value premium but may arise because the sample excludes delisted or bankrupt value stocks. Similarly, the weak performance of asset growth and accruals suggests that these anomalies are not robust in this survivorship-biased dataset.

## Limitations
This analysis is based solely on summary statistics and cannot account for transaction costs, portfolio rebalancing frictions, or capacity constraints. The sample is subject to survivorship bias because it includes only stocks that were index constituents at the end of the sample period, which may distort return predictability. The use of equal-weighted quintile portfolios and rank ICs does not control for correlations among features or for industry and macroeconomic exposures. The dataset covers a limited time span of 120 months, and the results may not generalize to other periods or markets. Finally, the leverage signal, while statistically significant, has a modest Sharpe ratio and may not survive after adjusting for multiple testing or implementing realistic trading costs.
