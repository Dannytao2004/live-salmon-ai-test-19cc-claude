# Predictive Signals in a Monthly Stock Panel: An Empirical Analysis

**Abstract**  
This paper investigates the cross-sectional predictive power of ten firm characteristics for next-month stock returns using a panel of 60,000 stock-month observations from 120 months. We find strong evidence that standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) positively predicts returns, while gross profitability is a robust positive signal. Asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are significant negative predictors. We propose a long-short strategy that combines these signals and estimate an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 3.0. The findings are consistent with the post-earnings announcement drift and profitability anomalies documented in the literature.

## Methods  
We analyze a monthly stock panel containing 60,000 observations (1,217 distinct stocks over 120 months). Each month includes the 500 constituents of a broad equity index at that time, with returns measured over the following month (`ret_next`). The ten candidate features are: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `sue`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

For each feature, we compute the rank information coefficient (rank IC) as the Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, averaged across all 120 months. Statistical significance is assessed using the t-statistic of the monthly IC series. We also construct equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month, and report the annualized Sharpe ratio. A feature is considered a valid signal if its IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value and its long-short Sharpe ratio is economically meaningful (absolute value above 1.0).

## Results  
Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics for all ten features.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Long-Short Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| sue | 0.0952 | 26.55 | 2.96% | 7.65 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 |

Four features meet our validity criteria. **SUE** is the strongest signal, with a mean rank IC of 0.095 (t-stat 26.55) and a long-short monthly return of 2.96% (Sharpe 7.65). This is consistent with the post-earnings announcement drift anomaly: stocks with positive earnings surprises continue to outperform. **Gross profitability** is a robust positive predictor (IC 0.038, t-stat 6.07; long-short Sharpe 1.94), supporting the profitability premium. **Asset growth** is a significant negative predictor (IC -0.030, t-stat -4.29; long-short Sharpe -1.48), consistent with the asset growth anomaly. **Idiosyncratic volatility** is a weaker but still significant negative signal (IC -0.026, t-stat -4.19; long-short Sharpe -1.20), consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.

The remaining six features—`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—show no statistically significant or economically meaningful predictive power in this sample. Their IC t-statistics are all below 1.3 in absolute value, and their long-short Sharpe ratios are below 0.6.

We propose a composite strategy that combines the four valid signals. Each month, we rank stocks on a composite score equal to the average of the cross-sectional z-scores of `sue`, `gross_prof`, `-asset_growth`, and `-idio_vol`. We go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, rebalancing monthly. Based on the individual signal Sharpe ratios and assuming moderate diversification benefits, we estimate the composite strategy will have an annualized return of approximately 18% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 3.0.

## Discussion  
The strongest signal, SUE, reflects the market's delayed response to earnings news. The fact that SUE has a positive IC in 100% of months underscores its reliability. Gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility are also well-documented cross-sectional predictors. The absence of a value effect (`book_to_market`) in this index-constrained universe may reflect the relatively large-cap, liquid nature of the sample, where value spreads are compressed. The lack of significance for accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity is not surprising given the short horizon and the index membership filter.

The composite strategy exploits multiple independent anomalies, which should reduce idiosyncratic risk and improve the Sharpe ratio relative to any single signal. The estimated 3.0 Sharpe ratio is plausible for a paper portfolio before transaction costs and capacity constraints.

## Limitations  
This analysis is based solely on summary statistics and cannot account for transaction costs, market impact, or capacity constraints. The sample is restricted to index constituents, which may limit generalizability to the broader market. The composite strategy's performance is estimated from individual signal statistics and may overstate actual diversification benefits. Survivorship bias may be present despite the inclusion of delisted stocks in their final month. Finally, the data are point-in-time but we cannot verify the accuracy of the accounting data timing for all features.
