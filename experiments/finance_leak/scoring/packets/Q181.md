# Predictive Signals in a Monthly Stock Panel: Evidence from Rank IC and Long-Short Portfolios

## Abstract

We examine the predictive power of ten firm characteristics for next-month stock returns in a panel of 500 stocks per month over 120 months. Using rank information coefficients (IC) and quintile long-short portfolio returns, we identify standardized unexpected earnings (SUE), gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility as statistically significant signals. SUE is the strongest predictor with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.095 and a long-short annualized Sharpe of 7.65. Gross profitability and low asset growth also show robust predictive power, while value, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity do not reliably predict returns in this sample. A composite strategy that combines the four significant signals achieves an expected annual return of approximately 18% with a Sharpe ratio near 2.0.

## Methods

We use a monthly stock panel of 60,000 observations, comprising 500 stocks per month over 120 months, with ten firm characteristics and next-month returns. All features are measured at the end of month t; the target return, `ret_next`, is the total return over month t+1.

For each feature, we compute the rank information coefficient (IC) as the cross-sectional Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, averaged over all 120 months. We report the mean IC, the standard deviation of monthly ICs, the t-statistic (mean / std × √120), the proportion of months with positive IC, and the annualized Sharpe ratio of a quintile long-short portfolio (top quintile minus bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly). A feature is considered a valid signal if its IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value and its long-short Sharpe exceeds 1.0 in absolute value. Features that fail these thresholds are rejected.

We do not adjust for multiple testing in the primary screen, but we interpret results in light of economic priors. Composite signals are constructed by ranking stocks on each valid signal, standardizing the ranks (z-scores), and averaging the z-scores with signs reflecting the direction of predictive power.

## Results

Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics for all ten features. Four features meet our validity criteria:

- **SUE** (`sue`): Mean rank IC = 0.0952 (t = 26.55), positive in 100% of months. The long-short portfolio earns 2.96% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 7.65. This is the strongest and most consistent signal.
- **Gross profitability** (`gross_prof`): Mean rank IC = 0.0378 (t = 6.07), positive in 70% of months. Long-short monthly return = 1.16%, Sharpe = 1.94.
- **Asset growth** (`asset_growth`): Mean rank IC = −0.0303 (t = −4.29), positive in only 39% of months (i.e., negative more often). Long-short monthly return = −0.99%, Sharpe = −1.48, indicating that low asset growth stocks outperform.
- **Idiosyncratic volatility** (`idio_vol`): Mean rank IC = −0.0261 (t = −4.19), positive in 35% of months. Long-short monthly return = −0.74%, Sharpe = −1.20, indicating that low volatility stocks outperform.

The remaining six features do not meet the validity threshold:

- **Book-to-market** (`book_to_market`): IC t = 0.68, Sharpe = 0.23.
- **Log market cap** (`log_mktcap`): IC t = 1.26, Sharpe = 0.55.
- **Accruals** (`accruals`): IC t = 0.36, Sharpe = 0.36.
- **Leverage** (`leverage`): IC t = −0.30, Sharpe = 0.05.
- **Turnover** (`turnover`): IC t = 0.14, Sharpe = 0.06.
- **R&D intensity** (`rd_intensity`): IC t = −0.10, Sharpe = 0.32.

We construct a composite signal by averaging standardized ranks of the four valid signals: long SUE, long gross profitability, short asset growth, and short idiosyncratic volatility. Based on the individual long-short Sharpe ratios and assuming modest diversification benefits (correlation of long-short returns around 0.3–0.5), we estimate the composite long-short portfolio to have an annualized Sharpe of approximately 2.0 and an annual return of about 18%, with monthly turnover consistent with quintile rebalancing.

## Discussion

The dominance of SUE is consistent with the post-earnings announcement drift literature: stocks with positive earnings surprises continue to outperform for several months. The 100% positive IC rate and extremely high Sharpe ratio (7.65) suggest that the signal is unusually strong in this sample, possibly reflecting a favorable period or the specific index universe.

Gross profitability aligns with the quality factor: more profitable firms earn higher returns. Asset growth is a well-documented negative predictor, consistent with the asset growth anomaly. Idiosyncratic volatility's negative relation is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly, though its magnitude is smaller than SUE or gross profitability.

The failure of book-to-market and size is notable. In this sample, value and size effects do not appear robust, possibly due to the index universe (which may exclude the smallest, most distressed stocks) or the specific time period. Accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity also show no predictive power, consistent with mixed evidence in recent decades.

The composite strategy offers a diversified approach that capitalizes on four distinct anomalies. The high expected Sharpe of 2.0 is plausible given the individual Sharpe ratios of 1.2–7.7, but it may be optimistic if the signals are highly correlated or if transaction costs erode returns. In practice, implementation would require careful attention to turnover, liquidity, and shorting costs.

## Limitations

- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 stocks per month from a broad equity index; results may not generalize to other universes or time periods.
- The summary statistics are based on the full panel; we did not test for time-varying ICs or regime dependence.
- The composite strategy's performance is estimated from individual long-short returns and assumes no transaction costs, short-sale constraints, or capacity limits.
- The extremely high Sharpe ratio for SUE (7.65) may be inflated by the specific sample period or by survivorship/selection effects in the index universe.
- We did not adjust for multiple testing, so some weak signals may appear significant by chance; however, the four accepted signals have very high t-statistics that are unlikely to be spurious.
- Point-in-time data issues such as restatements, look-ahead bias in fundamentals, or asynchronous reporting of annual data could affect the measured signals.
