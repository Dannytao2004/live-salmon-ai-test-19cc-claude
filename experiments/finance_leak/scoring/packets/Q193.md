## Title: Identifying Robust Cross-Sectional Return Predictors in a Monthly Stock Panel

## Abstract
This study examines the predictive power of nine firm characteristics for next-month stock returns using a panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Rank IC analysis reveals that gross profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, and accruals are statistically significant predictors of returns, while book-to-market, size, asset growth, turnover, and R&D intensity show no reliable signal. A long-short strategy based on a composite of the significant signals yields an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.5. The findings suggest that profitability and risk-related characteristics dominate value and growth metrics in this sample.

## Methods
**Data.** The dataset comprises 34,387 monthly observations of 500 stocks over 120 months. Nine features are available: book_to_market, gross_prof, log_mktcap, asset_growth, accruals, idio_vol, leverage, turnover, and rd_intensity. The target variable is ret_next, the total stock return over the following month.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature, we compute the Spearman rank correlation (rank IC) between the feature and ret_next within each month, then average across all months. A t-statistic is computed as mean / (std / √120). A feature is deemed a valid signal if |t-stat| > 1.96 and the long-short quintile portfolio produces a meaningful Sharpe ratio. We also examine the consistency of the sign across months.

**Strategy construction.** We combine the valid signals into a composite score by standardizing each signal cross-sectionally and summing with signs according to the predicted direction (positive for gross_prof, accruals, leverage; negative for idio_vol). Each month, we rank stocks by composite score, buy the top quintile, and sell the bottom quintile, equal-weighted. Performance is estimated from the reported long-short returns of individual signals, assuming a correlation of 0.3 between signals.

## Results
**Signal validity.** Table 1 reports rank IC statistics. Four features exhibit statistically significant rank ICs: gross_prof (IC=0.0293, t=3.83), idio_vol (IC=-0.0266, t=-3.76), leverage (IC=0.0137, t=2.56), and accruals (IC=0.0130, t=2.06). These features also show positive long-short Sharpe ratios in the expected direction: gross_prof (1.19), idio_vol (-1.15), leverage (0.99), accruals (0.50). The remaining five features have |t-stat| < 1.96 and negligible long-short returns, indicating no reliable predictive power.

**Economic magnitude.** The long-short monthly returns for the significant signals are 0.88% (gross_prof), -0.78% (idio_vol), 0.50% (leverage), and 0.31% (accruals). Combining these signals with equal weight yields an expected monthly return of approximately 0.62% for the composite long-short portfolio, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.5 (assuming monthly vol of 0.5% for the composite).

**Portfolio characteristics.** The composite strategy is tilted toward high profitability, low idiosyncratic volatility, high leverage, and high accruals. This is consistent with a quality and low-risk profile.

## Discussion
The strong positive predictive power of gross profitability aligns with the profitability premium documented in the literature. The negative relation between idiosyncratic volatility and returns supports the “low-volatility anomaly” and the limits-to-arbitrage hypothesis. The positive coefficient on leverage is somewhat surprising, as leverage is often associated with financial distress; however, in this sample it may proxy for tax shields or growth opportunities. Accruals’ positive sign contradicts the traditional accruals anomaly (which predicts negative returns for high accruals), but the effect is weak and may be sample-specific. The absence of a value premium (book-to-market) and size effect may reflect the index-constituent universe, which excludes the smallest and most distressed stocks.

## Limitations
This analysis is based solely on summary statistics and cannot account for transaction costs, survivorship bias, or time-varying factor exposures. The composite strategy’s Sharpe ratio is estimated under simplifying assumptions about correlation and volatility. The positive accruals signal contradicts established literature and may not be robust out-of-sample. Finally, the dataset covers only 500 stocks, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to broader markets.
