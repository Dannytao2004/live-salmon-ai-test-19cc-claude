# Leverage as the Only Robust Cross-Sectional Signal in a 120-Month Equity Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate nine stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns in a 34,708 stock-month panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Using monthly Spearman rank ICs and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, only leverage shows a statistically meaningful positive relation: mean rank IC = 0.0164, t-stat = 2.74, and a high-minus-low leverage quintile return of 0.52% per month. That implies a simple annualized long-short return of about 6.24% before costs, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. The other eight characteristics have small, insignificant, or internally inconsistent signals. A monthly rebalanced high-leverage-minus-low-leverage strategy is therefore the only defensible strategy in this panel, though survivorship bias and multiple testing require caution.

## Methods
The panel contains one row per stock per month, with 500 distinct stocks and months 1 through 120. The number of stocks per month grows from 143 to 500, so the panel is unbalanced and the early sample contains fewer names.

The target is `ret_next`, the stock’s total return in month t+1. All predictors are measured at the end of month t. For annual-report variables—`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, and `rd_intensity`—values come from the latest annual report available at the end of month t. Market-based variables are also measured at month t: `log_mktcap` is end-of-month market capitalization; `idio_vol` is annualized residual volatility over the 60 trading days ending at month t; and `turnover` is average monthly share turnover over the 3 months ending at month t. Thus, no predictor intentionally uses information from month t+1, although the universe itself is defined by index membership at month 120, which introduces survivorship bias.

I use the provided signal statistics rather than re-estimating from raw data. For each feature, I consider the average monthly rank IC, its t-statistic, the sign consistency, and the equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile return. I treat a signal as valid only if it has a t-statistic above roughly 2, a consistent IC and long-short sign, and a plausible economic interpretation. Because nine signals are tested, a t-statistic near 2 should be viewed as fragile evidence.

## Results
The full signal evidence is summarized below.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | LS monthly return | LS annualized Sharpe | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| book_to_market | -0.0017 | -0.28 | -0.05% | -0.08 | Reject |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | 0.12% | 0.22 | Reject |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | -0.07% | -0.14 | Reject |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | 0.37% | 0.74 | Reject |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | 0.24% | 0.41 | Reject |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | 0.13% | 0.19 | Reject |
| leverage | 0.0164 | 2.74 | 0.52% | 0.92 | Valid |
| turnover | -0.0005 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.23 | Reject |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | -0.06% | -0.11 | Reject |

Only `leverage` passes the screening threshold. Its positive rank IC indicates that higher leverage at month t predicts higher `ret_next`. The effect is economically weak but statistically stronger than any other signal: the top leverage quintile outperforms the bottom quintile by 0.52% per month, or 6.24% annualized on a simple basis, with a Sharpe ratio of 0.92 before costs. The leverage IC is positive in 59% of months, which is better than a coin flip but still far from overwhelming.

The remaining signals are not usable. `asset_growth` and `accruals` have positive point estimates but t-statistics of only 1.16 and 1.03. `book_to_market` is slightly negative and insignificant, opposite the usual value premium. `gross_prof` is positive but tiny. `log_mktcap` has a near-zero IC and a negative long-short return. `idio_vol` is positive but insignificant and opposite the usual low-volatility anomaly. `turnover` and `rd_intensity` are essentially zero and inconsistent across IC and portfolio sorts.

## Discussion
The main finding is that leverage is the only characteristic in this panel with a credible cross-sectional relation to next-month returns. The positive sign is intuitively consistent with a distress-risk premium: firms with higher financial leverage may earn higher expected returns to compensate investors for bearing greater default risk. However, the effect could also be amplified by the panel’s survivorship structure. Because the universe consists of stocks that are index constituents at month 120, highly levered firms that failed or were delisted before month 120 are excluded, potentially biasing the high-leverage portfolio upward.

The proposed strategy is simple: each month, buy the top quintile of `leverage` and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted. Rebalance monthly. Based on the provided long-short statistics, the expected gross annual return is about 6.24%, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. After transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact, the net return would likely be lower, perhaps materially so depending on turnover and capacity. Because leverage is constructed from annual reports, the underlying signal updates slowly, which may reduce turnover relative to price- or volume-based signals; however, equal-weight rebalancing and universe changes still generate trading costs.

The failure of the other eight signals is notable. Standard anomalies such as value, profitability, asset growth, accruals, low volatility, and turnover do not appear reliably in this panel. That may reflect the short 120-month sample, the specific 500-stock universe, the expanding sample composition, or the lack of controls for sector, size, beta, and momentum. The single significant result should not be treated as a strong standalone investment case.

## Limitations
- The universe is defined by index membership at month 120, so delisted or removed firms are excluded, creating survivorship bias that likely inflates the high-leverage result.
- Nine signals are tested, and leverage’s t-statistic of 2.74 is only borderline after adjusting for multiple testing.
- I cannot verify outliers, missing data, exact report lags, or sector/industry composition from the summary statistics alone.
- The strategy is evaluated before transaction costs, borrow fees, and market impact, which are likely to reduce net performance.
- Annual-report variables are stale within the year and may not reflect current firm financial condition.
- The sample covers only 120 months and one broad index universe, so the result may be regime-specific.
- Equal-weighted quintile portfolios ignore liquidity, capacity, and trading constraints.
