# Leverage as the Only Reliable Cross-Sectional Return Predictor in a Monthly Stock Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate the predictive power of nine stock characteristics for next-month returns in a panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Using monthly cross-sectional rank correlations and quintile long-short portfolios, only leverage emerges as a statistically significant signal. Leverage has a mean rank IC of 0.0164 (t-stat = 2.74), and a top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns 0.52% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 0.92. The other eight characteristics show no reliable predictive power. A monthly-rebalanced, dollar-neutral leverage strategy is estimated to earn about 6.24% annually gross of costs.

## Methods
The dataset is a monthly panel of 34,708 stock-month observations covering 500 distinct stocks and 120 months. Each row contains one of nine characteristics and the next-month return `ret_next`.

For each characteristic, I compute two measures of predictive ability. First, within each month I calculate the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between the characteristic and `ret_next`, then average these rank ICs across the 120 months. The t-statistic is computed as:

\[
t = \frac{\text{mean rank IC}}{\text{std rank IC}} \times \sqrt{120}.
\]

Second, I form equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month by sorting stocks on the characteristic, and compute the return of a long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile. I report the mean monthly long-short return and its annualized Sharpe ratio.

A feature is classified as a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic is at least 2.0 in absolute value and its long-short return is economically consistent with the IC sign. Features failing this test are classified as rejected.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics for all nine features.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| book_to_market | -0.0017 | -0.28 | -0.05% | -0.08 |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | 0.12% | 0.22 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | -0.07% | -0.14 |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | 0.37% | 0.74 |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | 0.24% | 0.41 |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | 0.13% | 0.19 |
| leverage | 0.0164 | 2.74 | 0.52% | 0.92 |
| turnover | -0.0005 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.23 |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | -0.06% | -0.11 |

Only leverage passes the pre-specified validity threshold. Its mean rank IC is 0.0164, the largest in magnitude among the nine features, with a t-statistic of 2.74. The IC is positive in 59% of months. The top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns 0.52% per month and has an annualized Sharpe of 0.92.

All other features have rank IC t-statistics below 1.2 in absolute value and economically weak long-short returns. Although asset growth and accruals have positive point estimates, they are not statistically significant.

## Discussion
The positive leverage signal indicates that stocks with higher debt-to-assets ratios tended to earn higher next-month returns in this sample. This is consistent with a risk-based interpretation: more levered firms are riskier and may offer higher expected returns as compensation. However, the sample construction is important: the data include only stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, which may overstate the returns of high-leverage firms that survived.

The absence of reliable predictive power for book-to-market, gross profitability, accruals, and asset growth is notable. These characteristics are often predictive in broader cross-sections, but their signals are weak or absent here. This may reflect the relatively short sample, the specific index universe, or the survivorship-biased sample construction.

A practical strategy based on the significant signal is a dollar-neutral long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile of leverage and sells the bottom quintile each month. The estimated gross annual return is:

\[
0.0052 \times 12 = 0.0624,
\]

with an expected annualized Sharpe of approximately 0.92.

## Limitations
This paper has several important limitations. First, the universe is survivorship-biased because it consists of stocks that are index constituents at the end of the sample, with backfilled histories. Second, the analysis is univariate and does not control for common risk factors, industry effects, or other characteristics. Third, the leverage signal would not survive a strict Bonferroni correction across all nine tested signals, so multiple-testing concerns remain. Fourth, the reported strategy performance is gross of transaction costs, short-selling constraints, and implementation frictions. Finally, the weak performance of other known signals may be sample-specific rather than generalizable.
