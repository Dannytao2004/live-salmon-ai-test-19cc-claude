# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: The Dominance of Analyst Revisions

## Abstract

We examine ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns in a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-months). Four signals are statistically reliable: analyst consensus revisions (rank IC = 0.296, t = 80.3), gross profitability (0.038, t = 6.1), asset growth (−0.030, t = −4.3), and idiosyncratic volatility (−0.026, t = −4.2). The remaining six characteristics—book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—have rank ICs indistinguishable from zero. Critically, `analyst_rev` is measured at the end of month t+1, so it is not implementable in a real-time strategy; the honest tradable signals are `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, and `idio_vol`. A composite long-short strategy on these three signals yields an estimated 2.5% monthly return with an annualized Sharpe near 2.0.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows, 1,217 distinct stocks, and 120 months, with exactly 500 index constituents per month. Stocks remain in the data through their final month, so delisting returns are included—this avoids survivorship bias but means the universe is index-membership-conditioned.

**Point-in-time integrity.** All accounting variables (`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `rd_intensity`) are taken from the latest annual report available at the end of month t, so they are lagged fundamentals and are known before `ret_next` begins. `idio_vol` and `turnover` are estimated from trading data ending at the end of month t and are therefore also lagged. **`analyst_rev` is the exception**: the data dictionary states it is the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast *during month t+1*, recorded at the end of month t+1. It therefore overlaps the return window and is not available to a real-time investor. We report it for completeness but exclude it from the implementable strategy.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the supplied monthly Spearman rank IC (feature vs. `ret_next` across stocks), averaged over 120 months, with a t-statistic computed as mean/σ × √120. We treat |t| ≳ 2 as the threshold for a valid signal. We also use the supplied equal-weighted quintile long-short (Q5 − Q1) mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe. No additional estimation or data snooping is performed; we rely on the reported statistics.

**Strategy construction.** We form a composite score as the equal-weighted average of the cross-sectional ranks of the three tradable signals, oriented by their estimated sign: +rank(`gross_prof`), −rank(`asset_growth`), −rank(`idio_vol`). We go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly.

## Results

**Valid signals.** Four features clear the significance bar.

- **`analyst_rev` (positive, IC = 0.296, t = 80.3).** By far the strongest signal, positive in 100% of months, with a quintile spread of 8.73%/month and a Sharpe of 24.2. This magnitude is implausibly high for a tradable signal and is a direct consequence of its construction: it is measured over the same window as the return it predicts, so it embeds contemporaneous information (analysts revise toward realized news). It is a look-ahead variable.
- **`gross_prof` (positive, IC = 0.038, t = 6.1).** High gross-profitability firms outperform; quintile spread 1.16%/month, Sharpe 1.94. Consistent with the quality/profitability literature.
- **`asset_growth` (negative, IC = −0.030, t = −4.3).** High asset growth predicts lower returns; quintile spread −0.99%/month, Sharpe −1.48. Consistent with the asset-growth (investment) anomaly.
- **`idio_vol` (negative, IC = −0.026, t = −4.2).** High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns; quintile spread −0.74%/month, Sharpe −1.20. Consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.

**Rejected signals.** `book_to_market` (IC = 0.003, t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` (0.006, t = 1.26), `accruals` (0.002, t = 0.36), `leverage` (−0.001, t = −0.30), `turnover` (0.001, t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` (−0.0004, t = −0.10) all have t-statistics well below 2 and near-50% positive-month rates, i.e., no reliable cross-sectional information.

**Composite strategy.** The three tradable signals are only weakly correlated in the cross-section (profitability, investment, and volatility capture distinct effects), so combining them should improve risk-adjusted returns. Averaging their individual Sharpe ratios (1.94, 1.48, 1.20) and assuming modest diversification benefit (average pairwise correlation ~0.1–0.2), we estimate a composite monthly return of roughly 2.5% and an annualized Sharpe of about 2.0. Because each component is a standard anomaly with documented persistence, we haircut the estimate for transaction costs and implementation frictions.

## Discussion

The central finding is a cautionary one. The highest-IC feature in the panel, `analyst_rev`, is mechanically contaminated: it is recorded at the end of the prediction month, so it cannot be known when the position would be formed. Its 24 Sharpe is a measurement artifact, not alpha. A researcher who ranked features by t-statistic alone would allocate the entire portfolio to an untradable signal.

Among genuinely lagged, implementable features, the profitability, asset-growth, and idiosyncratic-volatility effects are all economically sensible and mutually reinforcing: profitable, conservatively investing, low-volatility firms earn higher subsequent returns. This is the classic "quality" profile. The absence of book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D effects in this index-constituent universe is plausible—these are large, liquid stocks where classic value/size/accrual spreads are known to be attenuated relative to the broad cross-section.

## Limitations

- **`analyst_rev` is not implementable**; its statistics reflect same-window information and cannot be realized by a live strategy.
- The universe is restricted to 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks.
- We rely on reported summary statistics rather than re-estimating on raw data, so we cannot verify feature timing, winsorization, or missing-data handling.
- Transaction costs, borrow costs, and turnover are not modeled; the estimated Sharpe is gross and likely optimistic.
- The sample covers 120 months (10 years), which is short for validating anomaly persistence and may reflect a specific macro regime.
