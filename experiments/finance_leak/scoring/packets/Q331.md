# Post-Earnings Announcement Drift and the Cross-Section of Returns: Evidence from a Point-in-Time Equity Panel

## Abstract
We examine ten stock-level characteristics in a point-in-time monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) is the only robust cross-sectional predictor of next-month returns, with a mean rank IC of 0.0998 and a t-statistic of 23.25. A long-short portfolio that buys the top SUE quintile and sells the bottom SUE quintile earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31 before costs. The other nine characteristics—value, profitability, size, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show no statistically reliable predictive power. We propose a monthly-rebalanced SUE quintile long-short strategy.

## Methods
We analyze a monthly stock panel containing 60,000 stock-month observations: 500 index constituents per month for 120 months. The dataset is point-in-time, so accounting data are from the latest annual report available at month-end, and the target variable is each stock’s total return over the next month, `ret_next`.

For each month, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between each feature and `ret_next`. We average these monthly rank ICs across all 120 months and compute a t-statistic as the mean divided by the standard deviation times the square root of the number of months. We also form equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month and report the average return of a long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile. A feature is treated as a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic is at least 2 in absolute value and its sign is stable across months.

## Results
SUE is by far the strongest signal. Its mean monthly rank IC is 0.0998, with an IC t-statistic of 23.25. The SUE signal is positive in 99% of months. The equal-weighted top-minus-bottom SUE quintile portfolio returns 3.04% per month on average, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31.

The remaining features are weak. Asset growth and accruals have modestly positive rank ICs of 0.0061 and 0.0055, respectively, but their t-statistics are only 1.64 and 1.34. All other features have absolute t-statistics below 1.2 and economically negligible long-short returns.

## Discussion
SUE is a well-known earnings-momentum signal. High SUE stocks—those with recent earnings well above consensus expectations—continue to outperform in the following month, while low SUE stocks underperform. The effect is economically large and statistically overwhelming in this sample. The proposed strategy is an equal-weighted, monthly-rebalanced portfolio that buys the top quintile of SUE and sells the bottom quintile. At the observed average long-short return of 3.04% per month, the implied annualized return is approximately 36.5% before costs, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31.

The other candidate signals do not meet the statistical threshold. While some, such as asset growth and accruals, have positive point estimates, their evidence is too weak to treat them as reliable standalone signals in this sample.

## Limitations
The performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, short-sale fees, borrow availability, and market impact, which would reduce net returns. The results are in-sample and may overstate out-of-sample performance; no out-of-sample or walk-forward validation is available. The high Sharpe ratio is likely optimistic and may not survive implementation frictions or risk adjustments. The universe is limited to 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to smaller or non-index stocks. SUE requires analyst consensus estimates, which may bias the sample toward larger and more widely followed firms.
