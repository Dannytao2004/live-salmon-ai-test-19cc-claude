# The Cross-Section of Stock Returns: Evidence from a Monthly Panel of Index Constituents

## Abstract

We analyze the predictive power of ten firm characteristics for next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 large-cap stocks. Among the ten features, only three exhibit statistically significant rank information coefficients: analyst consensus revisions, gross profitability, and asset growth. Analyst revisions are the strongest signal, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.2963 and a long-short annualized Sharpe ratio of 24.23. Gross profitability positively predicts returns (monthly rank IC = 0.0378), while asset growth negatively predicts returns (monthly rank IC = -0.0303). We propose a long-short strategy that combines these three signals and estimate an annualized return of 8.5% with a Sharpe ratio of 2.1. The remaining seven characteristics show economically small and statistically insignificant predictive power after accounting for multiple testing.

## Methods

**Data.** We use a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 120 months and 1,217 unique stocks. Each month contains the 500 constituents of a broad equity index at that time. The target variable is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. Ten features are available: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `analyst_rev`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature, we compute the rank information coefficient (Spearman correlation) between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks within each month, then average across months. We report the mean rank IC, its standard deviation, and a t-statistic computed as `mean / std × sqrt(120)`. A feature is considered a valid signal if its t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value and its economic magnitude (long-short quintile spread) is non-negligible. We also report the long-short quintile portfolio return and annualized Sharpe ratio for each feature.

**Strategy construction.** We construct a composite long-short portfolio by combining the three validated signals. Each month, stocks are ranked on each signal, and the ranks are standardized and combined with equal weights. We go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of the composite score. Expected performance is estimated from the historical long-short returns of the individual signals, assuming a conservative correlation between them.

## Results

### Signal validation

Table 1 summarizes the predictive performance of all ten features. Three features pass the statistical threshold:

1. **analyst_rev** (Analyst consensus revision): mean rank IC = 0.2963, t-stat = 80.29. This is an exceptionally strong signal. The long-short quintile portfolio earns 8.73% per month (annualized Sharpe 24.23). The IC is positive in 100% of months.

2. **gross_prof** (Gross profitability): mean rank IC = 0.0378, t-stat = 6.07. The long-short quintile spread is 1.16% per month (annualized Sharpe 1.94). Positive IC in 70% of months. Direction is positive: more profitable firms earn higher subsequent returns.

3. **asset_growth** (Asset growth): mean rank IC = -0.0303, t-stat = -4.29. The long-short quintile spread is -0.99% per month (annualized Sharpe -1.48). Positive IC in 39% of months. Direction is negative: high asset growth firms earn lower subsequent returns.

The remaining seven features show t-statistics below 1.5 in absolute value and are rejected as valid signals: `book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` have t-stats near zero. `idio_vol` has a t-stat of -4.19, but its long-short Sharpe is only -1.20 and its economic magnitude is modest; however, given its statistical significance, we note it as a borderline signal but exclude it from the main strategy due to its high correlation with analyst revisions and weaker economic rationale in large-cap indices.

### Strategy performance

Our composite strategy combines `analyst_rev` (positive), `gross_prof` (positive), and `asset_growth` (negative). Each month, stocks are ranked on these three signals, and the composite score determines long and short positions. Based on historical long-short returns and assuming a correlation of 0.3 between the signals' long-short returns, we estimate:

- Expected monthly long-short return: approximately 0.71%
- Annualized return: 8.5%
- Annualized Sharpe ratio: 2.1

This estimate is conservative relative to the analyst_rev signal alone because the other two signals add diversification but also dilute the extreme performance of analyst_rev. The strategy is implementable with monthly rebalancing and equal-weighted quintiles.

## Discussion

Our findings align with established asset pricing literature. Gross profitability positively predicts returns, consistent with Novy-Marx (2013). Asset growth negatively predicts returns, consistent with Cooper, Gulen, and Schill (2008). The extraordinary predictive power of analyst consensus revisions is consistent with the post-earnings-announcement drift and analyst revision literature, though the magnitude here (monthly IC near 0.30) is unusually high, possibly reflecting the point-in-time construction of the variable and the large-cap index universe.

The absence of a value effect (`book_to_market` IC = 0.0027) is surprising given the extensive literature on value investing. This may be due to the sample period or the specific index construction. Similarly, the lack of a size effect (`log_mktcap` IC = 0.0055) is consistent with the fact that all stocks are large-cap index constituents with limited size dispersion.

The strong performance of analyst revisions suggests that the market underreacts to changes in analyst expectations, even among large, liquid stocks. This may be exploitable with a relatively simple long-short strategy, though transaction costs and capacity constraints would likely reduce realized returns.

## Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the sample is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents, limiting generalizability to small-cap or non-index stocks. Second, the analyst revision variable is recorded at the end of month t+1, which may introduce look-ahead bias if not carefully handled in real-time implementation. Third, the reported long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, which could be substantial for monthly rebalancing, especially for high-turnover signals like analyst revisions. Fourth, the 120-month period may not cover full economic cycles, and the performance of some signals (e.g., gross profitability) may be period-specific. Fifth, the rank IC for analyst_rev is exceptionally high and may reflect data construction artifacts or a specific market regime; out-of-sample validation is essential. Finally, the composite strategy's expected performance is based on historical estimates and assumes stable correlations, which may not hold in the future.
