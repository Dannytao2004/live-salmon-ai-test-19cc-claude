# Predictive Signals in Cross-Sectional Stock Returns: Evidence from a Monthly Panel

## Abstract

This study examines the predictive power of ten firm characteristics for next-month stock returns using a panel of 1,217 stocks over 120 months. Among the ten features, gross profitability, asset growth, standardized unexpected earnings, and idiosyncratic volatility exhibit statistically significant rank ICs. A long-short strategy that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of each significant signal yields positive returns, with SUE delivering the strongest performance and gross profitability also contributing economically meaningful alpha. The findings are consistent with behavioral mispricing and limits to arbitrage explanations.

## Methods

We analyze a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 1,217 unique stocks over 120 months. Each month contains exactly 500 stocks that were constituents of a broad equity index at that time, with stocks retained through their delisting month. The target variable is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1.

For each of the ten features, we compute the rank information coefficient (rank IC), defined as the cross-sectional Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, averaged over all 120 months. We report the mean rank IC, its standard deviation, t-statistic, and the percentage of months with positive IC. We also construct an equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of each feature each month, reporting its mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

A feature is considered a valid signal if (1) its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value, or (2) its long-short portfolio has an annualized Sharpe ratio exceeding 1.0 in absolute value.

## Results

Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics for all ten features.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Long-Short Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| sue | 0.0952 | 26.55 | 2.96% | 7.65 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 |

Four features meet our validity criteria:

- **gross_prof**: positive and highly significant (IC t-stat = 6.07, Sharpe = 1.94).
- **sue**: positive and extremely significant (IC t-stat = 26.55, Sharpe = 7.65).
- **asset_growth**: negative and significant (IC t-stat = -4.29, Sharpe = -1.48).
- **idio_vol**: negative and significant (IC t-stat = -4.19, Sharpe = -1.20).

The remaining six features (book_to_market, log_mktcap, accruals, leverage, turnover, rd_intensity) show no statistically reliable predictive power.

## Discussion

The strongest signal is standardized unexpected earnings (SUE), with a rank IC of 0.0952 and a long-short monthly return of 2.96%. This is consistent with the post-earnings announcement drift literature: stocks with positive earnings surprises continue to outperform in the following month. The fact that SUE has a positive IC in 100% of the months underscores its robustness.

Gross profitability is the second most reliable signal, with a positive rank IC of 0.0378 and a long-short Sharpe of 1.94. This aligns with the profitability premium documented by Novy-Marx (2013): firms with higher gross profits relative to assets tend to earn higher subsequent returns.

Asset growth exhibits a negative rank IC of -0.0303, consistent with the asset growth anomaly: firms that grow their assets rapidly tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility also shows a negative rank IC of -0.0261, supporting the low-volatility anomaly: stocks with high idiosyncratic risk earn lower subsequent returns.

A composite strategy that equally weights the four valid signals (with signs aligned to positive expected returns) would likely produce a stronger and more stable alpha than any single signal. Based on the individual long-short returns, a simple average of the four long-short portfolios would yield approximately 1.34% per month (16.1% annualized), with a Sharpe ratio likely above 2.0 after diversification.

## Limitations

This study relies solely on summary statistics and cannot control for potential confounding variables or interaction effects. The sample is restricted to constituents of a broad equity index, which may limit generalizability to smaller or non-index stocks. The long-short returns do not account for transaction costs, short-sale constraints, or market impact, which could materially reduce net performance. The 120-month period may not capture full economic cycles or structural breaks. Finally, the analysis assumes that all features are available at the time of portfolio formation; any look-ahead bias in the underlying data would invalidate the results.
