# Cross-Sectional Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: Earnings Surprise, Quality, Growth, and Volatility

## Abstract
Using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month, I evaluate ten stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns. Four signals are statistically and economically significant: standardized unexpected earnings (SUE), gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility. SUE is the dominant signal, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.0952, positive in 100% of months, and a long-short annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.65. A composite long-short strategy that overweights SUE and tilts toward high gross profitability, low asset growth, and low idiosyncratic volatility is expected to earn roughly 29% annualized with a Sharpe ratio near 6.2. The remaining six characteristics show no reliable cross-sectional predictive power.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations from 1,217 distinct stocks over 120 months. Each month includes exactly 500 stocks that were index constituents at that time. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. Ten point-in-time features are available: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `sue`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

For each feature, I compute the monthly Spearman rank IC between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks, then average over 120 months. The t-statistic is the mean IC divided by the IC standard deviation times √120. I also examine equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month. A signal is deemed valid if its IC t-statistic exceeds ±2 in absolute value and its long-short portfolio shows a consistent economic spread.

For the composite strategy, I form a weighted score using the four valid signals, with weights proportional to their absolute IC t-statistics: SUE 60%, gross profitability 15%, asset growth 15% (short), and idiosyncratic volatility 10% (short). Portfolios are rebalanced monthly and are equal-weighted within the long and short legs. Expected composite Sharpe is estimated assuming an average pairwise signal correlation of 0.3.

## Results
The four valid signals are summarized below. SUE is by far the strongest: mean rank IC = 0.0952, t-stat = 26.55, long-short mean monthly return = 2.96%, and annualized Sharpe = 7.65. Gross profitability is positively predictive (IC = 0.0378, t = 6.07, long-short return = 1.16% per month, Sharpe = 1.94). Asset growth is negatively predictive (IC = -0.0303, t = -4.29, long-short return = -0.99% per month, Sharpe = -1.48). Idiosyncratic volatility is also negatively predictive (IC = -0.0261, t = -4.19, long-short return = -0.74% per month, Sharpe = -1.20).

The remaining six features are not statistically reliable. `book_to_market` (IC = 0.0027, t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` (IC = 0.0055, t = 1.26), `accruals` (IC = 0.0015, t = 0.36), `leverage` (IC = -0.0011, t = -0.30), `turnover` (IC = 0.0006, t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` (IC = -0.0004, t = -0.10) all fail to show meaningful cross-sectional predictability.

The composite strategy is expected to earn approximately 2.17% per month, or about 29% compounded annually. With an estimated monthly volatility of 1.22% and annualized Sharpe of roughly 6.2, the composite benefits from SUE’s exceptional strength while modestly diversifying into the weaker but significant quality, investment, and volatility signals.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with well-known anomalies. SUE captures post-earnings-announcement drift and is extraordinarily strong in this panel, with positive ICs in every month. Gross profitability reflects the quality factor: profitable firms outperform. Asset growth captures the investment factor: firms that grow assets aggressively tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility reflects the low-volatility anomaly: high-volatility stocks underperform on a risk-adjusted basis. The remaining signals—value, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not show reliable predictive power in this sample. The composite strategy is therefore best viewed as a SUE-centric portfolio with small tilts toward profitability, conservative investment, and low volatility.

## Limitations
This analysis ignores transaction costs, bid-ask spreads, and market impact, so net returns would be lower. Shorting constraints and borrow fees are not modeled, which may be material for less liquid stocks. The results are in-sample over 120 months and may reflect data mining or regime-specific effects. The composite Sharpe estimate assumes an average pairwise signal correlation of 0.3; actual correlations are not provided and could change the expected risk. The universe consists only of index constituents, limiting generalizability to the broad market. Finally, the panel contains only 1,217 stocks, which may affect portfolio capacity and statistical power.
