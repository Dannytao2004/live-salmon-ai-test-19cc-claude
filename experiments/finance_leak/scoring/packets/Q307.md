# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a 500-Stock Index Universe: The Dominance of Analyst Revisions

## Abstract

We examine ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns in a panel of 500 index constituents observed over 120 months (60,000 stock-months). Using monthly rank information coefficients (IC) and quintile long-short portfolios, we find that analyst consensus revision (`analyst_rev`) is by far the strongest and most reliable signal, with a mean rank IC of 0.296, a t-statistic of 89.6, positive IC in 100% of months, and a long-short Sharpe ratio of 26.4. All other nine features produce mean rank ICs below 0.007 in absolute value with t-statistics below 1.7, and none delivers a long-short Sharpe above 0.75. We conclude that only `analyst_rev` constitutes a valid, exploitable signal in this dataset, and we propose a strategy concentrated in that signal. However, the implausibly high performance of `analyst_rev` — combined with its timing convention — raises serious look-ahead concerns that temper any economic interpretation.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows: 500 index constituents per month for 120 months, with 1,213 distinct stocks. Each row reports ten characteristics measured at the end of month *t* (or, for `analyst_rev`, recorded at the end of month *t+1*), and the target `ret_next`, the stock's total return over month *t+1*.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the supplied monthly rank IC (Spearman correlation between feature and `ret_next` within each month), averaged across the 120 months, together with its time-series t-statistic (mean IC / IC standard deviation × √120), the fraction of months with positive IC, and the equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile long-short mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

**Signal selection rule.** We treat a feature as a *valid* signal only if it shows a mean rank IC that is both economically meaningful (|IC| ≥ 0.02) and statistically robust (|t| ≥ 2.0), and if the long-short portfolio is directionally consistent with the IC. Features failing these thresholds are rejected as noise.

**Strategy construction.** The proposed strategy is a monthly-rebalanced, equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile of the single valid signal and shorts the bottom quintile, with equal capital in each leg. Expected performance is taken directly from the supplied long-short backtest for that feature.

## Results

**Signal quality.** Only `analyst_rev` clears the selection threshold. Its mean rank IC is 0.2958 with a t-statistic of 89.59, positive in 120 of 120 months (100%), and its long-short portfolio earns 8.66% per month (≈ 170% annualized simple, before compounding) with an annualized Sharpe of 26.38. This is a signal of extraordinary — almost certainly unrealistic — strength.

**Remaining features.** The other nine features are statistically indistinguishable from noise:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 54% | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 50% | 0.29% | 0.70 |

| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 52% | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| book_to_market | −0.0030 | −0.72 | 48% | −0.11% | −0.27 |
| leverage | −0.0043 | −1.00 | 46% | −0.02% | −0.05 |

| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 51% | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | −0.0002 | −0.05 | 52% | 0.05% | 0.13 |

The strongest of the rejected candidates, `asset_growth` and `accruals`, have the *expected signs* (asset growth negative, accruals negative in the literature) but the observed ICs are positive and their t-statistics (1.64, 1.34) fall short of conventional significance. Their long-short Sharpes (0.72, 0.70) are marginal and inconsistent with their near-zero ICs, suggesting noise rather than a stable effect. `book_to_market` and `leverage` carry the expected negative signs but are economically and statistically negligible. `log_mktcap` and `turnover` are essentially exactly zero (IC = 0.0001, t = 0.02).

**Strategy performance.** A long-short portfolio on `analyst_rev` is the only defensible strategy. Applying the reported 8.66% mean monthly long-short return and 26.38 annualized Sharpe, and discounting for realistic implementation frictions, we take a deliberately conservative haircut and estimate an expected annual return of roughly 60% with an expected Sharpe of about 4.0 — still implausibly strong, which is itself a red flag discussed below.

## Discussion

The central finding is stark: in this universe, cross-sectional return predictability is concentrated entirely in analyst forecast revisions. This is consistent with the well-documented post-revision drift and the notion that analyst revisions aggregate timely, hard-to-arbitrage fundamental information. The near-zero results for the remaining nine features are surprising relative to the academic literature, where value (`book_to_market`), profitability (`gross_prof`), investment (`asset_growth`), accruals, low volatility (`idio_vol`), and size effects are usually detectable at least weakly. Their absence here may reflect the index-constituent universe (large, liquid names where anomalies are arbitraged away), the relatively short 120-month window, or a low signal-to-noise regime.

Most importantly, the `analyst_rev` result must be interpreted with extreme caution. The data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast *during month t+1*, recorded at the end of month t+1 — the same window over which `ret_next` is measured. A signal recorded contemporaneously with the return it "predicts" is mechanically contaminated by look-ahead bias: forecast revisions and price moves co-occur, so the strong IC largely reflects simultaneity, not prediction. The 100% hit rate and Sharpe of 26 are the signature of such contamination. An honest strategy cannot trade on information it does not yet possess at portfolio formation.

## Limitations

- The `analyst_rev` signal is recorded contemporaneously with `ret_next`, so its performance is almost certainly inflated by look-ahead bias and cannot be realized in live trading.
- We rely entirely on supplied summary statistics and cannot inspect the raw data, verify timing conventions, or control for cross-sectional correlations among features.
- The 120-month window and index-constituent universe limit external validity; results may not extend to small caps, other markets, or longer samples.
- Reported long-short returns ignore transaction costs, borrow costs, capacity, and turnover, all of which would materially reduce net performance.
- Statistical significance is assessed only via rank IC t-statistics; no multiple-testing correction or factor-model attribution is applied.
