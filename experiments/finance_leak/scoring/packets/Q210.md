# Cross-Sectional Equity Return Predictors in a Broad Index Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns in a 120-month panel of 500 equity index constituents. Using monthly cross-sectional rank ICs and quintile long-short portfolio tests, four signals—gross profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, and accruals—show statistically reliable predictive power. A composite equal-weight long-short strategy based on these signals is estimated to earn about 7.4% annually with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.3 before transaction costs. Several well-known characteristics, including book-to-market, size, and turnover, do not meet standard significance thresholds in this sample.

## Methods
The dataset contains 34,387 stock-month observations across 120 months and up to 500 stocks per month. For each month, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between each feature and next-month returns (`ret_next`), then average these rank ICs over all 120 months. t-statistics are calculated as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation, multiplied by the square root of the number of months. A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value. We also examine equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gross_prof | 0.0293 | 3.83 | 0.88% | 1.19 |
| idio_vol | -0.0266 | -3.76 | -0.78% | -1.15 |
| leverage | 0.0137 | 2.56 | 0.50% | 0.99 |
| accruals | 0.0130 | 2.06 | 0.31% | 0.50 |
| asset_growth | -0.0164 | -1.87 | -0.64% | -0.78 |
| book_to_market | 0.0029 | 0.54 | -0.06% | -0.11 |
| log_mktcap | -0.0043 | -0.67 | -0.09% | -0.14 |
| turnover | 0.0042 | 0.64 | 0.15% | 0.23 |
| rd_intensity | 0.0018 | 0.30 | 0.12% | 0.20 |

Four features pass the pre-specified significance threshold: `gross_prof` is positively related to next-month returns, `idio_vol` is negatively related, and both `leverage` and `accruals` are positively related. `asset_growth` is close but does not pass the 5% threshold. The remaining features show no reliable predictive power.

## Discussion
The strongest signal is gross profitability, with a mean rank IC of 0.0293 and a quintile long-short monthly return of 0.88%. High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower future returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly. Leverage and accruals are weaker but statistically significant; notably, accruals enters with a positive sign, contrary to the traditional accruals anomaly, which may reflect the specific accrual definition or sample composition.

We propose an equal-weight composite signal that ranks stocks on the four valid predictors, using the sign of each signal: long high gross profitability, low idiosyncratic volatility, high leverage, and high accruals; short the opposite. The average monthly long-short return of the four individual signals is approximately 0.62%, implying an annual return of about 7.4%. Assuming moderate cross-signal correlation, the annualized Sharpe ratio is estimated to be approximately 1.3 before transaction costs.

## Limitations
This study uses only current index constituents observed historically, which introduces survivorship bias. The analysis does not account for transaction costs, market impact, or portfolio turnover constraints. The statistical tests do not adjust for multiple testing; under a Bonferroni correction, only gross profitability and idiosyncratic volatility would remain significant. The positive accruals coefficient is inconsistent with much of the prior literature and may be sample-specific. The sample covers 120 months, which is relatively short for evaluating cross-sectional return premia.
