# Report Q183

# Title
No Tradable Monthly Equity Signals After Removing Look-Ahead Analyst Revisions

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 stocks per month. The only feature with strong rank IC is `analyst_rev` (mean IC = 0.296, t-stat = 89.6), but its data definition records it at the end of month t+1, making it contemporaneous with `ret_next` and unavailable at the month-t signal date. All other features have mean rank ICs between -0.004 and +0.006 with |t-stat| < 2, and long-short Sharpe ratios between -0.27 and +0.72. After excluding the look-ahead feature, no characteristic provides robust, tradable predictive power. The appropriate strategy is therefore no active signal-based trading.

## Methods
The panel contains 60,000 stock-month observations from 1,213 stocks over 120 months. Each month has 500 index constituent stocks. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. Signal evaluation uses monthly Spearman rank ICs averaged across months; the t-stat is mean IC / IC standard deviation × √120. Long-short portfolios buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature with equal weights.

A feature is considered valid only if it is (1) observable at the end of month t, (2) statistically significant at conventional levels (|t| > 2), and (3) economically meaningful after trading costs. The data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, recorded at the end of month t+1. Since `ret_next` is also realized over month t+1, `analyst_rev` embeds future information relative to the portfolio formation date and is rejected as a look-ahead signal. The remaining features are point-in-time as of month t or from the latest annual report available at month t.

## Results
`analyst_rev` has an extreme apparent rank IC of 0.2958, a t-stat of 89.59, a long-short mean monthly return of 8.66%, an annualized Sharpe of 26.38, and positive IC in 100% of months. This is not a credible real-time signal; it is mechanically related to `ret_next` because of its timing.

Among the non-look-ahead features, the highest absolute t-stat is `asset_growth` at 1.64, followed by `accruals` at 1.34 and `idio_vol` at 1.14. None reaches the conventional |t| > 2 threshold. The largest long-short Sharpe is 0.72 for `asset_growth`, but its mean monthly return is only 0.28%, and its t-stat is insignificant after testing ten features. `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` all have t-stats between -1.00 and 0.93 and long-short Sharpe ratios between -0.27 and 0.26. No robust tradable signal remains.

## Discussion
The `analyst_rev` result is a textbook look-ahead bias. A real-time investor at the end of month t cannot observe analyst revisions that occur during month t+1. Its implausibly high IC and Sharpe ratio confirm that it is not a tradable predictor in this setup. The remaining features are statistically indistinguishable from zero at conventional levels. Some signs align loosely with known anomalies—for example, `book_to_market` is weakly negative and `gross_prof` weakly positive—but the magnitudes are small and the t-stats are low. With ten features tested, a maximum |t| of 1.64 is consistent with noise. Transaction costs, shorting constraints, and implementation frictions would further erode any weak long-short returns. The honest conclusion is that this panel contains no reliable tradable monthly equity signal after excluding the look-ahead feature.

## Limitations
- Only 120 months and 500 stocks per month are available, limiting statistical power.
- Transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact are not modeled.
- Equal-weighted quintile portfolios may not be implementable in practice.
- Annual accounting variables can be stale for up to 12 months.
- No out-of-sample validation or formal multiple-testing adjustment is performed beyond noting the issue.
- We cannot test lagged versions of `analyst_rev` or alternative signal transformations.
- The universe is restricted to index constituents and may not generalize to broader markets.
- No sector neutrality, risk-model neutralization, or factor attribution is included.
