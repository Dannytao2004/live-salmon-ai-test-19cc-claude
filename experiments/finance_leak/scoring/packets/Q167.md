# Cross-Sectional Predictors of Monthly Stock Returns: Evidence from a 120-Month Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate nine stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Using rank information coefficients and long-short quintile tests, we find three statistically significant signals: gross profitability is a positive predictor, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negative predictors. A composite long-short strategy based on these three signals is estimated to earn approximately 11.6% per year with an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 1.8 before costs. The remaining six characteristics do not exhibit significant cross-sectional predictive power.

## Methods
The dataset consists of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 1,217 distinct stocks and 120 months, with exactly 500 stocks per month. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. For each feature, we compute the monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` and average it across the 120 months to obtain the mean rank IC. Statistical significance is assessed using a t-statistic defined as mean rank IC divided by its time-series standard deviation, multiplied by sqrt(120). A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2 in absolute value. As a supplementary economic measure, we also inspect equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature.

## Results
Three features pass the significance threshold. Gross profitability (`gross_prof`) has a mean rank IC of 0.0378 with a t-statistic of 6.07 and a top-minus-bottom quintile return of 1.16% per month. Asset growth (`asset_growth`) has a mean rank IC of -0.0303 with a t-statistic of -4.29 and a top-minus-bottom quintile return of -0.99% per month, implying that low asset growth outperforms high asset growth. Idiosyncratic volatility (`idio_vol`) has a mean rank IC of -0.0261 with a t-statistic of -4.19 and a top-minus-bottom quintile return of -0.74% per month, implying that low idiosyncratic volatility outperforms high idiosyncratic volatility.

The remaining six features are not statistically significant at conventional levels: `book_to_market` (t=0.68), `log_mktcap` (t=1.26), `accruals` (t=0.36), `leverage` (t=-0.30), `turnover` (t=0.14), and `rd_intensity` (t=-0.10). Their long-short quintile Sharpe ratios are also economically weak, ranging from 0.05 to 0.55.

Combining the three validated signals, we form a composite score:
\[
\text{score} = z(\text{gross\_prof}) - z(\text{asset\_growth}) - z(\text{idio\_vol})
\]
where \(z(\cdot)\) denotes cross-sectional z-scores within each month. The strategy buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of this composite score with equal weighting each month. Using reversed long-short returns for the negative signals, the expected monthly return is approximately \((1.16\% + 0.99\% + 0.74\%)/3 = 0.96\%\), or about 11.6% annualized. Assuming the three sleeve long-short returns are moderately correlated, the expected annualized Sharpe ratio is approximately 1.8 before transaction costs.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with several well-known anomalies. Gross profitability captures a quality dimension: more profitable firms tend to outperform. Asset growth captures the investment effect: firms that grow assets aggressively tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility captures the low-risk anomaly: high-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks tend to earn lower subsequent returns. These three signals appear robust in this sample, with rank ICs stable enough to produce t-statistics above 4 in absolute value. The composite strategy diversifies across quality, investment, and low-risk dimensions and is expected to be more stable than any single signal alone.

## Limitations
Performance estimates are before transaction costs, borrow costs, and short-sale frictions. The strategy is evaluated in-sample and may not persist out-of-sample. The universe consists of broad equity index constituents and may not represent the full investable market. The combined Sharpe estimate is inferred without observing the actual correlation among the three long-short signal portfolios.
