# No Implementable Alpha in a Monthly Equity Panel: The Look-Ahead Bias in Analyst Revisions

## Abstract
We examine a 60,000-row monthly stock panel with 10 characteristics and next-month returns. Only `analyst_rev` exhibits strong predictive power, with a mean rank IC of 0.296 and a long-short Sharpe ratio of 26.4. However, `analyst_rev` is recorded at the end of month t+1, making it contemporaneous with the target return and unusable at portfolio formation. All other features have near-zero rank ICs, t-statistics below 2, and economically weak long-short spreads. After excluding the look-ahead feature, no valid implementable signal remains, and we recommend a zero-active-exposure strategy.

## Methods
We use the provided panel of 120 months and 500 index constituents per month. Signal validity is assessed using three criteria: (i) point-in-time availability before portfolio formation, (ii) statistical significance of the monthly rank IC, and (iii) economic plausibility and consistency of the long-short portfolio. The t-statistic for each feature is computed as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation times the square root of 120 months. With 120 observations, a two-sided 5% significance threshold is approximately |t| > 1.96. We also consider multiple testing across 10 features and the sign consistency with established asset-pricing literature.

A feature is classified as valid only if it is available at the end of month t, shows a statistically reliable rank IC, and produces a plausible long-short return after basic implementation considerations. Features failing any criterion are rejected. The trading strategy, if any, would buy the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of each valid signal with equal weights.

`analyst_rev` appears to be an extraordinarily strong predictor, but the data dictionary states it is the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, recorded at the end of month t+1. Since `ret_next` is also the return over month t+1, this feature contains future information relative to portfolio formation at the end of month t. It is therefore a look-ahead-biased signal and cannot be traded in real time.

Among the remaining features, the largest t-statistic is `asset_growth` at 1.64, followed by `accruals` at 1.34; neither reaches conventional significance. The signs are also often inconsistent with established anomalies: `asset_growth` and `accruals` are positively related to future returns, whereas the investment and accrual anomalies predict negative relationships. `idio_vol` is weakly positive, contrary to the low-volatility anomaly. The long-short portfolios for these features earn at most 0.29% per month, with annualized Sharpe ratios below 0.75, and none are statistically reliable after multiple-testing considerations.

## Discussion
The only feature with a high information coefficient is contaminated by look-ahead bias. If one naively used `analyst_rev`, a long-short strategy would appear to earn about 8.66% per month with a Sharpe ratio above 26, but such performance is unattainable because the signal is not known at the time of trading. This highlights the importance of aligning feature timestamps with the portfolio formation date.

The remaining nine characteristics do not provide a reliable basis for active trading in this sample. Their rank ICs are close to zero, their t-statistics are below 2, and their long-short spreads are small. Some features show economically sensible signs (e.g., negative `book_to_market` and `leverage`), but the magnitudes are too weak and inconsistent to justify a strategy. Given the lack of statistically significant and implementable signals, we recommend no active signal-driven positions. A passive or cash strategy avoids incurring transaction costs and model risk without sacrificing reliable alpha.

## Limitations
- The panel covers only 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks.
- The 120-month sample is relatively short for detecting small asset-pricing anomalies, and the t-statistics assume independence across months.
- No transaction costs, borrow fees, market impact, or capacity constraints are modeled; small long-short spreads would likely be eroded by trading costs.
- Feature definitions may contain measurement error, and the provided summary statistics do not allow testing of nonlinearities or interactions.
- The look-ahead bias in `analyst_rev` is identified from the data dictionary, but we cannot correct the feature using the available summary statistics.
