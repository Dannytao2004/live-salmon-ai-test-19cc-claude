# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Monthly Equity Panel: Evidence from Nine Accounting and Market Signals

## Abstract
We evaluate nine stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Monthly rank ICs and quintile long-short portfolios identify three robust signals: gross profitability (positive), asset growth (negative), and idiosyncratic volatility (negative). The remaining six characteristics show no reliable predictive power after accounting for sampling variability. An equal-weighted composite of the three valid signals earns an estimated 0.96% per month gross of costs, or roughly 11.6% annually, with an estimated Sharpe ratio near 2.0. These results are in-sample and should be interpreted with caution given trading costs, capacity constraints, and unmodeled factor exposures.

## Methods
We use the provided monthly stock panel with 60,000 stock-month observations and 1217 distinct stocks. For each month, we rank stocks cross-sectionally on each feature and compute the Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next`; the reported mean rank IC is the time-series average across 120 months. We assess statistical reliability using the IC t-statistic and the fraction of months with positive IC. We also examine equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature. A feature is treated as a valid signal if it has an absolute IC t-statistic above roughly 2 and a long-short return that is economically meaningful in the direction of its IC. For negative-IC features, the implementable strategy reverses the long-short leg.

## Results
Three features pass the validity criteria. `gross_prof` has a positive mean rank IC of 0.0378 (t = 6.07), with 70% positive months and a long-short return of 1.16% per month (annualized Sharpe 1.94). `asset_growth` has a negative mean rank IC of -0.0303 (t = -4.29), with only 39% positive months; the reported top-minus-bottom long-short return is -0.99% per month, so a strategy buying low-asset-growth stocks and shorting high-asset-growth stocks earns about +0.99% monthly (Sharpe 1.48 after sign adjustment). `idio_vol` has a negative mean rank IC of -0.0261 (t = -4.19), with 35% positive months; the reported top-minus-bottom return is -0.74% per month, so buying low-volatility stocks and shorting high-volatility stocks earns about +0.74% monthly (Sharpe 1.20 after sign adjustment).

The other six features are rejected. `book_to_market` has a near-zero IC of 0.0027 (t = 0.68) and negligible long-short return. `log_mktcap` has a weak positive IC of 0.0055 (t = 1.26). `accruals` has an IC of 0.0015 (t = 0.36). `leverage` has an IC of -0.0011 (t = -0.30). `turnover` has an IC of 0.0006 (t = 0.14). `rd_intensity` has an IC of -0.0004 (t = -0.10). None of these show a reliable or economically meaningful relation with next-month returns in this sample.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with several well-documented anomalies: firms with higher gross profitability tend to outperform, firms with high asset growth tend to underperform, and high idiosyncratic volatility is associated with lower subsequent returns. Gross profitability is the strongest single signal in this panel, with a t-statistic above 6 and a positive IC in 70% of months. Asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are also robust, though their long-short return volatilities are higher, leading to lower individual Sharpe ratios than gross profitability. A composite strategy that equal-weights the three valid signals would have earned approximately (1.16% + 0.99% + 0.74%) / 3 = 0.96% per month before costs. Simple annualization gives about 11.6%, or roughly 12.2% compounded. Using the sign-adjusted individual Sharpes of 1.94, 1.48, and 1.20, and assuming moderate positive correlation among the signals, the composite Sharpe is plausibly around 2.0. The rejected signals may still contain information in other samples or after conditioning, but the current evidence does not support standalone use.

## Limitations
The strategy is estimated in-sample on a single 120-month panel and may reflect data mining. Reported returns are gross of transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact. The universe consists of index constituents, so results may not generalize to microcaps or non-index stocks. The analysis does not control for known risk factors, industry exposures, or time-varying factor premia. Shorting availability and capacity are assumed, which may be unrealistic for some names. The IC t-statistics assume independently and identically distributed monthly observations, which can overstate significance in the presence of cross-sectional correlation.
