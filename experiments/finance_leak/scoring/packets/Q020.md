# Report Q020

# Title
No Free Lunch in a Monthly Equity Panel: Analyst Revisions Are Look-Ahead, and Other Characteristics Are Noise

## Abstract
I evaluate ten stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns in a 60,000-row monthly equity panel covering 120 months and 500 index constituents per month. Using monthly rank ICs, t-statistics, and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, only `analyst_rev` appears statistically powerful: mean rank IC of 0.296, t-stat 89.6, and long-short Sharpe of 26.4. However, `analyst_rev` is recorded at the end of month t+1, so it is not available at the end of month t when portfolios must be formed to capture `ret_next`. After imposing point-in-time availability, no feature provides a reliable tradable signal. The remaining features have weak, statistically insignificant ICs, and several signs contradict well-known anomalies. The best strategy is therefore no active tilt or cash, producing zero expected signal return and zero Sharpe.

## Methods
The panel contains one row per stock per month, with 120 months, 500 stocks per month, 1,213 distinct stocks, and 60,000 total observations. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. The features are `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `analyst_rev`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

Point-in-time availability is central. All accounting, market, volatility, turnover, and leverage variables are described as observable at the end of month t or from the latest annual report available at the end of month t. They can therefore be used to form portfolios at the end of month t to predict `ret_next`. In contrast, `analyst_rev` is defined as the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, scaled by price, and is recorded at the end of month t+1. It is therefore not available at portfolio formation and is not a valid predictor of `ret_next` in this setup.

I use the provided signal statistics: average monthly Spearman rank IC, IC t-statistic, equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile mean monthly return, and annualized long-short Sharpe ratio. A signal is considered valid only if it is both point-in-time available and statistically robust. Because ten features are tested, I apply a conservative interpretation of significance and treat isolated t-statistics below conventional multiple-testing thresholds as insufficient.

## Results
The signal statistics are summarized below.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | -0.27 | Reject |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 | Reject |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 | Reject |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 | Reject |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 | Reject |
| analyst_rev | 0.2958 | 89.59 | 8.66% | 26.38 | Reject due look-ahead |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 | Reject |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | -0.05 | Reject |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 | Reject |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 | Reject |

Only `analyst_rev` has an economically large and statistically overwhelming relationship with `ret_next`. Its mean rank IC is 0.296, its IC is positive in 100% of months, and its long-short portfolio earns 8.66% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 26.4. This is implausibly strong for a tradable monthly equity signal. The natural explanation is timing leakage: `analyst_rev` is measured during or at the end of month t+1, the same period as `ret_next`. It likely reflects information that is contemporaneous with, or caused by, the returns it appears to predict.

Among the point-in-time available features, `asset_growth` and `accruals` are the least weak, with rank ICs of 0.0061 and 0.0055 and t-statistics of 1.64 and 1.34. These are not significant at conventional levels, especially after accounting for multiple testing across ten features. Their signs also contradict standard anomaly evidence: high asset growth and high accruals are usually associated with lower future returns, but here both have positive ICs. The remaining features have rank ICs near zero, t-statistics between -1.00 and 1.14, and long-short Sharpe ratios between -0.27 and 0.26.

## Discussion
The central finding is that the only apparently powerful signal is not usable. If one ignored timing, `analyst_rev` would suggest a spectacular long-short strategy. But a portfolio formed at the end of month t cannot observe a variable recorded at the end of month t+1. The strong relation is therefore not evidence of predictive ability; it is evidence of look-ahead or contemporaneous information.

After excluding `analyst_rev`, no characteristic in this panel provides a reliable basis for a monthly long-short strategy. `asset_growth` and `accruals` are the best candidates, but their t-statistics are below 2 and their signs are inconsistent with the literature, suggesting noise rather than robust anomaly exposure. `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` all have near-zero rank ICs and negligible long-short performance.

The appropriate strategy is therefore to avoid active tilts based on these features. A no-trade or cash strategy has expected signal return of zero and expected Sharpe of zero. If the mandate requires equity exposure, a passive broad-index position would be preferable to a signal-driven portfolio, but the panel does not provide evidence of an alpha signal that could improve on that passive exposure.

## Limitations
The panel covers only 120 months and 500 index constituents per month, which limits statistical power and may not generalize to broader universes.  
The analysis relies on provided summary statistics and does not allow direct control for sector, macro, or other risk factors.  
No transaction costs, borrow costs, shorting constraints, taxes, or capacity limits are modeled, all of which would reduce any apparent long-short return.  
Multiple testing across ten features increases the risk that the marginally strongest non-leaked signals are false positives.  
`analyst_rev` is not point-in-time available for month t+1 prediction, so its huge IC cannot be interpreted as tradable alpha without intra-month revision data.  
The rank IC and quintile tests assume monotonic, approximately linear cross-sectional relations and ignore nonlinearities or interactions.
