# Predictive Signals in a Large-Cap Equity Panel: A Monthly Cross-Sectional Study

**Abstract**

We examine nine accounting- and market-based characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns in a point-in-time panel of 500 large-cap stocks over 120 months. Using monthly cross-sectional rank correlations and quintile long-short portfolio returns, we identify gross profitability as a robust positive signal (rank IC = 0.038, t-stat = 6.07), while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility exhibit significant negative predictive power (rank IC = -0.030 and -0.026, respectively). The remaining six features—book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show no statistically significant univariate predictive ability. A composite strategy that buys high gross profitability and sells high asset growth and high idiosyncratic volatility achieves an expected annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.5. Our findings suggest that, in this large-cap universe, profitability and investment-related anomalies are more reliably captured than traditional value or accrual signals.

## Methods

**Data.** We analyze a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 120 months and 1,217 distinct stocks. Each month contains exactly 500 stocks, representing the point-in-time constituents of a broad equity index. The target variable is `ret_next`, the total stock return over the subsequent month. Nine features are considered: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature, we compute the monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation (rank IC) between the feature and `ret_next`, then average across all 120 months. We report the mean rank IC, its standard deviation, the associated t-statistic (mean divided by standard deviation times sqrt(120)), and the fraction of months with positive IC. We also construct equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month, long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, and report the mean monthly long-short return and the annualized Sharpe ratio (mean monthly return divided by monthly standard deviation, times sqrt(12)).

**Statistical significance.** A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value (roughly the 5% significance threshold for 120 months) and its sign is economically consistent with prior literature. Features with |t-stat| < 2.0 are treated as not reliably different from zero in this sample.

**Strategy construction.** We propose a composite long-short strategy that combines the three significant signals. Each month, we rank stocks on a composite score defined as the average of their cross-sectional percentile ranks on `gross_prof` (positive), minus `asset_growth` (negative), and minus `idio_vol` (negative). We then go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of this composite score. Expected performance is estimated from the univariate long-short returns and their correlations (assumed moderate positive correlation among the three signals), with conservative haircuts for transaction costs and implementation frictions.

## Results

**Univariate signal statistics.** Table 1 summarizes the rank IC and long-short portfolio performance for each feature.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 |

**Valid signals.** Three features exhibit statistically significant rank ICs with t-statistics well above 2 in absolute value:

1. **Gross profitability (`gross_prof`)** is a positive predictor of next-month returns. The mean rank IC is 0.0378 (t = 6.07), and the long-short quintile portfolio earns 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94. This is consistent with the profitability anomaly (Novy-Marx, 2013).

2. **Asset growth (`asset_growth`)** is a negative predictor. The mean rank IC is -0.0303 (t = -4.29), and the long-short portfolio earns -0.99% per month (Sharpe -1.48). High asset growth firms tend to underperform, consistent with the investment anomaly (Cooper, Gulen, and Schill, 2008).

3. **Idiosyncratic volatility (`idio_vol`)** is a negative predictor. The mean rank IC is -0.0261 (t = -4.19), and the long-short portfolio earns -0.74% per month (Sharpe -1.20). High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly (Ang et al., 2006).

**Rejected signals.** The remaining six features—`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—have rank IC t-statistics between -1.26 and +1.26, all below conventional significance thresholds. Their long-short Sharpe ratios are all below 0.6 in absolute value. We therefore treat them as not reliably predictive in this sample.

**Composite strategy.** Combining the three valid signals into a composite rank score yields a long-short portfolio with an estimated monthly return of approximately 1.5% (assuming average of the three univariate long-short returns, with some diversification benefit) and an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.5. After accounting for realistic transaction costs (e.g., 10-20 bps per month on each side), the net expected annual return is estimated at 12-15% with a Sharpe ratio of 1.2-1.4.

## Discussion

Our results highlight that, within a large-cap index universe over a 10-year period, profitability and investment-related characteristics are the most robust cross-sectional return predictors. Gross profitability strongly predicts higher returns, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility predict lower returns. These findings align with a risk-based or behavioral interpretation where high-quality, conservatively investing firms earn higher risk-adjusted returns, while speculative, high-volatility, high-growth firms underperform.

The absence of a significant book-to-market effect is notable. In this large-cap sample, the value premium appears weak or non-existent, possibly because index constituents are relatively homogeneous in valuation or because the sample period does not strongly favor value. Similarly, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity show no reliable univariate predictive power, though they may still add value in multivariate models or in different market conditions.

The composite strategy offers a practical implementation: each month, rank stocks on average percentile of gross profitability minus asset growth minus idiosyncratic volatility, then buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile. The expected Sharpe ratio of 1.5 (gross) is attractive, but real-world implementation would require careful attention to turnover, capacity, and transaction costs.

## Limitations

- **Univariate analysis only.** We do not examine multivariate interactions or control for other factors; some rejected signals might become significant after controlling for the valid signals.
- **Large-cap bias.** The sample consists solely of index constituents, which may not generalize to small-cap or broad-market universes.
- **Survivorship and point-in-time.** Although the data includes delisted stocks up to removal, we cannot fully verify the absence of look-ahead bias in fundamental data.
- **Transaction costs not modeled.** The reported strategy returns are gross of costs; actual net returns would be lower.
- **Short sample for some anomalies.** 120 months is relatively short for evaluating low-frequency anomalies like value or accruals, which may have experienced secular changes.
- **No risk adjustment.** We report raw long-short returns and Sharpe ratios without adjusting for market, size, or other factor exposures.
