# Which Firm Characteristics Predict Next-Month Returns?

## Abstract
We examine ten equity characteristics in a monthly panel of 500 index stocks over 120 months. Only four characteristics show reliable cross-sectional predictive power for next-month returns: standardized unexpected earnings and gross profitability are positive signals, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negative signals. A composite strategy that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of an equal-weighted combination of these four signals has an estimated annualized return of approximately 35% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 5.0 before costs. The remaining six characteristics do not have statistically significant rank ICs.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations for 500 index constituents per month. For each characteristic, we compute its monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` and average these rank ICs over 120 months. A t-statistic is computed as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation, multiplied by the square root of the number of months. A characteristic is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic has absolute value above 2.0. We also report equal-weighted top-minus-bottom quintile long-short returns for each characteristic. To combine signals, we standardize each valid characteristic cross-sectionally each month, assign signs according to its expected direction, and form a composite score.

## Results
Four features pass the significance threshold. `sue` is the strongest signal, with a mean rank IC of 0.0952 and a t-statistic of 26.55. Its top-minus-bottom quintile spread averages 2.96% per month. `gross_prof` has a mean rank IC of 0.0378 (t = 6.07) and a monthly long-short return of 1.16%. `asset_growth` has a mean rank IC of -0.0303 (t = -4.29) and a monthly long-short return of -0.99%, implying that low asset growth firms outperform high asset growth firms. `idio_vol` has a mean rank IC of -0.0261 (t = -4.19) and a monthly long-short return of -0.74%, implying that low idiosyncratic volatility firms outperform high idiosyncratic volatility firms.

The remaining features do not show reliable predictive power: `book_to_market` (t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` (t = 1.26), `accruals` (t = 0.36), `leverage` (t = -0.30), `turnover` (t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` (t = -0.10).

For the composite strategy, the aligned monthly long-short spreads are 1.16%, 0.99%, 2.96%, and 0.74%. Assuming the four signals are approximately independent, the expected monthly return of an equal-weighted composite top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio is approximately (1.16% + 0.99% + 2.96% + 0.74%) / √4 = 2.93%, or 35.1% annualized. The expected annualized Sharpe ratio is approximately 5.0.

## Discussion
The results strongly support a post-earnings-announcement drift effect: `sue` is positive in every month and has the highest IC and long-short return. Gross profitability is consistent with a quality or profitability premium. The negative premium on asset growth is consistent with the asset growth anomaly, and the negative premium on idiosyncratic volatility is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly. The absence of a significant value effect for `book_to_market` and the weak size effect for `log_mktcap` may be specific to this index universe or sample period.

## Limitations
The performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, market impact, short-sale constraints, and financing costs. The composite return and Sharpe estimates assume the four signals are approximately independent and that historical relations are stable. The analysis uses the same sample for signal selection and performance estimation, so in-sample optimism is likely. The universe of 500 index stocks may not generalize to smaller or less liquid stocks.
