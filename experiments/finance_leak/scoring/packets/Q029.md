# Cross-Sectional Equity Return Predictability: Gross Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We examine nine firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month equity returns using a monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Rank IC tests identify gross profitability as a significant positive signal, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are significant negative signals. The remaining six characteristics have rank ICs statistically indistinguishable from zero. A composite long-short strategy exploiting the three significant signals is estimated to earn approximately 11.6% annually with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.0 before transaction costs.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations: 1,217 distinct stocks over 120 months, with 500 stocks in each month. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. For each of the nine features, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month and average over all 120 months to obtain the mean rank IC. The associated t-statistic is computed as mean rank IC divided by the standard deviation of monthly ICs and multiplied by sqrt(120). A feature is treated as a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value. We also report equal-weighted top-minus-bottom quintile long-short returns for each feature.

To combine signals, each month we convert the three valid signals into cross-sectional percentile ranks, orient them so that higher ranks indicate expected higher returns, and take an equal-weighted average. The composite strategy buys the top quintile of the ranked composite and sells the bottom quintile, equal-weighted within each leg.

## Results
Three features are statistically significant predictors of next-month returns.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

`gross_prof` is a positive signal, while `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` are negative signals. Reversing the long-short legs for the negative signals gives monthly long-short returns of approximately 0.99% for low-minus-high asset growth and 0.74% for low-minus-high idiosyncratic volatility.

The remaining six features are not statistically significant and are rejected as signals: `book_to_market` (t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` (t = 1.26), `accruals` (t = 0.36), `leverage` (t = -0.30), `turnover` (t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` (t = -0.10).

For the composite strategy, the three oriented long-short portfolios have expected annual returns of 13.9%, 11.9%, and 8.9%. Equal-weighting them gives an expected annual return of approximately 11.6%. Assuming a pairwise correlation of about 0.3 among the three long-short portfolios, the estimated composite annualized volatility is about 5.5%, implying an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 2.0 before transaction costs.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with several well-documented anomalies. Gross profitability predicts returns positively, supporting a quality-based interpretation: more profitable firms tend to outperform. Asset growth predicts returns negatively, consistent with the asset growth anomaly: firms that grow assets aggressively tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility predicts returns negatively, consistent with the low-volatility/low-idiosyncratic-risk anomaly.

Notably, `book_to_market` is not a significant predictor in this sample. This may reflect the sample composition of broad index constituents and the specific time period, or the possibility that value effects are weaker in large-cap index names. `log_mktcap` is also insignificant, suggesting no reliable size premium in this sample.

## Limitations
The estimated strategy returns are gross of transaction costs, short-selling costs, and liquidity constraints. The composite Sharpe ratio depends on an assumed pairwise correlation of about 0.3 among the three signals. The sample contains only index constituents, so results may not generalize to the full cross-section of equities. The analysis is based on a single historical period and may be susceptible to data mining or regime dependence.
