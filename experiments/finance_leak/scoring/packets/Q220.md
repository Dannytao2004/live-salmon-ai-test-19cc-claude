# Predictive Signals in a Survivorship-Biased Equity Panel: A Reassessment of Leverage and Fundamental Anomalies

## Abstract

This study investigates the cross-sectional predictive power of nine firm characteristics for next-month stock returns in a panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Among the tested features, only leverage exhibits a statistically significant rank correlation with future returns, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.0164 (t-stat = 2.74) and a positive long-short spread. All other features, including book-to-market, gross profitability, and asset growth, fail to show reliable predictive ability after accounting for multiple testing and the survivorship bias inherent in the panel. A long-short strategy based on leverage quintiles yields an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 0.92, but this result must be interpreted with caution given the data limitations.

## Methods

### Data
The dataset consists of 34,708 monthly observations spanning 120 months for 500 unique stocks that were constituents of a broad equity index at the end of the sample period. Stocks enter the panel when they join the index, resulting in an unbalanced panel with a median of 268 stocks per month. Nine firm characteristics are available: book-to-market, gross profitability, log market capitalization, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity. The target variable is the total stock return over the following month (`ret_next`).

### Signal Evaluation
For each characteristic, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation (rank IC) between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, then average these correlations across all 120 months. The statistical significance of the mean rank IC is assessed using a t-statistic calculated as the mean divided by the standard deviation of the monthly ICs, scaled by the square root of the number of months. A feature is deemed a valid signal if its mean rank IC has an absolute t-statistic exceeding 2.0 (approximately the 5% significance level for a two-sided test with 120 observations).

### Portfolio Construction
For each valid signal, we construct an equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the feature each month. Portfolio performance is evaluated using the mean monthly return and the annualized Sharpe ratio (mean monthly return divided by standard deviation of monthly returns, multiplied by the square root of 12).

## Results

### Signal Validity

Table 1 reports the mean rank IC, IC standard deviation, t-statistic, and consistency for each feature.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC Std | t-stat | % Months IC > 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| book_to_market | -0.0017 | 0.0681 | -0.28 | 51% |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.0569 | 0.23 | 50% |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.0590 | 0.26 | 56% |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 0.0525 | 1.16 | 52% |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 0.0624 | 1.03 | 54% |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.0712 | 0.86 | 50% |
| **leverage** | **0.0164** | **0.0654** | **2.74** | **59%** |
| turnover | -0.0005 | 0.0571 | -0.10 | 52% |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.0554 | 0.20 | 52% |

Only leverage passes the statistical threshold with a t-statistic of 2.74, indicating a reliably positive cross-sectional relationship between leverage and next-month returns. All other features have t-statistics well below 2.0, suggesting no consistent predictive power in this sample. The percentage of months with positive ICs is near 50% for most features, further supporting the absence of directional consistency.

### Leverage Long-Short Strategy

The long-short portfolio based on leverage quintiles earns a mean monthly return of 0.52% with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. This translates to an expected annual return of approximately 6.4% for a zero-investment portfolio, assuming monthly rebalancing and no transaction costs.

### Robustness of Non-Signals

Among the rejected signals, asset_growth and accruals show modest positive mean ICs (0.0055 and 0.0059) with t-statistics above 1.0, hinting at weak predictive tendencies. However, their long-short Sharpe ratios are 0.74 and 0.41, respectively, and their ICs are not statistically significant at conventional levels. The well-documented value anomaly (book-to-market) and size effect (log market capitalization) are notably absent, consistent with the survivorship bias that excludes stocks that performed poorly and were removed from the index.

## Discussion

The finding that leverage positively predicts returns is consistent with a risk-based interpretation: highly leveraged firms are more sensitive to macroeconomic conditions and therefore earn a premium for bearing financial distress risk. However, this result stands in contrast to some prior literature that finds a negative relationship between leverage and returns in certain subsamples or time periods. The positive sign may be specific to the post-2000 period, the particular index constituents, or the survivorship-biased sample.

The absence of significant predictive power for book-to-market, gross profitability, and asset growth is striking. In a broad, unbiased sample, these characteristics typically exhibit significant rank ICs. The likely explanation is the survivorship bias inherent in the panel construction: the sample includes only stocks that were index constituents at the end of month 120, thereby excluding firms that were delisted, bankrupted, or removed from the index due to poor performance. This biases the sample toward relatively successful firms and attenuates the cross-sectional dispersion in characteristics that drive anomalies.

The positive mean IC for leverage may also be inflated by survivorship bias. If highly leveraged firms that survived were those with strong fundamentals or favorable industry conditions, the observed positive relationship may not generalize to a broader universe. Moreover, the unbalanced nature of the panel—with fewer stocks in early months—may introduce time-varying composition effects that distort the average IC.

## Limitations

1. **Survivorship bias**: The panel includes only stocks that were index constituents at the end of the sample, excluding delisted or removed firms, which likely attenuates or distorts anomaly signals.
2. **Unbalanced panel**: The number of stocks per month varies from 143 to 500, which may introduce time-varying composition effects and reduce the reliability of early-month IC estimates.
3. **No multiple testing adjustment**: With nine features tested, the single significant result (leverage) could arise by chance; a Bonferroni-corrected threshold would require a t-statistic above approximately 2.9.
4. **No transaction costs**: The reported long-short Sharpe ratio of 0.92 ignores trading costs, which can be substantial for monthly rebalanced portfolios, especially in small or illiquid stocks.
5. **Limited sample period**: The 120-month window may not capture full economic cycles, and the results may be period-specific.
6. **Feature construction assumptions**: Characteristics are derived from annual report data with potential look-ahead bias if reporting dates are not properly aligned with the month-end.
