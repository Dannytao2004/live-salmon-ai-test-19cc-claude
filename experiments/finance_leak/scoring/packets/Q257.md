# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Large-Cap Equity Panel: Evidence from Nine Accounting and Market Characteristics

## Abstract

We examine nine firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents (34,708 stock-months). Only one characteristic, leverage, exhibits a statistically meaningful rank information coefficient (mean IC = 0.0164, t = 2.74), translating into a long-short portfolio Sharpe of 0.92. The remaining eight signals (book-to-market, gross profitability, log market cap, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, turnover, and R&D intensity) produce rank ICs indistinguishable from zero (|t| < 1.2) and economically trivial long-short spreads. We conclude that the panel offers essentially one usable signal, and that a leverage-based long-short or leverage-tilted strategy is the only defensible allocation, with expected annualized returns near 6% at a Sharpe just below 1.0.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 34,708 monthly observations on 500 stocks over months 1–120. Coverage is unbalanced: the universe grows from 143 stocks in month 1 to 500 by month 120, implying a survivorship-oriented construction (constituents of a broad index as of month 120, with history backfilled to index entry). Returns (`ret_next`) are next-month total returns; all features are observable at month-end *t*.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the provided monthly Spearman rank correlation (rank IC) between the feature and `ret_next`, averaged across the 120 months. We assess statistical significance via the reported t-statistic, `t = mean(IC)/std(IC) × √120`, and economic significance via the equal-weighted quintile long-short (Q5 − Q1) mean monthly return and its annualized Sharpe ratio. We treat |t| ≥ 2 and a long-short Sharpe materially above zero as joint evidence of a valid signal.

**Strategy construction.** For the surviving signal we form a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio (top quintile minus bottom quintile). Because the panel is a large-cap index universe with moderate breadth (median 268 names/month), we assume realistic implementation frictions of roughly 10–15 bps per side per month, applied to the reported gross spread.

## Results

**Signal-by-signal.** The signal statistics table is unambiguous. Leverage is the only feature with a t-statistic above 2 (mean IC = 0.0164, IC std = 0.0654, t = 2.74), a positive sign in 59% of months, a gross long-short mean of 0.52% per month, and an annualized Sharpe of 0.92. Every other feature fails at least one dimension:

- **Asset growth** (IC = 0.0055, t = 1.16) and **accruals** (IC = 0.0059, t = 1.03) have the next-largest ICs but are statistically insignificant; their long-short Sharpes (0.74 and 0.41) are driven by return volatility rather than reliable rank correlation.
- **Idiosyncratic volatility** (IC = 0.0056, t = 0.86) and **log market cap** (IC = 0.0014, t = 0.26) are indistinguishable from noise.
- **Gross profitability** (IC = 0.0012, t = 0.23), **R&D intensity** (IC = 0.0010, t = 0.20), **book-to-market** (IC = −0.0017, t = −0.28), and **turnover** (IC = −0.0005, t = −0.10) all have |t| well below 1 and hit rates near 50–52%.

Notably, the classic value and profitability premia are absent here: book-to-market's long-short spread is −0.05% per month and gross profitability's is +0.12%, both economically negligible. This is consistent with a large-cap, index-constituent universe in which valuation and quality signals have been largely arbitraged.

**Strategy.** A leverage-based long-short portfolio (long high-leverage, short low-leverage quintiles) earns 0.52% per month gross, or about 6.4% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe of 0.92. Assuming 12 bps of round-trip cost per month (moderate turnover in a quintile portfolio), net monthly return is roughly 0.40%, or ~4.9% annualized, with net Sharpe near 0.70. A blended strategy that adds small tilts to asset growth and accruals does not improve risk-adjusted performance given their insignificant ICs and would dilute the one robust signal.

## Discussion

The central finding is the scarcity of usable signals. In a 120-month sample, an IC t-statistic of 2.74 for leverage is meaningful but not overwhelming; with 120 observations, the 95% confidence interval on the IC is roughly [0.005, 0.028], so the true effect could be half the point estimate. The absence of value, profitability, and low-volatility effects is notable and plausibly attributable to the universe: index constituents at month 120 are large, liquid, well-followed firms where mispricing is arbitraged quickly. The leverage result is directionally consistent with a distress/risk-premium interpretation (higher-leverage firms earn higher returns as compensation for financial risk), though it could also reflect sector composition (e.g., financials and utilities) rather than a pure firm-level effect. The long-short Sharpe of 0.92 is attractive on paper but should be discounted for the unbalanced, survivorship-tinged panel and for the fact that only one of nine tested signals survives — a pattern that raises multiple-testing concerns.

## Limitations

- Only one of nine signals clears the significance bar; with nine tests, a single t = 2.74 could arise partly by chance (multiple testing).
- The panel is an index-constituent universe backfilled to entry, inducing survivorship and look-ahead membership bias that likely inflate measured performance.
- IC t-statistics assume independent monthly observations; autocorrelation in signal ICs would reduce effective sample size and weaken significance.
- The leverage effect may proxy for industry membership rather than a firm-level premium, and no neutralization is applied.
- Reported long-short returns are gross; net-of-cost performance depends on turnover assumptions we cannot verify from the summary.
- Equal-weighted quintile spreads ignore capacity, borrow costs on the short leg, and the small number of names per month early in the sample.
