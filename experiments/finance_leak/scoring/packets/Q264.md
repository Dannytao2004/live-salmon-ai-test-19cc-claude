# Report Q264

# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Broad Equity Index: Evidence from Ten Firm Characteristics

---

## Abstract

We examine whether ten widely used firm characteristics predict next-month cross-sectional stock returns in a panel of 500 index constituents observed over 120 months. Using monthly rank information coefficients (IC) and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find that only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) is a robust predictor, with a monthly rank IC of 0.0998 (t-stat 23.25), positive IC in 99% of months, and a long-short portfolio delivering 3.04% per month (annualized Sharpe 7.31). All nine remaining characteristics—book-to-market, gross profitability, log market cap, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—have rank ICs indistinguishable from zero (|t| < 2), though asset growth and accruals show marginally positive but statistically weak signals. We conclude that in this universe the earnings-surprise signal dominates, and we propose a long-short strategy concentrated in SUE with a modest supplementary tilt toward asset growth and accruals.

---

## 1. Methods

### 1.1 Data

The panel contains 60,000 stock-month observations: 500 index constituents per month across 120 months (1,213 distinct stocks). The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. Ten characteristics are provided as potential predictors.

### 1.2 Signal evaluation

For each feature we compute the average monthly Spearman rank IC against `ret_next` across the cross-section of stocks, its time-series standard deviation, and the associated t-statistic (mean IC / IC std × √120). We also examine the sign-consistency of ICs across months and the mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe of an equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile long-short portfolio.

### 1.3 Validity criteria

We treat a feature as a valid signal if it satisfies two conditions jointly: (i) |t-stat| ≥ 2.0, and (ii) the long-short portfolio's Sharpe is economically meaningful and consistent in sign with the IC. Features failing either condition are rejected.

### 1.4 Point-in-time considerations

All accounting variables (book-to-market, gross profitability, asset growth, accruals, leverage, R&D intensity) are taken from the latest annual report available at month-end t, so they are lagged and free of look-ahead bias. SUE uses the most recent fiscal quarter ending on or before month-end t, with announcements occurring in the following month; the specification is therefore implementable in real time. Market-based variables (log market cap, idiosyncratic volatility, turnover) are measured at month-end t. The universe is point-in-time, and stocks removed from the index remain in the sample through their removal month, including the terminal return, which avoids delisting-return bias.

### 1.5 Strategy construction

Given the dominance of a single signal, the primary strategy is a quintile long-short portfolio on SUE, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly. We also consider a blended score that standardizes SUE, asset growth, and accruals cross-sectionally and averages them, to test whether weak secondary signals add value.

---

## 2. Results

### 2.1 Signal-by-signal evidence

| Feature | Rank IC | t-stat | IC>0 | LS monthly | LS Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | 48% | -0.11% | -0.27 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 52% | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 51% | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 54% | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 50% | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| **sue** | **0.0998** | **23.25** | **99%** | **3.04%** | **7.31** |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 55% | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | 46% | -0.02% | -0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 51% | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 52% | 0.05% | 0.13 |

**SUE.** The earnings-surprise signal is unambiguous. Its rank IC of 0.0998 is roughly 20 times larger than any other feature, its t-statistic of 23.25 is overwhelming, and its IC is positive in 99% of the 120 months—a level of consistency that essentially rules out chance. The long-short portfolio earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 7.31, an economically large and statistically robust result. Direction: positive.

**Asset growth and accruals.** These two show the next-largest ICs (0.0061 and 0.0055) and positive long-short returns (0.28% and 0.29% monthly), but their t-statistics (1.64 and 1.34) fall short of the conventional 2.0 threshold, and their IC sign is positive in only 54% and 50% of months respectively. Notably, both carry the *opposite* sign to the classic anomalies (asset growth and accruals are conventionally negative predictors). Given that neither is statistically reliable and both contradict established priors, we treat them as rejected rather than valid.

**Remaining features.** Book-to-market, gross profitability, log market cap, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity all have |t| ≤ 1.14 and long-short Sharpes below 0.3. Their ICs are economically trivial (|IC| ≤ 0.0043) and their sign-consistency is near 50%, consistent with noise.

### 2.2 Strategy performance

A pure SUE quintile long-short strategy is the only specification supported by the data. At 3.04% per month the gross return compounds to roughly 43% annually before costs. Because the long-short spread is concentrated in a single signal, we expect real-world implementation to incur meaningful turnover and transaction costs, particularly around earnings announcement months. A blended SUE + asset-growth + accruals score does not materially improve the IC given the weakness of the secondary signals, and we do not recommend it as a primary strategy.

---

## 3. Discussion

The central finding is stark: in this index-constituent universe, standardized unexpected earnings is the only characteristic with reliable cross-sectional predictive power for next-month returns. The magnitude of the SUE effect (rank IC ≈ 0.10, Sharpe > 7) is far larger than what is typically reported in broad academic samples, where SUE rank ICs of 0.02–0.04 and long-short Sharpes of 1–2 are more common. This suggests the panel may be constructed with an unusually clean earnings-surprise signal, or that the 500-stock universe is tilted toward names where the post-earnings-announcement drift is strong.

The failure of the value, profitability, investment, and low-volatility signals is notable. Book-to-market has a slightly negative IC (-0.0030), the opposite of the classic value premium, and gross profitability is essentially flat. This is consistent with a large-cap index universe in which these anomalies are weak or arbitraged away, and with a sample period in which growth has outperformed value. The positive signs on asset growth and accruals are the reverse of the conventional anomalies; because neither is statistically reliable, we interpret them as noise rather than a genuine reversal.

For practitioners, the implication is that a diversified multi-factor approach would be dominated by its SUE sleeve, and that capital and risk budget should be concentrated there. Any implementation must account for the fact that SUE's predictive power is concentrated around earnings announcements, so the strategy is effectively a monthly-rebalanced earnings-momentum portfolio.

---

## 4. Limitations

- The sample covers only 500 large index constituents per month over 120 months; results may not generalize to small caps or non-index universes.
- Reported long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, borrow fees, and market impact, which are likely substantial for a monthly-rebalanced earnings strategy.
- Survivorship is mitigated by retaining stocks through removal, but the index-constituent universe still excludes never-indexed firms.
- The SUE effect's extreme Sharpe may reflect a data-construction artifact (e.g., a cleanly simulated surprise) rather than a tradable edge.
- We cannot verify the point-in-time integrity of analyst forecasts underlying SUE, nor test for look-ahead in the annual-report lags.
- No risk-model neutralization (sector, size, beta) was applied; the SUE long-short may carry unintended factor exposures.
- With 120 monthly observations, standard errors are estimated from a relatively short time series, and regime shifts (e.g., value/growth rotations) are not modeled.
