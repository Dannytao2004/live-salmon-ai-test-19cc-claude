# Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility: Signals in a 120-Month Equity Panel

## Abstract
Using 60,000 stock-month observations on 1,217 index constituents over 120 months, I evaluate nine monthly stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns. Three signals survive standard rank-IC and long-short tests: gross profitability is positively related to future returns, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negatively related. The other six characteristics—book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show weak or statistically insignificant predictive power in this sample. An equal-weight long-short composite of the three valid signals earns roughly 0.96% per month gross, or about 12% annualized, with an estimated annualized Sharpe near 2.1. These results support quality, investment, and low-risk explanations, but they are gross of trading costs and shorting frictions.

## Methods
The panel contains one row per stock per month, with each month including the 500 index constituents at that time. The target is `ret_next`, the stock’s total return over month \(t+1\). I evaluate each feature using the average monthly Spearman rank IC between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks. The reported t-statistic is the mean IC divided by the standard deviation of monthly ICs, multiplied by \(\sqrt{120}\). I also use the provided equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios: buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature, rebalanced monthly.

A feature is treated as a valid signal if it has a meaningful average rank IC, a t-statistic above roughly 2 in absolute value, and a long-short return with the same sign. For negative signals, the economically useful trade is the reverse of the table’s top-minus-bottom portfolio: buy the bottom quintile and sell the top quintile. For the strategy, I form an equal-weight composite of the signed valid signals: long high `gross_prof`, long low `asset_growth`, and long low `idio_vol`, while shorting the opposite legs. Expected performance is estimated from the average of the three signed long-short monthly returns, annualized both simply and compounded, with Sharpe adjusted conservatively for likely positive factor correlations.

## Results
The strongest evidence is for `gross_prof`: mean rank IC = 0.0378, t-stat = 6.07, top-minus-bottom long-short return = +1.16% per month, annualized Sharpe = 1.94. This is a positive signal: more profitable firms outperform less profitable firms.

`asset_growth` is also strong, but negative: mean rank IC = -0.0303, t-stat = -4.29, top-minus-bottom long-short return = -0.99% per month, annualized Sharpe = -1.48. Thus, low-asset-growth firms outperform high-asset-growth firms. After signing the trade as low-minus-high, the expected monthly return is +0.99%.

`idio_vol` is negative: mean rank IC = -0.0261, t-stat = -4.19, top-minus-bottom long-short return = -0.74% per month, annualized Sharpe = -1.20. Low-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks outperform high-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks. After signing the trade as low-minus-high, the expected monthly return is +0.74%.

The remaining signals are not reliable in this sample. `book_to_market` has mean IC = 0.0027, t-stat = 0.68, and a long-short Sharpe of only 0.23. `log_mktcap` has mean IC = 0.0055, t-stat = 1.26, Sharpe = 0.55. `accruals` has mean IC = 0.0015, t-stat = 0.36, Sharpe = 0.36. `leverage` has mean IC = -0.0011, t-stat = -0.30, Sharpe = 0.05. `turnover` has mean IC = 0.0006, t-stat = 0.14, Sharpe = 0.06. `rd_intensity` has mean IC = -0.0004, t-stat = -0.10, Sharpe = 0.32.

A simple equal-weight composite of the three valid signals has an expected monthly return of approximately \((1.16\% + 0.99\% + 0.74\%)/3 = 0.96\%\). That corresponds to about 11.6% simple annualized return, or about 12.2% compounded. The individual signed Sharpes are 1.94, 1.48, and 1.20. If the signals were uncorrelated, the composite Sharpe would be about 2.65; with realistic positive correlation among equity style factors, I conservatively estimate a composite annualized Sharpe around 2.1.

## Discussion
The results are economically sensible. `gross_prof` captures quality/profitability: profitable firms tend to have better fundamentals and may be underappreciated by the market. `asset_growth` captures the investment anomaly: firms growing assets aggressively often subsequently earn lower returns, consistent with overinvestment or empire-building. `idio_vol` captures the low-risk anomaly: high-volatility stocks underperform on a risk-adjusted basis, possibly because investors overpay for lottery-like or high-attention stocks.

The rejected signals are also plausible. `book_to_market` may be weak in this particular 120-month sample, which may be dominated by periods when value did not strongly outperform. `log_mktcap` has only modest positive IC, suggesting no strong size premium among these index constituents. `accruals` has the expected positive sign—higher accruals should predict lower returns—but the effect is tiny and insignificant here. `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` show no reliable cross-sectional predictive power. Because this is an index-constituent universe, the sample excludes many microcaps where some anomalies are stronger.

## Limitations
The results are gross of transaction costs, market impact, and borrow fees, which could materially reduce the net long-short return. The composite Sharpe estimate depends on factor correlations that are not observable from the summary statistics. The sample covers 120 months and a specific index-constituent universe, so it may not generalize to other periods or to small-cap stocks. Shorting constraints could prevent implementation of the negative legs, especially for high-volatility or high-growth stocks. The analysis uses only univariate signals and does not control for industry, risk-model exposures, or multiple-testing bias. Finally, monthly rebalancing may generate high turnover, and the data do not allow direct estimation of capacity or slippage.
