# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a 500-Stock Monthly Panel: The Dominance of Analyst Revisions

## Abstract

We examine ten firm characteristics for their ability to predict next-month stock returns in a panel of 500 index constituents observed monthly over 120 months (60,000 stock-months). Using rank information coefficients (IC) and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find that only four features carry statistically meaningful predictive power. Analyst consensus revision is by far the strongest signal (mean rank IC = 0.296, t = 80.3, 100% positive months), followed by gross profitability (IC = 0.038, t = 6.07), asset growth (IC = -0.030, t = -4.29), and idiosyncratic volatility (IC = -0.026, t = -4.19). The remaining six features—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show no reliable cross-sectional relationship with future returns. A composite strategy tilting toward high analyst revisions, high gross profitability, low asset growth, and low idiosyncratic volatility delivers an estimated annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.4, though we caution that the analyst-revision signal raises severe look-ahead and implementation concerns.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 observations: 500 stocks per month across 120 months (1,217 distinct stocks). Each row reports ten point-in-time characteristics measured at month-end *t* and the subsequent month's total return, `ret_next`. The universe is a point-in-time index membership set, so delisted/distressed names remain until removal, mitigating survivorship bias.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we computed the within-month Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` (the rank IC), averaged across months, and assessed significance via the IC t-statistic (mean/σ × √120). We also formed equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios (top minus bottom quintile) to translate signal strength into a tradable return, reporting mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe.

**Decision rule.** We treat a feature as a valid signal if |t-stat| exceeds roughly 2 and the sign is stable across the sample (share of positive-IC months materially different from 50%). Features failing this bar are rejected as noise.

## Results

**Strongest signal — analyst revisions.** `analyst_rev` has a mean rank IC of 0.296 with a t-statistic of 80.3 and a positive IC in 100% of the 120 months. Its quintile long-short portfolio earns 8.73% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 24.2. This magnitude is implausibly large for a genuinely tradable signal and reflects the feature's construction: it is recorded at the end of month *t+1*, i.e., contemporaneously with the return window. It is therefore a near-mechanical restatement of month *t+1* price/earnings news and embeds look-ahead bias.

**Profitability.** `gross_prof` is a robust long-standing anomaly proxy: IC = 0.038 (t = 6.07), 70% positive months, long-short 1.16%/month, Sharpe 1.94. Direction is positive (buy profitable firms).

**Investment and risk.** `asset_growth` predicts negatively (IC = -0.030, t = -4.29; long-short -0.99%/month, Sharpe -1.48), consistent with the asset-growth/investment anomaly. `idio_vol` also predicts negatively (IC = -0.026, t = -4.19; long-short -0.74%/month, Sharpe -1.20), consistent with the low-volatility anomaly.

**Rejected features.** `book_to_market` (IC = 0.003, t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` (IC = 0.006, t = 1.26), `accruals` (IC = 0.002, t = 0.36), `leverage` (IC = -0.001, t = -0.30), `turnover` (IC = 0.001, t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` (IC = -0.0004, t = -0.10) all have t-statistics well below conventional thresholds and long-short Sharpes near zero. Their month-to-month IC signs are close to a coin flip (45–56% positive), indicating no reliable cross-sectional information.

## Discussion

The results are economically coherent for three of the four surviving signals. Gross profitability captures the quality/profitability premium; asset growth reflects the well-documented investment factor (firms that expand aggressively underperform); idiosyncratic volatility reflects the low-volatility anomaly. These three are mutually reinforcing and could plausibly be combined into a "quality-minus-junk" style tilt.

Analyst revisions dominate statistically but should be treated with extreme skepticism. The data dictionary explicitly states the revision is "recorded at the end of month t+1," the same period over which `ret_next` is measured. The 100% hit rate and Sharpe of 24 are the signature of a contaminated, near-contemporaneous variable rather than a forecastable signal. A realistic implementation would require the revision to be observable *before* the return window begins, which the provided data do not support.

A defensible composite uses only the three clean signals: long high `gross_prof`, short high `asset_growth`, short high `idio_vol`, equal-weighted. Given individual Sharpes of 1.94, -1.48, and -1.20 and modest pairwise correlation, a combined long-short book plausibly achieves an annualized Sharpe in the 1.8–2.2 range and a gross annualized return around 20–25%, before transaction costs.

## Limitations

- The analyst-revision signal is measured contemporaneously with the target month and almost certainly embeds look-ahead bias; its reported performance is not achievable in live trading.
- The sample covers a single 120-month window of index constituents; results may not generalize to other periods, universes, or market regimes.
- Quintile long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, borrow fees, and market impact, which would materially reduce net performance for high-turnover signals.
- No risk-model neutralization (size, sector, beta) was applied, so some signal returns may reflect unintended factor exposures rather than pure alpha.
- IC t-statistics assume independent monthly observations; serial correlation in factor returns would inflate apparent significance.
