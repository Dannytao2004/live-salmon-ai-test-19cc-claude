# Title
Cross-Sectional Return Signals in a Monthly Equity Panel: Profitability, Accruals, Idiosyncratic Volatility, and Leverage

## Abstract
We evaluate nine stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns using 34,387 stock-month observations from 500 index constituents over 120 months. Using monthly Spearman rank ICs and quintile long-short portfolios, we identify four statistically reliable signals: gross profitability, accruals, leverage (positive), and idiosyncratic volatility (negative). The strongest signals are gross profitability and low idiosyncratic volatility, with annualized long-short Sharpe ratios above 1.1. An equal-weighted composite of the four oriented signals has an estimated gross annual return of about 7.4% and Sharpe ratio of roughly 1.5 before costs. The remaining characteristics—book-to-market, log market cap, asset growth, turnover, and R&D intensity—lack robust predictive power in this sample.

## Methods
The panel contains one row per stock per month, with `ret_next` defined as the stock return over month \(t+1\). I evaluate each feature using two standard cross-sectional diagnostics:

1. **Monthly rank IC**: the Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, averaged over all 120 months. The reported t-stat is \(\text{mean IC} / \text{std IC} \times \sqrt{120}\).
2. **Quintile long-short portfolio**: each month, stocks are sorted into quintiles by the feature; the long-short return is the equal-weighted return of the top quintile minus the bottom quintile. The annualized Sharpe ratio is \(\text{mean monthly LS return} / \text{std monthly LS return} \times \sqrt{12}\).

I classify a feature as a valid signal if it has a clear directional interpretation and an absolute IC t-stat of at least approximately 2.0, with corroboration from the long-short portfolio. For negatively oriented signals, the implementable strategy buys the bottom quintile and sells the top quintile.

## Results
The four valid signals are:

| Feature | Direction | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | LS annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | Positive | 0.0293 | 3.83 | 0.88% | 1.19 |
| `accruals` | Positive | 0.0130 | 2.06 | 0.31% | 0.50 |
| `idio_vol` | Negative | -0.0266 | -3.76 | -0.78% | -1.15 |
| `leverage` | Positive | 0.0137 | 2.56 | 0.50% | 0.99 |

Gross profitability is the strongest positive signal: high-profitability firms outperform low-profitability firms by 0.88% per month in an equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio. Idiosyncratic volatility is the strongest negative signal: high-idio-vol stocks underperform low-idio-vol stocks, so the implementable low-minus-high portfolio earns about 0.78% per month. Leverage and accruals are positive but weaker; accruals in particular runs contrary to the classic accrual anomaly and should be treated cautiously despite its marginally significant IC t-stat of 2.06.

The remaining features are rejected:

- `book_to_market`: mean rank IC 0.0029, t-stat 0.54, long-short Sharpe -0.11.
- `log_mktcap`: mean rank IC -0.0043, t-stat -0.67, long-short Sharpe -0.14.
- `asset_growth`: mean rank IC -0.0164, t-stat -1.87, long-short Sharpe -0.78. This is economically plausible but falls just below the conventional |t| = 2 threshold.
- `turnover`: mean rank IC 0.0042, t-stat 0.64, long-short Sharpe 0.23.
- `rd_intensity`: mean rank IC 0.0018, t-stat 0.30, long-short Sharpe 0.20.

I propose an equal-weighted composite strategy: each month, compute cross-sectional ranks for `gross_prof`, `accruals`, `leverage`, and negative `idio_vol`; average the ranks into a composite score; buy the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted. The oriented individual long-short returns average about 0.62% per month, implying roughly 7.4% annualized arithmetic return. Under mild positive cross-signal correlation assumptions, the composite annualized Sharpe is approximately 1.5 before transaction costs.

## Discussion
The results support two robust cross-sectional patterns: profitability matters positively, and idiosyncratic volatility matters negatively. These are consistent with quality/profitability and low-volatility anomalies. The positive leverage and accruals signals are less conventional. Positive leverage could reflect index-constituent composition, while positive accruals contradicts the standard accrual anomaly and may be a sample-specific or survivorship-driven artifact. Because the panel consists of stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, the sample is not point-in-time and likely overrepresents surviving, successful firms. This can bias both the level of long-short returns and the estimated signal strengths.

## Limitations
- The sample includes only stocks that survive to become index constituents at the end of month 120, creating survivorship and index-inclusion bias.
- Estimated long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact, which could be material for monthly rebalanced quintile portfolios.
- Multiple testing across nine signals is not formally adjusted; accruals and leverage are only marginally significant.
- The unbalanced panel has increasing stock coverage over time, so cross-sectional ICs may not be perfectly comparable across early and late months.
- The analysis is in-sample and does not include out-of-sample validation or robustness to alternative portfolio construction methods.
