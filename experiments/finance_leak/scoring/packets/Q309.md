# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: Only Leverage Passes

## Abstract
We evaluate nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns using 120 monthly cross-sections of a 500-stock equity panel. Signals are assessed using average Spearman rank IC, IC t-statistics, and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio returns. Only leverage is statistically significant: mean rank IC = 0.0164 (t = 2.74), with a long-short spread of 0.52% per month and an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. A monthly long-short leverage strategy has an expected gross annual return of 6.24%. The remaining eight characteristics show weak or statistically insignificant relations. These results should be interpreted cautiously because the universe is composed of end-of-sample index constituents, inducing survivorship bias and potential look-ahead effects.

## Methods
The dataset contains 34,708 monthly observations for 500 stocks over 120 months. For each stock-month, the target is the next-month total return, `ret_next`. The nine candidate features are `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

We use the provided signal statistics. For each feature, the mean rank IC is the average within-month Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next`. The IC t-statistic is `mean(IC) / std(IC) × sqrt(120)`. The long-short portfolio buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the feature each month, equal-weighted. We classify a feature as a valid signal if it has a statistically significant rank IC t-statistic (|t| > 2) and a direction consistent with its long-short return. Expected annual return is estimated as 12 times the mean monthly long-short return.

## Results
Only `leverage` passes the significance threshold. Its mean rank IC is 0.0164 with a t-statistic of 2.74, and it is positive in 59% of months. The equal-weighted long-short portfolio returns 0.52% per month, or 6.24% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. This is the only feature with a t-statistic above 2.

The other features are not statistically reliable. `asset_growth` and `accruals` have positive but insignificant mean rank ICs of 0.0055 (t = 1.16) and 0.0059 (t = 1.03), respectively. `idio_vol` is also weak (IC = 0.0056, t = 0.86). `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` have mean rank ICs close to zero, with t-statistics ranging from -0.28 to 0.26. Their long-short returns are small and inconsistent in sign.

## Discussion
The positive leverage-return relation suggests that, in this panel, high-leverage stocks outperform low-leverage stocks. This could reflect compensation for distress or financing risk, or it could be specific to the survivor-based sample. The estimated economic magnitude is moderate: a gross annualized return of 6.24% with a Sharpe ratio of 0.92. A simple implementable strategy is a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top leverage quintile and shorts the bottom leverage quintile.

The other characteristics do not provide robust standalone signals in this sample. `asset_growth` and `accruals` show some positive but insignificant predictive power, and their long-short spreads are not strong enough to justify inclusion in the final strategy given the multiple-testing burden. The lack of significance for traditional value, profitability, size, and accrual signals may be due to the short 120-month sample, the survivor universe, or measurement noise in annual-report variables.

## Limitations
The universe consists only of stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, which induces survivorship and look-ahead bias. The panel is unbalanced: the number of stocks grows from 143 to 500 over time, so early-month estimates rely on a smaller and potentially non-representative cross-section. Expected performance is gross of transaction costs, bid-ask spreads, market impact, and shorting costs. With only 120 monthly observations and nine tested signals, multiple-comparison concerns are material. Finally, annual-report characteristics may be stale or measured with error, and the leverage signal may proxy for omitted risk or distress effects.
