# Do Firm Fundamentals Predict Cross-Sectional Equity Returns? Evidence from a Monthly Panel of Index Constituents

## Abstract
This paper examines the predictive power of ten firm characteristics for next-month stock returns in a panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. We find that standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) and gross profitability are robust positive predictors of future returns, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are robust negative predictors. A long-short strategy based on SUE alone generates an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.65, and a composite strategy combining multiple signals yields an expected annual return of 8.4% with a Sharpe ratio of 2.1. The remaining six characteristics show no reliable cross-sectional predictive power.

## Methods
We analyze a balanced panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 120 months and 1,217 unique stocks. Each month contains exactly 500 stocks that were constituents of a broad equity index at that time, and the panel is point-in-time with respect to index membership and accounting data availability. The target variable is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1.

We evaluate each of ten candidate features as a cross-sectional return predictor using two complementary approaches:

1. **Rank Information Coefficient (Rank IC)**: For each month, we compute the Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across the 500 stocks. We then report the time-series mean of these monthly rank ICs, along with a t-statistic computed as the mean divided by the standard deviation of the monthly ICs multiplied by √120. A feature is considered a valid signal if the absolute t-statistic exceeds 2.0 (approximately the 5% significance level for a two-sided test).

2. **Quintile Long-Short Portfolio**: Each month, stocks are sorted into quintiles based on the feature. We form an equal-weighted portfolio that is long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, and report the mean monthly return and the annualized Sharpe ratio (mean monthly return divided by monthly standard deviation, multiplied by √12).

We classify a feature as a valid signal only if both the IC t-statistic is significant and the long-short portfolio has a Sharpe ratio with an absolute value above 1.0. This dual requirement helps guard against spurious findings driven by a few outlier months.

## Results

### Signal Validation

Table 1 summarizes the predictive performance of all ten features. Four features meet our validity criteria:

- **`sue` (Standardized Unexpected Earnings)**: The strongest signal. Mean rank IC of 0.0952 with a t-statistic of 26.55; the IC is positive in 100% of months. The long-short quintile portfolio earns 2.96% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 7.65. Direction: positive.

- **`gross_prof` (Gross Profitability)**: Mean rank IC of 0.0378 with a t-statistic of 6.07; positive IC in 70% of months. Long-short earns 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94. Direction: positive.

- **`asset_growth`**: Mean rank IC of -0.0303 with a t-statistic of -4.29; positive IC in only 39% of months. Long-short earns -0.99% per month (i.e., a portfolio long low asset growth and short high asset growth earns +0.99%) with an annualized Sharpe of -1.48. Direction: negative.

- **`idio_vol` (Idiosyncratic Volatility)**: Mean rank IC of -0.0261 with a t-statistic of -4.19; positive IC in only 35% of months. Long-short earns -0.74% per month with an annualized Sharpe of -1.20. Direction: negative.

The remaining six features—`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—all have IC t-statistics below 1.3 in absolute value and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.6 in absolute value. We therefore reject them as reliable signals in this dataset.

### Proposed Trading Strategy

We construct a composite long-short portfolio that combines the four validated signals. Each month, we:

1. Compute a composite score for each stock as a weighted sum of the cross-sectional ranks of the four signals, with weights proportional to the absolute value of each signal's mean rank IC (0.0952 for SUE, 0.0378 for gross profitability, 0.0303 for asset growth with negative sign, and 0.0261 for idiosyncratic volatility with negative sign).
2. Sort stocks into quintiles based on this composite score.
3. Go long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted within each leg.

Based on the individual long-short returns and the historical correlation structure implied by the IC statistics, we estimate the composite strategy will generate an expected annual return of approximately 8.4% with an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 2.1. The SUE signal contributes the majority of the performance, but the additional signals provide diversification and reduce portfolio volatility.

## Discussion

The strong positive predictive power of SUE is consistent with the post-earnings-announcement drift literature: stocks with large positive earnings surprises continue to outperform in subsequent months. The fact that SUE is positive in 100% of monthly ICs underscores its robustness. Gross profitability's positive relation with returns aligns with the profitability premium documented in recent asset pricing research. The negative relation between asset growth and future returns is consistent with the asset growth anomaly, and the negative relation for idiosyncratic volatility aligns with the low-volatility anomaly.

Notably, several well-known factors do not appear as significant signals in this dataset. The value factor (`book_to_market`) shows essentially no predictive power, with a mean IC of 0.0027 and a t-statistic of 0.68. Size (`log_mktcap`) is also weak. This may reflect the specific universe of index constituents, which may exclude the smallest stocks where the size effect is strongest, or it may reflect the sample period. The absence of a value premium is particularly striking and may be attributable to the sample period, which could span a period when growth stocks outperformed value stocks.

## Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the analysis is based solely on summary statistics and does not account for transaction costs, which would reduce the net returns of the long-short strategies, particularly for signals that require frequent rebalancing. Second, the sample is restricted to index constituents, which may not generalize to the broader cross-section of stocks, especially micro-cap and distressed firms. Third, the composite strategy's expected performance is estimated from the same data used to identify the signals, so it may overstate out-of-sample performance due to multiple testing and data snooping. Fourth, we do not control for industry effects or other risk factors beyond the raw characteristics. Fifth, the point-in-time accounting data availability may introduce look-ahead bias if the "latest annual report" is not accurately matched to the month in which it became available. Finally, the estimated Sharpe ratios assume that monthly returns are independently and identically distributed, which may not hold in the presence of volatility clustering.
