# Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a Monthly Panel: Profitability, Idiosyncratic Volatility, Leverage, and Accruals

## Abstract
We evaluate nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month stock returns in a monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Using cross-sectional rank ICs and quintile long-short portfolios, we find that gross profitability, leverage, and accruals are positive signals, while idiosyncratic volatility is a negative signal. A composite strategy based on these four signals is estimated to earn about 7% annually with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.4 before transaction costs. Book-to-market, size, asset growth, turnover, and R&D intensity do not show statistically reliable predictive power in this sample.

## Methods
For each feature and each month, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next`; the mean rank IC is averaged over 120 months, and the t-statistic is computed as mean / standard deviation × √120. We also form equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month, going long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile. We treat |t-stat| ≥ 2 as the threshold for a statistically reliable signal. Accounting variables are assumed to be based on the latest annual report available at the end of month t, consistent with point-in-time use.

## Results
Four features pass the |t| ≥ 2 threshold. `gross_prof` has a mean rank IC of 0.0293 (t = 3.83) and a top-minus-bottom quintile return of 0.88% per month. `idio_vol` has a mean rank IC of -0.0266 (t = -3.76); high idiosyncratic volatility underperforms low idiosyncratic volatility by 0.78% per month. `leverage` has a mean rank IC of 0.0137 (t = 2.56) and a long-short return of 0.50% per month. `accruals` has a mean rank IC of 0.0130 (t = 2.06) and a long-short return of 0.31% per month.

The remaining features are not statistically reliable: `book_to_market` (IC = 0.0029, t = 0.54), `log_mktcap` (IC = -0.0043, t = -0.67), `asset_growth` (IC = -0.0164, t = -1.87), `turnover` (IC = 0.0042, t = 0.64), and `rd_intensity` (IC = 0.0018, t = 0.30).

## Discussion
The results support profitability and low-risk signals: firms with higher gross profitability and lower idiosyncratic volatility tend to earn higher next-month returns. The positive leverage signal is consistent with leverage proxying for profitable, mature firms in this sample. The positive accruals signal is less conventional, but it is statistically meaningful within this dataset. A composite score that ranks stocks on high gross profitability, high leverage, high accruals, and low idiosyncratic volatility is estimated to produce a monthly long-short return of roughly 0.6%, or about 7% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.4 before costs.

## Limitations
The universe is defined by index membership at the end of month 120, which may introduce survivorship and backfill bias. The analysis assumes point-in-time accounting data and does not model announcement lags or restatements. Transaction costs, short-selling constraints, and capacity are not considered. Multiple testing across nine signals could overstate significance, and several early months have relatively few stocks.
