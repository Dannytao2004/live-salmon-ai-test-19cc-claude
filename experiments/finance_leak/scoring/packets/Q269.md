# Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a Monthly Index Panel: Profitability, Investment, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We examine ten stock-level characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a 120-month panel of 500 broad-index stocks. Three signals—gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility—are statistically reliable and economically sensible: high gross profitability, low asset growth, and low idiosyncratic volatility predict higher returns. Analyst revisions exhibit the strongest univariate correlation but are not investable because they are measured contemporaneously with the target return. A composite long-short quintile strategy based on the three valid signals is estimated to earn roughly 11.6% annually with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.0 before costs.

## Methods
The dataset is a point-in-time monthly panel of 500 stocks per month for 120 months. For each feature, we measure cross-sectional predictive ability using the Spearman rank correlation between the feature at the end of month \(t\) and `ret_next`, the total stock return over month \(t+1\). The reported rank IC is the average of these monthly cross-sectional correlations. Statistical significance is assessed with a t-statistic: mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation, multiplied by \(\sqrt{120}\).

We also report equal-weighted long-short quintile returns: each month, stocks are sorted into quintiles on a feature, and the strategy buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile. A feature is considered a valid signal only if it is statistically significant and is available at the end of month \(t\). We require \(|t| > 2\).

One important data alignment issue affects `analyst_rev`. According to the data dictionary, `analyst_rev` is the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month \(t+1\), recorded at the end of month \(t+1\). Because `ret_next` is also the return over month \(t+1\), this variable is contemporaneous with the target and would not have been known at portfolio formation. Therefore it is excluded as a usable signal despite its high univariate statistics.

## Results
Three features pass the significance threshold and are available at formation:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

The signs imply that high gross profitability, low asset growth, and low idiosyncratic volatility predict higher next-month returns. These are consistent with the profitability, investment, and low-risk/volatility anomalies documented in the literature.

`analyst_rev` has the largest univariate rank IC, 0.2963, with a t-statistic of 80.29 and a long-short monthly return of 8.73%. However, because it is measured during month \(t+1\), it is not a valid predictive signal in this exercise and is rejected.

The remaining features are not statistically significant. `book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` all have absolute t-statistics below 1.3, and their long-short Sharpe ratios are close to zero.

## Discussion
The three valid signals can be combined into a single composite score. I propose a monthly equal-weight long-short quintile strategy that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of a composite score equally combining:

\[
\text{score} = z(\text{gross\_prof}) - z(\text{asset\_growth}) - z(\text{idio\_vol})
\]

The equal-weight combination of the three aligned long-short quintile portfolios has an expected gross monthly return of approximately \((1.16\% + 0.99\% + 0.74\%) / 3 = 0.96\%\), or about 11.6% annualized. Assuming modest diversification across the three signals, the expected annualized Sharpe ratio is approximately 2.0. This estimate is intentionally conservative relative to the strong univariate Sharpe ratios because the signals are likely positively correlated.

## Limitations
The performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, short-selling costs, borrowing costs, and capacity constraints. The composite Sharpe ratio is approximated from univariate long-short statistics without directly estimating the correlation matrix of the signal portfolios. The sample is limited to 500 broad-index constituents and may not generalize to smaller stocks or other markets. Finally, the strong analyst revision signal is excluded due to look-ahead bias; a properly lagged version could be an important signal if available.
