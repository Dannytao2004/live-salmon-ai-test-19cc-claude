# Report Q097

# No Robust Point-in-Time Equity Signals After Removing Look-Ahead Bias in Analyst Revisions

## Abstract
We evaluate 10 candidate cross-sectional stock characteristics in a 120-month panel of 500 stocks per month. Analyst revisions show an extraordinarily strong rank IC of 0.296 (t-stat 89.6) and long-short Sharpe of 26.4, but this variable is recorded at the end of the return month and is therefore look-ahead biased. After excluding it, no feature has a statistically significant rank IC or economically reliable long-short spread after accounting for multiple testing. We recommend against deploying a signal-based long-short strategy on these predictors; expected active alpha is zero before costs. The results highlight how look-ahead bias can create illusory but unrealizable performance.

## Methods
We use a monthly stock panel with 120 months, 500 stocks per month, and 10 characteristics. For each feature, we compute the within-month Spearman rank correlation between the feature and next-month returns, averaged across months. The t-statistic is calculated as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation times the square root of the number of months. We also construct equal-weight top-quintile minus bottom-quintile long-short portfolios each month and report mean monthly returns and annualized Sharpe ratios.

A feature is considered a valid tradable signal only if it is known at the end of month *t* and has statistically reliable predictive power. We pay particular attention to the data dictionary timing of each variable.

## Results
The standout feature is `analyst_rev`, with a mean rank IC of 0.2958, a t-statistic of 89.6, 100% of months with positive IC, and a long-short monthly return of 8.66%. However, `analyst_rev` is defined as the change in the FY1 EPS forecast during month *t+1*, recorded at the end of month *t+1*. It is therefore not available at the time the return prediction would need to be made. Its strong predictive power is contemporaneous with the return and cannot be captured in a real-time strategy.

Among the remaining nine point-in-time features, none passes standard statistical thresholds. The largest rank IC t-statistics are `asset_growth` at 1.64 and `accruals` at 1.34. Their long-short Sharpe ratios are 0.72 and 0.70, respectively, which are modest and do not survive multiple-testing adjustment across 10 candidate features. All other features have rank IC t-statistics near or below 1.0 and economically small long-short returns. Thus, after removing the look-ahead signal, there is no robust evidence that any characteristic predicts next-month returns.

## Discussion
The only strong predictor in this dataset is exactly the one that cannot be used in real time. This is consistent with analyst revisions being partly a response to contemporaneous stock-price moves rather than a pre-existing signal. The mild positive long-short returns for `asset_growth` and `accruals` may reflect chance, data mining, or omitted risk factors rather than reliable anomalies. Given the sample size and multiple testing, the prudent conclusion is that no robust point-in-time cross-sectional signal exists among these features. A passive market exposure is preferable to trading on weak or biased signals.

## Limitations
This analysis uses summary statistics only and does not model transaction costs, market impact, short-selling constraints, or capacity. Results are in-sample and may be overstated when many features are examined. The look-ahead conclusion for `analyst_rev` depends on the data dictionary timing being correct; if a lagged point-in-time version were available, it would need separate evaluation.
