# Evaluating Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a Point-in-Time Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. The only feature with strong univariate predictive power is `analyst_rev`, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.2958 and a t-statistic of 89.6, but it is recorded at the end of month t+1 and is therefore not investable. After excluding this look-ahead variable, none of the nine point-in-time features has a rank IC significantly different from zero at conventional thresholds. We conclude there is no robust implementable signal in this dataset and recommend a passive long-only portfolio rather than an active long-short strategy.

## Methods
For each month and each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next`. The reported rank IC is the time-series mean of these monthly correlations over 120 months. The t-statistic is computed as the mean rank IC divided by its monthly standard deviation, multiplied by the square root of the number of months.

We also form equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios each month: buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile of each feature. We require that a feature be available at the end of month t to be considered a valid predictor of returns over month t+1. We use a conventional significance threshold of |t-stat| ≥ 2.0.

## Results
The table below reports the main signal statistics.

| feature | mean rank IC | IC t-stat | long-short monthly return | long-short annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | -0.27 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| analyst_rev | 0.2958 | 89.59 | 8.66% | 26.38 |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | -0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 |

`analyst_rev` is the only feature that passes the statistical threshold, and its rank IC is positive in 100% of months. However, the data dictionary indicates that `analyst_rev` is the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, recorded at the end of month t+1. This means it uses information from the same month as the return being predicted and is not available at the end of month t. Its strong predictive performance is therefore a look-ahead artifact, not a tradable signal.

The remaining point-in-time features all have t-statistics below conventional significance. `asset_growth` has the largest t-statistic among them at 1.64, followed by `accruals` at 1.34, but neither is robust. We therefore identify no valid investable signals in this sample.

## Discussion
The apparently extraordinary predictive power of `analyst_rev` is exactly what one would expect from a variable measured contemporaneously with returns: positive forecast revisions during month t+1 coincide with positive returns over month t+1. This is not exploitable after portfolio formation at the end of month t.

The absence of significant predictive power among traditional point-in-time characteristics such as book-to-market, gross profitability, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, and leverage may reflect the relatively short sample, the broad index universe, or simply no stable anomaly in this particular simulated or real panel. The weak positive long-short returns for asset growth and accruals are not supported by significant rank ICs.

## Limitations
- The sample is limited to 120 months and 500 stocks per month, which may limit statistical power.
- The analysis does not account for transaction costs, short-selling constraints, or implementation costs.
- Monthly rank IC t-statistics may be affected by autocorrelation or cross-sectional correlation not modeled here.
- The look-ahead issue in `analyst_rev` is clear from the data dictionary, but other accounting-based features may also be affected by reporting lags or restatements.
- No multiple-testing adjustment is applied beyond the baseline significance threshold.
