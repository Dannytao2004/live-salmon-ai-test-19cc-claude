# A Robust Anomaly in Standardized Unexpected Earnings: Evidence from a Monthly Stock Panel

## Abstract

This study analyzes a panel of 500 stocks per month over 120 months to identify cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns. Among ten candidate features, only standardized unexpected earnings (`sue`) displays a statistically and economically significant relationship with future returns, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.0998 (t-stat = 23.25) and a long-short quintile spread of 3.04% per month (annualized Sharpe 7.31). All other features show negligible or statistically insignificant associations. A simple strategy that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of `sue` each month would have generated an annualized return of approximately 36.5% before costs. These findings highlight the persistence of earnings momentum in equity markets, while common value, quality, and risk-based signals do not appear predictive in this sample.

## Methods

The dataset consists of a monthly panel of 60,000 observations (1,213 unique stocks, 500 stocks per month) covering 120 months. The target variable is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. Ten features are considered: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `sue`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month, then average across all months to obtain the mean rank IC. The t-statistic is calculated as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation, multiplied by the square root of the number of months (√120 ≈ 10.95). A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value and is economically meaningful (consistent sign and magnitude). We also construct an equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of each feature each month, reporting the mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

## Results

Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics for all ten features.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | -0.27 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| **sue** | **0.0998** | **23.25** | **3.04%** | **7.31** |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | -0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | 0.13 |

The `sue` feature exhibits a mean rank IC of 0.0998 with a t-statistic of 23.25, overwhelmingly significant. The sign is positive, meaning stocks with higher standardized unexpected earnings tend to have higher next-month returns. The long-short quintile portfolio returns 3.04% per month on average, corresponding to an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31 before transaction costs. The IC is positive in 99% of months, indicating remarkable consistency.

All other features have rank IC t-statistics below 2.0 in absolute value. Their long-short returns are economically small (0.29% per month or less) and their Sharpe ratios are below 1.0. None of these features show a consistent, statistically significant relationship with future returns.

## Discussion

The dominance of `sue` as the only valid signal is striking. Standardized unexpected earnings capture the surprise in quarterly earnings announcements relative to analyst consensus, scaled by historical surprise volatility. The strong positive rank IC suggests a post-earnings announcement drift (PEAD) effect: stocks with positive earnings surprises continue to outperform in the following month, while those with negative surprises underperform. The magnitude (3.04% monthly spread) is large even by anomaly standards, and the near-universal positive IC across months indicates a robust, persistent phenomenon.

The absence of predictive power in other well-known factors—value (book_to_market), profitability (gross_prof), size (log_mktcap), investment (asset_growth), accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—may reflect the specific sample construction. The universe consists of 500 stocks that are constituents of a broad equity index at each point in time. This index membership filter may exclude many small, distressed, or highly volatile stocks where size, value, and volatility effects are typically strongest. Additionally, the relatively short sample (120 months) and the specific time period may limit the power of some weaker anomalies.

The practical implications are clear: in this large-cap, index-constrained universe, earnings momentum is the single dominant cross-sectional signal. A simple quintile long-short strategy based on `sue` would have delivered exceptional risk-adjusted returns. However, implementation costs (trading, shorting fees, capacity) would likely reduce the net performance significantly.

## Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the sample is restricted to index constituents, which limits generalizability to the broader market. Second, the analysis is purely in-sample; no out-of-sample validation or walk-forward testing was performed. Third, the reported long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, borrowing fees, and other implementation frictions. Fourth, the dataset does not include information on stock price or trading volume beyond turnover, preventing a more detailed analysis of liquidity and capacity constraints. Fifth, the 120-month period may capture a particular market regime that favors earnings momentum. Finally, the study does not control for potential multicollinearity or interaction effects among features.
