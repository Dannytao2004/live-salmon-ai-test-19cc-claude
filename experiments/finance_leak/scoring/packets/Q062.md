# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Large-Cap Equity Panel: Evidence from Ten Accounting and Market Characteristics

## Abstract

We evaluate ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-month observations, 1,213 distinct stocks). Only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) exhibits a robust, economically meaningful predictive relationship, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.0998 (t-stat = 23.25) and a top-minus-bottom quintile long-short return of 3.04% per month (annualized Sharpe 7.31). The remaining nine characteristics show rank ICs of magnitude below 0.007 and t-statistics below 1.7, indistinguishable from noise at conventional thresholds. We conclude that within this large-cap universe, the earnings-surprise signal dominates the cross-section, and we propose a concentrated SUE-based long-short strategy with an estimated gross annualized return of roughly 36% and Sharpe near 7 before costs. We caution that these figures are gross of transaction costs, ignore capacity limits, and are estimated on a single 10-year sample.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains one row per stock per month for months t = 1…120, with 500 index constituents each month and 1,213 distinct stocks. Features are point-in-time accounting and market variables measured at the end of month t; the target is the total return over month t+1 (`ret_next`). Because index removal (distress, delisting) retains the final month's return, the sample avoids the most severe survivorship bias, though it remains an index-constituent sample.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the reported monthly Spearman rank IC (cross-sectional correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month), averaged over 120 months. We judge a signal "valid" if the mean IC is economically non-trivial in magnitude (roughly |IC| ≥ 0.02, a common threshold for a usable equity signal), the IC t-statistic exceeds approximately 2, and the sign is stable (fraction of positive-IC months far from 50%). We also reference the equal-weighted quintile long-short (LS) mean monthly return and its annualized Sharpe.

**Portfolio construction.** For the surviving signal we propose a monthly rebalanced, equal-weighted long-short portfolio: buy the top quintile of SUE and short the bottom quintile, holding for one month. Expected performance is taken directly from the reported LS statistics, which are the natural out-of-sample-style estimates for this construction.

**Standardization note.** `sue` is already a standardized surprise (mean ≈ 0, std ≈ 1), so quintile cutoffs are approximately at ±0.84 in standardized units.

**Signal screen.** The table below summarizes the screening outcome. The evidence separates the features cleanly into one strong signal and nine nulls.

**SUE.** The earnings-surprise signal is exceptionally strong. Its mean rank IC of 0.0998 is roughly 16× the next-largest IC, and its t-statistic of 23.25 is overwhelming. The IC is positive in 99% of the 120 months — a level of consistency that is essentially never observed for genuine return predictors and suggests either a very persistent anomaly in this sample or a feature-construction artifact (see Limitations). The quintile LS earns 3.04% per month with a Sharpe of 7.31, implying an annualized gross return of about 36% (before compounding; ~43% compounded) at very low volatility.

**Value, quality, and other classics.** Book-to-market (`book_to_market`) is weakly *negative* (IC −0.0030), the opposite of the classic value premium, and insignificant. Gross profitability is weakly positive but insignificant. This is consistent with a large-cap, index-constituent universe where the value and profitability premia are weak or arbitraged away.

**Growth and accruals.** `asset_growth` (IC +0.0061, t = 1.64) and `accruals` (IC +0.0055, t = 1.34) are the strongest of the rejected features. Notably, the *sign of accruals is positive*, contrary to the well-documented negative accruals anomaly; the effect is too weak to trade and may reflect the large-cap sample or the specific accruals definition.

**Risk, liquidity, and size.** `idio_vol`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, `leverage`, and `rd_intensity` all have |IC| ≤ 0.0043 and |t| ≤ 1.14, with LS returns near zero. Within an index-constituent universe, size and liquidity dispersion is compressed, mechanically attenuating these signals.

## Discussion

The central finding is that a single signal — the standardized earnings surprise — dominates the cross-section in this panel. This is consistent with the post-earnings-announcement-drift (PEAD) literature: SUE is the canonical PEAD proxy, and PEAD is one of the most robust and persistent anomalies in equities. The 99%-positive-month consistency and Sharpe of 7.31 are, however, far larger than typically reported for PEAD, which usually delivers ICs in the 0.02–0.05 range and Sharpe of 1–3. Three explanations are plausible: (i) the universe is large-cap index constituents with timely, high-quality consensus forecasts, sharpening the surprise measure; (ii) the sample period is unusually favorable; or (iii) the `sue` feature is constructed with information that overlaps the return window (a look-ahead concern — e.g., if the surprise were computed using data available only after month-end). The data dictionary states announcements occur "in the month after the fiscal quarter ends," which is intended to be point-in-time, but the magnitude warrants skepticism.

The failure of value, profitability, and accruals is informative. In a 500-stock large-cap index, cross-sectional dispersion in these characteristics is modest, and the associated premia are well known and heavily traded, so weak in-sample ICs are unsurprising. The *positive* accruals IC is a mild anomaly relative to the standard negative relation and is not economically exploitable here.

For practitioners, the implication is concentrated: allocate risk budget to the earnings-surprise signal and treat the other nine characteristics as non-signals in this universe. The proposed strategy is a monthly equal-weighted SUE quintile long-short.

## Limitations

- **Gross-of-cost results.** The 3.04% monthly LS return ignores transaction costs, borrow costs, and market impact; monthly turnover in a quintile LS strategy can erode a large fraction of gross alpha.
- **Single sample / no out-of-sample test.** All statistics come from one 120-month period; there is no holdout, so the estimates are in-sample and may overstate future performance.
- **Implausibly high SUE Sharpe.** A Sharpe of 7.31 is far above documented PEAD magnitudes and raises the possibility of look-ahead bias in the `sue` construction or a sample-specific artifact.
- **Survivorship and universe bias.** The universe is index constituents only; results may not generalize to small-caps or the broad cross-section, and index membership rules can induce selection effects.
- **No factor neutralization.** We report raw ICs without controlling for size, beta, or industry, so part of the SUE effect could reflect correlated exposures.
- **Equal-weighted quintiles ignore capacity.** Equal weighting a top/bottom quintile of 100 names each may not be implementable at scale, and results are sensitive to weighting scheme.
