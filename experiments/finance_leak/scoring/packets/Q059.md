# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Index Panel: Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics for predicting next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Applying a strict point-in-time availability rule, we reject `analyst_rev` because it is recorded at the end of month t+1 and therefore embeds look-ahead bias relative to `ret_next`. Among the remaining features, gross profitability has a positive rank IC of 0.0378, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility have negative rank ICs of -0.0303 and -0.0261, all with large t-statistics. A monthly equal-weighted long-short composite of these three signals has an expected gross annualized return of about 11.6% and an estimated Sharpe ratio near 2.2. The other characteristics show no reliable cross-sectional predictive power.

## Methods
We use a monthly panel with 60,000 stock-month observations, 1,217 distinct stocks, and 500 stocks per month over 120 months. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. For each feature, we require that the value be available at the end of month t, before the return period begins.

The data dictionary shows that `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` are all measured using information available at or before the end of month t. In contrast, `analyst_rev` is the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, recorded at the end of month t+1. It therefore overlaps the prediction month and cannot be used to predict `ret_next` in a real-time strategy.

For each available feature, we use the reported monthly Spearman rank IC, its t-statistic across the 120 months, and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolio returns. A feature is considered a valid signal if it is available at formation and has a statistically reliable rank IC, typically |t-stat| > 2. For signals with negative rank IC, the profitable long-short direction is reversed: buy the bottom quintile and sell the top quintile. We then form a simple equal-weighted composite long-short strategy using the valid signals.

## Results
Three features are valid and statistically significant after enforcing point-in-time availability.

| Feature | Direction | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Long-Short Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | Positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | Negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | Negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

For `asset_growth` and `idio_vol`, the table reports the long-short return in the direction of the raw feature. The profitable direction is reversed: short high asset growth and short high idiosyncratic volatility. In that orientation, `asset_growth` earns +0.99% monthly with a Sharpe of 1.48, and `idio_vol` earns +0.74% monthly with a Sharpe of 1.20.

The other features are not reliable. `book_to_market` has a mean rank IC of 0.0027 with t-stat 0.68; `log_mktcap` has 0.0055 with t-stat 1.26; `accruals` has 0.0015 with t-stat 0.36; `leverage` has -0.0011 with t-stat -0.30; `turnover` has 0.0006 with t-stat 0.14; and `rd_intensity` has -0.0004 with t-stat -0.10. None clears a reasonable significance threshold.

`analyst_rev` has an extremely strong reported rank IC of 0.2963 and t-stat 80.29, but it is not a valid predictor because it is recorded after the return month. Its apparent power comes from contemporaneous information, not from a tradable forecast at the end of month t.

We propose a composite strategy that equal-weights the three valid signals. The composite score is:

`score = rank(gross_prof) - rank(asset_growth) - rank(idio_vol)`

Each month, buy the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of this score, with equal weights within each side. The average profitable long-short return across the three individual signals is (1.16% + 0.99% + 0.74%) / 3 = 0.9633% per month, or about 11.6% annualized on a simple basis. Assuming moderate rather than zero correlation among the three signals, we estimate a gross annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.2.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with well-documented cross-sectional patterns. Gross profitability is a quality signal: more profitable firms tend to outperform. Asset growth is an investment signal: firms that expand their balance sheets aggressively tend to underperform, so the profitable trading direction is short high-growth firms. Idiosyncratic volatility is a low-volatility signal: high idiosyncratic volatility stocks tend to underperform, so the profitable direction is short high-idio-vol stocks.

The rejected characteristics are either economically weak in this panel or subsumed by the valid signals. The most important methodological finding is that `analyst_rev` must be excluded despite its enormous reported IC. Because it is measured during month t+1, using it to predict `ret_next` would be a classic look-ahead error. Any strategy that includes it would report spectacular but untradable performance.

The composite strategy is intentionally simple. It avoids estimating unstable covariance matrices and relies on signals with strong univariate evidence. Still, its expected performance is gross of transaction costs and assumes that the long-short portfolios can be implemented at reasonable cost.

## Limitations
- The sample covers only 120 months and a fixed universe of 500 index constituents, so results may not generalize to other periods or broader universes.
- Transaction costs, borrow costs, slippage, and market impact are ignored, and these can materially reduce a monthly long-short strategy’s net return.
- The combined Sharpe estimate assumes diversification across the three signals; if their returns are positively correlated, the realized Sharpe will be lower.
- Annual-report-based characteristics may be stale, restated, or subject to reporting lags that are not fully captured by the monthly panel.
- `analyst_rev` is excluded because of clear look-ahead bias, but other subtle timing or survivorship biases may remain.
