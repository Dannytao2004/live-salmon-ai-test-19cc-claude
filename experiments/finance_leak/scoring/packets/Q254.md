# Point-in-Time Equity Return Signals: Profitability, Asset Growth, and Idiosyncratic Volatility

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a point-in-time panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. After enforcing that all signals are available at the portfolio formation date, gross profitability is a positive return predictor, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negative predictors. Analyst revisions have the strongest univariate rank IC but are not usable because they are recorded at the end of the future return month. A monthly long-short composite of the three valid signals has an estimated gross annual return of about 20% and an annualized Sharpe ratio near 2.6.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations from months 1 to 120, with 500 index constituents per month. The target is `ret_next`, the stock return over month t+1. For each feature and each month, I compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next`. The reported mean rank IC is the average of these monthly rank correlations. The t-statistic is computed as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation and multiplied by `sqrt(120)`.

A feature is treated as a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value and if the feature is available at the end of month t, before the return is realized. I also examine quintile long-short portfolio returns. I reject `analyst_rev` despite its very high rank IC because the data dictionary states it is recorded at the end of month t+1, making it contemporaneous with the target return rather than a valid ex ante predictor.

## Results
Three features pass the point-in-time and statistical significance screens:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Direction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `gross_prof` | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | Positive |
| `asset_growth` | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | Negative |
| `idio_vol` | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | Negative |

`analyst_rev` has an extremely large mean rank IC of 0.2963 and a t-statistic of 80.29, but it is rejected because it is recorded at the end of month t+1. The other features—`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—have rank IC t-statistics below conventional statistical significance thresholds.

## Discussion
The results suggest that, in this sample, more profitable firms, firms with slower asset growth, and firms with lower idiosyncratic volatility earn higher next-month returns. These results are consistent with prior literature on quality, investment, and low-volatility anomalies.

I propose a monthly rebalanced long-short strategy based on a composite score:

\[
\text{Score} = z(\text{gross\_prof}) - z(\text{asset\_growth}) - z(\text{idio\_vol})
\]

Each month, the strategy buys the top quintile of the composite score and sells the bottom quintile. Using the three long-short spreads and assuming approximate independence, the expected composite monthly return is

\[
(1.16\% + 0.99\% + 0.74\%) / \sqrt{3} \approx 1.67\%
\]

which annualizes to approximately 20.0% gross. The corresponding annualized Sharpe ratio is estimated at roughly 2.6. This is a gross estimate before transaction costs, short-sale frictions, and capacity constraints.

## Limitations
The main limitation is that expected returns are gross and do not include transaction costs, borrow costs, or the price impact of monthly rebalancing. The composite Sharpe estimate assumes the three valid signals are approximately independent and their historical premia persist. Additionally, the sample covers 500 index constituents, so the results may not generalize to smaller or less liquid stocks. Finally, `analyst_rev` is excluded because of look-ahead bias, but this means the strongest univariate statistical relation in the data is not tradable at the assumed formation date.
