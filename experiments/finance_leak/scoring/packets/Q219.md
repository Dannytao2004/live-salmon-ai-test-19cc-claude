# Point-in-Time Evaluation of Cross-Sectional Equity Signals

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. The only statistically significant raw signal is analyst consensus revision, with a monthly rank IC of 0.296 and a t-stat of 89.6, but this variable is measured during month t+1 and recorded at the end of month t+1, so it is contaminated by look-ahead bias and is not implementable. After requiring point-in-time availability and a conventional |t-stat| > 2 threshold, no features qualify as valid return predictors. We recommend against an active signal-based long-short strategy and use a passive equal-weight market portfolio as the fallback.

## Methods
The dataset is a balanced monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. For each feature, I compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` each month, then average these rank ICs across months. The t-stat is computed as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation, multiplied by sqrt(120). Long-short returns are equal-weighted top-quintile minus bottom-quintile portfolios.

I apply two screening criteria. First, a feature must be known at the end of month t to predict `ret_next` over month t+1. Second, I require |t-stat| > 2 for statistical significance. The variable `analyst_rev` is measured during month t+1 and recorded at the end of month t+1, the same period as the target return, so it is excluded as look-ahead biased.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics and screening decision.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | Reject: weak |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | Reject: weak |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | Reject: weak |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | Reject: below threshold |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | Reject: below threshold |
| analyst_rev | 0.2958 | 89.59 | Reject: look-ahead bias |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | Reject: weak |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | Reject: weak |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | Reject: weak |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | Reject: weak |

No features survive the point-in-time and significance screens. `analyst_rev` would generate an 8.66% monthly long-short return and an annualized Sharpe of 26.38 if used, but it is not known at the portfolio formation date.

## Discussion
The only strong predictor in the raw data is `analyst_rev`, but its timing makes it a contemporaneous measure of revisions and returns rather than a forecast. This is a clear example of look-ahead bias: using end-of-month t+1 information to predict month t+1 returns.

The remaining features are statistically indistinguishable from noise. `asset_growth` and `accruals` have positive point estimates, but their t-stats of 1.64 and 1.34 do not clear the conventional significance threshold. We therefore recommend no active long-short strategy. A passive equal-weight market portfolio has an expected monthly return of 0.00936, annualized to approximately 0.1123, with an annualized volatility of about 0.3922 and an expected annual Sharpe of about 0.286.

## Limitations
- The analysis uses summary statistics only and does not account for transaction costs, short-sale constraints, or capacity.
- The sample is limited to 120 months and 500 stocks, which limits power to detect weak signals.
- Multiple testing and data mining can produce false positives among weak signals.
- A hard |t-stat| > 2 threshold may reject economically small but real signals such as `asset_growth` or `accruals`.
- `analyst_rev` is rejected because of look-ahead timing; a properly lagged analyst revision variable was not available.
