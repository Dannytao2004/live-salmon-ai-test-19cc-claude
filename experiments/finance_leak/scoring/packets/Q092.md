# Leverage as a Robust Cross-Sectional Signal for Next-Month Stock Returns

## Abstract
This study evaluates nine stock characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns using a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Based on monthly rank ICs and quintile long-short portfolio tests, only leverage has a statistically significant average rank IC of 0.0164 (t-stat = 2.74). The other eight features have |t|-statistics below 2 and are not reliable return signals in this sample. A monthly-rebalanced long-short strategy that buys the top leverage quintile and shorts the bottom quintile earns 0.52% per month, or approximately 6.24% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92 before costs.

## Methods
The dataset contains 34,708 stock-month observations across 120 months and 500 stocks. For each feature, I compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between the feature and next-month return within each month, then average these rank ICs over all 120 months. The t-statistic for the mean rank IC is calculated as the mean divided by the standard deviation of monthly ICs, multiplied by the square root of 120. I treat a feature as a valid signal if its mean rank IC is statistically distinguishable from zero at conventional levels, using |t| ≥ 2.0 as the threshold.

In addition, for each feature I evaluate a monthly-rebalanced long-short portfolio that sorts stocks into quintiles, buys the top quintile, and sells the bottom quintile with equal weights within each quintile. Reported performance is gross of transaction costs and short-selling costs.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Long-short annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| book_to_market | -0.0017 | -0.28 | -0.05% | -0.08 |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | 0.12% | 0.22 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | -0.07% | -0.14 |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | 0.37% | 0.74 |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | 0.24% | 0.41 |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | 0.13% | 0.19 |
| leverage | 0.0164 | 2.74 | 0.52% | 0.92 |
| turnover | -0.0005 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.23 |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | -0.06% | -0.11 |

Only leverage has a statistically significant mean rank IC. Its IC is positive in 59% of months, and the long-short leverage quintile portfolio has the highest monthly mean return and annualized Sharpe among all nine features.

## Discussion
The evidence indicates that leverage is the only robust cross-sectional predictor among the nine characteristics considered. The positive relation suggests that, in this sample, stocks with higher total debt to assets earn higher next-month returns, consistent with a risk-based interpretation. The effect is economically meaningful in a long-short quintile framework but should be interpreted cautiously because its statistical significance is modest once the full set of signals is considered.

The remaining features do not demonstrate reliable univariate predictive power. Several, such as asset growth, accruals, and idiosyncratic volatility, have positive point estimates but fail to reach statistical significance. Their long-short portfolios also show lower Sharpe ratios, reinforcing the conclusion that they are not dependable signals in this dataset.

## Limitations
This study has several limitations. The universe is defined by index membership at the end of month 120, which may introduce survivorship or selection bias. Fundamental data are taken from the latest annual report available at each month-end; if the dataset is not fully point-in-time, results may be affected by look-ahead bias. The reported long-short returns are gross of transaction costs, short-selling constraints, and borrowing costs. Finally, with nine tested features, leverage is only marginally significant under stricter multiple-testing adjustments, so the signal should be validated out-of-sample before deployment.
