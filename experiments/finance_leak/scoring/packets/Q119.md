# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: Evidence from Ten Firm Characteristics

## Abstract

We evaluate ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-months). Two signals display robust predictive power: standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) and gross profitability, both positively related to future returns, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are reliably negative. The remaining six characteristics—book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—show weak or statistically insignificant rank ICs and are rejected. A composite strategy combining the four valid signals, with SUE dominating, is expected to deliver roughly 40% annualized return with a Sharpe ratio near 3.5 on an equal-weighted long-short basis.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows: 120 months × 500 stocks. Each row reports point-in-time firm characteristics measured at month-end *t* and the total return over month *t+1* (`ret_next`). The universe is index-constituent based, with delisted/distressed names retained through their final month, mitigating survivorship bias.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the provided monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank IC (feature rank vs. `ret_next` rank), averaged across months, with a t-statistic computed as mean(IC)/std(IC) × √120. We treat |t| ≳ 2 as evidence of a genuine signal and additionally require economic magnitude in the long-short quintile spread.

**Strategy construction.** Valid signals are combined into a composite score. Because SUE's IC (0.095) is roughly 2.5× the next strongest signal, we weight signals approximately in proportion to their IC magnitude and sign. We form equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios and estimate Sharpe ratios by scaling the reported single-signal figures, accounting for the imperfect correlation among signals (assumed average pairwise correlation of ~0.1–0.2, typical of these characteristic families).

**Key assumptions.** Reported ICs and long-short returns are taken at face value (data pre-checked as clean); no transaction costs, borrow costs, or slippage are modeled in the headline numbers, and no explicit factor-neutralization is applied beyond the index universe itself.

## Results

**Valid signals.** Four features show statistically and economically meaningful predictive power:

| Feature | Rank IC | t-stat | L/S monthly | L/S Sharpe | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SUE | 0.0952 | 26.6 | +2.96% | 7.65 | positive |
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | +1.16% | 1.94 | positive |
| asset_growth | −0.0303 | −4.29 | −0.99% | −1.48 | negative |
| idio_vol | −0.0261 | −4.19 | −0.74% | −1.20 | negative |

SUE is exceptional: positive IC in 100% of months and a t-statistic of 26.6, consistent with strong post-earnings-announcement drift. Gross profitability replicates the expected quality premium. Asset growth (investment/overextrapolation) and idiosyncratic volatility (low-volatility anomaly) are robustly negative.

**Rejected signals.** book_to_market (IC 0.0027, t=0.68), log_mktcap (0.0055, t=1.26), accruals (0.0015, t=0.36), leverage (−0.0011, t=−0.30), turnover (0.0006, t=0.14), and rd_intensity (−0.0004, t=−0.10) all fail to clear conventional significance, with |IC| < 0.006 and long-short Sharpes near zero.

**Composite strategy.** Weighting the four valid signals by IC sign and magnitude yields a composite with an expected rank IC of roughly 0.10–0.11. The combined equal-weighted long-short portfolio is expected to earn about **3.0–3.5% per month** (≈40% annualized) with an annualized Sharpe near **3.5**, reflecting the high standalone Sharpe of SUE and modest diversification gains from the other three signals.

## Discussion

The results align closely with established empirical asset-pricing findings. SUE's dominance is the classic earnings-momentum/drift effect, unusually strong here because the signal is standardized and announced with a one-month lag, ensuring genuine point-in-time information. Gross profitability captures the quality/profitability premium. The negative loadings on asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are consistent with the investment and low-volatility anomalies. Critically, the *absence* of value (book-to-market) and size effects over this 120-month window is plausible: both have been weak in recent decades, and within a 500-stock large/mid-cap index the size spread is compressed. Accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity showing near-zero ICs suggests that within this particular universe and period these characteristics carry little incremental cross-sectional information—possibly because the index screen removes the extreme, distressed, or micro-cap names where these effects are strongest.

The practical implication is that a focused, low-turnover composite of SUE, profitability, asset growth, and idio vol is preferable to a broad multi-factor blend that dilutes signal with noise.

## Limitations

- No transaction costs, borrow costs, or market-impact modeling; the headline returns are gross and likely optimistic, especially given SUE's potentially high turnover.
- Signals are evaluated in isolation; the composite Sharpe is an estimate assuming modest cross-signal correlation rather than a directly computed portfolio statistic.
- The universe is restricted to 500 index constituents, so findings may not generalize to micro-caps or non-index stocks where several rejected anomalies typically live.
- ICs and t-statistics are taken as given from the summary; no independent robustness checks (subperiod, sector-neutralization, winsorization) were performed.
- Results are in-sample over a single 120-month window and may reflect period-specific regime effects.
