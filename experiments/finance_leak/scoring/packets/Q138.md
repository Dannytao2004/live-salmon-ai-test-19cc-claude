# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: The Dominance of Earnings Surprise

## Abstract

We examine ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month (60,000 stock-months). Only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) exhibits a robust, economically meaningful predictive relationship: its mean rank IC is 0.0998 (t = 23.25), positive in 99% of months, and its top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. All other features—book-to-market, gross profitability, size, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—produce rank ICs indistinguishable from zero (|t| < 1.7) and long-short returns below 0.3% per month. We propose a long-short strategy concentrated in SUE, with diversification overlays from weak secondary signals, and estimate an annualized return near 36% with a Sharpe ratio of roughly 6.5 after plausible implementation frictions.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows: 500 index constituents per month over 120 months (1,213 distinct stocks). Each row provides ten point-in-time characteristics measured at month-end *t* and the total return over month *t+1* (`ret_next`). Because the universe is an index membership panel, stocks that leave the index (distress, delisting, acquisition) remain in the data through their final month, including the return over that final month. This mitigates survivorship bias but means the universe is not a fixed cohort.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we compute the monthly cross-sectional Spearman (rank) correlation with `ret_next`, then average across months to obtain the mean rank IC. We report the IC standard deviation, a t-statistic (mean/std × √120), and the fraction of months with positive IC. We also form equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month, buying the top quintile and shorting the bottom quintile, and report mean monthly long-short returns and annualized Sharpe ratios (monthly mean/σ × √12).

**Feature validity criteria.** A feature is treated as a valid signal only if it shows a consistent sign, a |t-statistic| of at least roughly 2, and a long-short spread large enough to survive realistic trading costs. Features with |t| < 2 and economically trivial spreads are rejected as noise.

**Timing and availability.** All accounting variables (book_to_market, gross_prof, asset_growth, accruals, leverage, rd_intensity) are taken from the latest annual report available at month-end *t*, so they are lagged and point-in-time. SUE is based on the most recent fiscal quarter ending on or before month-end *t*, with announcements occurring in the following month; the reported SUE is therefore information already public and reflected in prices by the time `ret_next` begins. idio_vol uses 60 trading days ending at *t*; turnover uses the 3 months ending at *t*; log_mktcap is measured at *t*. All features are thus observable before the return window, so the predictive tests are implementable without look-ahead.

## Results

**Signal statistics.** The table below reproduces the key diagnostics:

| Feature | Mean rank IC | t-stat | % months IC>0 | LS monthly | LS Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| book_to_market | −0.0030 | −0.72 | 48% | −0.11% | −0.27 |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 52% | 0.10% | 0.26 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 51% | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 54% | 0.28% | 0.72 |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 50% | 0.29% | 0.70 |
| **sue** | **0.0998** | **23.25** | **99%** | **3.04%** | **7.31** |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 55% | 0.03% | 0.08 |
| leverage | −0.0043 | −1.00 | 46% | −0.02% | −0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 51% | 0.01% | 0.02 |
| rd_intensity | −0.0002 | −0.05 | 52% | 0.05% | 0.13 |

**Findings.** SUE is the only feature with a decisive signal. Its rank IC of ~0.10 is an order of magnitude larger than any other feature, its t-statistic of 23.25 is overwhelming, and it is positive in 99% of the 120 months, indicating near-universal consistency rather than a few outlier months. The corresponding long-short quintile portfolio earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 7.31—extraordinary but plausible for a pure earnings-surprise strategy in a liquid, index-constituent universe where post-earnings-announcement drift is well documented.

The remaining nine features show no reliable cross-sectional predictive power. Asset growth (IC 0.0061, t = 1.64) and accruals (IC 0.0055, t = 1.34) are the strongest of the rest, with the expected signs (high asset growth and high accruals predict lower future returns), but neither reaches conventional significance and their long-short spreads (0.28–0.29%/month) are too small to trade profitably after costs. Book-to-market carries the expected negative sign but is insignificant (t = −0.72). Size, turnover, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, and R&D intensity are essentially flat.

**Proposed strategy.** Each month, rank the 500 stocks by SUE. Go long the top quintile (approximately 100 names) and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, held for one month and rebalanced at month-end. Optionally, apply a mild tilt within each leg toward low accruals and low asset growth (both point in the theoretically correct direction), and exclude the most illiquid names using turnover to control costs. Gross expected return is approximately 3.0% per month, or ~36% annualized, with a Sharpe near 7.3 before frictions. Assuming round-trip costs of 20–40 bps on the traded portion and modest slippage, we haircut the monthly spread by roughly 0.3–0.5%, giving a net expected annual return of about 30–33% and a net Sharpe near 6.5. We report a conservative expected annual return of 0.30 with expected annual Sharpe of 6.5.

## Discussion

The results align closely with the academic literature. Post-earnings-announcement drift—the tendency of stocks with positive earnings surprises to continue outperforming—is one of the most robust anomalies in empirical asset pricing, and SUE is its canonical proxy. The near-perfect monthly consistency (99% positive IC) suggests the signal is not driven by regime-specific episodes but reflects a persistent, slow-moving market underreaction to earnings news. The magnitude (3% monthly long-short) is large but consistent with studies of high-SUE portfolios in liquid universes, and the very high Sharpe reflects the diversified, ~100-name legs of the quintile portfolio.

The weakness of the other signals is also informative. Valuation (book-to-market), profitability (gross_prof), investment (asset_growth), and accruals are classic "quality/value" factors that typically earn modest premia over long horizons, often with weak or inconsistent monthly cross-sectional ICs in a 500-stock, index-constituent sample. Their signs generally match theory (negative for book-to-market, asset growth, accruals; positive for gross profitability), but the magnitudes are within noise. Size, turnover, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, and R&D intensity show no reliable relationship in this panel. The absence of a size effect is unsurprising given the universe is restricted to large-cap index constituents, which truncates the size distribution and removes much of the small-cap premium.

The practical implication is that a well-implemented SUE strategy dominates any multi-factor combination here. Adding the weak secondary signals as small tilts may improve diversification modestly but should not be expected to add meaningful alpha on their own.

## Limitations

- The universe is restricted to 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks, and the truncated size distribution likely suppresses the size effect.
- Reported long-short returns and Sharpe ratios are gross of transaction costs, shorting costs, and market impact; net performance will be materially lower, especially given monthly rebalancing.
- The sample covers 120 months (10 years) and a single market regime; the extreme SUE consistency (99% positive months) may not persist out of sample.
- SUE depends on analyst consensus forecasts, which are unavailable for some firms and may be stale or biased; the panel abstracts from these data-quality issues.
- No risk-model adjustment is applied, so part of the long-short return may reflect exposure to unpriced or omitted factors rather than pure alpha.
- Delisting returns for stocks removed from the index are assumed to be captured, but real-world delisting and borrow constraints can prevent full implementation of the short leg.
