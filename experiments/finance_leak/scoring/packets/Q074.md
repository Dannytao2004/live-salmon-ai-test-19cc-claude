# Cross-Sectional Equity Return Prediction: Evidence from Nine Firm Characteristics

## Abstract
We examine 34,387 monthly stock observations from 500 index constituents over 120 months to evaluate nine firm characteristics as predictors of next-month returns. Using monthly rank information coefficients (ICs) and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find robust positive signals from gross profitability, leverage, and accruals, and a robust negative signal from idiosyncratic volatility. A composite strategy that buys high gross-profitable, high-leverage, high-accrual, low-idiosyncratic-volatility stocks and shorts the opposite quintiles earns an estimated 7.5% annualized return with an annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.1 before costs. The remaining characteristics—book-to-market, size, asset growth, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not show reliable predictive power in this sample.

## Methods
We use a monthly panel of 500 stocks that are constituents of a broad equity index at the end of month 120, with history back to month 1 or the month they joined the index. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month \(t+1\). For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month, then average across the 120 months. The reported t-stat is \(\text{mean IC} / \text{IC standard deviation} \times \sqrt{120}\). We also evaluate equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month. For the composite strategy, we align signals by economic direction: positive for gross profitability, leverage, and accruals; negative for idiosyncratic volatility. The composite is an equal-weighted combination of the four direction-adjusted long-short portfolios, rebalanced monthly. Performance estimates are gross of transaction costs and financing costs.

## Results
The strongest valid signal is gross profitability (`gross_prof`), with a mean rank IC of 0.0293 (t = 3.83), a long-short return of 0.88% per month, and an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.19. Idiosyncratic volatility (`idio_vol`) is a strong negative predictor: mean rank IC = -0.0266 (t = -3.76), long-short return = -0.78% per month, and Sharpe = -1.15. Leverage (`leverage`) is positive and significant: mean rank IC = 0.0137 (t = 2.56), long-short return = 0.50% per month, Sharpe = 0.99. Accruals (`accruals`) is weaker but statistically marginal: mean rank IC = 0.0130 (t = 2.06), long-short return = 0.31% per month, Sharpe = 0.50.

Other features fail to show reliable predictive power. Book-to-market has mean rank IC = 0.0029 (t = 0.54) and a long-short Sharpe of -0.11. Log market cap has mean rank IC = -0.0043 (t = -0.67) and Sharpe = -0.14. Asset growth has the expected negative sign but is below conventional significance: mean rank IC = -0.0164 (t = -1.87), long-short return = -0.64% per month, Sharpe = -0.78. Turnover has mean rank IC = 0.0042 (t = 0.64) and Sharpe = 0.23. R&D intensity has mean rank IC = 0.0018 (t = 0.30) and Sharpe = 0.20.

For the composite strategy, the direction-adjusted monthly returns are approximately: gross profitability +0.88%, low idiosyncratic volatility +0.78%, leverage +0.50%, and accruals +0.31%. The equal-weighted average is about 0.62% per month, or roughly 7.4% annualized simple and 7.7% compounded. Given the individual Sharpe ratios and likely positive but imperfect correlations among these signals, we estimate a composite annualized Sharpe ratio near 1.1. Before transaction costs, the strategy is economically meaningful, though modest in magnitude.

## Discussion
The results support a quality/low-volatility interpretation of cross-sectional returns. High gross profitability and low idiosyncratic volatility are associated with higher next-month returns, consistent with profitability and low-risk anomalies. Leverage also predicts positively in this sample, although the standard interpretation of leverage as a distress proxy would suggest the opposite sign; this may reflect value or sector exposures within the index universe. Accruals predict positively, which is opposite to the classic accruals anomaly, so we treat this signal as weak and potentially sample-specific. Asset growth has the expected negative sign and a non-trivial long-short return, but its t-stat of -1.87 is below the conventional 5% threshold; we therefore exclude it from the core composite while flagging it as a watchlist signal. Book-to-market, size, turnover, and R&D intensity show little reliable predictive power in this panel.

## Limitations
- Survivorship bias: the universe contains only stocks that remained index constituents at month 120, with backfilled history, which may overstate returns.
- The sample is relatively short at 120 months and has small early cross-sections, limiting statistical power and out-of-sample reliability.
- Equal-weighted long-short estimates ignore transaction costs, shorting borrow fees, market impact, and liquidity constraints.
- No risk-factor, industry, or sector neutralization is applied; results may reflect unintended exposures.
- Annual accounting data are lagged and may be restated, and feature definitions may not exactly match those in the academic literature.
- Rank IC and quintile returns are sensitive to outliers and non-linear relationships.
