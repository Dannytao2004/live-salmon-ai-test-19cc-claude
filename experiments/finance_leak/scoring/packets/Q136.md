# Cross-Sectional Return Prediction in a Monthly Index Panel: Evidence from Fundamental, Earnings, and Trading Signals

## Abstract
Using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month, I evaluate ten firm characteristics for predicting next-month stock returns. Four signals are statistically valid: standardized unexpected earnings (`sue`) and gross profitability (`gross_prof`) positively predict returns, while asset growth (`asset_growth`) and idiosyncratic volatility (`idio_vol`) negatively predict returns. The remaining six characteristics—`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—have weak and/or statistically insignificant rank ICs. A simple equal-weighted composite long-short strategy using the four valid signals has an estimated gross annual return of roughly 18% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of roughly 3.5. The strongest single signal is `sue`, with an average monthly rank IC of 0.0952 and positive ICs in 100% of months.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations across 120 months and 1,217 distinct stocks, with exactly 500 stocks per month. For each feature, I use the provided monthly Spearman rank IC with `ret_next`, averaged across months, along with its t-statistic and the performance of an equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile portfolio. I classify a signal as valid if it has a plausible economic interpretation and a large absolute t-statistic, primarily |t| > 2, while also considering the sign consistency and long-short Sharpe ratio. For the composite strategy, I orient each valid signal in its predictive direction: `sue` and `gross_prof` are used positively; `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` are used negatively. I assume equal weighting across the four oriented signals, monthly rebalancing, and long top-quintile / short bottom-quintile implementation.

The strongest evidence is for earnings surprise and quality/profitability, with negative effects for investment and volatility. The table below summarizes the signal tests.

The four valid signals have oriented long-short monthly returns of approximately +2.96% (`sue`), +1.16% (`gross_prof`), +0.99% (short `asset_growth`), and +0.74% (short `idio_vol`). An equal-weighted oriented composite therefore has an approximate gross monthly return of 1.46%, or roughly 18%–19% annualized when compounded. Because `sue` is by far the strongest signal, the composite remains attractive even if the other signals are only modestly correlated with it. Assuming moderate positive cross-signal correlation, I estimate an annualized Sharpe ratio near 3.5.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with well-known cross-sectional return patterns. `sue` captures post-earnings-announcement drift and is the dominant signal in this panel, with positive ICs in every month. `gross_prof` supports a quality/profitability premium: more profitable firms outperform. `asset_growth` is negative, consistent with the investment/asset-growth anomaly—firms expanding their balance sheets aggressively tend to underperform. `idio_vol` is negative, consistent with a low-volatility or lottery-demand effect. The rejected signals are either economically weak in this large-cap index sample, already priced, or too noisy: `book_to_market` and `log_mktcap` have near-zero ICs, while `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` have t-statistics far below conventional significance thresholds.

## Limitations
- The analysis is based on provided summary statistics, not raw data, so I cannot verify outliers, missingness, or exact feature definitions.
- Transaction costs, shorting costs, taxes, and market impact are ignored; the composite strategy requires monthly rebalancing and may face meaningful turnover.
- The universe is limited to 500 broad-index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to microcaps, small caps, or non-US equities.
- `sue` has an exceptionally high Sharpe ratio and 100% positive-month IC, which may reflect look-ahead bias, announcement-timing issues, or data-mining in the provided panel.
- Cross-signal correlations are not provided, so the composite Sharpe estimate relies on an assumption of moderate diversification.
- The sample covers only 120 months and may not include multiple full market regimes.
