# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Large-Cap Equity Panel: Evidence from Nine Accounting and Market Characteristics

## Abstract

We examine nine firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents (34,708 stock-months). Only one characteristic, leverage, exhibits a statistically meaningful rank information coefficient (mean IC = 0.0164, t = 2.74, 59% of months positive), with a long-short quintile spread of 0.52% per month and an annualized Sharpe of 0.92. The remaining eight signals are statistically indistinguishable from zero, with |t|-statistics below 1.2 and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.75. We conclude that the panel offers only weak evidence of cross-sectional predictability, consistent with an efficient large-cap universe, and propose a modest leverage-tilted long-short strategy with an expected annualized return near 6% and Sharpe near 0.9, subject to substantial estimation uncertainty.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 34,708 monthly observations on 500 stocks over months 1–120. The universe is defined by index membership at month 120, with history backfilled to month 1 or to the month of index inclusion. This induces a survivorship-like composition: the number of stocks grows monotonically from 143 in month 1 to 500 in month 120, so early months cover a smaller and, by construction, ultimately successful subset of firms. All features are lagged appropriately: accounting variables come from the latest annual report available at month-end t, and market variables (idio_vol, turnover, log_mktcap) are measured at month-end t. The target is the total return over month t+1.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation (rank IC) with ret_next within each month, then average across the 120 months. The t-statistic is the mean IC divided by the IC standard deviation, scaled by √120. We also report the equal-weighted long-short quintile spread (top minus bottom quintile) and its annualized Sharpe ratio (mean monthly spread / std × √12).

**Signal selection rule.** We treat a feature as a valid signal only if |mean IC t-stat| ≥ 2 and the long-short Sharpe is at least 0.5, requiring both statistical and economic significance. This guards against selecting on noise given nine simultaneous tests.

**Strategy construction.** For the surviving signal we form a monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio: long the top quintile, short the bottom quintile, rebalanced monthly. Expected return is the reported mean monthly spread annualized (×12); expected Sharpe is the reported annualized figure. No transaction costs are modeled explicitly, but we discuss the likely impact.

## Results

**Signal-by-signal evidence.** Table 1 (reproduced from the signal statistics) summarizes the findings.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | % months IC>0 | LS monthly | LS Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| book_to_market | −0.0017 | −0.28 | 51% | −0.05% | −0.08 |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | 50% | 0.12% | 0.22 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | 56% | −0.07% | −0.14 |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | 52% | 0.37% | 0.74 |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | 54% | 0.24% | 0.41 |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | 50% | 0.13% | 0.19 |
| **leverage** | **0.0164** | **2.74** | **59%** | **0.52%** | **0.92** |
| turnover | −0.0005 | −0.10 | 52% | 0.13% | 0.23 |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | 52% | −0.06% | −0.11 |

**Leverage.** Leverage is the only feature clearing our selection bar. Its mean rank IC of 0.0164 is roughly three times larger than any other feature and its t-statistic of 2.74 is the only one above 2. The positive sign indicates that higher-debt firms outperform lower-debt firms in this sample—the opposite of the classic "distress" interpretation of leverage, but consistent with a value/risk-premium reading in a large-cap, index-constituent universe where high leverage may proxy for financial maturity and cash-return discipline. The long-short spread of 0.52%/month translates to approximately 6.4% annualized with an annualized Sharpe of 0.92. The 59% positive-IC hit rate is modest but directionally consistent.

**Non-signals.** Book-to-market, gross profitability, log market cap, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, turnover, and R&D intensity all have |t| < 1.2 and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.75. Several have economically trivial spreads (|mean| ≤ 0.13%/month). Notably, the value (book-to-market) and profitability (gross_prof) premia, which are robust in broad historical samples, are absent here—likely because the universe is restricted to surviving large-cap index members, which compresses cross-sectional dispersion in these characteristics.

**Strategy backtest.** A monthly equal-weighted long-short portfolio on leverage (top vs. bottom quintile) would have delivered an average 0.52% per month, or roughly 6.4% annualized, with annualized volatility of about 7.0% (0.52%/0.92 × √12 ≈ 1.96% monthly × √12), giving a Sharpe of 0.92. The strategy's hit rate and t-statistic suggest the edge is real but not overwhelming.

## Discussion

The central finding is the near-total absence of cross-sectional predictability in eight of nine standard characteristics, with leverage as the sole exception. This pattern is consistent with two non-exclusive explanations. First, the universe is an index-constituent, large-cap sample. Well-known anomalies (value, profitability, low-volatility, investment) are strongest in small-cap and distressed segments; restricting to surviving index members removes much of the cross-sectional spread that drives those premia. Second, the sample is short (120 months) and the number of stocks grows over time, so early-period IC estimates rest on as few as 143 names—noisy and potentially unrepresentative.

The leverage result should be interpreted cautiously. A t-statistic of 2.74 across nine tested features implies a multiple-testing-adjusted significance that is marginal at best (a Bonferroni threshold of 0.05/9 ≈ 0.0056 corresponds to |t| ≈ 2.77). Leverage's t of 2.74 sits just below that threshold. Its economic magnitude (0.52%/month gross) is also small relative to plausible transaction costs for a monthly-rebalanced quintile portfolio, which for large-cap names might run 10–20 basis points per side, eroding a meaningful fraction of the gross spread. The strategy is therefore best viewed as a candidate for further out-of-sample validation rather than a deployable edge.

The absence of the value and profitability premia is itself informative: it suggests that in this particular survivorship-filtered, large-cap panel, accounting-based signals carry little next-month information, and that any predictability is more likely to reside in market-based or financing-related variables.

## Limitations

- **Survivorship and composition bias.** The universe is defined by index membership at month 120, so stocks that delisted or underperformed and left the index are excluded; this likely attenuates distress-related and value signals.
- **Short sample and unbalanced panel.** Only 120 months, with as few as 143 stocks in early months, makes IC estimates noisy and reduces power to detect modest signals.
- **Multiple testing.** Nine features were screened; the leverage t-statistic of 2.74 does not survive a strict Bonferroni correction, so the result may be a false positive.
- **No transaction costs or capacity analysis.** The reported long-short returns are gross; monthly rebalancing in large caps would incur nontrivial costs, and the strategy's capacity was not assessed.
- **Single-factor strategy.** We did not orthogonalize leverage against other characteristics (e.g., size, value), so the signal may proxy for correlated exposures rather than a distinct effect.
- **No out-of-sample split.** All statistics are in-sample; no holdout period was reserved to validate the leverage signal.
