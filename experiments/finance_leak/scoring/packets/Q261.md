# No Real-Time Alpha in a Monthly Equity Panel: The Perils of Look-Ahead Bias

## Abstract
We evaluate ten firm characteristics for predicting next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents per month. Most characteristics have near-zero Spearman rank information coefficients (ICs) and long-short portfolio Sharpes below 0.75, with none statistically significant after multiple-testing considerations. The only feature with an apparently strong relation, `analyst_rev` (mean rank IC 0.296, t = 89.6), is recorded at the end of month t+1 and therefore overlaps the return period, making it a look-ahead biased variable rather than a tradable signal. After excluding `analyst_rev`, no feature provides robust real-time predictive power, and a strategy based on these characteristics should have zero expected alpha.

## Methods
We use a monthly stock panel of 60,000 observations, covering 1,213 distinct stocks and 500 stocks per month over 120 months. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. The candidate features are `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `analyst_rev`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

For each feature, the provided summary statistics report the average monthly Spearman rank IC across stocks, its t-statistic (mean IC / IC standard deviation × √120), the fraction of months with positive IC, and the mean monthly return of an equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolio (long top quintile, short bottom quintile). We interpret a feature as a valid real-time signal only if it is available at the end of month t, shows a statistically significant rank IC (|t| > 1.96), and has an economically plausible sign. We also consider the multiple-testing burden of evaluating ten features.

## Results
Among the nine features available at the end of month t, mean rank ICs range from -0.0043 (`leverage`) to +0.0061 (`asset_growth`). Their t-statistics range from -1.00 to 1.64, all below the conventional 5% threshold. Long-short mean monthly returns range from -0.11% to +0.29%, with annualized Sharpes from -0.27 to 0.72. The strongest non-leakage signals are `asset_growth` (rank IC +0.0061, t = 1.64, long-short Sharpe 0.72) and `accruals` (rank IC +0.0055, t = 1.34, long-short Sharpe 0.70). However, both are only marginally significant before multiple-testing adjustment and have positive signs that contradict the well-documented negative asset-growth and accruals anomalies, suggesting they are likely sample-specific noise.

The feature `analyst_rev` stands out with a mean rank IC of 0.2958, t = 89.59, positive IC in 100% of months, and a long-short mean monthly return of 8.66% (annualized Sharpe 26.38). This magnitude is implausible for a real-time equity signal. Crucially, the data dictionary states that `analyst_rev` is recorded at the end of month t+1, the same period as `ret_next`. It is therefore contemporaneous with the target and unavailable at the time a trading decision would be made. Its apparent predictive power is a mechanical artifact of look-ahead bias.

## Discussion
The only feature that appears strongly predictive is not investable. Using `analyst_rev` in a backtest would produce spectacular but spurious returns because the signal is measured during the return month. This is a classic look-ahead trap. The remaining features are economically plausible in some cases, but their effect sizes are tiny and statistically indistinguishable from zero in this sample. Even the best non-leakage signals, `asset_growth` and `accruals`, fail standard significance thresholds after accounting for multiple testing, and their positive directions contradict established anomalies.

A real-time strategy based on these characteristics should therefore take no active positions. Holding cash or a passive index would yield the market return with no alpha. If forced to construct a market-neutral portfolio, it would have zero expected return and zero expected Sharpe. The main practical lesson is to verify point-in-time availability before using any feature in a predictive model.

## Limitations
The analysis relies on summary statistics; raw data and additional robustness checks are unavailable. The sample period and index universe may not generalize to other times or markets. Multiple testing across ten features increases the chance of spurious findings, and some true weak signals may be missed. Feature definitions may contain measurement error or timing mismatches beyond `analyst_rev`. Transaction costs and short-selling constraints are not considered.
