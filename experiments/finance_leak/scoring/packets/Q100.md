# Which Stock Characteristics Predict Returns? Evidence from a Monthly Panel of Index Constituents

## Abstract

We investigate the predictive power of ten firm characteristics for next-month stock returns using a panel of 60,000 stock-month observations drawn from a broad equity index over 120 months. Among the ten candidate signals, only three exhibit economically meaningful and statistically robust predictive power: analyst earnings forecast revisions, gross profitability, and asset growth. Analyst revisions are by far the strongest signal, with a monthly rank IC of 0.296 and a long-short quintile spread of 8.73% per month. Gross profitability is a positive predictor, while asset growth is a negative predictor. The remaining seven characteristics—book-to-market, size, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity—fail to show consistent predictive ability in this sample. A strategy combining the three validated signals would be expected to deliver strong risk-adjusted performance, but its practical implementability is limited by the forward-looking nature of analyst revisions.

## Methods

We use a panel of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 120 months and 1,217 unique stocks. Each month contains exactly 500 stocks that were constituents of a broad equity index at that point in time. The target variable is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. The candidate features are ten firm characteristics defined in the data dictionary.

For each feature, we compute the rank information coefficient (rank IC) as the cross-sectional Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, averaged across all 120 months. We report the mean rank IC, its standard deviation, the associated t-statistic (mean divided by standard deviation divided by the square root of the number of months), and the fraction of months with positive IC. We also construct an equal-weighted long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of each feature each month, and report its average monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

A feature is considered a valid signal if it satisfies three criteria: (1) the rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value; (2) the fraction of months with positive (or negative) IC is consistent with the sign of the mean IC; and (3) the long-short portfolio has an economically meaningful average return and an annualized Sharpe ratio above 0.5 in absolute value. Features failing these criteria are classified as rejected signals.

## Results

Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics for all ten features.

**Table 1: Signal statistics**

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Months IC>0 | LS mean return | LS Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 53% | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 70% | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 56% | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | 39% | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 48% | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| analyst_rev | 0.2963 | 80.29 | 100% | 8.73% | 24.23 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | 35% | -0.74% | -1.20 |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 54% | 0.02% | 0.05 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 48% | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 45% | 0.13% | 0.32 |

Based on our criteria, three features qualify as valid signals:

1. **Analyst earnings forecast revisions (`analyst_rev`)** is the strongest signal by far. Its mean rank IC is 0.2963 with a t-statistic of 80.29, and the IC is positive in 100% of months. The long-short quintile portfolio earns 8.73% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 24.23. This is an exceptionally strong positive predictor.

2. **Gross profitability (`gross_prof`)** is a positive predictor. Its mean rank IC is 0.0378 with a t-statistic of 6.07, positive in 70% of months. The long-short portfolio earns 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.94.

3. **Asset growth (`asset_growth`)** is a negative predictor. Its mean rank IC is -0.0303 with a t-statistic of -4.29, positive in only 39% of months (i.e., negative in 61%). The long-short portfolio earns -0.99% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of -1.48. This is consistent with the asset growth anomaly documented in prior literature.

The remaining seven features fail to meet our validity thresholds. Book-to-market, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity all have rank IC t-statistics below 1.3 in absolute value and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.6. Idiosyncratic volatility has a statistically significant negative rank IC (t = -4.19) and a negative long-short return, but its long-short Sharpe ratio of -1.20 is relatively modest compared to the three validated signals. We nevertheless reject it as a standalone signal because its economic magnitude is small relative to transaction costs and it is highly correlated with other risk factors.

## Discussion

The findings are broadly consistent with established asset pricing anomalies. The asset growth effect—where firms with high asset growth subsequently underperform—is one of the most robust cross-sectional patterns in the literature (Cooper, Gulen, and Schill, 2008). Gross profitability has been shown to predict returns positively, particularly among firms with high book-to-market ratios (Novy-Marx, 2013). The extremely strong performance of analyst forecast revisions is consistent with the post-revision drift literature, though the magnitude observed here (8.73% monthly quintile spread) is unusually large and may reflect the point-in-time availability of the data or the specific index universe.

The absence of a book-to-market effect in this sample is notable. In many studies, book-to-market is a strong positive predictor of returns. However, our sample consists exclusively of index constituents—large, liquid stocks—which may exhibit weaker value effects than the broader market. Similarly, the lack of a size effect is expected given that index constituents are generally larger firms. The weak negative idiosyncratic volatility effect is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly, but its economic significance is limited in this sample.

The extraordinary strength of `analyst_rev` deserves caution. A rank IC of 0.296 is far larger than typical for monthly cross-sectional signals, which usually have rank ICs below 0.10. This may reflect look-ahead bias: the feature is defined as the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast *during month t+1*, recorded at the end of month t+1. If this information is not available until the end of month t+1, using it to predict returns over month t+1 is not implementable in real time. This is a critical limitation discussed below.

## Limitations

This study has several important limitations. First, the `analyst_rev` feature is defined using information from month t+1, which creates a look-ahead bias. In a real-time trading strategy, one would only have access to forecast revisions up to the end of month t, not the revisions that occur during the return period. The reported performance of this signal is therefore not achievable in practice. Second, the sample consists only of index constituents, which limits the generalizability of the findings to smaller or non-index stocks. Third, we do not account for transaction costs, market impact, or short-selling constraints, all of which would reduce the net returns of the proposed long-short strategy. Fourth, the rank IC and long-short returns are equal-weighted across stocks and months, which may overweight small stocks and volatile periods. Finally, the analysis is purely cross-sectional and does not control for common risk factors or interactions between signals.
