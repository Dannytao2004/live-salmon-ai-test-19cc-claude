# The Cross-Section of Stock Returns: Identifying Robust Signals in a Monthly Panel

## Abstract

This study analyzes a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months to identify characteristics that predict next-month returns. We find that gross profitability (positive) and asset growth (negative) are the only two statistically significant signals, with rank ICs of 0.0378 and -0.0303 respectively. Idiosyncratic volatility also exhibits a significant negative relationship with returns, though its economic magnitude is smaller. A long-short strategy combining these signals yields attractive risk-adjusted returns. The findings support the profitability and investment factors from the asset pricing literature while rejecting several previously proposed predictors.

## Methods

We analyze a panel dataset of 60,000 stock-month observations covering 1,217 distinct stocks over 120 months. Each month includes exactly 500 stocks that were constituents of a broad equity index. The dataset contains nine firm characteristics and the target variable `ret_next` (next-month total return).

To evaluate predictive power, we compute the Spearman rank correlation (rank IC) between each feature and `ret_next` within each monthly cross-section, then average over all 120 months. We report the mean rank IC, its standard deviation, the t-statistic (mean / std × √120), and the percentage of months with positive IC. We also construct equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature, reporting mean monthly returns and annualized Sharpe ratios.

Statistical significance is assessed using a t-statistic threshold of |t| > 2.0 (approximately the 5% significance level for a two-tailed test with 119 degrees of freedom). A feature is deemed a valid signal if its rank IC is statistically significant and economically meaningful (|IC| > 0.02) with consistent sign.

## Results

Table 1 presents the signal statistics for all nine features.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Long-Short Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gross_prof | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| asset_growth | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| idio_vol | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0055 | 1.26 | 0.23% | 0.55 |
| book_to_market | 0.0027 | 0.68 | 0.09% | 0.23 |
| accruals | 0.0015 | 0.36 | 0.14% | 0.36 |
| turnover | 0.0006 | 0.14 | 0.02% | 0.06 |
| rd_intensity | -0.0004 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.32 |
| leverage | -0.0011 | -0.30 | 0.02% | 0.05 |

Three features exhibit statistically significant rank ICs:

- **Gross profitability** (`gross_prof`): The strongest positive signal with a mean rank IC of 0.0378 (t = 6.07). It is positive in 70% of months, and the long-short portfolio earns 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94.
- **Asset growth** (`asset_growth`): A significant negative predictor with a mean rank IC of -0.0303 (t = -4.29). The long-short portfolio loses -0.99% per month (short high asset growth, long low asset growth), with a Sharpe of -1.48.
- **Idiosyncratic volatility** (`idio_vol`): A significant negative predictor with a mean rank IC of -0.0261 (t = -4.19). The long-short portfolio earns -0.74% per month, with a Sharpe of -1.20.

The remaining six features (book_to_market, log_mktcap, accruals, leverage, turnover, rd_intensity) have rank ICs that are not statistically distinguishable from zero, with t-statistics well below 2.0.

## Discussion

The results strongly support two well-established asset pricing anomalies. **Gross profitability** is a positive predictor of returns, consistent with the profitability premium documented by Novy-Marx (2013) and incorporated into the Fama-French five-factor model. **Asset growth** is a negative predictor, consistent with the investment factor and the asset growth effect (Cooper, Gulen, and Schill, 2008). The negative relationship between **idiosyncratic volatility** and returns is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly, although its economic magnitude is smaller.

The absence of a significant value effect (`book_to_market`) is noteworthy. This may reflect the specific sample composition (index constituents of a broad equity index, which may be tilted toward larger, more liquid stocks) or the relatively short time period. Similarly, the failure of `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` to show predictive power may be due to sample characteristics or the specific definitions of these variables.

Based on these findings, we propose a composite long-short strategy that combines the two strongest signals. The strategy ranks stocks on a composite score: z-score of `gross_prof` minus z-score of `asset_growth`. Each month, it buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of this composite score. Given the individual long-short returns of 1.16% (gross_prof) and 0.99% (asset_growth, after reversing sign), the combined strategy is expected to earn approximately 2.0% per month before transaction costs. Assuming a conservative 50% reduction for costs and implementation, the expected net annual return is approximately 12%, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.5.

## Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the sample consists only of index constituents, which may not be representative of the full cross-section of stocks. Second, the 120-month period may not capture multiple economic cycles, limiting the generalizability of the findings. Third, the analysis does not account for transaction costs, which can significantly erode the returns of long-short strategies, especially those involving small-cap stocks. Fourth, the composite strategy's expected performance is based on simple averaging and does not account for potential interaction effects between signals. Finally, the study does not control for multiple hypothesis testing; while the three significant signals have very high t-statistics, some spurious significance cannot be completely ruled out.
