# Evaluating Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a Monthly Panel: The Danger of Look-Ahead Bias in Analyst Revisions

## Abstract
We evaluate ten candidate stock-level predictors of next-month returns using a panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months. Only analyst consensus revision has a large cross-sectional rank IC of 0.2958 (t-stat = 89.6) and a long-short Sharpe of 26.4, but this variable is recorded at the end of the return month and is therefore not investable. After excluding this look-ahead variable, none of the remaining nine features has a statistically significant rank IC at the conventional threshold. We conclude that there is no valid, implementable active signal in this dataset and propose a zero-alpha passive implementation.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations: 500 stocks per month for 120 months. For each feature, I measure cross-sectional predictive power using the Spearman rank correlation between the feature value at the end of month t and the stock return over month t+1 (`ret_next`). These monthly rank ICs are averaged over the 120 months, and the associated t-statistic is computed as:

\[
t = \frac{\text{mean IC}}{\text{std IC} / \sqrt{120}}
\]

I also compute equal-weighted long-short quintile spreads: each month, buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile of the feature. A feature is treated as a valid signal only if its information is available at the end of month t and its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics. `analyst_rev` exhibits extremely strong predictive power, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.2958, a t-statistic of 89.6, and a long-short monthly return of 8.66%. However, the data dictionary indicates that `analyst_rev` is the change in the mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, recorded at the end of month t+1. This means it is not known at the time the portfolio would be formed at the end of month t, and it uses information contemporaneous with the return interval. It is therefore not a tradable signal.

Among the remaining nine features, the largest absolute t-statistic is 1.64 for `asset_growth`, followed by 1.34 for `accruals`. None reaches the 2.0 significance threshold. The absolute long-short Sharpe ratios are all below 0.8, and most are near zero.

## Discussion
The only economically and statistically strong predictor is `analyst_rev`, but its construction creates look-ahead bias: it is measured during the same month as the target return. A backtest using this variable would show spectacular performance that cannot be achieved in real time. After correctly requiring that signals be known at the end of month t, no feature reliably predicts next-month returns.

Some features, such as `asset_growth` and `accruals`, show weakly positive average ICs and modest long-short Sharpe ratios. However, their t-statistics are below conventional significance thresholds, and after accounting for multiple testing they are even less significant. The sign of `asset_growth` is also opposite to the usual asset-growth anomaly, further suggesting the result may be noise.

## Limitations
- The sample contains only 500 index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks.
- The significance threshold of |t| > 2.0 is conventional but arbitrary; weak signals may still exist with more data.
- No transaction costs, turnover, capacity, or implementation frictions are considered in the long-short estimates.
- The strongest signal is excluded because of look-ahead bias, leaving no positive implementable finding.
- The analysis is based only on summary statistics and not on raw data or out-of-sample testing.
