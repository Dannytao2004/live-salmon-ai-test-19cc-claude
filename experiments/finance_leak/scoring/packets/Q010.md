# Standardized Unexpected Earnings as the Sole Robust Cross-Sectional Return Predictor

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a point-in-time panel of 500 stocks per month over 120 months. Using monthly rank ICs and quintile long-short portfolios, only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) emerges as a statistically and economically significant signal, with a mean rank IC of 0.0998 and a t-statistic of 23.25. The SUE top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31. Other candidate features, including asset growth and accruals, show small positive average returns but do not reach statistical significance after multiple-testing adjustment. A monthly-rebalanced long-short SUE strategy is expected to earn approximately 36.5% gross annualized return, though implementation costs would reduce net performance.

## Methods
The dataset consists of 60,000 stock-month observations across 120 months and 1,213 distinct stocks. Each month contains 500 index constituents, with point-in-time membership and returns up to removal. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month \(t+1\).

For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month and average it over all 120 months. The t-statistic is computed as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation, multiplied by \(\sqrt{120}\). To guard against multiple testing across ten features, we use a Bonferroni-adjusted 5% threshold of approximately \(t > 2.81\).

We also form equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month. The long-short return is the difference between the top-quintile and bottom-quintile portfolio returns. The annualized Sharpe ratio is reported for each long-short portfolio.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics and testing decisions.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | Rejected |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | Rejected |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | Rejected |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | Rejected |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | Rejected |
| sue | 0.0998 | 23.25 | Valid |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | Rejected |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | Rejected |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | Rejected |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | Rejected |

SUE is the only feature that passes the Bonferroni threshold. Its mean rank IC of 0.0998 is positive in 99% of months. The SUE long-short quintile portfolio earns 3.04% per month, with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31.

Among the rejected features, `asset_growth` and `accruals` have mildly positive long-short returns of 0.28% and 0.29% per month, respectively, but their rank ICs are small and statistically insignificant. The remaining features show no reliable relation with next-month returns.

## Discussion
The results indicate that recent earnings surprises, as captured by SUE, strongly predict next-month returns in this sample. The economic magnitude is large: a top-minus-bottom quintile portfolio earns approximately 3.04% per month before costs. This is consistent with the post-earnings-announcement drift anomaly, where stocks with positive earnings surprises continue to outperform stocks with negative surprises.

A practical strategy would rank all stocks each month by SUE, buy the top quintile, and sell the bottom quintile, rebalancing monthly. At the observed historical mean, this strategy has an expected gross annualized return of approximately 36.5% and an expected annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 7.3. However, these figures are almost certainly overstated for a real-world implementation because they ignore transaction costs, short-selling frictions, and capacity constraints.

Other features often associated with cross-sectional returns, such as value, profitability, asset growth, and accruals, do not provide robust independent signals in this particular sample after adjusting for multiple testing.

## Limitations
The sample is limited to 120 months and 500 index stocks per month, which may not generalize to other markets or time periods. The reported performance is gross of transaction costs, borrow costs, and price impact. SUE requires consensus analyst forecasts, which may bias the universe toward larger and more widely covered stocks. The long-short strategy assumes short selling is always feasible and ignores rebalancing frictions. Finally, the extremely high Sharpe ratio likely overstates implementable performance due to data-snooping and capacity concerns.
