# Report Q005

# No Implementable Cross-Sectional Return Predictors After Excluding a Look-Ahead Analyst Revision Signal

## Abstract
We evaluate ten monthly stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a point-in-time panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. The only feature with strong univariate predictive power is analyst earnings revisions, which has a mean rank IC of 0.296 and a long-short Sharpe of 26.4. However, that feature is recorded at the end of month t+1 and is therefore look-ahead biased and not implementable. Among the remaining nine point-in-time features, none has a two-sided rank IC t-statistic above 2; asset growth and accruals are the closest, with t-statistics of 1.64 and 1.34. We conclude that there is no robust implementable long-short signal in this panel and recommend no trading strategy based on these features.

## Methods
The dataset is a monthly panel with 60,000 stock-month observations from months 1 to 120, with 500 stocks per month. The target variable is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1.

For each feature, I compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, then average across all 120 months to obtain the mean rank IC. The IC t-statistic is computed as:

\[
t = \frac{\text{mean rank IC}}{\text{std rank IC}} \times \sqrt{120}
\]

I use a two-sided significance threshold of \(|t| \ge 2\). I also examine equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month.

A key data-quality check is that `analyst_rev` is described as the change in mean FY1 EPS forecast during month t+1, scaled by share price, and is recorded at the end of month t+1. Because the prediction target is the month t+1 return, this feature uses future information and cannot be used in a real-time strategy.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics.

The `analyst_rev` feature is extraordinarily strong: positive in 100% of months, with a mean rank IC of 0.2958 and a long-short monthly return of 8.66%. However, because it is recorded at the end of month t+1, it is contemporaneous with the return and cannot be used to predict it.

After excluding `analyst_rev`, no feature is statistically significant. `asset_growth` and `accruals` have weakly positive rank ICs and long-short returns around 0.28% to 0.29% per month, but their t-statistics are below conventional thresholds. Given that ten features are tested, these are likely consistent with noise.

## Discussion
The only strong predictor in this dataset is not a predictor at all: it is a contemporaneous analyst revision variable contaminated by look-ahead bias. This illustrates how easy it is to overstate backtest performance when a feature is measured after the return period begins or ends.

The absence of robust signals among the other nine features may reflect market efficiency in this large-index universe, stale annual-report fundamentals, or limited sample size. `asset_growth` and `accruals` directionally suggest some positive cross-sectional relation, but the evidence is too weak to recommend trading. A disciplined quant strategy should not allocate capital to signals with rank IC t-statistics below 2, especially after multiple-testing adjustments.

## Limitations
- The only strong signal, `analyst_rev`, is unusable because it is recorded at the end of month t+1.
- The sample covers only 120 months and 500 large-index stocks, so power is limited.
- No transaction costs, market impact, or capacity constraints are considered.
- Multiple hypothesis testing further weakens the marginal signals observed for `asset_growth` and `accruals`.
