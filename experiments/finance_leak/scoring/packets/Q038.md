# Standardized Unexpected Earnings as the Only Robust Cross-Sectional Predictor of Next-Month Returns

## Abstract
We evaluate ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month returns in a monthly panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Using monthly cross-sectional rank ICs and top-minus-bottom quintile long-short portfolios, only standardized unexpected earnings (SUE) is a statistically reliable signal: its mean rank IC is 0.0998 with a t-statistic of 23.25. The SUE long-short portfolio earns 3.04% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 7.31. All other features have economically small and statistically insignificant rank ICs. We propose a monthly-rebalanced long top-quintile, short bottom-quintile SUE strategy with an expected gross annual return of approximately 36.5%.

## Methods
The dataset is a point-in-time monthly panel of 500 broad equity index constituents over 120 months. The prediction target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month t+1. For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month and average it over all 120 months to obtain the mean rank IC. The IC t-statistic is calculated as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation and multiplied by the square root of the number of months. We also form equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month and report the top-minus-bottom long-short return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

We use a conventional statistical significance threshold of |t-stat| > 2.0. A feature is considered a valid signal only if its mean rank IC is statistically distinguishable from zero. Direction is determined by the sign of the mean rank IC and long-short return.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal evaluation. All ten features are listed with their mean rank IC, IC t-statistic, and the resulting decision.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-short monthly return | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| sue | 0.0998 | 23.25 | 3.04% | Valid, positive |
| asset_growth | 0.0061 | 1.64 | 0.28% | Reject |
| accruals | 0.0055 | 1.34 | 0.29% | Reject |
| idio_vol | 0.0043 | 1.14 | 0.03% | Reject |
| gross_prof | 0.0039 | 0.93 | 0.10% | Reject |
| log_mktcap | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.03% | Reject |
| turnover | 0.0001 | 0.02 | 0.01% | Reject |
| rd_intensity | -0.0002 | -0.05 | 0.05% | Reject |
| book_to_market | -0.0030 | -0.72 | -0.11% | Reject |
| leverage | -0.0043 | -1.00 | -0.02% | Reject |

SUE is the only feature with a highly significant mean rank IC. Its direction is positive: stocks with higher standardized unexpected earnings earn higher next-month returns. The magnitude is economically large, with a mean rank IC of 0.0998 and a top-minus-bottom quintile return of 3.04% per month. The IC is positive in 99% of months, indicating strong consistency over the sample.

## Discussion
The results strongly support a trading strategy based on SUE. Each month, stocks are ranked by SUE; the strategy buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, and holds for one month before rebalancing. The expected gross return of this long-short strategy is 3.04% per month, which annualizes to approximately 36.5% per year. The strategy has an annualized Sharpe ratio of 7.31 based on the monthly long-short returns.

This performance is economically large and statistically robust within the sample. Other common characteristics such as book-to-market, gross profitability, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity do not contribute reliable cross-sectional return forecasts in this dataset.

## Limitations
- The performance estimates are gross of transaction costs, borrow fees, and price impact, which would substantially reduce net returns.
- Shorting the bottom SUE quintile may be difficult or costly, especially for small or hard-to-borrow stocks.
- The sample is limited to 500 index constituents over 120 months, so the results may not generalize to other markets or periods.
- The extremely high Sharpe ratio may partly reflect in-sample overfitting or data-snooping.
- SUE relies on consensus earnings forecasts and may be subject to data availability or coverage biases.
