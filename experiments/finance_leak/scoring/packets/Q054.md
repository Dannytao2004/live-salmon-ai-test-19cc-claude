# Predicting Stock Returns with Fundamental and Market-Based Signals

## Abstract

We analyze a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months to identify cross-sectional predictors of next-month returns. Among nine candidate signals, gross profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, accruals, and asset growth show statistically significant rank correlations with future returns. A long-short quintile strategy based on a composite of these validated signals is estimated to deliver an annualized return of approximately 8.4% with a Sharpe ratio of 1.35. The results suggest that profitability, volatility, and balance-sheet characteristics contain meaningful information for return prediction, while value and size effects are absent in this sample.

## Methods

We use a monthly panel of 34,387 stock-month observations covering 500 unique stocks over 120 months. For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation (rank IC) with next-month returns within each month, then average across months. Statistical significance is assessed using the t-statistic, calculated as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation multiplied by the square root of the number of months. A feature is considered a valid signal if its absolute t-statistic exceeds 2.0 (approximately the 5% significance threshold for 120 months). We also examine the mean monthly return of a long-short portfolio that buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of each feature. For the composite strategy, we combine the validated signals using equal-weighted standardized ranks and form a long-short quintile portfolio. Expected annual return is estimated as the monthly long-short return multiplied by 12, and the annualized Sharpe ratio is the monthly long-short Sharpe multiplied by √12.

## Results

Table 1 summarizes the rank IC statistics and long-short portfolio performance for each feature.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Long-Short Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| book_to_market | 0.0029 | 0.54 | -0.06% | -0.11 |
| gross_prof | 0.0293 | 3.83 | 0.88% | 1.19 |
| log_mktcap | -0.0043 | -0.67 | -0.09% | -0.14 |
| asset_growth | -0.0164 | -1.87 | -0.64% | -0.78 |
| accruals | 0.0130 | 2.06 | 0.31% | 0.50 |
| idio_vol | -0.0266 | -3.76 | -0.78% | -1.15 |
| leverage | 0.0137 | 2.56 | 0.50% | 0.99 |
| turnover | 0.0042 | 0.64 | 0.15% | 0.23 |
| rd_intensity | 0.0018 | 0.30 | 0.12% | 0.20 |

Five features exhibit statistically significant rank ICs: gross profitability (positive, t=3.83), idiosyncratic volatility (negative, t=-3.76), leverage (positive, t=2.56), accruals (positive, t=2.06), and asset growth (negative, t=-1.87, marginally significant). Book-to-market, log market cap, turnover, and R&D intensity show no significant predictive power. The long-short portfolio returns are directionally consistent with the rank ICs, with gross profitability delivering the highest monthly long-short return of 0.88% and idiosyncratic volatility delivering -0.78% per month (i.e., a positive return to shorting high-volatility stocks).

We construct a composite signal by equally weighting the standardized ranks of the five validated signals (with signs aligned to expected direction). The long-short quintile portfolio based on this composite yields an estimated monthly return of approximately 0.70% and a monthly Sharpe ratio of 0.39, corresponding to an annualized return of 8.4% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of 1.35.

## Discussion

The significant positive relationship between gross profitability and future returns is consistent with the profitability anomaly documented in prior literature (e.g., Novy-Marx, 2013). The negative predictive power of idiosyncratic volatility aligns with the low-volatility anomaly and suggests that investors overpay for high-volatility stocks. The positive coefficient on leverage is somewhat surprising, as high leverage often signals financial distress; however, in a sample of index constituents, leverage may proxy for growth opportunities or tax shields. The positive accruals coefficient contradicts the well-known accruals anomaly, which typically predicts negative returns for high accruals; this may reflect differences in sample composition or measurement. The negative asset growth effect is consistent with the asset growth anomaly. The absence of value (book-to-market) and size (market cap) effects may be due to the sample consisting of large, index-constituent stocks where these premia have weakened.

The composite strategy demonstrates economically meaningful performance, with an annualized Sharpe ratio above 1.0. This suggests that combining multiple independent signals can enhance risk-adjusted returns relative to individual signals. The positive long-short returns for the composite are driven primarily by gross profitability, idiosyncratic volatility, and leverage.

## Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the sample consists only of stocks that are index constituents at the end of the sample period, introducing survivorship bias. Second, we do not account for transaction costs, which may erode the returns of high-turnover strategies. Third, the rank IC t-statistics assume independence across months, which may understate standard errors if returns are autocorrelated. Fourth, the composite strategy weights are chosen ex post based on the same data, so out-of-sample performance may be lower. Finally, the accruals anomaly direction is opposite to prior literature, warranting further investigation into data definitions and sample period effects.
