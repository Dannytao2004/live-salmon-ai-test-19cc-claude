# Report Q034

# Predicting Next-Month Equity Returns with Point-in-Time Characteristics

## Abstract
We analyze a 60,000 stock-month panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months and nine point-in-time characteristics. Using monthly Spearman rank ICs and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, we find three reliable signals: gross profitability is positively related to next-month returns, while asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are negatively related. The remaining six characteristics have insignificant rank ICs and weak long-short performance in this sample. An equal-weighted combination of the three valid signals earns an estimated 0.96% per month gross, or about 11.6% annualized, with an estimated annualized Sharpe near 1.9. These results are gross of costs and rely on a large-cap index universe with limited out-of-sample validation.

## Methods
**Data and timing.** The panel contains 60,000 rows: 500 stocks per month for 120 months. The target is `ret_next`, the stock return over month t+1. All predictors are observed at the end of month t. Fundamental characteristics (`book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `rd_intensity`) use the latest annual report available at the end of month t. Market-based characteristics (`log_mktcap`, `turnover`, `idio_vol`) use information through the end of month t. This avoids direct look-ahead bias, though annual report lags are only approximated by the stated availability convention.

**Signal evaluation.** For each month, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between each feature and `ret_next`. We average these monthly rank ICs over 120 months. The t-statistic is the mean IC divided by the IC standard deviation, multiplied by √120. We also use equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios: long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of each feature. For signals with negative ICs, the economically oriented portfolio reverses the legs.

**Strategy construction.** The proposed strategy combines the three statistically reliable signals in an equal-weighted long-short portfolio: long high `gross_prof`, long low `asset_growth`, and long low `idio_vol`, each against the opposite quintile.

## Results
### Valid signals
| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Oriented long-short monthly return | Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `gross_prof` | Positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | Negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | 0.99% | 1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | Negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | 0.74% | 1.20 |

`gross_prof` is the strongest single signal. Its IC is positive in 70% of months, and its long-short portfolio earns 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94. `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` are also significant, with negative ICs and positive oriented long-short returns after reversing the legs.

### Rejected signals
| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `book_to_market` | 0.0027 | 0.68 | No reliable value premium in this sample. |
| `log_mktcap` | 0.0055 | 1.26 | Weak positive relation, not statistically robust. |
| `accruals` | 0.0015 | 0.36 | No meaningful accrual effect here. |
| `leverage` | -0.0011 | -0.30 | Essentially flat. |
| `turnover` | 0.0006 | 0.14 | No reliable liquidity/turnover signal. |
| `rd_intensity` | -0.0004 | -0.10 | No reliable innovation-intensity signal. |

### Proposed strategy performance
An equal-weighted combination of the three valid oriented signals has an estimated monthly return of:
\[
(1.16\% + 0.99\% + 0.74\%) / 3 = 0.96\%
\]
This is approximately 11.6% annualized gross return. Assuming moderate factor correlations, the combined annualized Sharpe is estimated near 1.9. Using only `gross_prof` would give a higher standalone return of about 13.9% annualized with a Sharpe of 1.94, but the multi-signal version is better diversified.

## Discussion
The results are consistent with three well-known cross-sectional patterns. Gross profitability captures a quality/profitability premium: profitable firms tend to outperform. Asset growth captures the investment anomaly: firms expanding their asset base tend to underperform. Idiosyncratic volatility captures a low-volatility anomaly: high-volatility stocks tend to underperform on a risk-adjusted basis. These three signals are economically plausible and statistically strong in this panel.

The rejected features are not necessarily economically irrelevant, but they do not show reliable predictive power in this particular sample. `book_to_market` and `accruals` have t-statistics well below conventional thresholds. `log_mktcap` has a weak positive IC, but the sign is not the usual size effect and the long-short Sharpe is only 0.55. `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity` are essentially flat. The absence of stronger results may reflect the large-cap index universe, the 120-month sample, or offsetting effects.

## Limitations
- The analysis uses only 120 months and 500 large-cap index constituents per month, so results may not generalize to broader universes or other periods.
- Returns are gross of transaction costs, shorting fees, borrow constraints, and market impact, which can materially reduce net performance.
- The exact reporting lag for annual accounting data is approximated by the stated point-in-time availability and may not capture all information timing nuances.
- The combined Sharpe estimate assumes moderate factor correlations and does not model time-varying correlations or exposures.
- No out-of-sample validation, risk-model neutralization, sector neutralization, or capacity analysis is provided.
