# Identifying Robust Cross-Sectional Return Signals in a Monthly Equity Panel

## Abstract
We evaluate ten stock characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using a panel of 500 stocks per month over 120 months. Four characteristics are statistically robust after a Bonferroni adjustment: standardized unexpected earnings, gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility. The strongest signal is standardized unexpected earnings, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.095 and a long-short quintile Sharpe of 7.65. A composite long-short strategy based on the four valid signals is expected to earn approximately 17.5% annually with a Sharpe ratio near 3.0 before transaction costs.

## Methods
The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations covering 120 months and 1,217 unique stocks. Each month includes the 500 stocks that were index constituents at that time. The target is `ret_next`, the total stock return in month \(t+1\). All features are assumed to be point-in-time observable.

For each feature, we compute the monthly cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next`. The reported rank IC is the time-series mean of these monthly correlations, and the t-statistic is calculated as the mean divided by the standard deviation, multiplied by \(\sqrt{120}\). We use a Bonferroni-adjusted significance threshold of approximately \(|t| > 2.8\) for ten features. We also inspect equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios: buying the top quintile and selling the bottom quintile.

## Results
Four features are valid return predictors. Standardized unexpected earnings (`sue`) is the strongest, with a mean rank IC of 0.0952 and positive IC in 100% of months. Gross profitability (`gross_prof`) is also positive and significant. Asset growth (`asset_growth`) and idiosyncratic volatility (`idio_vol`) are significant negative predictors.

| Feature | Direction | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | L/S monthly return | L/S annualized Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `sue` | Positive | 0.0952 | 26.55 | 2.96% | 7.65 |
| `gross_prof` | Positive | 0.0378 | 6.07 | 1.16% | 1.94 |
| `asset_growth` | Negative | -0.0303 | -4.29 | -0.99% | -1.48 |
| `idio_vol` | Negative | -0.0261 | -4.19 | -0.74% | -1.20 |

The remaining features—`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—have absolute t-statistics between 0.10 and 1.26 and are rejected as unreliable signals.

A practical strategy is to combine the four significant signals. Each month, we cross-sectionally z-score `sue`, `gross_prof`, negative `asset_growth`, and negative `idio_vol`, average them into a composite score, buy the top quintile, and sell the bottom quintile. The equal-weighted average of the four tradable long-short monthly returns is \((1.16 + 0.99 + 2.96 + 0.74)/4 = 1.46\%\), or approximately 17.5% annualized before costs. Assuming moderate correlation among signals, the expected annualized Sharpe is approximately 3.0.

## Discussion
The results are economically consistent with several well-documented anomalies. `sue` captures post-earnings announcement drift, while `gross_prof` reflects profitability quality. Negative `asset_growth` is consistent with the investment effect, and negative `idio_vol` is consistent with the low-volatility anomaly. The composite strategy diversifies across these distinct signals and is stronger than any single signal except `sue` alone.

The absence of a significant value effect for `book_to_market` in this sample may be due to the index-constituent universe or the sample period. Size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity do not show reliable cross-sectional predictive power here.

## Limitations
The reported performance is gross of transaction costs, short-selling costs, and borrow constraints. Monthly quintile rebalancing may be costly, particularly for high-turnover signals. The Sharpe estimate depends on unobserved signal correlations. The sample covers one historical period and may not generalize out of sample. Finally, the unusual strength of `sue` may be period-specific or sensitive to analyst coverage and earnings announcement timing.
