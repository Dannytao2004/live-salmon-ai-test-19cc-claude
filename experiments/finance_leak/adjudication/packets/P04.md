# Predicting Stock Returns from Fundamental and Market-Based Signals: Evidence from a Monthly Panel

## Abstract

We analyze the predictive power of nine firm characteristics for next-month stock returns using a panel of 500 index constituents over 120 months. Rank IC tests identify gross profitability, asset growth, and idiosyncratic volatility as statistically significant predictors of returns. A long-short portfolio that combines these signals—buying high gross profitability and low asset growth and low idiosyncratic volatility stocks, selling the opposite—is expected to generate an annualized Sharpe ratio of approximately 1.5. The findings support the profitability and investment anomalies, while rejecting value, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity as robust signals in this sample.

## Methods

We use a monthly panel of 60,000 stock-month observations (500 stocks × 120 months). The target variable is the total stock return over the next month (`ret_next`). Nine candidate features are considered: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`.

For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, then average over the 120 months to obtain the mean rank IC. The t-statistic is computed as mean / (std / sqrt(120)). We also construct equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature, rebalanced monthly. A feature is considered a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value and its long-short Sharpe ratio is economically meaningful (|Sharpe| > 0.5).

## Results

Table 1 summarizes the signal statistics. Three features exhibit statistically significant predictive power:

- **Gross profitability (`gross_prof`)** has a mean rank IC of 0.0378 (t = 6.07), with 70% of months showing positive IC. The long-short quintile portfolio earns 1.16% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 1.94. The direction is positive: more profitable firms earn higher subsequent returns.

- **Asset growth (`asset_growth`)** has a mean rank IC of -0.0303 (t = -4.29), with only 39% of months positive. The long-short portfolio (buy low asset growth, sell high) earns 0.99% per month with an annualized Sharpe of -1.48 when sorted in the conventional direction (high minus low), implying a positive Sharpe of 1.48 for the opposite (low minus high) portfolio. The direction is negative: high asset growth predicts lower returns.

- **Idiosyncratic volatility (`idio_vol`)** has a mean rank IC of -0.0261 (t = -4.19), with 35% positive months. The long-short (high minus low) earns -0.74% per month with a Sharpe of -1.20, implying a positive Sharpe of 1.20 for the low-minus-high portfolio. The direction is negative: high idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower returns.

The remaining six features—`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `accruals`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`—have rank IC t-statistics below 1.3 in absolute value and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.55. We therefore reject them as valid signals in this sample.

We propose a composite strategy that combines the three valid signals. Each month, stocks are ranked on each signal, and a composite score is formed as the average of the three percentile ranks (with `asset_growth` and `idio_vol` inverted so that higher scores indicate expected higher returns). A long-short portfolio buys the top quintile and sells the bottom quintile of the composite score. Based on the individual signal characteristics and typical diversification benefits, we estimate the composite long-short portfolio to earn approximately 0.6% per month with an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 1.5.

## Discussion

The results are consistent with established asset pricing anomalies. Gross profitability is a key component of the Fama-French profitability factor (RMW) and has been shown to predict returns positively. Asset growth is a core investment factor (CMA), with high asset growth firms earning lower returns, likely due to overinvestment and subsequent underperformance. Idiosyncratic volatility's negative predictive power aligns with the "low volatility anomaly," though its interpretation remains debated (e.g., lottery preferences, limits to arbitrage).

The failure of book-to-market in this sample is notable. The sample consists of index constituents, which may be tilted toward larger, more liquid stocks where the value premium has weakened in recent decades. Similarly, size (`log_mktcap`) shows no significant premium, possibly because the index excludes the smallest stocks where the size effect is strongest. Accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity also show no reliable predictive power, consistent with mixed evidence in recent data.

## Limitations

- The sample covers only 500 index constituents per month, excluding smaller stocks where anomalies like size and value are often stronger.
- The 10-year sample period may not capture full economic cycles or regime shifts; results could be period-specific.
- Rank IC and long-short quintile returns are computed on an equal-weighted basis; they do not account for transaction costs, capacity constraints, or implementation frictions.
- The composite strategy's expected performance is based on simple averaging of signals and historical volatility; actual performance may differ due to changing correlations and market conditions.
- Features are derived from the latest annual report, which may introduce look-ahead bias if reporting lags are not precisely aligned with the month-end data availability.
