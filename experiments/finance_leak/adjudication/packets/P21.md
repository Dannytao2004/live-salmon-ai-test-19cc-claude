# Look-Ahead Bias in Analyst Revisions: A Cautionary Analysis of a Monthly Stock Panel

## Abstract
We examine 60,000 stock-month observations and ten firm characteristics for predicting next-month returns. The only feature with strong predictive power is `analyst_rev` (mean rank IC = 0.296, t-stat = 89.6; long-short return = 8.66% per month), but it is recorded at the end of month t+1 and therefore uses information from the return period. After excluding this look-ahead-biased feature, no characteristic has a statistically significant rank IC; the largest absolute t-statistic among the remaining features is only 1.64. A tradable long-short strategy based on the non-leaked features would have near-zero expected return. We conclude that the apparent alpha is an artifact of data timing rather than an investable signal.

## Methods
We use a monthly panel of 500 index constituents per month over 120 months, totaling 60,000 stock-month rows. The target is `ret_next`, the total return over month t+1. For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation with `ret_next` within each month, average it across months, and report the t-statistic of the mean IC. We also form equal-weighted long-short quintile portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature. All accounting variables are described as point-in-time at the end of month t. The critical exception is `analyst_rev`: it is recorded at the end of month t+1, so it is not available at the formation date for predicting month t+1 returns.

## Results
The signal statistics show that only `analyst_rev` has strong predictive power. Its mean rank IC is 0.2958, its IC t-statistic is 89.59, and its long-short portfolio earns 8.66% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 26.38. However, because `analyst_rev` is measured after the return month, this result is not tradable.

Among the remaining nine features, all are statistically weak. The largest absolute t-statistics are `asset_growth` (IC = 0.0061, t = 1.64) and `accruals` (IC = 0.0055, t = 1.34), neither of which crosses conventional significance thresholds. Other features have t-statistics near zero: `gross_prof` (0.93), `idio_vol` (1.14), `leverage` (-1.00), `book_to_market` (-0.72), `log_mktcap` (0.02), `turnover` (0.02), and `rd_intensity` (-0.05). Their long-short Sharpe ratios are also small, ranging from -0.27 to 0.72. With ten features tested, these weak results are consistent with noise.

## Discussion
The central finding is that the only strong predictor in the panel suffers from look-ahead bias. `analyst_rev` uses analyst forecast revisions from month t+1 to predict the return over month t+1, so it effectively incorporates information that would not have been known at the start of the return period. If one ignored the timing, a long-short strategy would appear extraordinarily profitable, but it cannot be implemented in real time.

After excluding `analyst_rev`, the evidence for tradable alpha is negligible. Several signs are also inconsistent with well-known anomalies: accruals are positively related to future returns here rather than negatively, and idiosyncratic volatility is positively related rather than negatively. This further suggests that the weak results are not robust economic signals but rather sample-specific noise. A prudent strategy is therefore to hold no active long-short position based on these features.

## Limitations
The sample covers only 120 months and 500 stocks per month, limiting statistical power for weak signals. The analysis ignores transaction costs, borrowing costs, and market impact, which would reduce any small long-short returns. Multiple testing across ten features increases the chance of spurious significance, and no formal multiple-testing adjustment is applied. The universe is limited to index constituents, so results may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks. The `analyst_rev` look-ahead bias highlights the need for careful point-in-time alignment; other subtle timing issues may remain. No risk model, sector neutrality, or factor hedging is used, so raw long-short returns may carry unintended exposures.
