## Title: Evaluating Cross-Sectional Return Predictors in a Broad Equity Index Panel

## Abstract
We investigate the predictive power of nine firm characteristics for next-month stock returns using a panel of 500 stocks over 120 months. Among these characteristics, only leverage demonstrates a statistically significant rank correlation with subsequent returns, with a mean monthly rank IC of 0.0164 (t-stat = 2.74). A long-short quintile portfolio based on leverage yields a monthly return of 0.52% with an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. The remaining eight characteristics fail to show reliable predictive ability, with rank IC t-statistics below 1.2 and long-short Sharpe ratios below 0.75. We propose a simple long-short strategy based on leverage and discuss its economic implications and limitations.

## Methods
We analyze a monthly panel of 34,708 stock-month observations covering 500 unique stocks over 120 months. The dataset includes nine candidate predictor variables: book-to-market, gross profitability, log market capitalization, asset growth, accruals, idiosyncratic volatility, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity. The target variable is the total stock return over the following month (`ret_next`).

For each feature, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation between the feature and `ret_next` within each month, then average these monthly rank ICs across all 120 months. We calculate the t-statistic as the mean rank IC divided by its standard deviation, multiplied by the square root of the number of months (120). We also construct equal-weighted long-short portfolios that buy the top quintile and sell the bottom quintile of each feature each month, and report the mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio.

A feature is deemed a valid signal if its rank IC t-statistic exceeds 2.0 in absolute value, indicating statistical significance at approximately the 5% level under a two-tailed test. Features with t-statistics below this threshold are considered not reliably predictive.

## Results
The table below summarizes the signal statistics for all nine features.

| Feature | Mean Rank IC | IC t-stat | Long-Short Monthly Return | Long-Short Annualized Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| book_to_market | -0.0017 | -0.28 | -0.05% | -0.08 |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | 0.12% | 0.22 |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | -0.07% | -0.14 |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | 0.37% | 0.74 |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | 0.24% | 0.41 |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | 0.13% | 0.19 |
| **leverage** | **0.0164** | **2.74** | **0.52%** | **0.92** |
| turnover | -0.0005 | -0.10 | 0.13% | 0.23 |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | -0.06% | -0.11 |

Only leverage exhibits a statistically significant rank IC, with a mean of 0.0164 and a t-statistic of 2.74. The positive sign indicates that stocks with higher leverage tend to have higher next-month returns. The long-short portfolio based on leverage produces an average monthly return of 0.52% and an annualized Sharpe ratio of 0.92. None of the other features have t-statistics exceeding 1.2, and their long-short Sharpe ratios are below 0.75, suggesting weak or no predictive power in this sample.

## Discussion
The finding that leverage is the only robust predictor is notable. In the academic literature, leverage has been shown to have a complex relationship with returns: high leverage can signal financial distress risk, which may be associated with higher expected returns as compensation for bearing that risk. The positive rank IC we observe is consistent with this risk-based explanation. However, the magnitude of the effect is modest: a monthly rank IC of 0.0164 implies that the feature explains only about 0.03% of the cross-sectional variance in returns, which is small but economically meaningful for a long-short strategy.

The absence of predictive power for well-known factors such as book-to-market, asset growth, and accruals is surprising. This may reflect the specific sample composition: the universe consists of stocks that are constituents of a broad equity index at the end of month 120, with their history back to index inclusion. This introduces survivorship and selection biases, as stocks that performed poorly and were removed from the index are excluded. Additionally, the sample period may not include sufficient economic variation to trigger the value or quality premia.

The long-short leverage strategy, with an annualized Sharpe of 0.92, would be attractive in practice, though transaction costs and short-selling constraints could reduce its net profitability.

## Limitations
This study has several limitations. First, the dataset is subject to survivorship bias because it only includes stocks that remain in the index at the end of the sample period. Second, the sample size of 500 stocks is relatively small, and the 120-month period may not cover a full economic cycle. Third, the analysis is based on rank IC and long-short quintile returns, which do not account for transaction costs, market impact, or short-selling constraints. Fourth, we did not adjust for multiple hypothesis testing; with nine features, one might expect at least one significant result by chance at the 5% level, although the leverage t-statistic of 2.74 is relatively robust. Finally, the positive leverage-return relationship may be driven by a few extreme observations or specific sectors, and we did not perform robustness checks such as excluding financials or using alternative leverage definitions.
