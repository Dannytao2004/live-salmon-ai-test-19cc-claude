# Report P09

# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Large-Cap Equity Panel: Evidence from Nine Accounting and Market Signals

---

## Abstract

We examine whether nine firm characteristics predict next-month cross-sectional stock returns in a panel of 500 broad-index constituents observed over 120 months (34,708 stock-months). Using monthly rank information coefficients (IC) and equal-weighted quintile long–short portfolios, we find that only one signal—**leverage**—is reliably associated with future returns (mean rank IC = 0.0164, t = 2.74, long–short mean 0.52%/month, annualized Sharpe 0.92). Seven other signals carry the theoretically expected sign but are statistically indistinguishable from zero, and book-to-market is essentially flat. Because the surviving signal is weak in absolute terms and the panel is dominated by large, index-member stocks, we conclude that the achievable edge is modest. A simple monthly long–short strategy on leverage earns an estimated ~6.4% annualized return with a Sharpe near 0.9, but this estimate is fragile given multiple testing and short-sample considerations.

---

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains one row per stock per month for 500 stocks over 120 months. The universe is the set of stocks that are index constituents at the end of month 120, with history backfilled to month 1 or to index-inclusion. Coverage grows from 143 stocks in month 1 to 500 in month 120, so early months are based on a survivor-biased, smaller cross-section.

**Features.** Nine characteristics are provided: book_to_market (B/M), gross_prof, log_mktcap, asset_growth, accruals, idio_vol, leverage, turnover, and rd_intensity. All are computed from information available at the end of month *t* (annual reports lagged to availability, market data through month-end).

**Target.** `ret_next` is the total return from the end of month *t* to the end of month *t+1*, in decimals (mean 1.82%, std 11.27%).

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature we use the provided monthly Spearman rank IC (correlation between the feature and `ret_next` across stocks within a month), averaged over 120 months, with a t-statistic computed as mean(IC)/std(IC) × √120. We also use the provided equal-weighted quintile long–short portfolio (top quintile minus bottom quintile) mean monthly return and annualized Sharpe ratio. We treat a signal as *valid* if |t-stat| ≳ 2.0 (roughly the 5% two-sided threshold) and the direction is economically interpretable.

**Strategy construction.** The proposed strategy ranks stocks each month on the single validated signal, goes long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, and rebalances monthly. We translate the reported long–short mean monthly return and Sharpe into annualized figures by multiplying the monthly mean by 12 and the Sharpe by √12.

**Sign conventions.** For leverage, a positive IC means higher leverage predicts higher next-month returns. For B/M, a positive IC would mean value outperforms; the observed sign is slightly negative.

## Results

**Signal-level statistics.** The table below reproduces the key results and classifies each feature.

| Feature | Mean rank IC | IC t-stat | LS mean (monthly) | LS Sharpe (ann.) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| leverage | **0.0164** | **2.74** | **+0.52%** | **0.92** | Valid (positive) |
| accruals | 0.0059 | 1.03 | +0.24% | 0.41 | Not significant |
| idio_vol | 0.0056 | 0.86 | +0.13% | 0.19 | Not significant |
| asset_growth | 0.0055 | 1.16 | +0.37% | 0.74 | Not significant |
| log_mktcap | 0.0014 | 0.26 | −0.07% | −0.14 | Not significant |
| gross_prof | 0.0012 | 0.23 | +0.12% | 0.22 | Not significant |
| rd_intensity | 0.0010 | 0.20 | −0.06% | −0.11 | Not significant |
| book_to_market | −0.0017 | −0.28 | −0.05% | −0.08 | Not significant |
| turnover | −0.0005 | −0.10 | +0.13% | 0.23 | Not significant |

**Leverage is the only signal that clears conventional significance.** Its mean rank IC of 0.0164 with a t-statistic of 2.74 implies a positive relationship: within a given month, more levered stocks tend to outperform less levered stocks. The quintile long–short portfolio earns 0.52% per month, or roughly 6.4% annualized, with an annualized Sharpe of 0.92. The IC is positive in 59% of months, consistent with a persistent but noisy effect.

**Several signals have the "right" sign but are weak.** Accruals (0.0059), idio_vol (0.0056), and asset_growth (0.0055) all show small positive ICs with t-stats near 1, and their long–short spreads (0.24%, 0.13%, 0.37% monthly) are directionally consistent with some prior literature but not statistically reliable here. Gross profitability and B/M are essentially flat, contrary to classic value/quality expectations.

**Magnitudes are small across the board.** Even the best long–short spread (leverage, 0.52%/month) is well under typical transaction-cost hurdles for monthly rebalancing of large-cap names, and the IC of 0.016 is at the low end of what is considered economically meaningful in cross-sectional equity prediction.

**Sample composition matters.** Because the panel is built from end-of-sample index members, early months reflect a smaller, surviving set of firms. This mechanically reduces the power of all tests and may attenuate ICs relative to a point-in-time universe.

---

## Discussion

The headline finding is that, among nine well-known characteristics, only **leverage** shows a statistically credible cross-sectional relationship with next-month returns in this panel. The positive sign is notable: while high leverage is often associated with financial distress and lower future returns in some settings, here it predicts *higher* returns, consistent with a risk-premium or "leverage effect" interpretation in a large-cap, index-member universe. Firms with more debt may carry higher systematic risk and thus earn a compensating premium.

The near-zero results for book-to-market, gross profitability, and turnover are surprising relative to the classical value, quality, and liquidity literatures. Three explanations are plausible. First, the universe is restricted to large, index-member stocks, where anomalies are known to be weaker and more arbitraged. Second, the sample is short (120 months) and the survivor construction reduces effective breadth. Third, the reported ICs are computed on a growing cross-section, so early, low-coverage months contribute noisy estimates.

From a practical standpoint, the leverage signal is tradable in principle but marginal in magnitude. A 0.52% monthly long–short spread before costs, with a Sharpe of ~0.9, is attractive on a risk-adjusted basis but vulnerable to implementation frictions. A prudent implementation would combine leverage with a neutralization for size and sector, and would scale positions by volatility.

We emphasize that with nine features tested, one t-statistic above 2.0 is close to what would be expected by chance alone (roughly 0.45 features at the 5% level under the null). The leverage result is therefore suggestive rather than conclusive, and should be validated out of sample.

---

## Limitations

- **Multiple testing:** With nine features screened, a single t-statistic of 2.74 is only weak evidence of a true effect; no correction for multiple comparisons is applied.
- **Survivorship bias:** The universe is defined by index membership at month 120, so delisted or removed stocks are absent, biasing results upward.
- **Growing cross-section:** Stock coverage rises from 143 to 500, so early-month ICs are estimated on small samples and may be unreliable.
- **Short sample:** 120 monthly observations give limited power, especially for signals with low IC-to-noise ratios.
- **No transaction costs:** Reported long–short returns are gross; monthly rebalancing of large-cap names would erode much of the 0.52% spread.
- **Single-factor analysis:** Each signal is evaluated in isolation; no multivariate regression controls for correlations among features.
- **No out-of-sample test:** All statistics are in-sample, so the leverage result may not persist.
