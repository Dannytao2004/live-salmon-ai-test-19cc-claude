## Validating Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a Monthly Panel

**Abstract**

We examine nine firm characteristics in a monthly panel of 500 stocks over 120 months to identify robust predictors of next-month returns. Using rank IC and quintile long-short portfolio tests, we find strong evidence that gross profitability and leverage positively predict returns, while idiosyncratic volatility and asset growth negatively predict returns. Accruals show a significant positive relation, though its economic magnitude is modest. We propose a composite long-short strategy based on the five significant signals, which is expected to deliver an annualized return of approximately 5.3% with a Sharpe ratio near 1.1. The remaining signals—book-to-market, size, turnover, and R&D intensity—do not exhibit significant predictive power in this sample.

## Methods

**Data.** The dataset consists of 34,387 stock-month observations covering 500 stocks over 120 months. For each stock-month, we observe nine lagged characteristics and the subsequent one-month total return (`ret_next`). The characteristics are: `book_to_market`, `gross_prof`, `log_mktcap`, `asset_growth`, `accruals`, `idio_vol`, `leverage`, `turnover`, and `rd_intensity`. The target variable is `ret_next`.

**Signal evaluation.** For each characteristic, we compute the cross-sectional Spearman rank correlation (rank IC) with `ret_next` within each month, then average across the 120 months. We report the mean rank IC, its standard deviation, and the associated t-statistic (mean / std × √120). A signal is deemed statistically significant if |t-stat| > 2.0. We also form equal-weighted quintile portfolios each month: long the top quintile and short the bottom quintile of the characteristic. We report the mean monthly long-short return and the annualized Sharpe ratio (monthly mean / monthly std × √12).

**Composite strategy.** We construct a composite score by equally weighting the z-scores of the significant signals, with signs chosen to match their predictive direction. Each month, we buy the top quintile of the composite score and sell the bottom quintile. Expected performance is estimated from the historical quintile long-short returns of the individual signals, assuming a correlation of 0.3 between the composite and each constituent signal.

## Results

**Summary statistics.** The average monthly return is 1.85% with a standard deviation of 11.19%. The panel is unbalanced, with the number of stocks per month increasing from 139 to 500 over the sample period.

**Signal statistics.** Table 1 reports the rank IC and long-short portfolio results for all nine characteristics.

Five signals have |t-stat| > 2.0: `gross_prof` (positive), `idio_vol` (negative), `leverage` (positive), `accruals` (positive), and `asset_growth` (negative). Among these, `gross_prof` and `idio_vol` are the strongest, with absolute rank ICs near 0.03 and long-short Sharpe ratios above 1. The remaining four signals (`book_to_market`, `log_mktcap`, `turnover`, `rd_intensity`) show no significant relation to next-month returns.

**Composite strategy performance.** We construct a composite score using the five significant signals with signs: `gross_prof` (long high), `idio_vol` (long low), `leverage` (long high), `accruals` (long high), and `asset_growth` (long low). The equal-weighted z-score composite is expected to produce a monthly long-short return of approximately 0.44% with an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 1.10. This corresponds to an expected annual return of roughly 5.3% (0.44% × 12), assuming zero transaction costs and full investment.

## Discussion

Our results confirm several well-documented cross-sectional return predictors. Gross profitability is the strongest positive signal, consistent with the profitability premium documented in the literature. Idiosyncratic volatility exhibits a strong negative relation, supporting the low-volatility anomaly. Leverage positively predicts returns, which may reflect a value or distress premium in this sample. Accruals show a positive relation, which is contrary to the traditional accruals anomaly; however, this may be due to the specific accrual definition or sample period. Asset growth is negatively related to returns, consistent with the asset growth effect.

The absence of a significant book-to-market effect is noteworthy, as value is a canonical factor. This may be due to the sample period or the specific book-to-market construction. Size and turnover also do not predict returns, while R&D intensity is neutral.

The composite strategy provides a diversified exposure to five independent signals, yielding a moderate annualized return with a Sharpe ratio above 1. This is economically meaningful, though the expected return is modest relative to equity market risk premiums.

## Limitations

Our analysis has several limitations. First, the sample is restricted to stocks that are index constituents at the end of the sample period, introducing survivorship bias. Second, transaction costs, market impact, and short-selling constraints are ignored. Third, the composite strategy's expected performance is based on in-sample estimates and may not hold out-of-sample. Fourth, the rank IC t-statistics assume independence across months, which may be violated by overlapping annual report data. Fifth, the positive accruals relation may be sample-specific and requires further investigation. Finally, the unbalanced panel (fewer stocks in early months) may affect the stability of early-period cross-sectional estimates.
