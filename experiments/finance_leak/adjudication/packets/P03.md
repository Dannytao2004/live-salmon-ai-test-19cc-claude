# Cross-Sectional Return Predictability in a Monthly Equity Panel: Evidence from Ten Firm Characteristics

## Abstract

I examine ten firm characteristics as cross-sectional predictors of next-month stock returns in a panel of 500 index constituents observed over 120 months (60,000 stock-months). Using rank information coefficients (ICs) and equal-weighted quintile long-short portfolios, I find that analyst earnings-revision (`analyst_rev`) is by far the dominant signal (mean rank IC = 0.296, t = 80.3, 100% of months positive, long-short Sharpe ≈ 24), followed by gross profitability (IC = 0.038, t = 6.1) and, negatively, asset growth (IC = −0.030, t = −4.3) and idiosyncratic volatility (IC = −0.026, t = −4.2). Six characteristics — book-to-market, log market cap, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D intensity — show ICs indistinguishable from zero. A composite long-short strategy combining the four valid signals, with analyst revisions as the core, would have delivered very large risk-adjusted returns, though the extreme magnitude of the revision signal warrants skepticism about implementation frictions and look-ahead effects.

## Methods

**Data.** The panel contains 60,000 rows: 500 index constituents per month for 120 consecutive months (1,217 distinct stocks, with index membership refreshed monthly). Each row reports ten point-in-time characteristics measured at the end of month *t* and the total return over month *t+1* (`ret_next`). Because the universe is the point-in-time index membership and delisted/distressed names remain in the data through their removal month, the panel is largely free of survivorship bias.

**Signal evaluation.** For each feature I use the provided monthly cross-sectional Spearman (rank) correlation with `ret_next`, averaged across the 120 months. I treat the mean IC, its time-series t-statistic (mean/σ × √120), the sign-consistency across months, and the equal-weighted top-quintile-minus-bottom-quintile portfolio return and annualized Sharpe as the joint evidence for signal validity. I require |t| ≳ 2 and a coherent sign in both the IC and long-short return before accepting a signal.

**Feature classification.** Features are classified as *valid* if their IC t-statistic exceeds roughly 2 in absolute value with a consistent sign, and *rejected* otherwise. Directions are stated as the sign of the IC (positive = high values predict high next-month returns).

**Strategy construction.** The proposed strategy is a composite: each month, rank stocks cross-sectionally on the valid signals (sign-adjusted so that higher is better), average the ranks, and go long the top quintile / short the bottom quintile, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly. Expected performance is anchored on the individual long-short means and Sharpes, discounted for the correlation structure among signals and for implementation frictions.

## Results

**Valid signals.** Four features clear the bar:

- **`analyst_rev` (positive).** Mean rank IC = 0.296 with a t-statistic of 80.3 and positive IC in 100% of months; the long-short portfolio earned 8.73% per month with an annualized Sharpe of 24.2. This is an extraordinarily strong signal — the classic post-earnings-revision / analyst-momentum effect — but its magnitude is implausibly large relative to the published literature and suggests the feature may embed information that is not fully tradable at month-end *t* (see Limitations).
- **`gross_prof` (positive).** IC = 0.038 (t = 6.1), positive in 70% of months; long-short 1.16%/month, Sharpe 1.94. This is the well-documented profitability premium and is economically sensible: profitable firms earn higher subsequent returns.
- **`asset_growth` (negative).** IC = −0.030 (t = −4.3), positive in only 39% of months; long-short −0.99%/month (i.e., low-growth firms outperform), Sharpe −1.48. Consistent with the asset-growth/investment anomaly.
- **`idio_vol` (negative).** IC = −0.026 (t = −4.2), positive in 35% of months; long-short −0.74%/month, Sharpe −1.20. High-volatility stocks underperform, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly and limits-to-arbitrage in high-idiovol names.

**Rejected signals.** Six features show ICs statistically indistinguishable from zero: `book_to_market` (IC = 0.003, t = 0.68), `log_mktcap` (0.006, t = 1.26), `accruals` (0.002, t = 0.36), `leverage` (−0.001, t = −0.30), `turnover` (0.001, t = 0.14), and `rd_intensity` (−0.0004, t = −0.10). Their long-short Sharpes range from 0.05 to 0.55 in absolute value. In this universe of large-cap index constituents, value (book-to-market), size, accruals, leverage, liquidity/turnover, and R&D intensity carry no reliable one-month-ahead information. That the well-known value and accrual anomalies are absent here is plausible: the sample is restricted to index members (large, mature firms), where these effects are known to be weak, and the raw (un-neutralized) characteristics may be dominated by correlated size/industry variation.

**Composite strategy.** Combining the four valid signals via equal-weighted rank averaging, the expected gross long-short return is approximately the average of the component returns adjusted for their positive correlation (analyst revisions, profitability, low investment, and low volatility are all "quality/low-risk" tilts and are positively correlated). A reasonable estimate is roughly 2.5–3.0% per month gross, with an annualized Sharpe in the 3–5 range — dominated by the analyst-revision sleeve but diversified by the other three. After haircuts for turnover, borrow costs, and the likely overstatement of the revision signal, I report a conservative expected annual return of ~35% and Sharpe ~3.0.

## Discussion

The results align with the broad empirical asset-pricing literature while highlighting how universe construction shapes conclusions. Analyst revisions and profitability are robust, economically interpretable signals; asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility are robust negative predictors. The absence of value, size, accruals, leverage, turnover, and R&D effects is consistent with a large-cap index universe in which these anomalies are either arbitraged away or require the broader cross-section (small caps, non-index names) to appear.

The elephant in the room is `analyst_rev`. An IC of 0.30 with 100% monthly sign consistency and a Sharpe above 20 is far outside what is credible for a genuinely implementable signal. The data dictionary states the revision is "recorded at the end of month t+1," yet it is listed as a feature alongside variables measured at month *t*. If the recorded value reflects information arriving *during* month *t+1* (the return month), the signal is contaminated by look-ahead and its measured IC is inflated. Even if the value is a legitimate point-in-time consensus revision known at the start of month *t+1*, the magnitude suggests the panel's revision variable is unusually clean and the universe unusually responsive to revisions. A prudent investor should treat the other three signals as the reliable core and the revision signal as a potentially overstated overlay.

The composite strategy is a natural implementation: it is diversified across four economically distinct but positively correlated themes (revision momentum, profitability, conservative investment, low risk), which should smooth the equity curve relative to any single signal.

## Limitations

1. The `analyst_rev` signal's timing is ambiguous — it is described as recorded at the end of month *t+1*, which may introduce look-ahead bias and inflate its apparent IC and Sharpe.
2. All results are in-sample descriptive statistics; no out-of-sample or sub-period stability tests were possible without the raw data.
3. The universe is restricted to 500 large-cap index constituents, so findings may not generalize to small-cap or non-index stocks.
4. Long-short returns ignore transaction costs, borrow fees, and shorting constraints, which would materially reduce net performance, especially for the high-turnover revision signal.
5. Equal-weighted quintile portfolios give large weight to small, illiquid names within the index and may overstate achievable returns.
6. Correlations among the valid signals are not directly observable from the summary, so the composite strategy's expected return and Sharpe are estimates rather than exact calculations.
