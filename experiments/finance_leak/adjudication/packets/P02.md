# Point-in-Time Evaluation of Cross-Sectional Equity Signals in a Monthly Index Panel

## Abstract

We evaluate ten firm characteristics as predictors of next-month returns using a 120-month panel of 500 index constituents. After requiring statistical significance and point-in-time data availability, we identify gross profitability as a positive signal and asset growth and idiosyncratic volatility as negative signals. Standardized unexpected earnings is statistically the strongest signal but is rejected because the supplied definition likely uses actual earnings before their public announcement. An equal-weight long-short quintile strategy based on the three valid signals is estimated to earn 11.6% annually with an annualized Sharpe ratio of about 2.0 before transaction costs.

## Methods

The dataset contains 60,000 stock-month observations covering 120 months and 500 stocks per month. The prediction target is `ret_next`, the total stock return over month \(t+1\).

For each feature, we assess predictive power using monthly cross-sectional rank ICs. The reported rank IC is the Spearman correlation between the feature and `ret_next`, averaged across the 120 months. We use a t-statistic threshold of approximately \(|t| > 2\) for statistical significance. We also consider point-in-time availability: a signal is valid only if the information would have been known to an investor at the end of month \(t\).

All annual-report-based features are from the latest annual report available at the end of month \(t\), so they are point-in-time. Market-based features such as `log_mktcap`, `idio_vol`, and `turnover` are also measured at or before the end of month \(t\). However, `sue` requires special scrutiny: firms announce quarterly earnings in the month after the fiscal quarter ends. The supplied definition uses the most recent fiscal quarter ending on or before the end of month \(t\), which means that in quarter-end months the actual EPS may not yet be public. We therefore reject the supplied `sue` signal as likely affected by lookahead bias.

## Results

Three features pass both the statistical and point-in-time screens:

- `gross_prof`: mean rank IC = **+0.0378**, IC t-stat = **6.07**, long-short monthly return = **+1.16%**, annualized Sharpe = **1.94**.
- `asset_growth`: mean rank IC = **-0.0303**, IC t-stat = **-4.29**, long-short monthly return = **-0.99%** for the raw top-minus-bottom quintile. Reversing the portfolio gives **+0.99%** monthly.
- `idio_vol`: mean rank IC = **-0.0261**, IC t-stat = **-4.19**, raw long-short monthly return = **-0.74%**. Reversing the portfolio gives **+0.74%** monthly.

The remaining features are rejected because their rank ICs are statistically indistinguishable from zero or because of data availability:

- `book_to_market`: IC t-stat = 0.68, long-short Sharpe = 0.23.
- `log_mktcap`: IC t-stat = 1.26, long-short Sharpe = 0.55.
- `accruals`: IC t-stat = 0.36, long-short Sharpe = 0.36.
- `sue`: IC t-stat = 26.55, but likely contaminated by lookahead bias as discussed.
- `leverage`: IC t-stat = -0.30, long-short Sharpe = 0.05.
- `turnover`: IC t-stat = 0.14, long-short Sharpe = 0.06.
- `rd_intensity`: IC t-stat = -0.10, long-short Sharpe = 0.32.

## Proposed Trading Strategy

We propose an equal-weight combination of three long-short quintile portfolios, rebalanced monthly:

1. Long high `gross_prof`, short low `gross_prof`.
2. Long low `asset_growth`, short high `asset_growth`.
3. Long low `idio_vol`, short high `idio_vol`.

The historical average monthly long-short returns of these three sleeves are 1.16%, 0.99%, and 0.74%, respectively. The equal-weight expected monthly return is therefore approximately:

\[
(1.16\% + 0.99\% + 0.74\%) / 3 = 0.963\%
\]

This corresponds to an expected annual return of approximately **11.6%** before transaction costs. Assuming modest positive correlation among the sleeve returns, the expected annualized Sharpe ratio is approximately **2.0**.

## Discussion

The validated signals are economically intuitive. High gross profitability predicts higher future returns, consistent with the profitability anomaly. High asset growth predicts lower future returns, consistent with the investment and asset-growth anomaly. High idiosyncratic volatility predicts lower future returns, consistent with the low-volatility anomaly. These signals likely capture distinct dimensions of firm quality, valuation, and risk.

The rejection of `sue` is important. Although the summary statistics show extremely strong predictive power, the data dictionary implies that actual quarterly earnings may be used before they are announced. A properly lagged version of `sue` could be a valuable signal, but it cannot be validated from the supplied statistics.

## Limitations

- The supplied `sue` feature appears to suffer from lookahead bias and was excluded; a point-in-time lagged version would need separate evaluation.
- Expected strategy performance is based on summary statistics and an assumed correlation among signal sleeves, not on a full backtest.
- Transaction costs, short-selling costs, price impact, and capacity constraints are not considered.
- The sample covers only 120 months of index constituents, so results may not generalize to other markets or periods.
- Multiple testing across ten features may inflate the chance of false positive signals.
